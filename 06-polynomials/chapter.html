06 — Fast Polynomial Algorithms

Polynomial multiplication is the engine behind the next two chapters. This chapter explains the contracts behind NTT, arbitrary-mod strategies, division, product trees, evaluation, interpolation and Taylor shift.

1. Convolution and transforms

For \(C(x)=A(x)B(x)\), \(c_k=\sum_{i+j=k}a_ib_j\). Evaluating at sufficiently many points turns multiplication into pointwise products; interpolation returns the coefficients. FFT uses complex roots of unity, NTT roots in a finite field.

For transform length \(L=2^{s}\), a prime modulus \(p\) must satisfy \(L\mid p-1\) and a primitive \(L\)th root must exist. With \(998244353 = 119*2^{23}+1\), primitive root \(3\), lengths through \(2^{23}\) are available.

Transform contract

Complex FFT needs coefficient splitting and careful rounding. When the target modulus is arbitrary, prefer three NTT-friendly primes and reconstruct each coefficient by CRT, finally reducing modulo the target. The product bound, not the target modulus, determines how many primes guarantee integer reconstruction. If only the result modulo \(m\) matters, Garner reconstruction can avoid a huge integer. AtCoder Library’s convolution_ll is a reliable reference.

Complexity is \(O(n\log n)\) time and \(O(n)\) memory.

2. Polynomial division

Let rev_k(F) reverse coefficients after padding/truncating to length \(k\). For \(A=BQ+R\), \(\deg R<\deg B\), set \(k=\deg(A)-\deg(B)+1\). Reversal gives

\[ \operatorname{rev}_k(Q) \equiv \operatorname{rev}_k(A)\,\operatorname{rev}_k(B)^{-1}\pmod{x^k}. \]

Compute the FPS inverse (chapter 07), reverse back, and set \(R=A-BQ\). This costs \(O(M(n))\). Preconditions: field coefficients and nonzero leading coefficient.

3. Product tree, multipoint evaluation

For points \(x_i\), build a balanced tree \(P_v(x)=\prod_{i\in v}(x-x_i)\). At the root compute f mod P_root; descend with remainder mod P_child. At leaf \(i\), the constant remainder is \(f(x_i)\). Fast division at every level yields \(O(M(n)\log n)\).

Naive Horner is better when the degree or number of points is small; fast algorithms have large constants.

4. Interpolation

For distinct \(x_i\), let \(P(x)=\prod_i(x-x_i)\). Lagrange basis gives

\[ f(x)=\sum_i y_i\frac{P(x)}{(x-x_i)P'(x_i)}. \]

Evaluate \(P'\) at all points with the product tree. Put \(w_i=y_i/P'(x_i)\) at leaves and merge

\[ F_v=F_LP_R+F_RP_L. \]

The root is \(f\), in \(O(M(n)\log n)\). Duplicated points make \(P'(x_i)=0\); either reject inconsistent input or use Hermite interpolation.

For consecutive points \(0..n\), evaluation at arbitrary \(t\) can be done in \(O(n)\) using prefix/suffix products and \[ f(t)=\sum_i y_i\frac{\prod_{j\ne i}(t-j)}{i!(-1)^{n-i}(n-i)!}. \]

5. Taylor shift

Find coefficients of \(f(x+c)\). Since

\[ [x^k]f(x+c)=\sum_{i\ge k}a_i{i\choose k}c^{i-k}, \]

multiply by \(k!\) and rewrite as a convolution of reversed \(A_i=a_i i!\) with \(B_j=c^{j}/j!\), then divide the result by \(k!\). This is \(O(M(n))\), assuming factorials are invertible.

6. Product of many polynomials

Use a priority queue or divide-and-conquer product tree, multiplying similar degrees first. For total output degree \(N\), balanced multiplication is typically \(O(M(N)\log k)\). Always truncate to the maximum requested degree; for knapsack factors this can save orders of magnitude.

7. Failure checklist and sources

  • Transform length must divide MOD-1; it must also cover a.size+b.size-1.
  • Normalize negative residues before NTT.
  • CRT primes must support the same transform length.
  • Polynomial inverse requires nonzero constant coefficient; division instead relies on the divisor’s leading coefficient.
  • Multipoint interpolation requires distinct points.
