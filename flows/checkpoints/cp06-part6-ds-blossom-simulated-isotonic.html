Checkpoint VI — Data-Structure Graphs, General Blossom Matching, Simulated MCMF, and Isotonic Regression

Blind Diagnostic Recognition Across Virtual Hub Sparsification, Non-Bipartite Matching, Regret Heaps, Slope Trick, and Matroid Boundaries (Ch 22–Ch 24)

How to Use This Blind Recognition Checkpoint

Chapters 22 through 24 sit at the architectural frontier of competitive programming network flows. At this level, an explicit graph is either too dense to materialize (\(O(N^2)\) transitions across intervals, strings, or trees), non-bipartite (breaking standard flow reductions because odd cycles create half-integral LP vertices), too large for polynomial-time graph augmentation (\(N \le 2 \times 10^5\) with total flow \(F \le 10^{14}\), requiring regret-heap or slope-trick simulation of the residual network), or disguised as an order-constrained regression or matroid intersection problem.

This checkpoint tests your ability to read an untagged problem statement and choose the exact structural compilation before writing a single line of code:

  1. Read the untagged constraints and surface cues first. Every problem below omits chapter tags in its statement. Ask yourself:
    • Does an explicit flow or matching network have \(O(N^2)\) edges that factor through 1D ranges, 2D dominance boxes, bitwise prefixes, or Suffix Automaton subtrees (Ch 22.1)?
    • Is the underlying pairing graph non-bipartite (Ch 22.2–22.4), and if so, are we seeking a maximum matching (Edmonds' Blossom / Tutte Matrix) or falling into an NP-hard vertex-cover / odd-cycle trap (FM-22)?
    • Does \(N \ge 10^5\) forbid explicit MCMF (Ch 23), meaning we must translate augmenting cycles and residual back-edges into priority-queue regret keys, RLE Leftist Heaps on trees, WQS penalty binary search, or convex cost-function ternary search (FM-23)?
    • Is the objective a convex deviation \(\sum w_v |x_v - y_v|^p\) subject to poset inequalities \(x_u \le x_v\) (Ch 24.1 Isotonic Regression), an intersection of two matroids (Ch 24.2), or a non-intersecting planar DAG path determinant (Ch 24.3 LGV Lemma vs. FM-24)?
  2. Commit to a 4-Step Compilation Hypothesis before opening any collapsible callout:
    • Step 1 (Diagnostic Signal): Pinpoint the constraint bound and structural invariant that rules out naive DP, greedy, or raw Dinic/MCMF.
    • Step 2 (Algebraic / Logical Model): Write the indicator variables, dual potentials, residual path cost equations, or level-set slice cuts.
    • Step 3 (Mechanical Compilation): Specify the virtual hub topology, regret-heap state transitions, or closure cut capacities.
    • Step 4 (Toy Trace & Failure Guard): Test a 3–4 element trace and verify the boundary conditions (FM-22–FM-24).
  3. Use the Progressive Hints Sparingly: Open Hint 1 only if you cannot identify the target paradigm after 5 minutes; open Hint 2 to verify the algebraic reduction or heap key formula; open the Full 4-Step Compilation & Solution to audit your complete derivation.

1. Rapid-Fire Diagnostic Triage Drills

Before tackling the eight full-scale checkpoint problems, test your diagnostic instincts on these six rapid-fire triage scenarios. Each drill contains a genuine frontier reduction paired against a classic lookalike decoy or failure mode (FM-22–FM-24).

Drill 1.1 — Range-to-Range Virtual Hub Sparsification (Ch 22.1)

Scenario: You are given \(N \le 5 \times 10^4\) weighted intervals \([l_i, r_i]\) with traversal cost \(c_i\). From interval \(i\), a path may transition to any interval \(j\) whose index lies in \([L_i, R_i]\). Find \(K \le 10\) vertex-disjoint paths from interval \(1\) to interval \(N\) of minimum total cost.

NoteHint 1 — Structural Bottleneck

Why does naive MCMF TLE, and why can you not simply run \(K\) independent shortest-path passes without a residual network?

NoteHint 2 — Hub Orientation

Do you need a Segment Tree Out-Tree (root-to-leaves), an In-Tree (leaves-to-root), or both? Count the exact number of virtual vertices and edges.

TipTriage Resolution & Compilation — Drill 1.1
  • Verdict: Genuine Ch 22.1 + Ch 17 (Segment Tree Out-Tree Sparsified Small-\(K\) MCMF).
  • Why Naive Fails: Wiring \(i_{\text{out}} \to j_{\text{in}}\) directly for all \(j \in [L_i, R_i]\) creates \(O(N^2) \approx 2.5 \times 10^9\) edges.
  • Mechanical Compilation:
    1. Split each interval \(i\) into \(i_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{ cost } c_i} i_{\text{out}}\) to enforce vertex-disjointness and charge \(c_i\).
    2. Build a Segment Tree Out-Tree over indices \(1 \dots N\), where each internal node \(u\) connects downward to its children \(u \to \text{lc}(u)\) and \(u \to \text{rc}(u)\) with capacity \(\infty\) and cost \(0\), and each leaf \(j\) connects to \(j_{\text{in}}\) with \((\text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } 0)\).
    3. For each interval \(i\), decompose \([L_i, R_i]\) into \(O(\log N)\) canonical Segment Tree nodes \(u_1, \dots, u_k\) and wire \(i_{\text{out}} \to u_r\) with \((\text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } 0)\).
    4. Because \(K \le 10\) is tiny and initial edge costs \(c_i \ge 0\) need no SPFA pass (or one DAG DP pass), run at most \(K = 10\) iterations of Primal–Dual Johnson Dijkstra in \(O(K \cdot N \log^2 N)\) time!
  • Failure Mode Guard (FM-22a): Never give internal Out-Tree downward edges unit capacity (\(1\)); multiple paths from different intervals \(i_1, i_2\) may share the same Segment Tree ancestor node \(u\) on their way to distinct leaves in \(u\)’s subtree.

Drill 1.2 — Non-Bipartite Matching vs. Vertex Cover & Tutte Pfaffians (Ch 22.2–22.4 & FM-22)

Scenario A: Given a general undirected graph \(G = (V, E)\) with \(N \le 500\) vertices and \(M \le 10^4\) edges, find the minimum size of a vertex subset \(S \subseteq V\) such that every edge \(uv \in E\) has at least one endpoint in \(S\).

Scenario B: Given the same graph \(G = (V, E)\) with \(N \le 400, M \le 10^4\), find the size of a maximum matching \(\nu(G)\) AND identify every vertex \(v \in V\) that belongs to every maximum matching of \(G\).

NoteHint 1 — Bipartite vs. Non-Bipartite Duality

Does König’s Theorem (\(\tau(G) = \nu(G)\), minimum vertex cover equals maximum matching) hold when \(G\) contains odd cycles?

NoteHint 2 — Structural Characterization of Essential Vertices

In Scenario B, how does the Gallai–Edmonds decomposition \(V = D(G) \cup A(G) \cup C(G)\) (or the inverse of the symbolic skew-symmetric Tutte matrix \(T(G)\)) classify vertices that can avoid being matched in some maximum matching?

TipTriage Resolution & Compilation — Drill 1.2
  • Scenario A Verdict: DECOY / NP-Hard Trap (FM-22: General Graph Minimum Vertex Cover).
    • Diagnostic Proof: On a general graph with odd cycles, the LP relaxation of Minimum Vertex Cover has half-integral extreme points (\(x_v = 1/2\) on a triangle \(K_3\), giving LP value \(1.5\) while \(\nu(K_3) = 1\) and \(\tau(K_3) = 2\)). Minimum Vertex Cover on general graphs is NP-Complete; neither Max-Flow nor Edmonds’ Blossom can solve it!
  • Scenario B Verdict: Genuine Ch 22.3–22.4 (Gallai–Edmonds Decomposition / Tutte Matrix Inverse).
    • Method 1 (Combinatorial — Gallai–Edmonds Forest): Find one maximum matching \(M^*\) via Edmonds’ Blossom algorithm in \(O(V^3)\). Build the alternating Blossom forest rooted at all unmatched vertices \(R = V \setminus V(M^*)\) simultaneously. Every vertex \(v\) reached at an even distance from \(R\) (including \(R\) itself and all vertices inside contracted blossoms at even levels) belongs to \(D(G)\), meaning there exists an even-length alternating path from an unmatched vertex to \(v\) that shifts the matching to leave \(v\) exposed. Thus \(v\) belongs to every maximum matching if and only if \(v \notin D(G)\)!
    • Method 2 (Algebraic — Tutte Matrix over \(\mathbb{F}_p\)): Construct the \(N \times N\) skew-symmetric Tutte matrix \(T_{uv} = \pm \xi_{uv}\) for random \(\xi_{uv} \in \mathbb{F}_p\). By the Tutte–Berge and Schur-complement rank theorem, when \(G\) has a perfect matching, \(v\) can be exposed in a maximum matching of \(G \setminus \{v\}\) only if \(\operatorname{rank}(T) = N\) is impossible; in general, after augmenting \(G\) with \(\operatorname{def}(G) = N - 2\nu(G)\) universal dummy vertices connected to all \(v \in V\) so the augmented graph \(G'\) has a perfect matching, a vertex \(v \in V\) is inessential (\(v \in D(G)\)) if and only if there exists a dummy vertex \(d\) such that \((T(G')^{-1})_{v, d} \neq 0\) in a single \(O(N^3)\) matrix inversion!

Drill 1.3 — 1D Transportation: Prefix Balance vs. Regret Heap vs. WQS (Ch 23.1–23.2 & FM-23)

Scenario A: Given \(N \le 10^5\) points on a 1D line at \(x_1 < x_2 < \dots < x_N\) with supplies \(a_i\) and demands \(b_i\) such that \(\sum_{i=1}^N a_i = \sum_{i=1}^N b_i\), route all supplies to demands minimizing \(\sum_{i,j} f_{ij} |x_i - x_j|\).

Scenario B: Given \(N \le 2 \times 10^5\) stock prices \(p_1, \dots, p_N\), perform at most \(K\) disjoint buy-then-sell transactions (\(i < j\), profit \(p_j - p_i\)) maximizing total profit.

NoteHint 1 — Conservation Across Cut Points

In Scenario A, since total supply equals total demand, how much net flow must cross the segment \([x_i, x_{i+1}]\) in every feasible routing that has no opposing flows?

NoteHint 2 — Concave Transaction Count

In Scenario B, what is the shape of \(f(k)\), the maximum profit using at most \(k\) transactions, as \(k\) varies from \(0\) to \(N/2\)?

TipTriage Resolution & Compilation — Drill 1.3
  • Scenario A Verdict: DECOY (\(O(N)\) Prefix Flow Conservation).
    • Diagnostic Proof: When \(\sum a_i = \sum b_i\) on a 1D line (or tree), any optimal routing never sends flow simultaneously left-to-right and right-to-left across the same segment \([x_i, x_{i+1}]\). Therefore, the exact flow across segment \(i \leftrightarrow i+1\) is deterministically fixed as \(\left|\sum_{k=1}^i (a_k - b_k)\right|\), giving total cost \(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1} (x_{i+1} - x_i) \left|\sum_{k=1}^i (a_k - b_k)\right|\) in \(O(N)\) time with zero heaps! Regret heaps (Ch 23.3) are only needed when \(\sum_{\text{holes}} c_j > \sum_{\text{mice}} 1\) (unbalanced capacities where we must choose which holes absorb flow).
  • Scenario B Verdict: Genuine Ch 23.2 (WQS Binary Search + 1D Regret Heap / DP).
    • Mechanical Compilation: In the MCMF model where source supplies \(K\) units of flow along the time spine \(1 \to 2 \to \dots \to N\), each augmenting path corresponds to one transaction \([i, j]\) (or merging two adjacent transactions \([i_1, j_1], [i_2, j_2]\) into \([i_1, j_2]\) by cancelling the middle sell-buy pair). By MCMF augmenting-path monotonicity (Ch 17.2), the maximum profit \(f(k)\) is concave in \(k\). Apply WQS (Aliens) binary search on a per-transaction penalty \(\lambda \ge 0\), reducing the subproblem to unlimited transactions with fee \(\lambda\) in \(O(N \log(\max p_i))\) time!

Drill 1.4 — Tree \(L_2\) Isotonic Regression: 整体二分 + Closure vs. Slope Merge (Ch 24.1)

Scenario: Given a rooted tree \(T = (V, E)\) with \(N \le 1000\) vertices, target values \(y_v \in \mathbb{R}\), and positive weights \(w_v > 0\), choose values \(x_v \in \mathbb{R}\) satisfying \(x_{\text{parent}(v)} \le x_v\) for every non-root vertex \(v\), minimizing the weighted \(L_2\) error \(\sum_{v \in V} w_v (x_v - y_v)^2\).

NoteHint 1 — Poset vs. Tree Topology

What is the directional derivative of \(w_v (x_v - y_v)^2\) at \(x_v = \lambda\), and why does thresholding \(z_v = [x_v > \lambda]\) turn the recursion into Maximum Weight Closure (Ch 09)?

NoteHint 2 — Exploiting Tree Structure

Because the poset is a tree rather than a general DAG, can you bypass Dinic entirely?

TipTriage Resolution & Compilation — Drill 1.4
  • Verdict: Genuine Ch 24.1 (Tree \(L_2\) Isotonic Regression).
  • General Poset Compilation (整体二分 / Parallel Binary Search + Dinic):
    • For any test threshold \(\lambda\), an optimal block average \(x_S^*\) exceeds \(\lambda\) if and only if choosing \(z_v = 1\) (meaning \(x_v > \lambda\)) subject to closure constraints \(u \preceq v \implies (z_u = 1 \implies z_v = 1)\) yields negative directional derivative \(\sum_{v: z_v=1} 2 w_v (\lambda - y_v) < 0\), i.e., positive weight \(\sum_{v: z_v=1} w_v (y_v - \lambda) > 0\).
    • For \(L_2\) loss, the unconstrained optimum of a pooled component \(S\) is the weighted mean \(\bar{y}_S = \frac{\sum_{v \in S} w_v y_v}{\sum_{v \in S} w_v}\), which can also be found exactly by testing midpoints in 整体二分.
  • Tree-Specific Fast Compilation (\(O(N \log N)\) Convex Piecewise-Quadratic Hull Merge):
    • Because \(T\) is a tree (not a general DAG), closure on a tree is solvable in linear time, and bottom-up Slope Trick / Leftist Heap merging maintains the derivative \(F'_u(x)\) of the subtree convex cost function in \(O(N \log N)\) time without any flow calls!

Drill 1.5 — Graphic \(\cap\) Partition Matroid Intersection vs. Max-Flow Boundary (Ch 24.2)

Scenario: Given an undirected multigraph \(G = (V, E)\) with \(|V| \le 100\) and \(M \le 500\) colored edges where color \(k\) may be used at most \(c_k\) times, find the maximum-cardinality subset \(I \subseteq E\) that forms an acyclic forest in \(G\) and obeys all color quotas (Rainbow Spanning Forest).

NoteHint 1 — Why Max-Flow Cannot Enforce Acyclicity

Can an \(S\)-\(T\) flow network enforce that a chosen subset of edges in a general graph \(G\) contains no cycles?

NoteHint 2 — The Two Matroids

Identify \(\mathcal{M}_1 = (E, \mathcal{I}_1)\) and \(\mathcal{M}_2 = (E, \mathcal{I}_2)\), and state how the bipartite exchange graph \(\mathcal{D}(I)\) is constructed at each augmentation step.

TipTriage Resolution & Compilation — Drill 1.5
  • Verdict: Genuine Ch 24.2 (Graphic Matroid \(\cap\) Partition Matroid Intersection).
  • Why Flow Fails: A bipartite flow network can intersect two Partition Matroids (left degree quotas \(\cap\) right degree quotas), because both rank functions factor through disjoint vertex stars. However, the Graphic Matroid \(\mathcal{M}_1 = (E, \{I \subseteq E : I \text{ is a forest}\})\) has circuits equal to simple cycles of arbitrary length in \(G\), which cannot be encoded as a flow conservation or min-cut constraint on a fixed network!
  • Mechanical Compilation:
    • Maintain current independent set \(I \in \mathcal{I}_1 \cap \mathcal{I}_2\).
    • Build the bipartite exchange graph on \(I \cup (E \setminus I)\):
      1. Source set \(X_1 = \{e \in E \setminus I : I \cup \{e\} \text{ is a forest}\}\); for \(y \in (E \setminus I) \setminus X_1\), add directed edges \(x \to y\) for every \(x \in I\) on the fundamental tree cycle of \(y\) in \(I\).
      2. Sink set \(X_2 = \{e \in E \setminus I : \text{cnt}_I(\text{color}(e)) < c_{\text{color}(e)}\}\); for \(y \in (E \setminus I) \setminus X_2\), add directed edges \(y \to x\) for every \(x \in I\) with \(\text{color}(x) = \text{color}(y)\).
    • Find a shortest \(X_1 \to X_2\) path \(P\) via BFS (shortest in hop count to prevent shortcutting chordal dependencies!) and update \(I \gets I \triangle V(P)\) in \(O(r \cdot M \cdot |V|)\) total time.

Drill 1.6 — Non-Intersecting Lattice Paths: LGV Determinant vs. MCMF (Ch 24.3 & FM-24)

Scenario: Given an \(N \times M\) grid (\(N, M \le 1000\)) with obstacles #, count the number of pairs of vertex-disjoint monotone right-down paths from \((1, 1)\) to \((N, M)\) (excluding the shared endpoints \((1,1)\) and \((N,M)\)) modulo \(10^9 + 7\) (Codeforces 348D — Turtles).

NoteHint 1 — Counting vs. Optimization

Max-Flow can decide if \(2\) disjoint paths exist (\(0\) or \(1\)), and MCMF can maximize weight along \(2\) disjoint paths, but what tool counts the number of disjoint path tuples modulo \(10^9 + 7\)?

NoteHint 2 — Planar Non-Crossing Guarantee

Why does every crossing permutation \(\sigma = (2\;1)\) between sources \(A_1 = (1, 2), A_2 = (2, 1)\) and sinks \(B_1 = (N-1, M), B_2 = (N, M-1)\) force the two paths to intersect on the grid?

TipTriage Resolution & Compilation — Drill 1.6
  • Verdict: Genuine Ch 24.3 (\(2 \times 2\) Lindström–Gessel–Viennot Determinant).
  • Mechanical Compilation:
    1. Any two internal-vertex-disjoint paths from \((1, 1)\) to \((N, M)\) must leave \((1, 1)\) via \(A_1 = (1, 2)\) and \(A_2 = (2, 1)\) and enter \((N, M)\) via \(B_1 = (N-1, M)\) and \(B_2 = (N, M-1)\).
    2. By grid planarity, any path from \(A_1 = (1, 2)\) (top-right) to \(B_2 = (N, M-1)\) (bottom-left) must cross any path from \(A_2 = (2, 1)\) (bottom-left) to \(B_1 = (N-1, M)\) (top-right). Thus the non-crossing condition (FM-24) holds strictly!
    3. Run two \(O(NM)\) grid DPs from \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) to compute \(e(A_i, B_j) \bmod (10^9 + 7)\), the total number of monotone paths from \(A_i\) to \(B_j\), and return the \(2 \times 2\) LGV determinant: \[\det \begin{pmatrix} e(A_1, B_1) & e(A_1, B_2) \\ e(A_2, B_1) & e(A_2, B_2) \end{pmatrix} = e(A_1, B_1)e(A_2, B_2) - e(A_1, B_2)e(A_2, B_1) \pmod{10^9 + 7}.\]
  • Failure Mode Guard (FM-24): If the DAG is non-planar or \(A_1 \to B_2\) and \(A_2 \to B_1\) can bypass each other without sharing a vertex, \(\det M\) computes the signed sum \(N(\text{id}) - N((2\;1))\) rather than \(N(\text{id})\)!

Problem #233 (CP-VI.1) — Online Tree Dynamic Shortest Augmenting Path & 1D Spine Regret

Primary Problem ([NEERC 2016] Mole Tunnels — Luogu P6122): A complete binary tree has \(N \le 10^5\) vertices numbered \(1 \dots N\), where the parent of \(v > 1\) is \(\lfloor v/2 \rfloor\). Initially, vertex \(v\) contains \(c_v \ge 0\) units of food (\(\sum c_v \ge M\)). Then \(M \le 10^5\) moles arrive online one by one at vertices \(p_1, p_2, \dots, p_M\). Each mole must walk along tree edges to a distinct unit of food; every traversed tree edge costs \(1\) unit of distance. After each mole \(k = 1, \dots, M\) arrives, output the minimum total distance walked by the first \(k\) moles.

Companion Variant (Codeforces 802O — April Fools’ Problem Hard): Over \(N \le 5 \times 10^5\) days, preparing a problem on day \(i\) costs \(a_i\) and printing a prepared problem on day \(j \ge i\) costs \(b_j\) (at most one prepared and one printed per day). Prepare and print exactly \(K\) problems at minimum total cost.

NoteHint 1 — Recognizing the Residual Network Structure
  • For Mole Tunnels: Notice that moles arrive online and each arrival corresponds to one unit of flow augmentation from \(p_k\) to the sink \(T\) in the tree network. Why can a new mole at \(p_k\) traverse a tree edge \((u, \lfloor u/2 \rfloor)\) with cost \(-1\) instead of \(+1\)? And what special property does a complete binary tree on \(N \le 10^5\) nodes have regarding ancestor depth?
  • For April Fools’ Hard: This is a 1D time-spine network where flow enters at day \(i\) (cost \(a_i\)), travels rightward \(i \to i+1\), and exits at day \(j \ge i\) (cost \(b_j\)) with total flow fixed at \(K\). How do you decouple the exact cardinality constraint \(K\) before sweeping the 1D spine?
NoteHint 2 — State Maintenance & Regret Keys
  • For Mole Tunnels: Every simple augmenting path from \(p_k\) to some available food node \(v^*\) in the residual tree decomposes at its Lowest Common Ancestor \(L = \operatorname{LCA}(p_k, v^*)\) into an upward walk \(p_k \to L\) and a downward walk \(L \to v^*\). Because tree height is \(\lfloor \log_2 N \rfloor \le 17\), each node \(u\) only needs to maintain the minimum residual cost \(f[u]\) (and target leaf best[u]) to reach a node with \(c_v > 0\) inside \(u\)’s subtree!

      • R_slope[u]: the asymptotic slope of \(F_u(x)\) as \(x \to +\infty\), initialized to \(+w_u\) (from \(w_u |x - y_u|\)).

Initially for node \(u\)’s own term \(w_u |x - y_u|\), insert (y_u, 2 * w_u) into \(H_u\) (since the slope jumps from \(-w_u\) to \(+w_u\) at \(x = y_u\)).
    • Merging Children: For each child \(c\) of \(u\), after converting \(F_c \to \tilde{F}_c\) (so R_slope[c] == 0), merge \(H_c\) into \(H_u\) in \(O(\log N)\) time (and add R_slope[c] = 0 to R_slope[u]).
    • Suffix-Minimum Truncation (\(F_u \to \tilde{F}_u\)): To flatten all positive slopes on the right of \(F_u(x)\) so that R_slope[u] becomes \(0\):
      • While R_slope[u] > 0:
        • Pop the largest breakpoint \((x_{\max}, \Delta)\) from the max-heap \(H_u\).
        • If \(\text{R\_slope}[u] \ge \Delta\): subtract \(\text{R\_slope}[u] \gets \text{R\_slope}[u] - \Delta\) (discarding this breakpoint completely).
        • Else (\(\text{R\_slope}[u] < \Delta\)): reduce \(\Delta \gets \Delta - \text{R\_slope}[u]\), set \(\text{R\_slope}[u] \gets 0\), push \((x_{\max}, \Delta)\) back into \(H_u\), and break!
    • At the root \(r\), after computing \(\tilde{F}_r(x)\), evaluate \(\tilde{F}_r(-\infty)\) by summing the intercept at \(+\infty\) (or accumulating the cost shift during each slope truncation: truncating \(\delta\) units of positive slope at \(x_{\max}\) shifts the left asymptote or accumulates exact minimum value!) in \(O(N \log N)\) total time!
  4. Step 4 — Toy Trace & Failure Guard:
    • Suppose parent \(1\) (\(y_1 = 10, w_1 = 1\)) has child \(2\) (\(y_2 = 4, w_2 = 1\)) with constraint \(x_1 \le x_2\).
      • Child \(2\): \(F_2(x) = |x - 4|\) has R_slope = 1 and \(H_2 = \{(4, 2)\}\). Truncating \(F_2 \to \tilde{F}_2\) reduces (4, 2) to (4, 1) and sets R_slope[2] = 0 (representing \(\tilde{F}_2(x_1) = \max(4 - x_1, 0)\)).

TipFull 4-Step Compilation & Solution — Problem #240

Part A: Joyner–Edmonds General Graph Vertex Deletion Game (Ch 06.3 + Ch 22.2–22.4)

  1. Step 1 — Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal:
    • Impartial token-movement games without vertex repetition are isomorphic to testing whether \(v_0\) is an essential (critical) vertex of maximum matching: \[\text{Alice wins from } v_0 \iff \nu(G \setminus \{v_0\}) = \nu(G) - 1 \iff v_0 \notin D(G).\]
    • Because \(G\) is a general undirected graph (\(N \le 500\)), bipartite flow fails (FM-22), requiring Edmonds’ Blossom + Gallai–Edmonds Decomposition (Ch 22.2–22.3) or Tutte Matrix Inversion (Ch 22.4).
  2. Step 2 — Proof That the Matching Game Theorem Holds on Non-Bipartite Graphs:
    • Case 1 (\(v_0\) belongs to every maximum matching of \(G\)): Fix any maximum matching \(M^*\) of \(G\). Alice’s first move goes along the matched edge \((v_0, u_1) \in M^*\). If Bob ever moves from \(u_k\) to an \(M^*\)-unmatched vertex \(w\), then the trajectory \(v_0 - u_1 - v_1 - \dots - u_k - w\) is an even-length \(M^*\)-alternating path from \(v_0\) to an unmatched vertex \(w\); flipping \(M^*\) along this path produces a new maximum matching \(M'\) of the same size \(|M^*|\) that leaves \(v_0\) unmatched, contradicting the hypothesis that \(v_0\) belongs to every maximum matching! Thus Bob is forced to always move into \(M^*\)-matched vertices, and Alice can always reply along \(M^*\).
    • Case 2 (\(v_0\) is omitted by some maximum matching \(M^*\) of \(G\)): Then \(M^*\) is a maximum matching of \(G \setminus \{v_0\}\). Wherever Alice moves first (\(v_0 \to u_1\)), \(u_1\) must be matched in \(M^*\) (otherwise \(M^* \cup \{(v_0, u_1)\}\) would be a larger matching in \(G\)!). Thus Bob steals the \(M^*\)-strategy on \(G \setminus \{v_0\}\) and wins!
  3. Step 3 — Mechanical \(O(N^3)\) Compilation for All \(N\) Vertices Simultaneously:
    • Approach 1 (Gallai–Edmonds Alternating Blossom Forest):
      1. Compute one maximum matching \(M^*\) of \(G\) using Edmonds’ Blossom algorithm in \(O(N^3)\).
      2. Initialize a BFS queue with all unmatched vertices \(R = V \setminus V(M^*)\) labeled as EVEN (type = 0).
      3. Run one pass of Blossom alternating-forest expansion from \(R\) (contracting odd cycles whenever two EVEN vertices share an edge, which marks all vertices inside the contracted blossom as EVEN!).
      4. A vertex \(v \in V\) can be left unmatched in some maximum matching (\(v \in D(G)\), so Bob wins) if and only if \(v\) is reached with label EVEN! Otherwise (ODD or unvisited, \(v \in A(G) \cup C(G)\)), Alice wins!
    • Approach 2 (Randomized Tutte Matrix over \(\mathbb{F}_p\), \(p = 10^9 + 7\)):
      1. Compute \(\nu(G) = \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{rank}(T(G))\) via Gaussian elimination.
      2. Add \(d = N - 2\nu(G)\) dummy vertices connected to all \(v \in V\) with random skew-symmetric entries to form an \((N + d) \times (N + d)\) invertible Tutte matrix \(T'\), and invert \((T')^{-1}\) in \(O(N^3)\).
      3. Alice wins from \(v_0\) iff \((T')^{-1}_{v_0, u} = 0\) for all \(d\) dummy vertices \(u \in \{N+1, \dots, N+d\}\)!
  4. Step 4 — Toy Trace & Failure Guard:
    • Trace on a Paw Graph: triangle \(\{1, 2, 3\}\) plus pendant edge \((3, 4)\). Here \(\nu(G) = 2\) (unique maximum matching structure leaves one of \(\{1, 2\}\) unmatched while matching \(3 - 4\) and the other of \(\{1, 2\}\)).
      • Unmatched vertex in \(M^* = \{(1, 2), (3, 4)\}\)? None—\(G\) has a perfect matching of size \(2\)! Thus \(R = \emptyset\), \(D(G) = \emptyset\), and Alice wins from every vertex \(1, 2, 3, 4\)!
      • What if we delete vertex \(4\), leaving just the triangle \(K_3 = \{1, 2, 3\}\)? Then \(\nu(K_3) = 1\), say \(M^* = \{(1, 2)\}\) with \(R = \{3\}\) (EVEN). Edge \((3, 1)\) makes \(1\) ODD with mate \(2\) EVEN, and edge \((3, 2)\) closes an odd cycle \(\{1, 2, 3\}\), contracting the entire triangle into a single EVEN blossom! Thus \(D(K_3) = \{1, 2, 3\}\), and Bob wins from every vertex (Alice moves \(1 \to 2\), Bob moves \(2 \to 3\), Alice has no moves!).
    • Failure Guard: In Approach 1, when running the final Gallai–Edmonds forest pass from \(R = V \setminus V(M^*)\), you must still contract blossoms! On \(K_3\) with \(M^* = \{(1, 2)\}\) and \(R = \{3\}\), a naive alternating BFS without blossom contraction would mark \(3\) as EVEN, \(1\) as ODD, \(2\) as EVEN, and miss that \(1\) also becomes EVEN around the odd cycle \(3 - 2 - 1\)!

3.2 Part VI Failure Mode Self-Audit (FM-22–FM-24)

Before concluding Part VI, verify that you can immediately spot and defuse each of the following fatal failure modes:

  • FM-22a (Bidirectional or Unit-Capacity Hub Short-Circuiting in Ch 22.1):
    • The Trap: Wiring a single Segment Tree with undirected edges (or wiring both \(i \to \text{SegTree}\) and \(\text{SegTree} \to j\) on the same Segment Tree copy), or assigning capacity \(1\) to internal Segment Tree edges.
    • The Fix: Always build two separate trees—an In-Tree (leaves \(\to\) root) and an Out-Tree (root \(\to\) leaves)—and assign internal tree edges capacity \(\infty\) and cost \(0\).
  • FM-22b (Applying Bipartite Duality or Max-Flow to Odd Cycles in Ch 22.2–22.4):
    • The Trap: Assuming Minimum Vertex Cover equals Maximum Matching on a general graph, or running a naive alternating BFS without blossom contraction when computing the Gallai–Edmonds set \(D(G)\).
    • The Fix: On non-bipartite graphs, Vertex Cover is NP-Complete; use Gallai–Edmonds Decomposition (\(V = D(G) \cup A(G) \cup C(G)\)) with full odd-cycle blossom contraction (or Tutte Matrix inverse over \(\mathbb{F}_p\)) to identify essential vertices and maximum matchings.
  • FM-23a (Missing the Simultaneous Dual-Heap Regret Push in Ch 23.3–23.4):
    • The Trap: When matching a supply node \(a \in Q_X(l)\) and a demand node \(b \in Q_Y(l)\) at LCA \(l\) on a tree, pushing a regret key into only one heap (or forgetting to coalesce multiplicities \(k = \min(\text{cnt}_a, \text{cnt}_b)\) via RLE).
    • The Fix: Because a later ancestor \(l'\) of \(l\) can steal either the supply endpoint (cancelling \(b\)) or the demand endpoint (cancelling \(a\)), you must push (2 d(l) - b, k) into \(Q_X(l)\) and (2 d(l) - a, k) into \(Q_Y(l)\) simultaneously as single RLE pairs!
  • FM-23b (Confusing Pointwise Sum with Minkowski Sum in Slope Trick Ch 23 / Ch 24.1):
    • The Trap: Merging breakpoint \(x\)-coordinates when computing \(\min_{x_1 + x_2 = x} (F_1(x_1) + F_2(x_2))\).
    • The Fix: Merging breakpoint coordinates computes Pointwise Addition \(F_1(x) + F_2(x)\); Infimal Convolution (Minkowski Sum) merges horizontal slope segments (or shifts breakpoints by interval bounds).
  • FM-24a (Shortcut Augmentation in Matroid Intersection Ch 24.2):
    • The Trap: Augmenting along an arbitrary \(X_1 \to X_2\) path in the exchange graph \(\mathcal{D}(I)\) instead of a shortest path.
    • The Fix: Chordal edges in \(\mathcal{D}(I)\) correspond to coupled dependencies in the circuits of \(\mathcal{M}_1\) or \(\mathcal{M}_2\); only a path with the minimum number of edges (or minimum (cost, edge_count) in weighted matroid intersection) is guaranteed to preserve independence in \(\mathcal{I}_1 \cap \mathcal{I}_2\) after symmetric difference \(I \triangle V(P)\)!
  • FM-24b (Non-Planar Crossing Cancellation in LGV Determinant Ch 24.3):
    • The Trap: Using \(\det[e(A_i, B_j)]\) to count vertex-disjoint path tuples on a DAG where a twisted permutation \(\sigma \neq \text{id}\) can route \(A_i \to B_{\sigma(i)}\) without sharing any vertices.
    • The Fix: The Gessel–Viennot involution cancels intersecting path tuples of opposite parity; it equals the count of non-intersecting identity tuples \(N(\text{id})\) if and only if every non-identity permutation \(\sigma \neq \text{id}\) is topologically forced to intersect!
