Part I Blind Diagnostic Assessment (Chapters 01–04 · Untagged Contest Problems & Failure-Mode Triage)

When you solve an exercise at the end of Chapter 2 (“Residual Graph Geometry and Cut Edge Classification”) or Chapter 4 (“Flows with Demands and Lower-Bound Circulations”), the chapter heading itself hands you half the solution. Before reading a single word of the problem statement, you already know whether to run Tarjan’s SCC algorithm on \(G_{f^*}\), split vertices into \(v_{\text{in}} \to v_{\text{out}}\), or introduce super-terminals \(S^*, T^*\) for affine slack variables \(\tilde{f}(e) = f(e) - l(e)\).

In a live ICPC, NOI, Codeforces, or AtCoder contest, problems arrive completely untagged. You are given an English, Chinese, or Japanese narrative, a set of numerical constraints (\(N, M, K, V_{\max}\)), and a two-second time limit. Your most decisive competitive skill is blind diagnostic recognition: translating non-graph surface clues into an algebraic conservation law or cut polynomial, and then compiling that algebra into an exact capacity network without falling into any of the six foundational failure modes (FM-01–FM-06).

1. Rapid-Fire Diagnostic Triage Drills (FM-01–FM-06)

Before tackling the eight full contest problems in Section 2, test your diagnostic reflexes on six short modelling scenarios. Each scenario either contains a subtle modelling bug (FM-01–FM-06) or asks you to identify the minimal graph compilation fix. Formulate your diagnosis before expanding the answer callout.

Drill 1.1 — The Greedy Shortest-Path Deletion Trap

You are given a directed unit-capacity graph \(G = (V, E)\) (\(N \le 500, M \le 1000\)) and asked to find the maximum number of edge-disjoint directed paths from \(1\) to \(N\). A teammate proposes the following \(O(M^2)\) algorithm:

  1. Run BFS from \(1\) to \(N\) to find a shortest directed path \(P\).
  2. If no path exists, stop and return the count of paths found.
  3. Delete all edges of \(P\) from \(G\) and repeat from Step 1.

Construct a 4-vertex counterexample where this algorithm fails, identify the exact Part I failure mode, and state how the residual graph fixes it.

TipAnswer & Diagnosis — Drill 1.1
  • Failure Mode: FM-01 (Greedy Path Deletion without Reverse Residual Cancellation, Chapter 1).
  • 4-Vertex Counterexample: Let \(V = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}\) with five directed unit edges: \((1 \to 2)\), \((2 \to 3)\), \((3 \to 4)\), \((1 \to 3)\), and \((2 \to 4)\).
    • Suppose Step 1 finds the 3-edge path \(P_1 = (1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4)\) and deletes its three edges.
    • The remaining graph has only \((1 \to 3)\) and \((2 \to 4)\), which are disconnected! The greedy algorithm halts and reports \(1\) path, whereas \(2\) edge-disjoint paths exist: \((1 \to 2 \to 4)\) and \((1 \to 3 \to 4)\).
  • Exact Residual Fix: Instead of deleting \((2 \to 3)\), skew-symmetric flow conservation (Ch 01.2) sets \(c_f(2 \to 3) = 0\) and opens a backward residual arc \((3 \to 2)\) with capacity \(c_f(3 \to 2) = 1\). The second augmenting path \((1 \to 3 \to 2 \to 4)\) traverses \((3 \to 2)\), which algebraically cancels \(f(2 \to 3)\) from \(1\) back to \(0\) and splices the two prefixes and suffixes into \((1 \to 3 \to 4)\) and \((1 \to 2 \to 4)\).

Drill 1.2 — The Saturated-Edge Cut Illusion

After running Dinic’s algorithm to completion on a directed network \(G = (V, E, c, s, t)\), a contestant wants to list all edges \(e = (u \to v)\) that belong to at least one minimum \(s\)-\(t\) cut (可行割边). They output every original edge \(e = (u \to v)\) that is saturated (\(f^*(e) = c(e)\), i.e., \(c_{f^*}(u \to v) = 0\)).

Why is saturation insufficient, what is the smallest counterexample, and what exact \(O(V + E)\) test on \(G_{f^*}\) is required?

TipAnswer & Diagnosis — Drill 1.2
  • Failure Mode: FM-02 (The Saturated \(\implies\) Cut Edge Fallacy, Chapter 2).
  • Counterexample: Consider \(s \xrightarrow{1} u \xrightarrow{1} v \xrightarrow{1} t\) with an alternate parallel bypass \(u \xrightarrow{1} w \xrightarrow{1} v\).
    • The maximum \(s\)-\(t\) flow is \(1\). Suppose Dinic routes \(1\) unit along \(s \to u \to v \to t\).
    • Then edge \((u \to v)\) is saturated (\(f^*(u, v) = 1 = c(u, v)\)), yet the total capacity between \(u\) and \(v\) is \(2\) while the minimum cut of the network is \(1\) (either \((s \to u)\) or \((v \to t)\)).
    • In \(G_{f^*}\), the forward bypass \(u \xrightarrow{1} w \xrightarrow{1} v\) and the backward arc \(v \xrightarrow{1} u\) form a directed residual cycle \(u \to w \to v \to u\), so \(u\) and \(v\) belong to the same Strongly Connected Component (\(\operatorname{scc}(u) = \operatorname{scc}(v)\)) and can never be separated by any minimum cut!
  • Exact Residual SCC Criterion ([AHOI2009] 最小割 Theorem, Ch 02.2): Run Tarjan’s SCC algorithm on \(G_{f^*}\). A saturated edge \(e = (u \to v)\) belongs to at least one minimum cut iff \(\operatorname{scc}(u) \neq \operatorname{scc}(v)\), and belongs to every minimum cut iff \(\operatorname{scc}(u) = \operatorname{scc}(s) \land \operatorname{scc}(v) = \operatorname{scc}(t)\).

Drill 1.3 — The Unsplit Middle Bottleneck & Undirected Bridge Trap

In a 3-stage matching problem (Luogu P1402 酒店之王), each guest \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) must be assigned one compatible room \(r \in \{1, \dots, P\}\) and one compatible dish \(d \in \{1, \dots, Q\}\). A contestant wires \(S \xrightarrow{1} \text{Room}_r \xrightarrow{1} \text{Guest}_i \xrightarrow{1} \text{Dish}_d \xrightarrow{1} T\) using a single vertex for \(\text{Guest}_i\). Later, on an undirected vertex-cut problem, they split each vertex \(v\) into \(v_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{1} v_{\text{out}}\), and for each undirected edge \(\{u, v\}\) they add an undirected capacity-\(\infty\) edge between \(u_{\text{out}}\) and \(v_{\text{in}}\) (i.e., both \(u_{\text{out}} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{\text{in}}\) and \(v_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{\infty} u_{\text{out}}\)).

What goes wrong in both constructions?

TipAnswer & Diagnosis — Drill 1.3
  • Failure Mode: FM-03 (Unsplit Internal Vertex Capacities & Undirected Bridge Short-Circuiting, Chapter 3).
  • Bug 1 (Unsplit Guest Vertex): If Guest \(i\) likes two available rooms \(r_1, r_2\) and two available dishes \(d_1, d_2\), a single vertex \(\text{Guest}_i\) can absorb \(2\) units of flow from \(r_1, r_2\) and emit \(2\) units to \(d_1, d_2\), counting one guest as two satisfied people! Every middle bottleneck entity must be split into \(\text{Guest}_{i,\text{in}} \xrightarrow{1} \text{Guest}_{i,\text{out}}\).
  • Bug 2 (Reversed \(\infty\)-Arc on Split Vertices): Adding \(v_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{\infty} u_{\text{out}}\) allows flow arriving at \(v_{\text{in}}\) (say from some \(w_{\text{out}} \to v_{\text{in}}\)) to immediately exit through \(v_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{\infty} u_{\text{out}}\) without ever crossing the internal bottleneck arc \(v_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{1} v_{\text{out}}\)! An undirected edge \(\{u, v\}\) between vertex-split nodes must always be compiled as two separately directed \(\infty\)-bridges: \[u_{\text{out}} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{\text{in}} \quad \text{and} \quad v_{\text{out}} \xrightarrow{\infty} u_{\text{in}}.\]

Drill 1.4 — The Time-Expanded Infinite Loop & Missing Holdover Bug

In a discrete-time evacuation problem (Luogu P2754 家园), \(K\) astronauts at station \(0\) must reach the Moon (\(N+1\)) using periodic shuttles. A solver loops \(T = 1, 2, 3, \dots\), adding shuttle transit arcs \((u_{t-1}, T-1) \xrightarrow{H_i} (u_t, T)\) and running Dinic from scratch at each step \(T\) until \(\text{MaxFlow} \ge K\). However, they do not add arcs \((v, T-1) \to (v, T)\) for stations \(v\), and their code gets Time Limit Exceeded on an instance where the Moon is unreachable.

Identify all three defects in this time-expanded compilation.

TipAnswer & Diagnosis — Drill 1.4
  • Failure Mode: FM-04 (Time-Expanded Horizon Traps, Chapter 3).
  • Three Required Fixes:
    1. Missing Station Holdover Arcs: Without \((v, T-1) \xrightarrow{\infty} (v, T)\), astronauts who disembark at station \(v\) at time \(T-1\) vanish unless another shuttle leaves \(v\) at the exact same instant! Holdover arcs allow inventory/passengers to wait at station \(v\) across time steps.
    2. Unchecked Unreachability (Infinite Loop): Before starting the incremental time loop \(T = 1, 2, \dots\), you must verify via DSU or BFS on the static union of shuttle routes whether \(0 \leadsto N+1\) is reachable at all.
    3. Rebuilding Dinic from Scratch: Because adding layer \(T\) only appends new vertices and forward arcs to an existing network, you should keep the persistent residual network \(G_{f_{T-1}}\) and call dinic(S, T_sink) incrementally to augment only the newly unlocked flow!

Drill 1.5 — The Feedback-Arc Leakage Bug in Bounded \(s\)-\(t\) Max-Flow

To solve a Maximum \(s\)-\(t\) Flow problem with edge lower bounds \(l(e) \le f(e) \le u(e)\), a contestant adds the feedback arc \(e_{\text{fb}} = (t \to s, [0, \infty))\), computes \(M(v) = \text{in}_l(v) - \text{out}_l(v)\), and runs Phase 1 dinic(S*, T*). After verifying that Phase 1 saturates \(\Sigma^+\), they read off \(f_0 = \tilde{f}(t \to s)\) and immediately call Phase 2 dinic(s, t) on the residual network without modifying \(e_{\text{fb}}\). Their Phase 2 call returns \(\infty\). Why?

TipAnswer & Diagnosis — Drill 1.5
  • Failure Mode: FM-05 (Feedback-Arc Leakage & Slack Offset Omission, Chapter 4).
  • Why Phase 2 Returns \(\infty\): In the residual network after Phase 1, the feedback arc \(t \to s\) has residual capacity \(\infty - f_0 = \infty\), and its reverse companion arc \(s \to t\) has residual capacity \(f_0\)! Worse, if any \(s \leadsto t\) path exists, or directly along the reverse residual arc \(s \xrightarrow{f_0} t\), Phase 2 interacts with the artificial feedback edge. Specifically, if you leave the reverse residual arc \(s \to t\) active (or if you run dinic(t, s) for minimum flow while \(t \xrightarrow{\infty} s\) is still open), flow circulates around the artificial feedback loop!
  • Exact Protocol (Ch 04.2): After recording \(f_0 = \tilde{f}(t \to s) = \text{cap}[e_{\text{fb}} \oplus 1]\), you must zero out both half-edges of the feedback arc (cap[e_fb] = cap[e_fb ^ 1] = 0) before calling Phase 2 dinic(s, t) (for \(F_{\max} = f_0 + \Delta^+\)) or dinic(t, s) (for \(F_{\min} = f_0 - \Delta^-\)). Finally, reconstruct each edge’s physical flow as \(f(e) = l(e) + \tilde{f}(e)\).

Drill 1.6 — The Mixed-Graph Eulerian Orientation Trap

You are given a connected mixed graph \(G = (V, E_{\text{dir}} \sqcup E_{\text{undir}})\) (POJ 1637 Sightseeing Tour) and asked whether every undirected edge can be oriented so the resulting directed graph has an Eulerian circuit (\(\text{in}(v) = \text{out}(v)\) for all \(v \in V\)). A solver arbitrarily orients each undirected edge as \(u \to v\), computes \(D(v) = \text{in}(v) - \text{out}(v)\), wires \(S \xrightarrow{D(v)} v\) (for \(D(v) > 0\)) and \(v \xrightarrow{-D(v)} T\) (for \(D(v) < 0\)), and puts capacity \(1\) on each reversible edge \(u \to v\).

Why does this fail even on a single 2-vertex, 2-edge graph?

TipAnswer & Diagnosis — Drill 1.6
  • Failure Mode: FM-06 (Mixed Eulerian Parity Omission & Unit-vs-Two Delta Bug, Chapter 4).
  • Two Compounding Bugs:
    1. Each Reversal Changes \(\text{in}(v) - \text{out}(v)\) by \(2\), Not \(1\): When an initially oriented edge \(u \to v\) is reversed to \(v \to u\), \(\text{out}(u)\) decreases by \(1\) and \(\text{in}(u)\) increases by \(1\), so \(D(u) = \text{in}(u) - \text{out}(u)\) changes by \(+2\) (and \(D(v)\) changes by \(-2\)).
    2. Mandatory Parity Pre-Check: Because every reversal changes \(D(v)\) by \(\pm 2\), a valid orientation is immediately impossible if any vertex \(v \in V\) has \(D(v) \not\equiv 0 \pmod 2\) (equivalently, if the total degree \(\deg(v)\) is odd)!
  • Exact Compilation (Ch 04.3): First check that \(D(v) \equiv 0 \pmod 2\) for all \(v \in V\). Then divide by \(2\): wire \(S \xrightarrow{-D(v)/2} v\) when \(D(v) < 0\) (excess out-degree) and \(v \xrightarrow{D(v)/2} T\) when \(D(v) > 0\) (excess in-degree), with unit capacity \(1\) on each reversible undirected arc \(u \to v\), and check if \(\text{MaxFlow}(S, T) = \sum_{D(v) > 0} D(v)/2\).

Problem #193 — CSES 1711: Distinct Routes ([EN])

  • Judge URL: https://cses.fi/problemset/task/1711
  • Constraints: \(2 \le N \le 500\) rooms, \(1 \le M \le 1000\) one-way teleporters, Time Limit \(1.0\text{ s}\).
  • Untagged Problem Statement: A game consists of \(N\) rooms and \(M\) one-way teleporters between rooms. You start in room \(1\) and must reach room \(N\) each day. Every teleporter \(e = (u \to v)\) can be used at most once across the entire game. Determine the maximum number of days \(K\) you can play, and explicitly output the room sequence of each of the \(K\) routes.
NoteHint 1 — Recognition Trigger (Chapter & Paradigm Reveal)
  • Target Chapter: Chapter 1 (§1.4 Flow Decomposition Theorem & Menger’s Edge-Disjoint Connectivity).
  • Diagnostic Clue: “Each directed teleporter used at most once” + “maximize the number of \(1 \leadsto N\) routes and print them.” As we saw in Drill 1.1 (FM-01), greedily extracting shortest paths fails because an early path can straddle a bridge edge and block two disjoint paths. You must first compute the global maximum unit flow \(f^*\), and only after all residual cancellations have settled, decompose \(f^*\) into \(K = |f^*|\) directed \(1 \leadsto N\) paths.
NoteHint 2 — Algebraic Intermediate Model

For each teleporter \(e = (u \to v) \in E\), introduce a binary flow variable \(f(e) \in \{0, 1\}\). Enforce Kirchhoff nodal conservation at every interior room:

\[\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} f(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f(e) = 0 \qquad \forall v \in \{2, \dots, N-1\}\]

and maximize \(|f| = \sum_{e \in \delta^+(1)} f(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(1)} f(e)\). By the Flow Decomposition Theorem (Ch 01.4), any integral \(1\)-\(N\) flow \(f^*\) with \(f^*(e) \in \{0, 1\}\) is the edge-disjoint sum of \(K = |f^*|\) directed \(1 \leadsto N\) paths and zero or more directed cycles.

TipFull 4-Step Compilation & Solution — Problem #193 (CSES 1711)

Step 1 & Step 2: Recognition & Algebraic Formulation

Each teleporter \(e = (u \to v)\) has capacity \(c(e) = 1\). In any converged integral maximum flow \(f^*\), an original forward teleporter \(e = (u \to v)\) is active (\(f^*(e) = 1\)) if and only if its forward residual capacity is \(c_{f^*}(u \to v) = 0\) (equivalently, its companion backward arc \(v \to u\) has residual capacity \(1\)).

Step 3: Mechanical Graph Compilation & Path Extraction Contract

  1. Network Construction: Create vertices \(1, \dots, N\). For each input teleporter \((u, v)\), add a directed arc \(u \xrightarrow{1} v\) (keeping a flag or checking edge_idx % 2 == 0 to distinguish original forward arcs from synthetic reverse residual arcs).
  2. Blocking-Flow Solve: Run K = dinic(1, N). By Dinic’s unit-capacity complexity theorem (Ch 01.3), this takes \(O(M \min(N^{2/3}, M^{1/2})) \le 3 \cdot 10^4\) operations.
  3. Constructive Path Peeling (Ch 01.4):
    • Consider the subgraph \(E_{\text{active}} = \{e = (u \to v) \in E : f^*(e) = 1\}\). At every interior vertex \(v \notin \{1, N\}\), \(\deg^+_{\text{active}}(v) = \deg^-_{\text{active}}(v)\), while \(\deg^+_{\text{active}}(1) - \deg^-_{\text{active}}(1) = K\).
    • Repeat \(K\) times: start at \(u = 1\), and greedily follow any active original forward arc \(e = (u \to v)\) with \(f^*(e) = 1\), immediately setting \(f^*(e) \gets 0\) (marking it consumed) until reaching \(N\).
    • Subtlety (What if \(E_{\text{active}}\) contains a directed cycle?): In a unit network solved by Dinic from \(f \equiv 0\), augmenting along shortest level-graph paths rarely creates isolated flow cycles, but if a vertex \(v\) on the current walk has both an \(s \leadsto t\) path and a directed cycle through \(v\), a naive walk that enters the cycle simply loops back to \(v\) and then continues to \(N\)! To print strictly simple paths (or avoid any dead-end if a cycle touches \(1\)), either run a standard Eulerian/DFS post-order walk that splices out cycles, or walk \(1 \leadsto N\) via DFS and only record the simple stack path when \(N\) is reached.

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Broken-Gadget Audit

  • Toy Trace (\(N = 4, M = 5\)): Edges \(e_1=(1\to 2), e_2=(2\to 3), e_3=(3\to 4), e_4=(1\to 3), e_5=(2\to 4)\).
    • Augmentation 1 pushes \(1\) unit along \(1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4\). Now \(c_f(2\to 3)=0\) and reverse arc \(c_f(3\to 2)=1\).
    • Augmentation 2 pushes \(1\) unit along \(1 \to 3 \to 2 \to 4\), traversing the reverse arc \(3 \to 2\).
    • After Dinic terminates (\(K = 2\)), check original forward arcs with \(c_{f^*}(e) = 0\): they are \(e_1(1\to 2), e_3(3\to 4), e_4(1\to 3), e_5(2\to 4)\)—notice \(e_2(2\to 3)\) now has \(c_{f^*}(2\to 3) = 1\) (\(f^*(2\to 3) = 0\))!
    • Peeling \(2\) paths from \(E_{\text{active}}\) yields 1 -> 2 -> 4 (length 3) and 1 -> 3 -> 4 (length 3).
  • Broken-Gadget Warning: When scanning the adjacency list during path reconstruction, never allow the walk to traverse odd-indexed reverse residual arcs! A reverse residual arc \(3 \to 2\) with \(c_{f^*}(3\to 2) = 0\) just means the forward arc \(2 \to 3\) is unused (\(f^*(2\to 3) = 0\)). Only original forward arcs (id % 2 == 0 with cap[id] == 0) represent physical teleporters used in the solution.
  • Complexity: Time \(O(M\sqrt{N} + K \cdot N)\), Space \(O(N + M)\).

Problem #194 — Luogu P1345: [USACO 5.4] Telecowmunication (奶牛的电信, [EN]/[ZH])

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P1345
  • Constraints: \(2 \le N \le 100\) computers, \(1 \le M \le 600\) bidirectional cables, two distinct hub computers \(c_1, c_2 \in \{1, \dots, N\}\), Time Limit \(1.0\text{ s}\).
  • Untagged Problem Statement: Farmer John’s \(N\) computers are connected by \(M\) bidirectional cables so that data can travel between any two computers through a sequence of intermediate computers. An accident may destroy some computers (together with all cables attached to them), but cables themselves never fail on their own, and the two endpoint computers \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) can never be destroyed. Find the minimum number of computers that must be destroyed so that \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) can no longer communicate.
NoteHint 1 — Recognition Trigger (Chapter & Paradigm Reveal)

FM-03 Unsplit Vertex Capacities & Undirected Bridge Short-Circuiting Placing node capacities on incident edges (#194), or adding undirected edge \(u_{\text{in}} \leftrightarrow u_{\text{out}}\) (Drill 1.3), or connecting \(R_i \leftrightarrow C_j\) pairwise (#196). Edge capacities constrain per-neighbor flow rather than \(\sum_v f(v, u) \le c(u)\); an undirected internal bridge lets flow enter \(u_{\text{out}}\) and exit \(u_{\text{in}}\), bypassing \(c(u)\). Split every capacitated internal vertex into \(u_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{c(u)} u_{\text{out}}\) (directed!) and wire external edges as \(x_{\text{out}} \to y_{\text{in}}\) (Ch 03 §3.1, §3.3).
FM-04 Time-Expanded Horizon Traps Omitting waiting arcs \((u, t) \to (u, t+1)\), rebuilding \(G(\mathcal{T})\) from scratch at every timestamp, or binary searching \(\mathcal{T}\) without an unreachability guard (Drill 1.4). Entities forced to move at every step can collide or oscillate; rebuilding wastes \(\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{T}^2)\) work; disconnected destinations cause infinite horizon loops. Always ask if entities can wait in place (holdover arc capacity \(c_{\text{wait}}(u)\)); append layers incrementally in \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) Dinic phases per step up to a proven reachability bound \(\mathcal{T}_{\max}\) (Ch 03 §3.2).
FM-05 Lower-Bound Sign Inversion, Feedback Leakage & Slack Omission Computing \(M(v) = \text{out}_{\text{low}} - \text{in}_{\text{low}}\) (Drill 1.5), leaving \(t \to s\) active during Stage 2 adjustment, or forgetting to add \(+l(e)\) / minimize slack (#198, #199). Reversing \(M(v)\) swaps supply and demand; leaving \(t \to s\) open during \(s\)-\(t\) adjustment creates an \(\infty\)-capacity loop; unweighted slack arcs absorb flow meant for real cells. Memorize In minus Out: \(M(v) = \text{in}_{\text{low}}(v) - \text{out}_{\text{low}}(v)\); read \(f_0 = \text{flow}(t \to s)\), zero both e_fb and e_fb ^ 1 before Stage 2, and reconstruct \(f(e) = l(e) + f'(e)\) (Ch 04 §4.1–4.3).
FM-06 Mixed Eulerian Parity Omission & Unit-vs-Two Degree Delta Running Dinic on mixed Eulerian graphs without checking \(D(v) \equiv 0 \pmod 2\), or setting edge capacity to \(2\) instead of \(1\) (Drill 1.6). Reversing one undirected edge changes \(\text{in}(v) - \text{out}(v)\) by \(\pm 2\), so odd \(D(v)\) is algebraically impossible and \(1\) unit of flow represents \(1\) reversed edge (\(2\) degree units). Pre-orient undirected edges arbitrarily, abort immediately if \(D(v) = \text{in}(v) - \text{out}(v)\) is odd for any \(v\), and wire node demands \(|D(v)| / 2\) with unit-capacity (\(1\)) arcs (Ch 04 §4.4).
