Checkpoint IV — Part IV Blind Recognition: Fractional Cuts, Planar Duals, Min-Cut DP & Gomory–Hu Trees

How to Use This Blind Recognition Checkpoint

Chapters 14 through 16 elevated your minimum-cut toolkit along two major axes:

  1. Parametric & Fractional Linearization (Ch 14): Transforming non-linear ratio objectives \(\max \frac{f(\mathbf{x})}{g(\mathbf{x})}\) into sequence-of-cuts feasibility tests \(F(\lambda) = \max [f(\mathbf{x}) - \lambda g(\mathbf{x})] \ge 0\) via Dinkelbach’s root-finding algorithm — and compiled pairwise subgraph density \(|E(S)| - \lambda |S|\) into Goldberg’s positive-capacity shift network while separating sum-ratio density from bottleneck minimum-degree (\(k\)-core) peeling.
  2. Structural & Topological Cut Compression (Ch 15 and Ch 16):Recognizing when running Dinic on \(N = 10^5\) or across \(\binom{N}{2}\) pairs is fatal, and instead compiling cuts into planar dual shortest paths (\(O(N \log N)\)), Direction B Cut-State DP / Tropical Dynamic Segment Trees (\(O(N \log N)\) per update), Stoer–Wagner global min-cut (\(O(NM + N^2 \log N)\)), or Gomory–Hu / Gusfield cut trees (\(N - 1\) max-flows for all \(\binom{N}{2}\) undirected pairs).

In a live contest, the hardest decision in Part IV is triage before graph construction:

  • Does “maximize density” mean \(\frac{\sum_{e \in E(S)} w_e}{|S|}\) (which requires Dinkelbach + Goldberg’s \(s\)-\(t\) min-cut, Ch 14.2) or \(\min_{u \in S} \frac{\deg_S(u)}{\deg_G(u)}\) (which is a bottleneck \(k\)-core property solved by \(O((N+M)\log N)\) greedy peeling, Ch 14.3)?
  • Does a ratio on a tree or general graph reduce to a \(0\)-\(1\) closure cut, a \(O(N K^2)\) tree knapsack DP, or a negative-cycle Bellman–Ford / SPFA test?
  • When capacities sit on a grid or planar embedding with \(N, M \le 10^6\), how do you orient the dual edges (FM-15) and pair outer-boundary segments when there are \(2k\) alternating terminals?
  • When a problem gives you an explicit flow network or cut formula with \(N = 2 \times 10^5\), how do you resist building the graph (Direction A) and instead compile the \(S/T\) partition directly into a DP or \((+, \min)\) tropical matrix product (Direction B, Ch 16.1)?

The Three-Pass Blind Protocol

  1. Pass 1 — Diagnostic Triage & Decoy Rejection (10 minutes per problem): Read the untagged problem statement, inspect the constraints (\(N, M, K, Q\)) and the algebraic form of the objective, and write down:
    • Primary Mechanism: 0-1 Fractional Cut / Dinkelbach (Ch 14.1), Goldberg Maximum Density Subgraph (Ch 14.2), Bottleneck Peeling / Cycle-Ratio (Ch 14.3), Planar Dual s-t / Multi-Terminal Cut (Ch 15.1–15.3), Planar Face / Enclosure Query (Ch 15.4), Direction B Min-Cut DP / Tropical Segment Tree (Ch 16.1), Stoer–Wagner Global Min-Cut (Ch 16.3), or Gomory–Hu Cut Tree (Ch 16.4).
    • Direction Choice: Are you compiling an algebraic objective into a flow network (Direction A), or compiling an intractable flow/cut problem into a dual shortest path, DP, or tree structure (Direction B)?
  2. Pass 2 — Intermediate Algebraic Formulation (15 minutes per problem): Before drawing any vertex or edge, write the exact shifted cut polynomial, dual face potential system, tropical matrix transition, or cut-tree bottleneck invariant. Verify that every capacity is non-negative and that trivial cuts (\(S = \emptyset\)) are handled.
  3. Pass 3 — Progressive Hint Unfolding & Failure-Mode Audit: Only unfold Hint 1 if your Pass 1 triage stalls. Only unfold Hint 2 if your Pass 2 algebra fails to compile cleanly. Compare your completed derivation against the Full 4-Step Solution and audit yourself against FM-14 through FM-16 in Section 3.

Section 1: Rapid-Fire Diagnostic Triage Drills

Before tackling the eight full blind problems, test your diagnostic reflexes on these six scenario drills. For each scenario, identify the trap/decoy, the correct Part IV mechanism, and the critical mathematical invariant before opening the solution callout.

Drill 1.1 — Sum-Ratio Subgraph Density vs. Bottleneck Degree Ratio

Scenario: You are given an undirected graph \(G = (V, E)\) with \(N = 10^5\) vertices and \(M = 10^5\) edges. A subset of vertices \(B \subseteq V\) is forbidden. You must choose a non-empty subset \(S \subseteq V \setminus B\) to maximize:

  • Variant A: \(\frac{|E(S)|}{|S|}\), where \(E(S)\) is the set of edges with both endpoints in \(S\) (under smaller constraints \(N \le 1000, M \le 5000\)).
  • Variant B: \(\min_{u \in S} \frac{|\{v \in S : (u,v) \in E\}|}{\deg_G(u)}\) (under the full constraints \(N, M \le 10^5\)).

A teammate proposes running Dinkelbach binary search + Goldberg’s \(s\)-\(t\) min-cut on both variants. Critique this proposal.

NoteDrill 1.1 — Diagnostic Triage & Invariant
  • Decoy / Trap: Confusing the average internal degree objective \(\frac{2|E(S)|}{|S|}\) (sum-over-sum fractional program) with the minimum normalized internal degree objective \(\min_{u \in S} \frac{\deg_S(u)}{\deg_G(u)}\) (bottleneck \(k\)-core objective). Running max-flow on Variant B with \(N = 10^5\) will TLE immediately, and worse, a min-cut cannot directly enforce a per-vertex minimum degree threshold without cascading deletions.
  • Correct Mechanism:
    • Variant A (Ch 14.2): Dinkelbach/binary search on \(\lambda\) + Goldberg’s shifted \(s\)-\(t\) minimum cut on \(V \setminus B\). Because \(|E(S)| = \sum_{\{u,v\} \in E} x_u x_v\) is a sum of supermodular pairwise rewards, maximizing \(|E(S)| - \lambda |S|\) is a project-selection closure problem solvable by Dinic in \(O(\text{MaxFlow}(N, M) \log N)\).
    • Variant B (Ch 14.3): Greedy Bottleneck Peeling (Generalized \(k\)-Core) in \(O((N + M) \log N)\). Start with \(S_0 = V \setminus B\) and repeatedly pop the vertex \(u \in S\) with the smallest current ratio \(\frac{\deg_S(u)}{\deg_G(u)}\) using a min-heap, recording the best active set seen across all \(N - |B|\) steps.
  • Key Proof Invariant (Variant B): For the optimal subset \(S^*\) achieving bottleneck ratio \(\lambda^*\), the very first vertex \(v \in S^*\) ever popped by the global greedy order has \(S^* \subseteq S_{\text{current}}\) at the moment \(v\) is popped, so its current ratio is at least \(\frac{\deg_{S^*}(v)}{\deg_G(v)} \ge \lambda^*\). Since \(v\) minimizes the current ratio across all of \(S_{\text{current}}\), the entire set \(S_{\text{current}}\) at that step achieves bottleneck ratio \(\ge \lambda^*\).

Drill 1.2 — Goldberg’s Unshifted Negative Capacity & The Trivial \(S = \emptyset\) Cut Trap (FM-14)

Scenario: To test whether there exists a non-empty subgraph \(S \subseteq V\) with \(\frac{|E(S)|}{|S|} > \lambda\), a contestant writes the cut objective \(\min_{S \subseteq V} \left(\lambda |S| - |E(S)|\right)\), substitutes \(|E(S)| = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{u \in S} \deg_G(u) - \frac{1}{2}|\delta(S)|\), and connects each vertex \(u\) to the sink \(t\) with capacity \(c(u, t) = \lambda - \frac{1}{2}\deg_G(u)\) and each undirected edge \((u,v)\) with capacity \(\frac{1}{2}\). Why does this fail in two distinct ways?

NoteDrill 1.2 — Diagnostic Triage & Invariant
  • Failure Mode 1 (Negative Capacities, FM-14a): Whenever \(2\lambda < \deg_G(u)\), the sink capacity \(c(u, t) = \lambda - \frac{1}{2}\deg_G(u)\) is strictly negative, violating the non-negative capacity precondition of Dinic’s algorithm.
  • Failure Mode 2 (Trivial \(S = \emptyset\) Minimum Cut, FM-14b): Even after shifting by a large constant \(U \ge \Delta(G)\) so that \(c(s, u) = U\), \(c(u, v) = 1\), and \(c(u, t) = U + 2\lambda - \deg_G(u) \ge 0\), the cut value for any \(S \subseteq V\) is: \[\text{cap}(S \cup \{s\}, \bar{S} \cup \{t\}) = U \cdot |V| + 2\left(\lambda |S| - |E(S)|\right)\] Putting \(S = \emptyset\) always yields cut capacity \(U \cdot |V| + 0\). If \(\lambda\) is larger than the maximum possible density \(\lambda^*\), then \(S = \emptyset\) is the unique global minimum cut of value \(U \cdot |V|\)! A solver who checks max_flow < U * N works only for strict inequality \(> \lambda\), and if \(S = \emptyset\) ties with a non-empty \(S\) at \(\lambda = \lambda^*\), reading the residual reachability from \(s\) must take the maximal \(s\)-side closure (or test \(\lambda - \varepsilon\)) so \(S \neq \emptyset\) is recovered.

Drill 1.3 — Directed Planar Dual Orientation & Boundary Short-Circuiting (FM-15)

Scenario: On an \(N \times N\) directed grid (\(N = 500\)) with source \(s = (0,0)\) at the top-left and sink \(t = (N,N)\) at the bottom-right, east/south edges have cost \(w_e\) and west/north edges have cost \(w'_e\). You split the outer face into \(s^*\) (bottom/left boundary) and \(t^*\) (top/right boundary) and build the dual graph \(G^*\). How do you orient the dual edge \(e^*\) for a primal directed edge \(e = (u \to v)\), and what goes wrong if you connect internal faces to \(s^*\) or \(t^*\) with undirected edges?

NoteDrill 1.3 — Diagnostic Triage & Invariant
  • Right-Hand Rule Orientation (FM-15a): Stand at \(u\) looking toward \(v\) along the directed primal edge \(e = (u \to v)\). If the \(s\)-side of the cut is to your left and the \(t\)-side is to your right when traversing the dual path from \(s^*\) to \(t^*\), then the forward primal edge \(e = (u \to v)\) crosses from the \(s\)-side to the \(t\)-side if and only if the directed dual edge \(e^*\) crosses \(e\) from left to right (clockwise \(90^\circ\) rotation) with weight \(c(e)\). The reverse primal edge \((v \to u)\) compiles to the opposite directed dual edge with weight \(c(v \to u)\).
  • Boundary Short-Circuiting (FM-15b): If you make the edges incident to \(s^*\) or \(t^*\) undirected (or reverse the right-hand rule on one boundary), a dual path can enter \(s^*\) from an internal face and exit \(s^*\) at another boundary cell for cost \(0\), teleporting across the boundary without cutting the primal boundary edges between them. Every dual edge leaving \(s^*\) must strictly enter the grid interior, and every dual edge entering \(t^*\) must strictly leave the grid interior.

Drill 1.4 — Direction A vs. Direction B on Dense Structured Networks

Scenario: You are given \(N = 2 \times 10^5\) vertices arranged in a line \(1, 2, \dots, N\). Each vertex \(i\) has source capacity \(p_i\) and sink capacity \(s_i\). Furthermore:

  • Variant A: For every pair \(1 \le i < j \le N\), there is a directed edge \(i \to j\) of uniform capacity \(c\).
  • Variant B: There is a directed edge \(i \to i+1\) of capacity \(c_i\), and you must handle \(Q = 2 \times 10^5\) point updates to \((p_i, s_i, c_i)\), printing the global \(s\)-\(t\) max-flow after each update.

Why is running Dinic (Direction A) impossible in both variants, and how does Direction B (Ch 16.1) solve each in \(O(N^2)\) (or \(O(N \log N)\)) and \(O(Q \log N)\)?

NoteDrill 1.4 — Diagnostic Triage & Invariant
  • Why Direction A Fails: Variant A has \(\binom{N}{2} \approx 2 \times 10^{10}\) edges; Variant B requires \(2 \times 10^5\) full max-flow runs on \(2 \times 10^5\) vertices.
  • Direction B Compilation (Ch 16.1): By Max-Flow Min-Cut Duality, the max-flow equals the minimum over all partitions \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\) (\(x_i = 1\) means \(i \in S\), \(x_i = 0\) means \(i \in T\)) of:
    • Variant A (CF 724E): If \(k\) vertices prior to \(i\) are placed in \(S\), placing \(i \in T\) cuts the source edge \(p_i\) plus all \(k\) edges from earlier \(S\)-vertices to \(i\), costing \(p_i + k \cdot c\). Placing \(i \in S\) cuts the sink edge \(s_i\). This compiles to a 1D DP where state \(k = \sum_{j < i} x_j\) tracks the number of \(S\)-vertices so far (in \(O(N^2)\), or \(O(N \log N)\) via slope trick since the transitions are convex).
    • Variant B (CF 1919F2): At each vertex \(i\), the cut contribution depends only on \((x_{i-1}, x_i) \in \{0, 1\}^2\): \[M_i = \begin{pmatrix} p_i & p_i \\ s_i + c_{i-1} & s_i \end{pmatrix}\] in the tropical \((+, \min)\) semiring! Maintaining the segment tree product \(M_1 \otimes M_2 \otimes \dots \otimes M_N\) answers each point-update max-flow query in \(O(\log N)\) time.

Drill 1.5 — Gomory–Hu Supernode Contraction vs. Directed Graphs (FM-16)

Scenario: A problem gives a graph with \(N = 400\) vertices and asks for \(\sum_{1 \le u < v \le N} \text{mincut}(u, v)\).

  1. What happens if the graph is directed and you attempt to build a Gomory–Hu cut tree?
  2. In an undirected graph, during step \(k\) of Gomory–Hu construction, you run \(s\)-\(t\) min-cut and obtain partition \((X, Y)\). A contestant discards all edges outside the current supernode and runs the next \(s\)-\(t\) flow on the induced subgraph of the supernode instead of contracting the exterior tree components into supernodes. Why does this produce wrong cuts?
NoteDrill 1.5 — Diagnostic Triage & Invariant
  • Failure Mode 1 (Directed Graphs Violate Symmetric Submodularity, FM-16a): In a directed graph, \(\lambda(u, v) \neq \lambda(v, u)\) in general, and directed cuts do not form a symmetric tree metric (though Schnorr 1979 constructs a directed cut packing for \(\min(\lambda(u,v), \lambda(v,u))\), standard Gomory–Hu trees for all-pairs \(\lambda(u,v)\) do not exist on directed graphs).
  • Failure Mode 2 (Deleting Exterior Components Instead of Contracting, FM-16b): Flow between two vertices \(u, v\) inside the current supernode \(V_i\) can legally route out of \(V_i\) into an already-split tree component \(C\) and re-enter \(V_i\) through another boundary edge! Deleting \(V \setminus V_i\) underestimates the true \(u\)-\(v\) cut in \(G\). You must either contract each connected component of \(T \setminus \{V_i\}\) into a single composite vertex (Gomory–Hu) or use Gusfield’s algorithm (Ch 16.4), which runs all \(N-1\) max-flows directly on the original unmodified graph \(G\)!

Drill 1.6 — Multi-Terminal Outerplanar Cuts vs. General Multiway Cut

Scenario: An undirected planar grid has \(N \times M\) vertices (\(N, M \le 500\)). In each of \(Q = 50\) queries, \(k \le 50\) terminal points are placed around the outer boundary of the grid, each colored either \(0\) (white) or \(1\) (black). You must find the minimum weight of grid edges to delete so no white terminal can reach any black terminal. A contestant recognizes “more than 2 terminals = NP-hard Multiway Cut” and gives up. Why is this problem solvable in \(O(k^2 N M \log(NM) + k^3)\) per query?

NoteDrill 1.6 — Diagnostic Triage & Invariant
  • Why General Multiway Cut Doesn’t Apply Here: All \(k\) terminals lie on the outer boundary of a planar graph, and there are only two terminal colors \(\{0, 1\}\).
  • Correct Mechanism (Ch 15.3, CSP-S 2021 交通规划): Walk clockwise around the outer boundary. Whenever two adjacent boundary terminals have different colors (\(0 \leftrightarrow 1\)), the outer face segment between them is a transition dual vertex where a separating cut curve must start or end. Since colors alternate around a cycle, there are an even number \(2r \le k\) of transition dual vertices \(v^*_1, v^*_2, \dots, v^*_{2r}\) in cyclic order!
  • Non-Crossing Catalan Matching: Any minimal cut separating white from black terminals corresponds in the planar dual \(G^*\) to a non-crossing perfect matching of \(\{v^*_1, \dots, v^*_{2r}\}\) via shortest paths in \(G^*\). Run \(2r\) Dijkstra passes from \(v^*_1, \dots, v^*_{2r}\) on \(G^*\), then run a \(O(r^3)\) circular interval DP: \[dp[l][r] = \min\left( \text{dist}_{G^*}(v^*_l, v^*_r) + dp[l+1][r-1], \min_{m} (dp[l][m] + dp[m+1][r]) \right)\]

Problem #217 — Fortress Ratios & Dual-Attribute Team Selection (CF 553D & Luogu P6577 / P3701)

Part A (Codeforces 553D — Nudist Beach, Judge Link)

You are given an undirected graph \(G = (V, E)\) with \(N\) cities and \(M\) bidirectional roads (\(1 \le N, M \le 10^5\)). Exactly \(K\) cities (\(1 \le K < N\)) are already occupied by fortresses and cannot be selected. You must choose a non-empty subset of non-fortress cities \(S \subseteq V \setminus F\) to maximize the weakest city’s fortification ratio in \(S\):

\[\max_{\emptyset \neq S \subseteq V \setminus F} \min_{u \in S} \frac{|\{v \in S : \{u, v\} \in E\}|}{\deg_G(u)}\]

where \(\deg_G(u)\) is the total number of roads incident to \(u\) in the original graph \(G\) (including roads to fortresses). Output the size \(|S|\) and the vertices of an optimal set \(S\).

Part B (Luogu P6577 — 【模板】二分图最大权完美匹配 / Luogu P3701 — 「伪模板」主席树, Judge Link (P6577), Judge Link (P3701))

To contrast with Part A’s bottleneck graph peeling:

  1. Weighted Perfect Pairing Benchmark (P6577): Given a bipartite graph \(G = (L \cup R, E)\) with \(|L| = |R| = N \le 500\), \(M \le N^2\) edges, and edge weights \(w_{uv} \in [-10^9, 10^9]\), find a perfect matching of maximum total weight in \(O(N^3)\) time (tested as a rapid contrast: why does standard \(O(N^2 M)\) SPFA-based MCMF TLE on dense \(M = N^2\) graphs unless implemented with \(O(N^3)\) Hungarian / Kuhn–Munkres slack arrays?).
  2. Role-Modified Combat Capacities (P3701): Two players each have \(N \le 100\) fighters. Each fighter has a combat class in \(\{\text{J}, \text{HK}, \text{W}, \text{YYY}, \text{E}\}\) and base stamina \(a_i\) (or \(b_j\)). Class interactions follow a rock-paper-scissors tournament matrix, except that each team’s 'J' fighters gain \(+1\) stamina bonus for every 'YYY' fighter on the same team, and player 1 can perform at most \(M\) global upgrades. Find the maximum number of victorious bouts player 1 can achieve.
NoteProblem #217 — Hint 1 (Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Rejection)
  • Part A (CF 553D): Look closely at the objective: it is \(\max_S \min_{u \in S} \frac{\deg_S(u)}{\deg_G(u)}\), not \(\max_S \frac{\sum_{u \in S} \deg_S(u)}{\sum_{u \in S} \deg_G(u)}\). With \(N, M = 10^5\), running Goldberg’s max-density min-cut is both too slow and solves the wrong objective! What happens to the weakest vertex \(u = \arg\min_{v \in S} \frac{\deg_S(v)}{\deg_G(v)}\) if we try to improve the bottleneck ratio of \(S\)?
  • Part B (P6577 / P3701): In P6577, \(N = 500, M = 2.5 \times 10^5\) with negative weights requires augmenting along shortest paths while maintaining dual vertex potentials \(u_i + v_j \ge w_{ij}\) and per-right-vertex minimum slack \(\text{slack}[j] = \min_{i \in S} (u_i + v_j - w_{ij})\) in \(O(N)\) per step (\(O(N^3)\) total). In P3701, since the 'YYY' count on each side is fixed from the input, each fighter’s effective capacity is a known static integer \(a'_i\) (plus a shared pool of capacidad \(M\) from a throttled super-source \(s \to s'\) of capacity \(M\) to 'W'/'HK' fighters), reducing directly to bipartite max-flow!
NoteProblem #217 — Hint 2 (Intermediate Algebraic & Structural Model)
  • Part A (CF 553D): Define \(r_S(u) = \frac{\deg_S(u)}{\deg_G(u)}\). Notice the monotonicity property: if \(A \subseteq B\), then for every \(u \in A\), \(r_A(u) \le r_B(u)\). Why does Gusfield’s uncontracted algorithm work in Part A? Audit 1 — FM-14: Goldberg’s Unshifted Negative Capacity & The Trivial \(S = \emptyset\) Cut Trap (Ch 14.2)

Self-Audit Question: In Goldberg’s Maximum Density Subgraph network for maximizing \(\frac{|E(S)|}{|S|}\), why do we add a large shift constant \(U \ge \Delta(G)\) to every source edge \(c(s, u) = U\) and sink edge \(c(u, t) = U + 2\lambda - \deg_G(u)\), and how do you guarantee that the residual \(s\)-side set \(S\) is non-empty (\(S \neq \emptyset\))?

ImportantFM-14 Self-Audit Verification
  1. Why \(U \ge \Delta(G)\) is Mandatory: Without \(U\), the sink capacity \(2\lambda - \deg_G(u)\) is negative whenever \(\deg_G(u) > 2\lambda\), which invalidates Dinic’s non-negative capacity invariant. Adding \(U\) to both \(c(s, u)\) and \(c(u, t)\) adds exactly \(U\) to every vertex \(u\)’s contribution regardless of whether \(u \in S\) (cuts \(u \to t\)) or \(u \in \bar{S}\) (cuts \(s \to u\)), shifting every cut capacity by the constant \(+U \cdot |V|\) without changing the relative ordering of subsets \(S \subseteq V\).
  2. Preventing the Trivial \(S = \emptyset\) Cut: Because \(S = \emptyset\) always yields cut capacity \(U \cdot |V| + 2(\lambda \cdot 0 - 0) = U \cdot |V|\), any \(\lambda > \lambda^*\) has \(S = \emptyset\) as the unique minimum cut. During binary search, test strict feasibility max_flow < U * N (with \(\varepsilon = \frac{1}{N(N-1)}\) spacing between distinct rational densities \(\frac{E_1}{V_1} \neq \frac{E_2}{V_2}\)), and reconstruct the subgraph at the last strictly feasible \(\lambda_{\text{low}} < \lambda^*\) where \(S_{\min} = \{u \in V : s \rightsquigarrow_{G_f} u\}\) is guaranteed to be non-empty!

Audit 2 — FM-15: Directed Planar Dual Orientation & Boundary Short-Circuiting (Ch 15.2–15.3)

Self-Audit Question: When converting a directed planar \(s\)-\(t\) cut network into a dual shortest-path problem from \(s^*\) to \(t^*\), how do you verify in \(30\) seconds on scratch paper that your \(90^\circ\) rotation direction is not backwards, and why must outer-boundary edges incident to \(s^*\) and \(t^*\) be strictly one-way (or carefully directed)?

ImportantFM-15 Self-Audit Verification
  1. The \(1 \times 1\) Corner Cut Sanity Check: Always draw the \(1\)-vertex source cut \(S = \{s\}\) at the corner \(s = (0,0)\). The only primal edges in \(\delta^+(S)\) are the two outgoing edges from \((0,0)\): \((0,0) \to (0,1)\) and \((0,0) \to (1,0)\). Check that your dual graph routes \(s^* \to f_{(1,1)} \to t^*\) using the weights of those two outgoing edges from \((0,0)\), not the incoming edges \((0,1) \to (0,0)\) and \((1,0) \to (0,0)\)!
  2. No Free Boundary Teleportation: Never connect internal faces to \(s^*\) with zero-weight reverse edges or allow a path to enter \(s^*\) and exit \(s^*\) at another boundary face. In a multi-terminal boundary problem (CSP-S 2021 交通规划, Ch 15.3), the outer boundary segments between adjacent same-colored terminals are blocked (or simply omitted as dual terminals); only the \(2r\) boundary intervals where terminal colors switch \(0 \leftrightarrow 1\) become active dual terminals \(v^*_1, \dots, v^*_{2r}\).

Audit 3 — FM-16: Gomory–Hu Undirected Precondition & Supernode Contraction (Ch 16.4)

Self-Audit Question: Why does a Gomory–Hu cut tree fail to exist on directed graphs, and why is Gusfield’s algorithm preferred over classical Gomory–Hu contraction in competitive programming implementations?

ImportantFM-16 Self-Audit Verification
  1. Undirected Symmetry Requirement: On a directed cycle \(1 \xrightarrow{1} 2 \xrightarrow{1} 3 \xrightarrow{1} 1\), \(\text{mincut}(u, v) = 1\) for every ordered pair, yet any tree on 3 vertices has a leaf whose single incident edge must represent both \(\text{mincut}(u, v)\) and \(\text{mincut}(v, u)\) simultaneously — impossible when \(\text{mincut}(u, v) \neq \text{mincut}(v, u)\)! Furthermore, uncrossing of minimum cuts requires symmetric submodularity \(f(A) = f(V \setminus A)\).
  2. Why Gusfield Beats Manual Supernode Contraction: Classical Gomory–Hu requires contracting the connected components of \(T \setminus \{V_i\}\) into supernodes before each max-flow call (and failing to contract — e.g., running max-flow only on the induced subgraph of \(V_i\) — produces wrong cuts because paths can detour through \(V \setminus V_i\)). Gusfield’s algorithm runs all \(N - 1\) Dinic calls on the original, unmodified graph \(G\), updating only the \(N\)-element parent array if (vis[u] && p[u] == t) p[u] = s in 8 lines of bug-free code.

4.2 What Changes in Part V? (From Feasibility & Cut Capacity to Cost-Driven Optimization)

Across Parts I through IV, every flow edge had only a capacity \(c(e)\) (plus an optional lower bound \(l(e)\)). If we wanted to optimize a linear objective over subsets or assignments, we had to encode all costs into cut capacities (\(s\)-\(t\) minimum cuts) or binary search on a threshold (\(\lambda\)).

In Part V (Ch 17–Ch 21), every edge \(e = (u \to v)\) gains an independent unit cost \(w(e)\) alongside its capacity \([l(e), u(e)]\), unlocking five transformative modelling paradigms:

  1.   2. Convex Marginal Edge Splitting & Degree Penalties (Ch 18): Compiling separable convex costs \(\Phi(f_e)\) into parallel unit-capacity edges with non-decreasing marginal differences \(\Delta_k = \Phi(k) - \Phi(k-1)\), and modeling tournament triplets \(\binom{\text{deg}^+(u)}{2}\) in Landau’s theorem (Luogu P4249 剪刀石头布, WC2007).
  3. The Equation Differencing Method (\(\Delta E_t = E_t - E_{t-1}\), Ch 19): Compiling linear systems of range inequalities \(L_t \le \sum_{i \in I_t} x_i \le R_t\) into exact flow conservation at vertices by taking consecutive row differences — turning interval coverage and employee scheduling problems (NOI2008 志愿者招募, CF 802C) into mechanical network compilations without guesswork.
  4. Vertex-Disjoint Cycle Covers, Dynamic Server Activation & Hub Graphs (Ch 20): Modeling multi-vehicle routing, napkin/surgical-instrument sterilization delays (餐巾计划问题), and lazy waiting-multiplier queues (NOI2012 美食节).
