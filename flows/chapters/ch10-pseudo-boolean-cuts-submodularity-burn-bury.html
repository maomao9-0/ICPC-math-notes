Chapter 10 — Pairwise Binary Penalties, Submodularity & Burn-or-Bury

1. The General \(2 \times 2\) Binary Payoff Matrix & Cut Polynomial Expansion

In Chapter 9, every pairwise interaction between two binary decisions \(x_u, x_v \in \{0, 1\}\) was a hard prerequisite implication \(x_u = 1 \implies x_v = 1\), corresponding to an arc \(u \xrightarrow{\infty} v\) that forbids the state \((x_u, x_v) = (1, 0)\) at cost \(+\infty\).

However, most real-world and contest optimization problems involve soft, finite, and asymmetric pairwise interactions:

  • In a dual-core processor (POJ 3469), assigning module \(u\) to Core A (\(x_u = 1\)) and module \(v\) to Core B (\(x_v = 0\)) incurs a finite bus communication penalty \(w_{uv} > 0\), while assigning them to the same core costs \(0\).
  • In a classroom seating grid (Luogu P1646 Happiness), two deskmates \(u\) and \(v\) receive a synergy bonus \(E^{\text{art}}_{uv}\) if both choose Arts (\(x_u = x_v = 1\)), a different bonus \(E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\) if both choose Science (\(x_u = x_v = 0\)), and \(0\) if they split.
  • In an employee hiring network (Luogu P1791 / BZOJ 2039 人员雇佣), hiring both \(u\) and \(v\) yields \(2 E_{uv}\), leaving both unhired yields \(0\), and hiring one without the other incurs a friction penalty \(-E_{uv}\).

Can every such pairwise interaction be compiled into a directed \((S, T)\)-cut network with non-negative capacities? And when it can, what is the mechanical formula for the capacities of \(S \to u\), \(u \to T\), \(u \to v\), and \(v \to u\)? This section answers the second question via exact polynomial algebra; Section 2 answers the first via the Kolmogorov–Zabih Submodularity Theorem.

1.1 Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Reach for Pseudo-Boolean Cut Polynomial Compilation (known in the Japanese competitive programming community as Burn-or-Bury / 燃やす埋める, and in the Chinese community as Minimum Cut Modelling / 最小割模型 & 合并点边法) whenever a problem exhibits the following signature:

  • Independent Binary Choices with Overlapping Local Costs/Rewards: Each of \(N\) entities \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) must make a binary choice \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\) (e.g., Core A vs. Core B, Arts vs. Science, Keep vs. Discard, Vote \(1\) vs. Vote \(0\)).
  • Coupled Objective Function: The total cost to minimize (or total profit to maximize) is a sum of unary payoffs on individual variables \(x_i\), pairwise payoff tables on pairs \((x_u, x_v)\), and possibly unanimity bonuses on subsets \(C \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}\) that all choose the same state.
  • Why Naive DP, Greedy, and 2-SAT Fail:
    1. Why Greedy Fails: Flipping \(x_u\) from \(0\) to \(1\) may improve \(u\)’s unary payoff while simultaneously breaking synergies with neighbors \(v_1, v_2\) and creating conflicts with \(v_3\). Local greedy improvements get trapped in suboptimal configurations.
    2. Why Tree / Bitmask DP Fails: When the interaction graph is an \(H \times W\) grid (\(H, W \le 100\), treewidth \(100\)) or a general graph with \(N \le 1000\), dynamic programming requires \(2^{\text{tw}(G)}\) states, which is astronomically beyond time and memory limits.
    3. Why 2-SAT Fails: 2-SAT only decides whether there exists an assignment of zero total penalty when all clauses are hard (\(\infty\)). Whenever clauses have finite weights and we must find an assignment minimizing the sum of violated clause weights (i.e., Weighted Max-2-SAT / Markov Random Field energy minimization), 2-SAT has no mechanism to trade off competing finite penalties.

1.2 Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation of Cut Networks

Before touching a single pairwise matrix, we fix our Sign and Orientation Convention so that there is zero ambiguity between \(S\) and \(T\) throughout the chapter.

ImportantCanonical Indicator Convention (\(x_u = 1 \iff u \in V_S\), \(x_u = 0 \iff u \in V_T\))

Let \(\mathcal{N} = (V \cup \{S, T\}, E, c)\) be a directed flow network with source \(S\), sink \(T\), and non-negative arc capacities \(c: E \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\). Any \((S, T)\)-cut partitions the vertex set into \((V_S, V_T)\) with \(S \in V_S\) and \(T \in V_T\).

For every vertex \(u \in V\), define its binary source-side indicator variable:

\[x_u = [u \in V_S] = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } u \in V_S \text{ (assigned to the } S\text{-side)}, \\ 0 & \text{if } u \in V_T \text{ (assigned to the } T\text{-side)}. \end{cases}\]

By definition of the source and sink, \(x_S \equiv 1\) and \(x_T \equiv 0\). A directed arc \((u \to v) \in E\) is severed by the cut \((V_S, V_T)\) if and only if \(u \in V_S\) (\(x_u = 1\)) and \(v \in V_T\) (\(x_v = 0\)). Therefore, the cut capacity \(\text{cap}(V_S, V_T)\) is the exact quadratic pseudo-Boolean polynomial:

\[\boxed{\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}) = C_0 + \sum_{u \in V} c_{S \to u}(1 - x_u) + \sum_{u \in V} c_{u \to T}\,x_u + \sum_{\substack{u, v \in V \\ u \neq v}} c_{u \to v}\,x_u(1 - x_v)}\]

where \(C_0 = c_{S \to T}\) is a constant offset (an arc directly from \(S\) to \(T\) is always severed).

Look closely at the four elementary building blocks available in \(\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x})\) when all arc capacities must be non-negative (\(c_e \ge 0\)):

  1. Constant Offset \(C_0\): Contributes \(C_0\) to every cut, regardless of \(\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N\).
  2. Source-to-Vertex Arc \(S \xrightarrow{c} u\) (\(c \ge 0\)): Severed when \(u \in V_T\) (\(x_u = 0\)). Algebraically represents the unary penalty \(c \cdot (1 - x_u)\).
  3. Vertex-to-Sink Arc \(u \xrightarrow{c} T\) (\(c \ge 0\)): Severed when \(u \in V_S\) (\(x_u = 1\)). Algebraically represents the unary penalty \(c \cdot x_u\).
  4. Internal Directed Arc \(u \xrightarrow{c} v\) (\(c \ge 0\)): Severed when \(u \in V_S\) (\(x_u = 1\)) and \(v \in V_T\) (\(x_v = 0\)). Algebraically represents the directed cross-cut penalty \(c \cdot x_u(1 - x_v)\).

1.3 Expanding the General \(2 \times 2\) Binary Payoff Matrix

Now suppose two binary variables \(x_u, x_v \in \{0, 1\}\) interact via an arbitrary \(2 \times 2\) cost (penalty) table \(E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v)\) that we wish to minimize:

\(x_u \setminus x_v\) \(x_v = 0\) (\(v \in V_T\)) \(x_v = 1\) (\(v \in V_S\))
\(x_u = 0\) (\(u \in V_T\)) \(A = E_{u,v}(0, 0)\) \(B = E_{u,v}(0, 1)\)
\(x_u = 1\) (\(u \in V_S\)) \(C = E_{u,v}(1, 0)\) \(D = E_{u,v}(1, 1)\)

(Note on Maximization Problems: If a problem instead asks to maximize a \(2 \times 2\) reward table \(\Pi_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = \begin{pmatrix} \pi_{00} & \pi_{01} \\ \pi_{10} & \pi_{11} \end{pmatrix}\), we either pre-claim a baseline \(M_{uv}\)—such as \(M_{uv} = \max(\pi_{00}, \pi_{01}, \pi_{10}, \pi_{11})\) or \(M_{uv} = \pi_{00} + \pi_{11}\)—and minimize the regret table \(E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = M_{uv} - \Pi_{u,v}(x_u, x_v)\), or simply negate \(E_{u,v} = -\Pi_{u,v}\) and normalize unary offsets at the end.)

Because \((x_u, x_v)\) takes only four possible values in \(\{0, 1\}^2\), the function \(E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v)\) is uniquely and exactly represented by the multilinear Lagrange interpolation polynomial over the four corners of the unit square:

\[E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = A(1 - x_u)(1 - x_v) + B(1 - x_u)x_v + C x_u(1 - x_v) + D x_u x_v.\]

Let us expand the products \((1 - x_u)(1 - x_v) = 1 - x_u - x_v + x_u x_v\), \((1 - x_u)x_v = x_v - x_u x_v\), and \(x_u(1 - x_v) = x_u - x_u x_v\) to collect like powers of \(x_u\) and \(x_v\):

\[\begin{aligned} E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) &= A(1 - x_u - x_v + x_u x_v) + B(x_v - x_u x_v) + C(x_u - x_u x_v) + D x_u x_v \\ &= A + (C - A)\,x_u + (B - A)\,x_v - \underbrace{(B + C - A - D)}_{\Delta_{uv}}\,x_u x_v. \end{aligned}\]

Notice the quantity \(\Delta_{uv} := (B + C) - (A + D)\), which measures how much more expensive the disagreement states \((0, 1)\) and \((1, 0)\) are compared to the agreement states \((0, 0)\) and \((1, 1)\).

In our target cut polynomial \(\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x})\), we do not have a native edge for the product \(-x_u x_v\); instead, our internal directed edges \(u \to v\) and \(v \to u\) generate the bilinear cut terms \(x_u(1 - x_v)\) and \(x_v(1 - x_u)\). Fortunately, over all real numbers (and in particular on \(\{0, 1\}^2\)), the product \(-x_u x_v\) satisfies two fundamental linear identities:

\[-x_u x_v = x_u(1 - x_v) - x_u \qquad \text{and} \qquad -x_u x_v = x_v(1 - x_u) - x_v.\]

Therefore, if we split the cross-coefficient \(\Delta_{uv} = B + C - A - D\) into any two parts \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}\) satisfying \(\alpha + \beta = \Delta_{uv}\), we can rewrite \(-\Delta_{uv}\,x_u x_v\) as:

\[-\Delta_{uv}\,x_u x_v = \alpha\big(x_u(1 - x_v) - x_u\big) + \beta\big(x_v(1 - x_u) - x_v\big).\]

Substituting this back into \(E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v)\) yields the Master 1-Parameter Cut Polynomial Expansion:

\[\boxed{E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = \underbrace{A}_{\text{constant}} + \underbrace{(C - A - \alpha)\,x_u}_{\text{unary on } u} + \underbrace{(B - A - \beta)\,x_v}_{\text{unary on } v} + \underbrace{\alpha\,x_u(1 - x_v)}_{\text{arc } u \to v \text{ cap } \alpha} + \underbrace{\beta\,x_v(1 - x_u)}_{\text{arc } v \to u \text{ cap } \beta}}\]

where \(\alpha + \beta = B + C - A - D\).

Let us verify this identity on all four states \((x_u, x_v) \in \{0, 1\}^2\) to see how the five pieces conspire to reproduce \(A, B, C, D\):

  • State \((0, 0)\) (\(u \in V_T, v \in V_T\)): All four variable terms vanish, leaving \(A\).

  • State \((0, 1)\) (\(u \in V_T, v \in V_S\)): Only the \(x_v\) unary term and the \(v \to u\) cut term \(x_v(1 - x_u)\) are active:

    \[A + (B - A - \beta)(1) + \beta(1) = B.\]

  • State \((1, 0)\) (\(u \in V_S, v \in V_T\)): Only the \(x_u\) unary term and the \(u \to v\) cut term \(x_u(1 - x_v)\) are active:

    \[A + (C - A - \alpha)(1) + \alpha(1) = C.\]

  • State \((1, 1)\) (\(u \in V_S, v \in V_S\)): Both directed cut terms \(x_u(1 - x_v)\) and \(x_v(1 - x_u)\) vanish because \(u\) and \(v\) lie on the same side of the cut! Only the constant and the two unary terms survive:

    \[A + (C - A - \alpha) + (B - A - \beta) = B + C - A - (\alpha + \beta) = B + C - A - (B + C - A - D) = D.\]

1.4 Unary Normalization Recipes (差値正規化 vs. ゲタを履かせる)

After expanding every pairwise matrix \(E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v)\) across the entire graph and summing all unary contributions at each vertex \(u \in V\), we obtain a total unary function at vertex \(u\):

\[U_u(x_u) = p_u (1 - x_u) + q_u x_u,\]

where \(p_u, q_u \in \mathbb{R}\) may be negative because expressions like \((C - A - \alpha)\) or \((B - A - \beta)\) can have negative signs!

How do we compile \(U_u(x_u) = p_u(1 - x_u) + q_u x_u\) into non-negative capacities on \(S \to u\) and \(u \to T\)? Because \((1 - x_u) + x_u = 1\) identically for all \(x_u \in \{0, 1\}\), we can shift \(p_u\) and \(q_u\) simultaneously by any constant without changing the function! Two canonical recipes are used in the literature:

Recipe 1: Minimum Extraction / Pre-Flow Cancellation (差値正規化 — Recommended Default)

Let \(m_u = \min(p_u, q_u)\). Subtract \(m_u = m_u(1 - x_u) + m_u x_u\) from the unary terms and absorb \(m_u\) directly into the global constant offset \(C_0\):

\[U_u(x_u) = m_u + \underbrace{(p_u - m_u)}_{\ge 0}(1 - x_u) + \underbrace{(q_u - m_u)}_{\ge 0} x_u.\]

Now both coefficients \((p_u - m_u) \ge 0\) and \((q_u - m_u) \ge 0\) are guaranteed to be non-negative, even if \(p_u\) or \(q_u\) was originally negative! Even better, at least one of the two coefficients is zero:

  • If \(p_u \ge q_u\): Add \(q_u\) to the global constant \(C_0\), and add a single directed arc \(S \to u\) of capacity \(p_u - q_u \ge 0\).
  • If \(q_u > p_u\): Add \(p_u\) to the global constant \(C_0\), and add a single directed arc \(u \to T\) of capacity \(q_u - p_u > 0\).
TipFlow-Theoretic Intuition: 2-Edge Path Pre-Flow

When \(p_u > 0\) and \(q_u > 0\) are both positive, adding both \(S \xrightarrow{p_u} u\) and \(u \xrightarrow{q_u} T\) creates a 2-edge directed path \(S \to u \to T\) of bottleneck capacity \(m_u = \min(p_u, q_u)\). In any \((S, T)\)-cut, either \(u \in V_T\) (cutting \(S \to u\)) or \(u \in V_S\) (cutting \(u \to T\)), so at least \(m_u\) capacity is always severed! Recipe 1 simply pre-pushes \(m_u\) units of flow along \(S \to u \to T\) in \(O(1)\) time during graph construction, saturating one of the two arcs before Dinic’s algorithm even starts.

Recipe 2: Uniform Constant Pedestal Shift (ゲタを履かせる / “Putting on Geta Clogs”)

In Japanese 燃やす埋める tutorials, an alternative normalization adds a large positive constant \(M > \max_u(|p_u|, |q_u|)\) to both branches via \(0 = -M + M(1 - x_u) + M x_u\):

\[U_u(x_u) = -M + (p_u + M)(1 - x_u) + (q_u + M)x_u.\]

This makes both \(S \to u\) (capacity \(p_u + M > 0\)) and \(u \to T\) (capacity \(q_u + M > 0\)) strictly positive, at the price of inflating the max-flow value by \(N \cdot M\) and forcing Dinic to spend its first blocking-flow phase draining trivial 2-edge paths \(S \to u \to T\). In actual contest code, always use Recipe 1 to keep capacities small and the graph sparse; reserve Recipe 2 for theoretical proofs where strictly positive capacities are convenient.

2. The Kolmogorov–Zabih (2004) Submodularity Theorem (\(A + D \le B + C\))

We now confront the central theoretical question of binary minimum-cut modelling: Which \(2 \times 2\) cost matrices \(E_{u,v}\) can be represented by a flow network with non-negative capacities?

2.1 Statement and Proof of the Submodularity Theorem

ImportantTheorem 10.1 (Kolmogorov–Zabih 2004 / Freedman–Drineas 2005 — Binary Submodularity Theorem)

A quadratic pseudo-Boolean cost function

\[\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = C_0 + \sum_{u \in V} U_u(x_u) + \sum_{\{u, v\} \in \binom{V}{2}} E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v)\]

is graph-representable as the minimum \((S, T)\)-cut of a directed network \(\mathcal{N}\) with non-negative arc capacities (even if we are allowed to introduce an arbitrary number of auxiliary vertices \(w_1, \dots, w_k \in \{0, 1\}\)!) if and only if every pairwise interaction matrix \(E_{u,v} = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}\) satisfies the Submodularity Inequality:

\[\boxed{A + D \le B + C \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad E_{u,v}(0, 0) + E_{u,v}(1, 1) \le E_{u,v}(0, 1) + E_{u,v}(1, 0)}\]

In plain English: The sum of costs when \(u\) and \(v\) agree (\((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\)) must not exceed the sum of costs when \(u\) and \(v\) disagree (\((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\)).

Proof of Sufficiency (\(\Longleftarrow\): Constructive Graph Compilation)

Suppose every pair \(\{u, v\}\) satisfies \(A + D \le B + C\). Then the cross-cut defect

\[\Delta_{uv} := (B + C) - (A + D) \ge 0\]

is non-negative! Consequently, we can choose non-negative arc capacities \(\alpha \ge 0\) and \(\beta \ge 0\) such that \(\alpha + \beta = \Delta_{uv} \ge 0\) in the Master 1-Parameter Expansion from Section 1.3:

\[E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = A + (C - A - \alpha)x_u + (B - A - \beta)x_v + \alpha\,x_u(1 - x_v) + \beta\,x_v(1 - x_u).\]

Here:

  • The internal directed arcs \(u \to v\) (capacity \(\alpha \ge 0\)) and \(v \to u\) (capacity \(\beta \ge 0\)) have non-negative capacities.
  • The unary terms \((C - A - \alpha)x_u\) and \((B - A - \beta)x_v\) are accumulated into the vertex totals \(U_u(x_u)\) and \(U_v(x_v)\).
  • Finally, applying Recipe 1 (差値正規化) to each vertex’s accumulated unary function \(U_u(x_u)\) converts all unary terms into non-negative capacities on \(S \to u\) and \(u \to T\) plus a global scalar constant \(C_{\text{total}}\).

Thus \(\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = C_{\text{total}} + \text{cap}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x})\) for every \(\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N\), using zero auxiliary vertices!

Proof of Necessity (\(\Longrightarrow\): Why No Clever Auxiliary Gadget Can Rescue \(A + D > B + C\))

Contestants meeting a supermodular matrix (\(A + D > B + C\), such as a mutual-exclusion penalty \(D = +\infty, A = B = C = 0\)) often spend hours trying to invent an auxiliary-node gadget that represents \(E_{u,v}\) without negative capacities. We now prove that no such gadget can ever exist.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that a directed network \(\mathcal{N}\) with non-negative capacities \(c_e \ge 0\) represents \(f(\mathbf{x})\) using the original variables \(\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^n\) together with \(k\) auxiliary vertices \(\mathbf{w} \in \{0, 1\}^k\), in the sense that:

\[f(\mathbf{x}) = \min_{\mathbf{w} \in \{0, 1\}^k} \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w}) \qquad \forall \mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^n.\]

Let \(\mathbf{Z} = (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w}) \in \{0, 1\}^{n+k}\) denote the full cut assignment. For any two vectors \(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}, \mathbf{Z}^{(2)} \in \{0, 1\}^{n+k}\), let \(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)} \wedge \mathbf{Z}^{(2)}\) (componentwise minimum / intersection) and \(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)} \vee \mathbf{Z}^{(2)}\) (componentwise maximum / union) be their lattice meet and join.

  1. Every Single Non-Negative Cut Arc Is Submodular: Consider a single directed arc \(p \to q\) of capacity \(c_{pq} \ge 0\), which contributes \(\phi_{pq}(Z_p, Z_q) = c_{pq}\,Z_p(1 - Z_q)\). Its \(2 \times 2\) matrix is \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ c_{pq} & 0 \end{pmatrix}\), which satisfies \(0 + 0 \le 0 + c_{pq}\) since \(c_{pq} \ge 0\).

  2. Conic Combination Preserves Submodularity: Because \(\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{Z})\) is a sum of non-negative cut functions \(\phi_{pq}\), it satisfies the global submodular lattice inequality for all \(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}, \mathbf{Z}^{(2)} \in \{0, 1\}^{n+k}\):

    \[\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}) + \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{Z}^{(2)}) \ge \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)} \wedge \mathbf{Z}^{(2)}) + \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)} \vee \mathbf{Z}^{(2)}).\]

  3. Marginal Minimization (Projection) Preserves Submodularity: For any two assignments \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \mathbf{x}^{(2)} \in \{0, 1\}^n\), let \(\mathbf{w}^{*(1)} \in \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \mathbf{w})\) and \(\mathbf{w}^{*(2)} \in \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}^{(2)}, \mathbf{w})\) be the optimal auxiliary configurations. Setting \(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)} = (\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \mathbf{w}^{*(1)})\) and \(\mathbf{Z}^{(2)} = (\mathbf{x}^{(2)}, \mathbf{w}^{*(2)})\) yields:

    \[\begin{aligned} f(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}) + f(\mathbf{x}^{(2)}) &= \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, \mathbf{w}^{*(1)}) + \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}^{(2)}, \mathbf{w}^{*(2)}) \\ &\ge \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}\big(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \wedge \mathbf{x}^{(2)}, \,\mathbf{w}^{*(1)} \wedge \mathbf{w}^{*(2)}\big) + \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}\big(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \vee \mathbf{x}^{(2)}, \,\mathbf{w}^{*(1)} \vee \mathbf{w}^{*(2)}\big) \\ &\ge \min_{\mathbf{w}} \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}\big(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \wedge \mathbf{x}^{(2)}, \,\mathbf{w}\big) + \min_{\mathbf{w}} \Phi_{\mathcal{N}}\big(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \vee \mathbf{x}^{(2)}, \,\mathbf{w}\big) \\ &= f\big(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \wedge \mathbf{x}^{(2)}\big) + f\big(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \vee \mathbf{x}^{(2)}\big). \end{aligned}\]

  4. Restriction to Any Pair \(\{u, v\}\): Fixing all other coordinates \(x_r\) (\(r \notin \{u, v\}\)) and evaluating at \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} = (1, 0)\) and \(\mathbf{x}^{(2)} = (0, 1)\) gives \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \wedge \mathbf{x}^{(2)} = (0, 0)\) and \(\mathbf{x}^{(1)} \vee \mathbf{x}^{(2)} = (1, 1)\), forcing:

    \[E_{u,v}(1, 0) + E_{u,v}(0, 1) \ge E_{u,v}(0, 0) + E_{u,v}(1, 1) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad B + C \ge A + D. \qquad \blacksquare\]

2.2 Asymmetric Integer Compilation vs. Symmetric Half-Edge Compilation (合并点边法)

When \(A + D \le B + C\) holds, the Master 1-Parameter Expansion gives us an entire segment of valid splits \((\alpha, \beta)\) with \(\alpha + \beta = \Delta_{uv} = B + C - A - D \ge 0\). Which point on this segment should you choose in contest code? Two canonical choices dominate competitive programming:

Compilation Scheme 1: Asymmetric Integer Compilation (\(\alpha = \Delta_{uv}, \beta = 0\))

Set \(\alpha = \Delta_{uv} = B + C - A - D \ge 0\) and \(\beta = 0\) (or vice versa). The Master Expansion specializes to the Asymmetric Integer Formula:

\[\boxed{E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = A + (D - B)\,x_u + (B - A)\,x_v + \underbrace{(B + C - A - D)}_{\Delta_{uv} \ge 0}\,x_u(1 - x_v)}\]

(Equivalently, using \(x_u = 1 - (1 - x_u)\), we can write \(E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = D + (B - D)(1 - x_u) + (B - A)x_v + \Delta_{uv}\,x_u(1 - x_v)\), which directly exposes the \(S \to u\) and \(v \to T\) terms when \(B \ge D\) and \(B \ge A\)!)

Why Scheme 1 Is the General-Purpose Gold Standard:

  1. Zero Fractions: If \(A, B, C, D \in \mathbb{Z}\), then every unary coefficient and the directed arc capacity \(\Delta_{uv}\) are exact integers—no division by \(2\) and no \(2\times\) scaling is ever required!
  2. Half as Many Internal Arcs: It emits only one directed arc \(u \to v\) of capacity \(\Delta_{uv}\) instead of a bidirectional pair \(u \leftrightarrow v\).

Compilation Scheme 2: Symmetric Half-Edge Compilation (\(\alpha = \beta = \frac{1}{2}\Delta_{uv}\), Hu Botao’s 合并点边法)

In his landmark 2007 Chinese National Team paper (《最小割模型在信息学竞赛中的应用》), Hu Botao (Amber) introduced Vertex-Edge Merging (合并点边法), which sets \(\alpha = \beta = \frac{1}{2}\Delta_{uv} = \frac{B + C - A - D}{2}\). Substituting \(\alpha = \beta = \frac{1}{2}\Delta_{uv}\) into the Master Expansion yields:

\[\boxed{E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = A + \frac{C + D - A - B}{2}\,x_u + \frac{B + D - A - C}{2}\,x_v + \frac{B + C - A - D}{2}\Big(x_u(1 - x_v) + x_v(1 - x_u)\Big)}\]

Or, written symmetrically in terms of \((1 - x_u)\) and \(x_u\) when converting a same-side synergy reward \(+E^{\text{art}}_{uv}\,x_u x_v + E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}(1 - x_u)(1 - x_v)\) into regret from \(W_{\text{base}} = E^{\text{art}}_{uv} + E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\) (Luogu P1646 Happiness):

\[\begin{aligned} \text{Regret}_{uv}(x_u, x_v) &= E^{\text{art}}_{uv}(1 - x_u x_v) + E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\big(1 - (1 - x_u)(1 - x_v)\big) \\ &= \frac{E^{\text{art}}_{uv}}{2}\big((1 - x_u) + (1 - x_v)\big) + \frac{E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}}{2}(x_u + x_v) + \frac{E^{\text{art}}_{uv} + E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}}{2}\big(x_u(1 - x_v) + x_v(1 - x_u)\big). \end{aligned}\]

Look at the physical meaning of 合并点边法 (Vertex-Edge Merging):

  • Each endpoint \(u\) and \(v\) absorbs half of the \(S\)-side edge bonus \(\frac{1}{2}E^{\text{art}}_{uv}\) into its own \(S \to u\) and \(S \to v\) unary capacities!
  • Each endpoint \(u\) and \(v\) absorbs half of the \(T\)-side edge bonus \(\frac{1}{2}E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\) into its own \(u \to T\) and \(v \to T\) unary capacities!
  • An undirected conflict edge \(u \leftrightarrow v\) of capacity \(\frac{1}{2}(E^{\text{art}}_{uv} + E^{\text{sci}}_{uv})\) is placed between \(u\) and \(v\):
    • If \(u, v \in V_S\) (\(x_u = x_v = 1\)): both \(u \to T\) and \(v \to T\) are severed, paying \(\frac{1}{2}E^{\text{sci}}_{uv} + \frac{1}{2}E^{\text{sci}}_{uv} = E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\) (losing only the Science bonus!).
    • If \(u, v \in V_T\) (\(x_u = x_v = 0\)): both \(S \to u\) and \(S \to v\) are severed, paying \(\frac{1}{2}E^{\text{art}}_{uv} + \frac{1}{2}E^{\text{art}}_{uv} = E^{\text{art}}_{uv}\) (losing only the Arts bonus!).
    • If \(u\) and \(v\) disagree (\(x_u \neq x_v\)): one vertex severs its \(S\)-arc (\(\frac{1}{2}E^{\text{art}}_{uv}\)), the other severs its \(T\)-arc (\(\frac{1}{2}E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\)), AND the undirected edge \(u \leftrightarrow v\) is severed (\(\frac{1}{2}E^{\text{art}}_{uv} + \frac{1}{2}E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\)), paying a total of \(E^{\text{art}}_{uv} + E^{\text{sci}}_{uv}\) (losing both bonuses)!
WarningThe \(2\times\) Integer Scaling Rule for Symmetric Half-Edge Compilation

Because \(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{uv}\) can be a half-integer (.5) when \(\Delta_{uv}\) is odd, never use floating-point double capacities in Dinic’s algorithm when using Symmetric Half-Edge Compilation! Instead, multiply all capacities and \(W_{\text{base}}\) by \(2\) before running integer Dinic, and divide the final answer \((2 W_{\text{base}} - \text{MaxFlow})\) by \(2\).

2.3 Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace (Both Schemes Side by Side)

Let us trace a concrete 2-variable problem using both compilation schemes to verify that they produce the exact same cut value on all \(4\) states.

Suppose we want to minimize \(\Phi(x_1, x_2) = 5 x_1 + 2(1 - x_2) + E_{1,2}(x_1, x_2)\), where:

\[E_{1,2}(x_1, x_2) = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 9 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.\]

First, check the Kolmogorov–Zabih Submodularity Condition:

\[A + D = 4 + 2 = 6 \le B + C = 9 + 3 = 12 \quad \implies \quad \Delta_{12} = 12 - 6 = 6 \ge 0.\]

The matrix is strictly submodular! The true values of \(\Phi(x_1, x_2)\) on the four states \((x_1, x_2) \in \{0, 1\}^2\) are:

  • \(\Phi(0, 0) = 5(0) + 2(1) + 4 = 6\).
  • \(\Phi(0, 1) = 5(0) + 2(0) + 9 = 9\).
  • \(\Phi(1, 0) = 5(1) + 2(1) + 3 = 10\).
  • \(\Phi(1, 1) = 5(1) + 2(0) + 2 = 7\).

Trace A: Asymmetric Integer Compilation (\(\alpha = 6, \beta = 0\))

  1. Expand \(E_{1,2}(x_1, x_2)\):

    \[E_{1,2}(x_1, x_2) = 4 + (2 - 9)x_1 + (9 - 4)x_2 + 6\,x_1(1 - x_2) = 4 - 7x_1 + 5x_2 + 6\,x_1(1 - x_2).\]

  2. Combine with Unary Terms & Apply Recipe 1 (差値正規化):

    • Vertex \(1\): \(5x_1 - 7x_1 = -2x_1 = -2 + 2(1 - x_1) \implies\) add \(-2\) to \(C_0\), arc \(S \xrightarrow{2} 1\).
    • Vertex \(2\): \(2(1 - x_2) + 5x_2 = 2 + 3x_2 \implies\) add \(+2\) to \(C_0\), arc \(2 \xrightarrow{3} T\).
    • Global constant: \(C_0 = 4 - 2 + 2 = 4\).
    • Pairwise arc: \(1 \xrightarrow{6} 2\).

  3. Check All 4 Cuts:

    • \((0, 0)\): \(C_0 + c(S \to 1) = 4 + 2 = 6\).
    • \((0, 1)\): \(C_0 + c(S \to 1) + c(2 \to T) = 4 + 2 + 3 = 9\).
    • \((1, 0)\): \(C_0 + c(1 \to 2) = 4 + 6 = 10\).
    • \((1, 1)\): \(C_0 + c(2 \to T) = 4 + 3 = 7\).

Trace B: Symmetric Half-Edge Compilation (\(\alpha = \beta = 3\))

  1. Expand \(E_{1,2}(x_1, x_2)\):

    \[E_{1,2}(x_1, x_2) = 4 + \frac{3 + 2 - 4 - 9}{2}x_1 + \frac{9 + 2 - 4 - 3}{2}x_2 + 3\big(x_1(1 - x_2) + x_2(1 - x_1)\big) = 4 - 4x_1 + 2x_2 + 3|x_1 - x_2|.\]

  2. Combine with Unary Terms & Apply Recipe 1:

    • Vertex \(1\): \(5x_1 - 4x_1 = 1x_1 \implies\) arc \(1 \xrightarrow{1} T\).
    • Vertex \(2\): \(2(1 - x_2) + 2x_2 = 2 \implies\) add \(+2\) to \(C_0\) (zero unary arcs on vertex \(2\)!).
    • Global constant: \(C_0 = 4 + 2 = 6\).
    • Pairwise arcs: \(1 \xleftrightarrow{3} 2\) (\(1 \to 2\) cap \(3\) and \(2 \to 1\) cap \(3\)).

  3. Check All 4 Cuts:

    • \((0, 0)\): \(C_0 + 0 = 6\).
    • \((0, 1)\): \(C_0 + c(2 \to 1) = 6 + 3 = 9\).
    • \((1, 0)\): \(C_0 + c(1 \to T) + c(1 \to 2) = 6 + 1 + 3 = 10\).
    • \((1, 1)\): \(C_0 + c(1 \to T) = 6 + 1 = 7\).

Both networks produce the exact cut values \((6, 9, 10, 7)\), and both find the minimum cut \(\Phi(0, 0) = 6\)!

3. Higher-Order Conjunctions (\(k\)-Tuple Same-Side Bonuses 文理分科) & Auxiliary Hub Variables

In Section 2, we saw that any submodular quadratic (\(d = 2\)) pseudo-Boolean function can be compiled using only the original \(N\) vertices. What happens when a problem awards a collective unanimity bonus to a subset \(C \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}\) of size \(k = |C| \ge 3\)?

  • In Luogu P1361 — 小M的作物, planting all crops of a subset \(C\) in Field A (\(x_i = 1\;\forall i \in C\)) yields a bonus \(B_C > 0\), while planting all of them in Field B (\(x_i = 0\;\forall i \in C\)) yields a bonus \(B'_C > 0\).
  • In Luogu P4313 — 文理分科, a student at grid cell \((r, c)\) and their \(4\) orthogonal neighbors form a \(5\)-element cross \(C = N^+[r, c]\) that receives an extra happiness bonus if all \(5\) students choose Arts, or if all \(5\) choose Science.

Algebraically, an all-\(S\) unanimity bonus is the degree-\(k\) monomial \(+B_C \prod_{i \in C} x_i\), and an all-\(T\) unanimity bonus is the degree-\(k\) monomial \(+B'_C \prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i)\). Because every cut arc \(u \to v\) in a flow network is a quadratic polynomial \(c_{uv}\,z_u(1 - z_v)\), a network without auxiliary vertices can only ever represent polynomials of degree \(\le 2\)!

To compile degree-\(k\) conjunctions (\(k \ge 3\)), we must introduce Auxiliary Hub Variables (文理分科 Synergy Hubs) that reduce degree-\(k\) monomials to degree-\(2\) cut terms via marginal optimization.

3.1 Variational Identities for Higher-Order Conjunctions

Let \(C \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}\) be a non-empty subset of variables. How can we express the degree-\(|C|\) product \(\prod_{i \in C} x_i\) using only bilinear cut terms?

ImportantTheorem 10.2 (Auxiliary Hub Variational Identities for \(k\)-Way Unanimity)

For any binary vector \(\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N\) and any subset \(C \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}\):

  1. All-\(S\) Conjunction Identity (\(\prod_{i \in C} x_i = 1 \iff \forall i \in C, x_i = 1\)): Introducing an auxiliary binary hub variable \(y_C \in \{0, 1\}\), we have the exact identity:

    \[\boxed{\prod_{i \in C} x_i = \max_{y_C \in \{0, 1\}} \left[ y_C - \sum_{i \in C} M \cdot y_C(1 - x_i) \right]}\]

    for any penalty multiplier \(M \ge 1\) (in particular, for \(M = 1\) and for \(M = +\infty\)). Equivalently, multiplying by \(B_C > 0\) and converting to regret from the pre-claimed baseline \(+B_C\):

    \[B_C \prod_{i \in C} x_i = B_C - \min_{y_C \in \{0, 1\}} \left[ B_C(1 - y_C) + \sum_{i \in C} \infty \cdot y_C(1 - x_i) \right].\]

  2. All-\(T\) Conjunction Identity (\(\prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i) = 1 \iff \forall i \in C, x_i = 0\)): In boolean algebra, \(\prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i) = \max_{\tilde{z} \in \{0, 1\}} \big[\tilde{z} - \sum_{i \in C} M \cdot x_i \tilde{z}\big]\) where \(\tilde{z} \in \{0, 1\}\) indicates claiming the all-\(T\) bonus. To express \(x_i \tilde{z}\) as a directed cut term \(x_i(1 - z_C)\), we set the hub’s source-side indicator to be the complement \(z_C := 1 - \tilde{z} \in \{0, 1\}\) (so claiming the all-\(T\) bonus corresponds to placing the hub \(z_C\) on the \(T\)-side, \(z_C = 0\), alongside all \(i \in C\)!). Then:

    \[\boxed{\prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i) = \max_{z_C \in \{0, 1\}} \left[ (1 - z_C) - \sum_{i \in C} M \cdot x_i(1 - z_C) \right]}\]

    for any \(M \ge 1\) (and in particular \(M = 1\) or \(M = +\infty\)). Multiplying by \(B'_C > 0\) and converting to regret from \(+B'_C\):

    \[B'_C \prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i) = B'_C - \min_{z_C \in \{0, 1\}} \left[ B'_C\,z_C + \sum_{i \in C} \infty \cdot x_i(1 - z_C) \right].\]

Proof of Both Identities by Exhaustive Case Analysis

Let us verify the All-\(S\) identity for any \(M \ge 1\):

  • Case 1 (\(\forall i \in C, x_i = 1\)): Then \(1 - x_i = 0\) for every \(i \in C\), so the penalty sum \(\sum_{i \in C} M \cdot y_C(1 - x_i)\) is identically \(0\). The bracket reduces to \(\max_{y_C \in \{0, 1\}} y_C = 1\) (achieved at \(y_C = 1\), i.e., \(y_C \in V_S\)), matching \(\prod_{i \in C} x_i = 1\).
  • Case 2 (\(\exists i^* \in C \text{ with } x_{i^*} = 0\)): Let \(d = |\{i \in C : x_i = 0\}| \ge 1\) be the number of members of \(C\) that failed to join \(V_S\).
    • If the hub chooses \(y_C = 0\) (\(y_C \in V_T\)), the bracket evaluates to \(0 - 0 = 0\).
    • If the hub chooses \(y_C = 1\) (\(y_C \in V_S\)), the bracket evaluates to \(1 - M \cdot d \le 1 - 1 = 0\) (and equals \(-\infty\) when \(M = +\infty\)). Hence \(\max_{y_C \in \{0, 1\}} [y_C - M \cdot d \cdot y_C] = 0\) (achieved at \(y_C = 0\)), matching \(\prod_{i \in C} x_i = 0\)!

Symmetrically, for the All-\(T\) identity:

  • Case 1 (\(\forall i \in C, x_i = 0\)): The penalty sum \(\sum_{i \in C} M \cdot x_i(1 - z_C)\) vanishes, so \(\max_{z_C \in \{0, 1\}} (1 - z_C) = 1\) (achieved at \(z_C = 0\), i.e., \(z_C \in V_T\)), matching \(\prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i) = 1\).
  • Case 2 (\(\exists i^* \in C \text{ with } x_{i^*} = 1\)): Choosing \(z_C = 1\) (\(z_C \in V_S\)) gives \((1 - 1) - 0 = 0\), whereas choosing \(z_C = 0\) (\(z_C \in V_T\)) gives \(1 - M \cdot |\{i \in C : x_i = 1\}| \le 0\) (or \(-\infty\) for \(M = +\infty\)). Thus the maximum is \(0\) (achieved at \(z_C = 1\)), matching \(\prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i) = 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

3.2 Step 3: Mechanical Graph Compilation of \(S\)-Hubs and \(T\)-Hubs

Looking at the regret expressions in Theorem 10.2, every single term inside \(\min_{y_C, z_C \in \{0, 1\}}\) is a standard cut term with non-negative capacity:

  1. All-\(S\) Synergy Hub \(y_C\) (Reward \(+B_C \prod_{i \in C} x_i\)):
    • Pre-claim \(+B_C\) into \(W_{\text{base}}\).
    • Term \(B_C(1 - y_C) \implies\) directed arc \(S \xrightarrow{B_C} y_C\).
    • Term \(\infty \cdot y_C(1 - x_i)\) for each \(i \in C \implies\) directed arc \(y_C \xrightarrow{\infty} i\) (from the hub out to each member \(i \in C\)).
  2. All-\(T\) Synergy Hub \(z_C\) (Reward \(+B'_C \prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i)\)):
    • Pre-claim \(+B'_C\) into \(W_{\text{base}}\).
    • Term \(\infty \cdot x_i(1 - z_C)\) for each \(i \in C \implies\) directed arc \(i \xrightarrow{\infty} z_C\) (from each member \(i \in C\) into the hub).
    • Term \(B'_C\,z_C \implies\) directed arc \(z_C \xrightarrow{B'_C} T\).

  • All members in \(V_S\) (\(x_{i_1} = x_{i_2} = x_{i_3} = 1\)): Hub \(y_C\) stays in \(V_S\) (paying \(0\)), while hub \(z_C\) is forced into \(V_S\) by the incoming \(\infty\)-arcs \(i_r \xrightarrow{\infty} z_C\), severing \(z_C \xrightarrow{B'_C} T\). Total cut \(= B'_C \implies\) net reward \((B_C + B'_C) - B'_C = B_C\).
  • All members in \(V_T\) (\(x_{i_1} = x_{i_2} = x_{i_3} = 0\)): Hub \(z_C\) stays in \(V_T\) (paying \(0\)), while hub \(y_C\) is forced into \(V_T\) by the outgoing \(\infty\)-arcs \(y_C \xrightarrow{\infty} i_r\), severing \(S \xrightarrow{B_C} y_C\). Total cut \(= B_C \implies\) net reward \((B_C + B'_C) - B_C = B'_C\).
  • Split configuration (e.g., \(i_1 \in V_S\) and \(i_2 \in V_T\)): Now \(y_C\) is forced into \(V_T\) by \(y_C \xrightarrow{\infty} i_2\) (severing \(S \xrightarrow{B_C} y_C\)), AND \(z_C\) is forced into \(V_S\) by \(i_1 \xrightarrow{\infty} z_C\) (severing \(z_C \xrightarrow{B'_C} T\))! Note that no \(\infty\)-path passes from \(i_1\) to \(i_2\) because \(y_C\) only has outgoing arcs to \(C\) and \(z_C\) only has incoming arcs from \(C\). Both bonus arcs are severed, paying \(B_C + B'_C \implies\) net reward \(0\)!

3.3 The Submodular Representability Boundary (\(d = 2\) vs. \(d = 3\) vs. \(d \ge 4\))

Why did we need auxiliary vertices for \(k \ge 3\) when \(k = 2\) needed none? And can every higher-order submodular function be represented using auxiliary hubs?

  1. \(k = 2\) (Pairwise Synergies): For \(|C| = 2\), \(B_{uv}(1 - x_u x_v)\) is already quadratic, so by Section 2.2 we have a choice: either create an auxiliary hub \(y_{uv}\) (\(1\) extra vertex, \(3\) arcs), or eliminate the hub via Asymmetric Split (\(S \xrightarrow{B_{uv}} u\) and \(u \xrightarrow{B_{uv}} v\)) or Symmetric Half-Edge Split (合并点边法, \(S \xrightarrow{B_{uv}/2} u, v\) and \(u \xleftrightarrow{B_{uv}/2} v\)). When a graph has \(O(N^2)\) dense pairwise synergies (Luogu P1791 / BZOJ 2039 人员雇佣), eliminating the \(O(N^2)\) auxiliary hubs reduces the vertex count from \(O(N^2)\) to \(O(N)\)—the difference between TLE and \(15\text{ ms}\)!
  2. \(k = 3\) (Ternary Submodular Functions — Billionnet–Minoux 1985 / Kolmogorov–Zabih 2004): Every submodular function of \(k = 3\) binary variables is graph-representable using at most two auxiliary vertices (\(y_C\) and \(z_C\)) together with pairwise edges.
  3. \(k \ge 4\) (The Živný–Cohen–Jeavons 2009 Impossibility Boundary): For \(k \ge 4\), auxiliary hubs compile all negative posiform terms \(-B_C \prod_{i \in C} x_i\) and \(-B'_C \prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i)\), which covers \(100\%\) of unanimity-bonus problems in competitive programming. However, Živný, Cohen, and Jeavons (2009) proved a remarkable negative theorem: there exist submodular functions of \(k = 4\) variables (such as cardinality-threshold matroid rank functions) that cannot be represented as the minimum cut of any directed network with non-negative capacities, no matter how many auxiliary vertices are added!

3.4 Broken-Gadget Counterexample FM-10 — The Four Deadly Hub Bugs

WarningBroken-Gadget Counterexamples FM-10: Four Ways Contestants Break \(k\)-Way Synergy Hubs

When compiling a \(k\)-way unanimity bonus \(+B_C \prod_{i \in C} x_i\) for \(|C| = k \ge 3\), four subtle bugs repeatedly appear in contest submissions. Every single one corrupts the minimum cut:

Bug 1: Replacing a \(k\)-Tuple Hub (\(k \ge 3\)) with Pairwise Edges (Partial Disagreement Fallacy)

Suppose \(C = \{1, 2, 3\}\) grants a bonus \(B_C = 6\) if \(x_1 = x_2 = x_3 = 1\), so the true regret function is \(R(x_1, x_2, x_3) = 6(1 - x_1 x_2 x_3)\), which equals \(0\) on \((1, 1, 1)\) and \(6\) on all \(7\) other states. A contestant tries to avoid creating an auxiliary hub by placing symmetric unary capacities \(p\) on \(S \to i\) and undirected pairwise capacities \(q\) on \(i \leftrightarrow j\) for \(1 \le i < j \le 3\). Let \(s = x_1 + x_2 + x_3 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\) be the number of variables in \(V_S\). Any symmetric pairwise network on \(\{1, 2, 3\}\) evaluates to:

\[\text{Cut}(s) = (3 - s)p + s(3 - s)q.\]

Check the required values at \(s = 0, 1, 2\):

  • At \(s = 0\) (\(x = (0,0,0)\)): \(3p = 6 \implies p = 2\).
  • At \(s = 2\) (\(x = (1,1,0)\)): \(p + 2q = 6 \implies 2 + 2q = 6 \implies q = 2\).
  • Now evaluate at \(s = 1\) (\(x = (1,0,0)\)): \(\text{Cut}(1) = 2p + 2q = 2(2) + 2(2) = \mathbf{8 \neq 6}\)!

Why it fails: A pairwise cut polynomial is strictly quadratic in \(s\), so its second difference \(\Delta^2 \text{Cut}(s) = -2q\) is constant, whereas \(6(1 - x_1 x_2 x_3)\) is flat on \(s \in \{0, 1, 2\}\) (\(\Delta^2 = 0\)) and drops by \(6\) at \(s = 3\) (\(\Delta^2 = -6\)). Without an auxiliary hub, pairwise edges over-penalize \((1, 0, 0)\) or under-penalize \((1, 1, 0)\) (allowing partial payment for partial disagreement)!

Bug 2: Putting Finite Capacity \(B_C\) (Instead of \(+\infty\)) on Hub-to-Member Arcs

In Theorem 10.2, the mathematical identity holds even if the hub-to-member arcs \(y_C \to i\) have finite capacity \(B_C\) (corresponding to \(M = 1\)). Why do we mandate \(+\infty\) in our compilation protocol? Because contestants frequently write capacity \(B_C / |C|\) on \(y_C \to i\) (“splitting the bonus among the \(|C|\) members”). If \(\text{cap}(y_C \to i) = B_C / |C|\) and only one member \(i^* \in C\) chooses \(x_{i^*} = 0\), the solver leaves \(y_C \in V_S\) and severs only the single arc \(y_C \to i^*\) of cost \(B_C / |C| < B_C\), falsely keeping \(\frac{|C|-1}{|C|} B_C\) of a bonus that required \(100\%\) unanimity! Using \(+\infty\) on \(y_C \to i\) makes this bug impossible and also helps Dinic’s level graph prune dead ends faster.

Bug 3: Reversing the Hub Implication Orientation (H-10B)

Suppose for an All-\(S\) bonus (\(S \xrightarrow{B_C} y_C\)) you accidentally wire \(i \xrightarrow{\infty} y_C\) instead of \(y_C \xrightarrow{\infty} i\). Then when all \(i \in C\) are placed in \(V_T\) (\(x_i = 0\)) and the hub is placed in \(V_S\) (\(y_C = 1\)), every arc \(i \to y_C\) goes backward from \(V_T\) to \(V_S\), contributing \(0\) to the directed \((S, T)\)-cut! The solver keeps \(y_C \in V_S\) for free and collects \(B_C\) even when no member of \(C\) is in \(V_S\)! Mnemonic: Flow always travels \(S \to \text{All-}S\text{ Hub } y_C \to \text{Members } i \to \text{All-}T\text{ Hub } z_C \to T\).

Bug 4: Merging \(y_C\) and \(z_C\) into a Single Hub Node \(h_C\)

When a subset \(C\) has both an All-\(S\) bonus (\(B_C\)) and an All-\(T\) bonus (\(B'_C\)), a contestant creates a single hub node \(h_C\) with \(S \xrightarrow{B_C} h_C \xrightarrow{B'_C} T\) and wires \(h_C \xleftrightarrow{\infty} i\) for all \(i \in C\). This creates directed \(\infty\)-paths \(i_1 \xrightarrow{\infty} h_C \xrightarrow{\infty} i_2\) between every pair of members in \(C\), making it impossible for \(C\) to split between \(V_S\) and \(V_T\) in any finite cut! You must always allocate two separate nodes \(y_C\) and \(z_C\).

4. General Non-Submodular Quadratic Pseudo-Boolean Optimization — Roof Duality & QPBO (\(x_i\) vs. \(\bar{x}_i\) Double-Cover Graph)

What happens when a quadratic pseudo-Boolean minimization problem

\[\min_{\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N} \Phi(\mathbf{x}) = C_0 + \sum_{i=1}^N U_i(x_i) + \sum_{1 \le i < j \le N} E_{i,j}(x_i, x_j)\]

contains supermodular pairs where \(A + D > B + C\) (\(\Delta_{ij} = B + C - A - D < 0\))?

As we proved in Theorem 10.1, no \((S, T)\)-cut network on the variables \(x_1, \dots, x_N\) can represent \(E_{i,j}\) directly. In fact, minimizing a general supermodular quadratic pseudo-Boolean function is NP-hard (it immediately encodes Maximum Cut and Maximum Weight Independent Set on arbitrary graphs).

Nevertheless, Hammer, Hansen, and Simeone (1984) and Boros, Hammer, and Tavares (2002, 2007) discovered a remarkable construction—known in computer vision and combinatorial optimization as Roof Duality / Quadratic Pseudo-Boolean Optimization (QPBO)—that compiles any quadratic pseudo-Boolean function (with arbitrary submodular and supermodular terms!) into a minimum cut on a \(2N\)-vertex Double-Cover Network \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\).

4.1 Algebraic Derivation of the \(x_i\) vs. \(\bar{x}_i\) Double-Cover Graph

Why does a supermodular term fail in the original variables \(\mathbf{x}\)? Because after expanding \(E_{i,j}(x_i, x_j)\), a supermodular pair leaves a positive bilinear product:

\[+|\Delta_{ij}|\,x_i x_j \qquad \text{where } |\Delta_{ij}| = (A + D) - (B + C) > 0.\]

We cannot write \(+x_i x_j\) as a positive multiple of \(x_i(1 - x_j)\). However, suppose we introduce for each variable \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\) a formal negated twin variable \(\bar{x}_i \in \{0, 1\}\) intended to satisfy the coupling constraint:

\[\bar{x}_i = 1 - x_i \qquad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, N\}.\]

If \(\bar{x}_i = 1 - x_i\) and \(\bar{x}_j = 1 - x_j\), then \(x_j = 1 - \bar{x}_j\) and \(x_i = 1 - \bar{x}_i\)! Substituting these into the problematic product \(x_i x_j\) turns it into a standard directed cut term between \(x_i\) and \(\bar{x}_j\) (or symmetrically between \(x_j\) and \(\bar{x}_i\)):

\[x_i x_j = \frac{1}{2}\,x_i(1 - \bar{x}_j) + \frac{1}{2}\,x_j(1 - \bar{x}_i)!\]

And what about a standard submodular cut term \(x_i(1 - x_j)\)? Since \(x_i = 1 - \bar{x}_i\) and \(1 - x_j = \bar{x}_j\), we can also symmetrize it across the twin variables:

\[x_i(1 - x_j) = \frac{1}{2}\,x_i(1 - x_j) + \frac{1}{2}\,\bar{x}_j(1 - \bar{x}_i)!\]

Symmetrizing the unary terms in the exact same way (\(x_i = \frac{1}{2}x_i + \frac{1}{2}(1 - \bar{x}_i)\) and \(1 - x_i = \frac{1}{2}(1 - x_i) + \frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_i\)) yields the Skew-Symmetric Double-Cover Network \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) on \(2N + 2\) vertices:

\[V(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}) = \{S, T\} \cup \{x_1, \dots, x_N\} \cup \{\bar{x}_1, \dots, \bar{x}_N\}.\]

Explicit Construction Rules for \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) (Scaled by \(2\times\) for Integer Capacities)

To avoid \(\frac{1}{2}\) fractions, multiply \(\Phi(\mathbf{x})\) by \(2\). Then every term in \(2\Phi(\mathbf{x})\) compiles into a dual pair of twin arcs in \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) with non-negative integer capacities:

Term in Original \(\Phi(\mathbf{x})\) Algebraic Twin Identity in \(2\Phi(\mathbf{x}, \bar{\mathbf{x}})\) Twin Directed Arcs Added to \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\)
Unary penalty \(c(1 - x_i)\) (\(c > 0\)) \(c(1 - x_i) + c\,\bar{x}_i\) \(S \xrightarrow{c} x_i\) and \(\bar{x}_i \xrightarrow{c} T\)
Unary penalty \(c\,x_i\) (\(c > 0\)) \(c\,x_i + c(1 - \bar{x}_i)\) \(x_i \xrightarrow{c} T\) and \(S \xrightarrow{c} \bar{x}_i\)
Submodular pair \(\Delta\,x_i(1 - x_j)\) (\(\Delta > 0\)) \(\Delta\,x_i(1 - x_j) + \Delta\,\bar{x}_j(1 - \bar{x}_i)\) \(x_i \xrightarrow{\Delta} x_j\) and \(\bar{x}_j \xrightarrow{\Delta} \bar{x}_i\)
Supermodular pair \(|\Delta|\,x_i x_j\) (\(|\Delta| > 0\)) \(|\Delta|\,x_i(1 - \bar{x}_j) + |\Delta|\,x_j(1 - \bar{x}_i)\) \(x_i \xrightarrow{|\Delta|} \bar{x}_j\) and \(x_j \xrightarrow{|\Delta|} \bar{x}_i\)
Supermodular pair \(|\Delta|(1 - x_i)(1 - x_j)\) \(|\Delta|\,\bar{x}_i(1 - x_j) + |\Delta|\,\bar{x}_j(1 - x_i)\) \(\bar{x}_i \xrightarrow{|\Delta|} x_j\) and \(\bar{x}_j \xrightarrow{|\Delta|} x_i\)

4.2 Half-Integrality, Roof Duality & The Strong Persistency (Autarky) Theorem

Wait: if minimizing \(\Phi(\mathbf{x})\) is NP-hard in general, what is the catch in \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\)?

The catch is that a minimum \((S, T)\)-cut in \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) is not required to satisfy the coupling constraint \(\bar{x}_i = 1 - x_i\)! When Dinic’s algorithm computes a minimum cut \((V_S, V_T)\) of \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\), each variable pair \((x_i, \bar{x}_i)\) can land in one of four cut states, which correspond to a half-integral variable assignment \(\hat{x}_i \in \{0, \frac{1}{2}, 1\}\) defined by:

\[\boxed{\hat{x}_i := \frac{x_i + (1 - \bar{x}_i)}{2} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x_i \in V_S \text{ and } \bar{x}_i \in V_T \quad (\text{coupled: } x_i = 1), \\ 0 & \text{if } x_i \in V_T \text{ and } \bar{x}_i \in V_S \quad (\text{coupled: } x_i = 0), \\ \tfrac{1}{2} & \text{if } x_i, \bar{x}_i \in V_S \text{ or } x_i, \bar{x}_i \in V_T \quad (\text{uncoupled / frustrated!}). \end{cases}}\]

Even when some variables evaluate to \(\hat{x}_i = \frac{1}{2}\), the minimum cut of \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) satisfies three profound structural theorems:

ImportantTheorem 10.3 (Hammer–Hansen–Simeone 1984 / Boros–Hammer 2002 — Roof Duality & Strong Persistency)

Let \((V_S, V_T)\) be a minimum \((S, T)\)-cut in \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) (or specifically the canonical \(S\)-minimal cut \(S_{\min}\)), and let \(\hat{\mathbf{x}} \in \{0, \frac{1}{2}, 1\}^N\) be the induced half-integral assignment. Then:

  1. Exact Roof Dual / LP Relaxation Equivalence: \(\frac{1}{2}\text{MinCut}(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}})\) equals the exact minimum of the standard linearization LP relaxation of \(\Phi(\mathbf{x})\) over \(\mathbf{x} \in [0, 1]^N\), and \(\hat{\mathbf{x}} \in \{0, \frac{1}{2}, 1\}^N\) is an optimal half-integral LP solution. (When specialized to Maximum Weight Independent Set, this recovers the classic Nemhauser–Trotter Theorem (1975)!)

  2. Strong Persistency (強永続性 / Autarky Property): There exists a globally optimal binary assignment \(\mathbf{x}^* \in \arg\min_{\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N} \Phi(\mathbf{x})\) that agrees with \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}\) on every integral coordinate:

    \[\boxed{\forall i \in \{1, \dots, N\}, \qquad \hat{x}_i \in \{0, 1\} \implies x^*_i = \hat{x}_i}\]

    (Weak Persistency holds for any minimum cut of \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\): fixing \(x_i = \hat{x}_i\) for all \(\hat{x}_i \in \{0, 1\}\) preserves at least one global optimum. Strong Persistency holds when \((V_S, V_T)\) is chosen as the \(S\)-minimal cut \(S_{\min}\): every global optimum \(\mathbf{x}^*\) satisfies \(x^*_i = \hat{x}_i\) on the integral coordinates!)

  3. Exactness on Bipartite / Harary-Balanced Graphs: Suppose the signed interaction graph \(G = (V, E^+ \sqcup E^-)\)—where \(E^+\) are the submodular edges (\(\Delta_{uv} > 0\)) and \(E^-\) are the supermodular edges (\(\Delta_{uv} < 0\))—is Harary-balanced (i.e., no cycle in \(G\) contains an odd number of supermodular edges \(E^-\)). Then \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}} \setminus \{S, T\}\) disconnects into two isomorphic, non-interacting components, and taking \(S_{\min}\) on one component produces a 100% integral optimal binary solution \(\hat{\mathbf{x}} \in \{0, 1\}^N\)!

Why Strong Persistency Works (The Autarky Exchange Proof Sketch)

Why can we permanently lock every variable with \(\hat{x}_i \in \{0, 1\}\) without ever hurting the global optimum on the remaining \(\hat{x}_j = \frac{1}{2}\) variables?

Partition the variable indices \(\{1, \dots, N\}\) into the Resolved Integral Set \(I = \{i : \hat{x}_i \in \{0, 1\}\}\) (where \(x_i\) and \(\bar{x}_i\) lie on opposite sides of the cut) and the Unresolved Half-Integral Core \(U = \{j : \hat{x}_j = \frac{1}{2}\}\) (where \(x_j\) and \(\bar{x}_j\) lie on the same side of the cut).

Take any arbitrary binary assignment \(\mathbf{y} \in \{0, 1\}^N\), and define a hybrid binary assignment \(\mathbf{z} \in \{0, 1\}^N\) that copies \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}\) on the resolved set \(I\) and copies \(\mathbf{y}\) on the unresolved set \(U\):

\[z_k = \begin{cases} \hat{x}_k & \text{if } k \in I, \\ y_k & \text{if } k \in U. \end{cases}\]

In the doubled network \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\), consider altering the minimum cut \((x, \bar{x})\) only on the vertices of \(I\) by replacing \((x_i, \bar{x}_i)\) with \((y_i, 1 - y_i)\) for \(i \in I\). Because \((x, \bar{x})\) is a minimum cut of \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\), this alteration cannot decrease the cut capacity! Crucially, for any cross-edge between \(i \in I\) and \(j \in U\), because \(x_j\) and \(\bar{x}_j\) lie on the same side of the cut (say both in \(V_S\) or both in \(V_T\)), the sum of capacities severed by \(i\) on its twin edges to \(x_j\) and \(\bar{x}_j\) is constant whether \(x_i = 1, \bar{x}_i = 0\) or \(x_i = 0, \bar{x}_i = 1\)! Thus the boundary terms between \(I\) and \(U\) cancel out identically in the optimality inequality, yielding:

\[\Phi(\mathbf{z}) \le \Phi(\mathbf{y}) \qquad \forall \mathbf{y} \in \{0, 1\}^N.\]

Choosing \(\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x}^*\) (a global binary optimum) proves that \(\mathbf{z}\)—which agrees with \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}\) on all \(i \in I\)—is also a global binary optimum! \(\blacksquare\)

4.3 Step 4: Concrete Toy Trace of QPBO Persistency on a Frustrated Graph

Let us see Theorem 10.3 in action on a 4-vertex non-bipartite Maximum Weight Independent Set problem where bipartite variable flipping is impossible because of an odd cycle (\(K_3\)), yet QPBO still locks the persistent tail vertex!

Consider \(N = 4\) vertices with weights \(w_1 = 4, w_2 = 4, w_3 = 4\) forming a triangle \(K_3 = \{(1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 1)\}\), plus a 4th vertex \(4\) of weight \(w_4 = 10\) connected only to vertex \(3\) via edge \((3, 4)\).

  • Goal: Maximize \(\sum_{i=1}^4 w_i x_i\) subject to \(x_u x_v = 0\) for all edges \((u, v)\), or equivalently minimize the regret:

    \[\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^4 w_i(1 - x_i) + \sum_{(u, v) \in E} \infty \cdot x_u x_v \qquad (W_{\text{base}} = 4 + 4 + 4 + 10 = 22).\]

  • Build \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) (Scaled by \(2\times\), so \(2 W_{\text{base}} = 44\)):

    • Unary arcs: \(S \xrightarrow{w_i} x_i\) and \(\bar{x}_i \xrightarrow{w_i} T\) for \(i = 1, 2, 3, 4\) (with \(w = (4, 4, 4, 10)\)).
    • Supermodular conflict edges \(\infty \cdot x_u x_v\): for each edge \((u, v) \in \{(1,2), (2,3), (3,1), (3,4)\}\), add \(x_u \xrightarrow{\infty} \bar{x}_v\) and \(x_v \xrightarrow{\infty} \bar{x}_u\).

  • Compute the Minimum Cut in \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\):

    • Look at vertex \(4\) (\(w_4 = 10\)) and vertex \(3\) (\(w_3 = 4\)): the path \(S \xrightarrow{10} x_4 \xrightarrow{\infty} \bar{x}_3 \xrightarrow{4} T\) pushes \(4\) units of flow, saturating \(\bar{x}_3 \to T\) and leaving \(6\) units of residual capacity on \(S \to x_4\)!
    • Symmetrically, \(S \xrightarrow{4} x_3 \xrightarrow{\infty} \bar{x}_4 \xrightarrow{10} T\) pushes \(4\) units of flow, saturating \(S \to x_3\).
    • On the remaining triangle \(\{1, 2\}\), \(S \xrightarrow{4} x_1 \xrightarrow{\infty} \bar{x}_2 \xrightarrow{4} T\) pushes \(4\) units of flow, and \(S \xrightarrow{4} x_2 \xrightarrow{\infty} \bar{x}_1 \xrightarrow{4} T\) pushes \(4\) units of flow.
    • Now check reachability from \(S\) in the residual graph (\(S_{\min}\)):
      • \(x_4\) is still reachable from \(S\) (\(10 - 4 = 6 > 0\)), which forces \(\bar{x}_3 \in V_S\) via \(x_4 \xrightarrow{\infty} \bar{x}_3\)!
      • Meanwhile, \(x_3, \bar{x}_4, x_1, \bar{x}_1, x_2, \bar{x}_2\) are all unreachable from \(S\) (they lie in \(V_T\)).

  • Read Off the QPBO Half-Integral Assignment \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}\):

    • Vertex \(4\): \(x_4 \in V_S, \bar{x}_4 \in V_T \implies \mathbf{\hat{x}_4 = 1}\) (Resolved & Persistent!).
    • Vertex \(3\): \(x_3 \in V_T, \bar{x}_3 \in V_S \implies \mathbf{\hat{x}_3 = 0}\) (Resolved & Persistent!).
    • Vertices \(1\) and \(2\): \(x_1, \bar{x}_1 \in V_T\) and \(x_2, \bar{x}_2 \in V_T \implies \mathbf{\hat{x}_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}, \;\hat{x}_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}}\) (Unresolved Frustrated Core!).

Look at what QPBO accomplished in a single max-flow call: even though the graph contains an odd cycle \(K_3\), QPBO automatically proved that every globally optimal independent set must pick vertex \(4\) (\(\hat{x}_4 = 1\)) and exclude vertex \(3\) (\(\hat{x}_3 = 0\)), shrinking the NP-hard search space to the tiny frustrated core \(\{1, 2\}\)! And as we will see in Chapter 11, whenever the conflict graph is bipartite (or Harary-balanced), the frustrated \(\frac{1}{2}\)-core is guaranteed to be empty—so \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) folds in half to give the exact Bipartite Variable Inversion network!

5. The 4-Step Burn-or-Bury (燃やす埋める) Compilation Protocol

In the Japanese competitive programming literature (pioneered by noshi91, maspypy, and tatyam), compiling a binary decision problem into a minimum cut is affectionately called Burn-or-Bury (燃やす埋める, “Moyasu-Umeru”)—originally named after farming problems where each item \(i\) is either burned (\(x_i = 1\), placed in \(V_S\)) or buried (\(x_i = 0\), placed in \(V_T\)).

To guarantee that you never guess an edge orientation or sign in a live contest, execute the following 4-Step Burn-or-Bury Compilation Contract on scratch paper before writing a single line of code:

Algorithmic Contract: The 4-Step Compilation Pipeline

  1. Step 1 — Fix the Binary Dictionary & Optimistic Baseline (\(W_{\text{base}}\)):

    • Explicitly write down the physical meaning of \(x_i = 1\) (\(i \in V_S\)) and \(x_i = 0\) (\(i \in V_T\)) for every decision entity \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\).

    • If the problem is a maximization problem \(\max_{\mathbf{x}} \Psi(\mathbf{x})\), initialize an accumulator long long W_base = 0 and pre-claim every positive reward \(r > 0\) into W_base, converting the objective into regret minimization:

      \[\max_{\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N} \Psi(\mathbf{x}) = W_{\text{base}} - \min_{\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N} \Phi(\mathbf{x}).\]

  2. Step 2 — Write the Algebraic Regret / Cost Polynomial \(\Phi(\mathbf{x})\):

    • Express every unary cost/regret at vertex \(i\) as \(p_i(1 - x_i) + q_i x_i\).

    • Express every pairwise cost/regret table \(E_{u,v}(x_u, x_v) = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}\) and check the Kolmogorov–Zabih Submodularity Invariant:

      \[\Delta_{uv} = (B + C) - (A + D) \ge 0.\]

      (If \(\Delta_{uv} < 0\), stop immediately: you must either apply Bipartite Variable Inversion \(y_v = 1 - x_v\) on one side of a bipartition, as covered in Chapter 11, or use \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) from Section 4!)

    • Express every higher-order \(k\)-tuple unanimity bonus \(+B_C \prod_{i \in C} x_i\) (All-in-S) or \(+B'_C \prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i)\) (All-in-T) via Theorem 10.2’s auxiliary hub variables \(y_C, z_C \in \{0, 1\}\).

  3. Step 3 — Accumulate Unary Terms & Emit Directed Cut Arcs:
    • For each submodular pairwise table \(E_{u,v}\), apply either Asymmetric Integer Compilation (\(\alpha = \Delta_{uv}, \beta = 0\)) or Symmetric Half-Edge Compilation (合并点边法, \(\alpha = \beta = \frac{1}{2}\Delta_{uv}\)):
      • Add constant \(A\) to \(C_0\), accumulate \((C - A - \alpha)x_u\) onto \(q_u\), accumulate \((B - A - \beta)x_v\) onto \(q_v\), and emit internal arcs \(u \xrightarrow{\alpha} v\) and \(v \xrightarrow{\beta} u\).
    • After all pairwise unary shifts have been accumulated into \((p_i, q_i)\), apply Recipe 1 (差値正規化) at every vertex \(i\):
      • Let \(m_i = \min(p_i, q_i)\). Add \(m_i\) to \(C_0\).
      • If \(p_i > q_i\), emit \(S \xrightarrow{p_i - q_i} i\). If \(q_i > p_i\), emit \(i \xrightarrow{q_i - p_i} T\).
    • Emit all higher-order hub arcs (\(S \xrightarrow{B_C} y_C \xrightarrow{\infty} i\) and \(i \xrightarrow{\infty} z_C \xrightarrow{B'_C} T\)).
  4. Step 4 — Toy-Trace Verification & 64-Bit Overflow Audit:
    • Check that INF exceeds the maximum possible finite cut capacity \(\sum c_{\text{finite}}\) without overflowing int64_t when added during Dinic’s bottleneck calculation.
    • Return \(C_0 + \text{MaxFlow}(S, T)\) for minimization, or \(W_{\text{base}} - C_0 - \text{MaxFlow}(S, T)\) for maximization.

#073 (SS-1) — POJ 3469: Dual Core CPU

  • Judge URL: http://poj.org/problem?id=3469
  • Constraints: \(N \le 20\,000\) software modules, \(M \le 200\,000\) communication pairs; execution costs \(A_i, B_i \le 50\,000\), communication penalty \(w_k \le 50\,000\). Time Limit: 15.0 s (case-heavy input).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Each of \(N\) modules must be placed on either Core A or Core B. Module \(i\) costs \(A_i\) on Core A and \(B_i\) on Core B. Furthermore, for \(M\) given pairs \((u, v, w)\), executing \(u\) and \(v\) on different cores incurs a data-transfer penalty \(w > 0\). We must minimize the total execution plus communication cost.

This is the canonical Pairwise Markov Random Field (MRF) / Metric Labeling problem for \(K = 2\) labels: unary assignment costs coupled with symmetric pairwise separation penalties \(w_{uv}|x_u - x_v|\). Because \(N = 20\,000\) and \(M = 200\,000\) on a general graph, treewidth is unbounded and only an \(O(M\sqrt{N})\) flow solver with compact memory layout can pass.

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

Let \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\) indicate the core assignment of module \(i\):

  • \(x_i = 1\) (\(i \in V_S\)): Module \(i\) runs on Core B (incurring unary cost \(B_i\)).
  • \(x_i = 0\) (\(i \in V_T\)): Module \(i\) runs on Core A (incurring unary cost \(A_i\)).

For each communication pair \((u, v, w)\), the \(2 \times 2\) penalty matrix is \(E_{u,v} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & w \\ w & 0 \end{pmatrix}\), which satisfies \(A + D = 0 \le B + C = 2w\) (\(\Delta_{uv} = 2w > 0\), strictly submodular). Summing over all modules and pairs gives the exact cut polynomial:

\[\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \big(A_i(1 - x_i) + B_i x_i\big) + \sum_{(u, v, w)} \big(w\,x_u(1 - x_v) + w\,x_v(1 - x_u)\big).\]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical Graph Compilation)

  1. Unary Pre-Flow (差値正規化, Recipe 1): For each module \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\), let \(m_i = \min(A_i, B_i)\) and add \(m_i\) directly to base_cost:
    • If \(A_i > B_i\), emit \(S \xrightarrow{A_i - B_i} i\).
    • If \(B_i > A_i\), emit \(i \xrightarrow{B_i - A_i} T\). (This immediately eliminates half the source/sink edges and pre-pushes \(\sum_i \min(A_i, B_i)\) units of flow in \(O(N)\) time!)
  2. Bidirectional Communication Arcs: For each pair \((u, v, w)\), emit a single adjacency-list edge pair \(u \leftrightarrow v\) where both the forward edge \(u \to v\) and its residual reverse edge \(v \to u\) are initialized with capacity \(w\) (add_undirected_edge(u, v, w)). This halves the memory footprint of the \(M\) communication edges!

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

Suppose \(N = 3\) modules with \((A_1, B_1) = (10, 4)\), \((A_2, B_2) = (3, 12)\), \((A_3, B_3) = (8, 5)\), and one communication link \((1, 2, w = 5)\).

  • Recipe 1 Pre-Flow:
    • Module \(1\): \(m_1 = 4\), base_cost += 4, arc \(S \xrightarrow{6} 1\).
    • Module \(2\): \(m_2 = 3\), base_cost += 3, arc \(2 \xrightarrow{9} T\).
    • Module \(3\): \(m_3 = 5\), base_cost += 5, arc \(S \xrightarrow{3} 3\).
    • Total base_cost \(= 4 + 3 + 5 = 12\).
  • Communication Arc: \(1 \xleftrightarrow{5} 2\).
  • Min-Cut: Only a single augmenting path \(S \xrightarrow{6} 1 \xrightarrow{5} 2 \xrightarrow{9} T\) exists, pushing \(\min(6, 5, 9) = 5\) units of flow.
  • Total Minimum Cost: \(\text{base\_cost} + \text{MaxFlow} = 12 + 5 = 17\) (achieved by \(x_1 = 1\) [Core B, cost \(4\)], \(x_2 = 0\) [Core A, cost \(3\)], \(x_3 = 1\) [Core B, cost \(5\)], plus communication penalty \(5 \implies 4 + 3 + 5 + 5 = 17\)).

[!CAUTION] Failure-Mode Guard (POJ 3469): Adding only a one-way directed edge \(u \to v\) of capacity \(w\) penalizes \((x_u = 1, x_v = 0)\) but leaves \((x_u = 0, x_v = 1)\) with zero penalty! Always initialize rev.cap = w for symmetric separation costs.

Note3-Tier Progressive Hints (#073 — POJ 3469)
  • Hint 1: Express the cost of module \(i\) as \(A_i(1 - x_i) + B_i x_i\) and the communication penalty as \(w|x_u - x_v| = w\,x_u(1 - x_v) + w\,x_v(1 - x_u)\).
  • Hint 2: Use Recipe 1 (差値正規化) to extract \(\min(A_i, B_i)\) into a running sum so that each module needs only one edge to either \(S\) or \(T\).
  • Hint 3: Store each bidirectional communication link \((u, v, w)\) as a single forward/reverse edge pair with both capacities initialized to \(w\).

// Assumes reader's Dinic<long long> library with:
//   G.add_edge(u, v, forward_cap, reverse_cap = 0)
//   G.max_flow(S, T)
void solve_poj3469() {
    int N, M;
    if (!(cin >> N >> M)) return;
    int S = 0, T = N + 1;
    Dinic<long long> G(T + 1);

    long long base_cost = 0;
    for (int i = 1; i <= N; ++i) {
        long long A, B;
        cin >> A >> B;
        long long m = min(A, B);
        base_cost += m;
        if (A > B) {
            G.add_edge(S, i, A - B, 0); // Cut if i in V_T (Core A)
        } else if (B > A) {
            G.add_edge(i, T, B - A, 0); // Cut if i in V_S (Core B)
        }
    }

    for (int k = 0; k < M; ++k) {
        int u, v;
        long long w;
        cin >> u >> v >> w;
        // Bidirectional edge u <-> v of capacity w in both directions
        G.add_edge(u, v, w, w);
    }

    cout << base_cost + G.max_flow(S, T) << "\n";
}

#074 (SS-2) — Luogu P2057 [SHOI2007]: 善意的投票 (Well-Intentioned Vote)

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P2057
  • Constraints: \(N \le 300\) children, \(M \le \binom{N}{2}\) friendship pairs, initial preference \(c_i \in \{0, 1\}\). Time Limit: 1.0 s.

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Each child \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) has an initial preference \(c_i \in \{0, 1\}\) (nap vs. no nap) and chooses a final vote \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\). A conflict of \(+1\) occurs whenever a child votes against their own preference (\(x_i \neq c_i\)) and whenever two friends \(\{u, v\}\) cast opposite votes (\(x_u \neq x_v\)). Minimize total conflicts.

This is the unweighted Ferromagnetic Ising Model on an arbitrary friendship graph: a special case of #073 where all unary and pairwise capacities equal \(1\).

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

For each child \(i\), \([x_i \neq c_i] = (1 - x_i)\) if \(c_i = 1\), and \([x_i \neq c_i] = x_i\) if \(c_i = 0\). For each friend pair \(\{u, v\}\), \([x_u \neq x_v] = x_u(1 - x_v) + x_v(1 - x_u)\). Thus:

\[\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i : c_i = 1} (1 - x_i) + \sum_{i : c_i = 0} x_i + \sum_{\{u, v\} \in E} \big(x_u(1 - x_v) + x_v(1 - x_u)\big).\]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical Graph Compilation)

  • If \(c_i = 1\): add arc \(S \xrightarrow{1} i\) (severed if \(x_i = 0\)).
  • If \(c_i = 0\): add arc \(i \xrightarrow{1} T\) (severed if \(x_i = 1\)).
  • For each friend pair \(\{u, v\}\): add bidirectional unit edge \(u \xleftrightarrow{1} v\) (G.add_edge(u, v, 1, 1)).
  • Complexity: Because every arc in the network has unit capacity \(1\), Dinic’s algorithm runs in \(O(M\sqrt{N})\) time (\(\le 8 \cdot 10^5\) operations for \(N = 300\)).

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

Suppose \(N = 3\) with preferences \(c = (1, 1, 0)\) and friendships \(\{1, 3\}\) and \(\{2, 3\}\).

  • Arcs: \(S \xrightarrow{1} 1\), \(S \xrightarrow{1} 2\), \(3 \xrightarrow{1} T\), plus \(1 \xleftrightarrow{1} 3\) and \(2 \xleftrightarrow{1} 3\).
  • Augmenting paths \(S \to 1 \to 3 \to T\) (flow \(1\)), leaving \(3 \to T\) saturated! Vertex \(2\) has no augmenting path to \(T\).
  • Minimum cut \(= 1\) (child \(3\) flips their vote to \(x_3 = 1\) to agree with both friends \(1\) and \(2\), incurring \(1\) self-conflict instead of \(2\) friend conflicts).
Note3-Tier Progressive Hints (#074 — Luogu P2057)

  • Hint 2: Friend disagreement \([x_u \neq x_v]\) has penalty table \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\), which is submodular with \(\Delta = 2\).
  • Hint 3: Wire \(S \xrightarrow{1} i\) for \(c_i = 1\), \(i \xrightarrow{1} T\) for \(c_i = 0\), and \(u \xleftrightarrow{1} v\) for each friend pair.

#075 (Int-1) — Luogu P1361: 小M的作物 (Little M’s Crops)

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P1361
  • Constraints: \(N \le 1000\) crops, \(M \le 1000\) synergy combinations; individual profits \(a_i, b_i \le 1000\), combination bonuses \(c_{k,1}, c_{k,2} \le 1000\). Time Limit: 1.0 s.

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Each crop \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) is planted in Field A (\(x_i = 1\), profit \(a_i\)) or Field B (\(x_i = 0\), profit \(b_i\)). In addition, \(M\) subset combinations \((C_k, c_{k,1}, c_{k,2})\) are given: if all crops in \(C_k\) are planted in Field A, we gain \(+c_{k,1}\); if all crops in \(C_k\) are planted in Field B, we gain \(+c_{k,2}\). Maximize total profit.

Because \(|C_k|\) can be arbitrary (\(2 \le |C_k| \le N\)), pairwise edges cannot represent the degree-\(|C_k|\) conjunctions (FM-10 Bug 1). This is the pure archetype for Theorem 10.2’s Dual Synergy Hubs (\(y_k\) and \(z_k\)).

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

Pre-claim all possible profits into \(W_{\text{base}} = \sum_{i=1}^N (a_i + b_i) + \sum_{k=1}^M (c_{k,1} + c_{k,2})\) (or use Recipe 1 on unary terms). By Theorem 10.2, the regret polynomial is:

\[\Phi(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \big(a_i(1 - x_i) + b_i x_i\big) + \sum_{k=1}^M \left[ c_{k,1}(1 - y_k) + \sum_{i \in C_k} \infty \cdot y_k(1 - x_i) \right] + \sum_{k=1}^M \left[ c_{k,2}\,z_k + \sum_{i \in C_k} \infty \cdot x_i(1 - z_k) \right].\]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical Graph Compilation)

  • Vertex Allocation:
    • Crops: \(1 \dots N\).
    • Field A (All-in-S) hubs: \(y_k = N + k\) for \(k = 1 \dots M\).
    • Field B (All-in-T) hubs: \(z_k = N + M + k\) for \(k = 1 \dots M\).
    • Total vertices: \(N + 2M + 2 \le 3002\).
  • Arcs:
    • For each crop \(i\): add \(\max(a_i, b_i)\) to ans_base; if \(a_i > b_i\), wire \(S \xrightarrow{a_i - b_i} i\), else if \(b_i > a_i\), wire \(i \xrightarrow{b_i - a_i} T\).
    • For each combination \(k\): add \(c_{k,1} + c_{k,2}\) to ans_base; wire \(S \xrightarrow{c_{k,1}} y_k\), \(z_k \xrightarrow{c_{k,2}} T\), and for every \(i \in C_k\), wire \(y_k \xrightarrow{\infty} i\) and \(i \xrightarrow{\infty} z_k\).

  • Arcs: \(S \xrightarrow{10} 1\), \(2 \xrightarrow{10} T\), \(S \xrightarrow{6} y_1 \xrightarrow{\infty} \{1, 2\}\), and \(\{1, 2\} \xrightarrow{\infty} z_1 \xrightarrow{6} T\).

    • Path 2: \(S \xrightarrow{10} 1 \xrightarrow{\infty} z_1 \xrightarrow{6} T\) pushes \(6\) units of flow!

void solve_p1361() {

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P4313

    • Arts cross-hub \(y_u = NM + u \in [NM + 1, 2NM]\).

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation (合并点边法 with \(2\times\) Scaling)

For each grid edge \(e = \{u, v\}\), the \(2 \times 2\) reward table is \(\Pi_e = \begin{pmatrix} E^{\text{sci}}_e & 0 \\ 0 & E^{\text{art}}_e \end{pmatrix}\). Subtracting from \(E^{\text{art}}_e + E^{\text{sci}}_e\) gives the submodular regret matrix:

\[E_e(x_u, x_v) = \begin{pmatrix} E^{\text{art}}_e & E^{\text{art}}_e + E^{\text{sci}}_e \\ E^{\text{art}}_e + E^{\text{sci}}_e & E^{\text{sci}}_e \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Delta_e = E^{\text{art}}_e + E^{\text{sci}}_e \ge 0.\]

To keep every capacity an exact integer without division by \(2\), multiply the entire objective by \(2\)! Then for each edge \(e = \{u, v\}\):

\[2\,E_e(x_u, x_v) = E^{\text{art}}_e\big((1 - x_u) + (1 - x_v)\big) + E^{\text{sci}}_e(x_u + x_v) + \big(E^{\text{art}}_e + E^{\text{sci}}_e\big)\big(x_u(1 - x_v) + x_v(1 - x_u)\big).\]

Summing over all students \(u \in V_{\text{grid}}\) and all grid edges \(e \in E_{\text{grid}}\) yields:

\[2\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{u \in V_{\text{grid}}} \left[ \Big(2A_u + \sum_{e \ni u} E^{\text{art}}_e\Big)(1 - x_u) + \Big(2B_u + \sum_{e \ni u} E^{\text{sci}}_e\Big)x_u \right] + \sum_{e = \{u, v\} \in E_{\text{grid}}} \big(E^{\text{art}}_e + E^{\text{sci}}_e\big)|x_u - x_v|.\]

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Hiring candidate \(i\) (\(x_i = 1\)) costs a salary \(A_i \ge 0\). For every ordered pair \((i, j)\):

  • If both \(i\) and \(j\) are hired (\(x_i = x_j = 1\)), the company gains \(+E_{i,j}\) (so an unordered pair \(\{i, j\}\) yields \(+2 E_{i,j}\) when both are hired).
  • If \(i\) is hired and \(j\) is NOT hired (\(x_i = 1, x_j = 0\)), the company suffers a friction penalty of \(-E_{i,j}\)!
  • If \(i\) is not hired (\(x_i = 0\)), \((i, j)\) contributes \(0\).

Why is this an Advanced milestone? Because \(N = 1000\) and the matrix \(E_{i,j}\) is completely dense (\(\binom{N}{2} \approx 5 \cdot 10^5\) pairs)! If you treat each pair \(\{i, j\}\) as an auxiliary hub node (Luogu P4174 最大获利 style), the network has \(N + \binom{N}{2} \approx 5 \cdot 10^5\) vertices and \(1.5 \cdot 10^6\) edges with huge capacities, which TLEs and MLEs. Instead, expanding the \(2 \times 2\) matrix collapses the entire problem onto \(N + 2 = 1002\) vertices!

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation (\(O(N)\) Vertex Degree-Collapse)

For each unordered pair \(\{i, j\}\) (\(1 \le i < j \le N\)), combine the \((i, j)\) and \((j, i)\) contributions into a single \(2 \times 2\) reward matrix \(\Pi_{i,j}(x_i, x_j)\):

\(x_i \setminus x_j\) \(x_j = 0\) (Unhired) \(x_j = 1\) (Hired)
\(x_i = 0\) (Unhired) \(0\) \(-E_{i,j}\)
\(x_i = 1\) (Hired) \(-E_{i,j}\) \(+2 E_{i,j}\)

Let us apply the Asymmetric or Symmetric Cut Expansion directly to \(\Pi_{i,j}(x_i, x_j)\)! Using the identity \(2 x_i x_j = x_i + x_j - x_i(1 - x_j) - x_j(1 - x_i)\):

\[\begin{aligned} \Pi_{i,j}(x_i, x_j) &= 2 E_{i,j} x_i x_j - E_{i,j} x_i(1 - x_j) - E_{i,j} x_j(1 - x_i) \\ &= E_{i,j}(x_i + x_j) - 2 E_{i,j}\big(x_i(1 - x_j) + x_j(1 - x_i)\big)! \end{aligned}\]

Look at that miracle of cancellation:

  1. Every fraction \(\frac{1}{2}\) vanished completely—all coefficients are exact integers!
  2. When we sum \(E_{i,j}(x_i + x_j)\) over all unordered pairs \(1 \le i < j \le N\), each candidate \(i\) simply collects their row sum \(D_i := \sum_{j=1}^N E_{i,j}\) as a unary profit \(+D_i x_i\)!
  3. Each unordered pair \(\{i, j\}\) simply becomes an undirected edge \(i \leftrightarrow j\) of capacity \(2 E_{i,j}\)!

Therefore, the total net profit over all \(\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N\) is:

\[\Psi(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^N D_i - \left[ \sum_{i=1}^N D_i(1 - x_i) + \sum_{i=1}^N A_i x_i + \sum_{1 \le i < j \le N} 2 E_{i,j}\big(x_i(1 - x_j) + x_j(1 - x_i)\big) \right].\]

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

Let \(\sigma_k(\mathbf{x}) \in \{0, 1\}\) denote whether experiment \(k\) succeeds (\(\sigma_k = \prod_{i \in C_k} x_i\) if \(w_k = 1\), or \(\prod_{i \in C_k} (1 - x_i)\) if \(w_k = 0\)). The net payoff of experiment \(k\) is:

\[W_k \sigma_k(\mathbf{x}) - g \cdot f_k \big(1 - \sigma_k(\mathbf{x})\big) = \underbrace{W_k}_{\text{add to } W_{\text{base}}} - \underbrace{(W_k + g \cdot f_k)}_{\text{hub arc capacity!}}\big(1 - \sigma_k(\mathbf{x})\big).\]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical Graph Compilation)

  • Baseline: \(W_{\text{base}} = \sum_{k=1}^M W_k\) (pre-claim ONLY \(W_k\), NOT \(W_k + g \cdot f_k\)!).
  • Dog Unary Arcs: If \(s_i = 1\), wire \(S \xrightarrow{v_i} i\) (cost \(v_i\) if flipped to \(x_i = 0\)); if \(s_i = 0\), wire \(i \xrightarrow{v_i} T\) (cost \(v_i\) if flipped to \(x_i = 1\)).
  • Experiment Hub Arcs (\(h_k = N + k\)): Let \(C^{\text{regret}}_k = W_k + g \cdot f_k\).
    • If \(w_k = 1\) (All-in-S): wire \(S \xrightarrow{C^{\text{regret}}_k} h_k\) and \(h_k \xrightarrow{\infty} i\) for all \(i \in C_k\).
    • If \(w_k = 0\) (All-in-T): wire \(i \xrightarrow{\infty} h_k\) for all \(i \in C_k\) and \(h_k \xrightarrow{C^{\text{regret}}_k} T\).

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

Suppose \(N = 1\) dog with \(s_1 = 0, v_1 = 10\), and \(1\) friend experiment (\(f_1 = 1, g = 5\)) requiring \(w_1 = 1\) on \(C_1 = \{1\}\) with reward \(W_1 = 3\).

  • \(W_{\text{base}} = 3\), and hub capacity is \(W_1 + g = 3 + 5 = 8\).
  • Path \(S \xrightarrow{8} h_1 \xrightarrow{\infty} 1 \xrightarrow{10} T\) has bottleneck \(\min(8, 10) = 8\).
  • Net profit: \(W_{\text{base}} - \text{MinCut} = 3 - 8 = -5\) (we leave \(x_1 = 0\) to avoid paying \(v_1 = 10\), failing the friend’s experiment and paying \(-g = -5\)).

#080 (Mas-2) — Codeforces 1383 F: Special Edges

  • Judge URL: https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/1383/F
  • Constraints: \(N \le 10^4\) vertices, \(M \le 10^4\) directed edges, \(K \le 10\) special edges (the first \(K\) edges), \(Q \le 2 \cdot 10^5\) queries; all edge capacities \(w_e \in [0, 25]\). Time Limit: 5.0 s.

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

We are given a directed network with \(N, M \le 10^4\) where all capacities are tiny integers \(\le 25\). In each of \(Q = 2 \cdot 10^5\) independent queries, the capacities of the first \(K \le 10\) special edges are updated to new values \((w_1, \dots, w_K) \in [0, 25]^K\), and we must report the \((1, N)\) maximum flow.

Running Dinic from scratch \(Q = 2 \cdot 10^5\) times is \(10^5\times\) too slow. The diagnostic clue is \(K \le 10\): in any \((1, N)\)-cut \((V_S, V_T)\), each of the \(K\) special edges \(e_r = (u_r \to v_r)\) is either severed (\(\text{mask}_r = 1\)) or uncut (\(\text{mask}_r = 0\)). There are only \(2^K = 1024\) possible special-edge cut masks!

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation (\(2^K\) Parametric Cut Decomposition)

Separate the cut polynomial \(\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x})\) into the \(K\) special edges \(E_{\text{spec}} = \{e_0, \dots, e_{K-1}\}\) and the \(M - K\) static edges \(E_{\text{static}}\):

\[\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{w}) = \sum_{r=0}^{K-1} w_r\,x_{u_r}(1 - x_{v_r}) + \sum_{e \in E_{\text{static}}} c_e\,x_{u_e}(1 - x_{v_e}).\]

For any subset (bitmask) \(\text{mask} \in \{0, \dots, 2^K - 1\}\) of special edges that are NOT severed (i.e., forced to remain uncut by giving them capacity \(+\infty\), or bounded by \(w_{\max} = 25\)), define the residual static cut table:

\[F_{\text{static}}[\text{mask}] := \min_{\substack{\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^N \\ x_1 = 1, x_N = 0}} \left( \sum_{r \in \text{mask}} \infty \cdot x_{u_r}(1 - x_{v_r}) + \sum_{e \in E_{\text{static}}} c_e\,x_{u_e}(1 - x_{v_e}) \right).\]

Then by Max-Flow Min-Cut Duality, for any query weight vector \(\mathbf{w} = (w_0, \dots, w_{K-1})\), the minimum \((1, N)\)-cut is simply the minimum over all \(2^K\) masks of (uncut mask’s static cut \(+\) sum of severed special edges outside mask):

\[\boxed{\text{MaxFlow}(\mathbf{w}) = \min_{\text{mask} \in \{0, \dots, 2^K - 1\}} \left( F_{\text{static}}[\text{mask}] + \sum_{r \notin \text{mask}} w_r \right)}\]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Incremental Residual Tree & Rollback)

How do we compute \(F_{\text{static}}[\text{mask}]\) for all \(2^K = 1024\) masks without running Dinic \(1024\) times?

  1. Base Max-Flow (\(\text{mask} = 0\)): Set all \(K\) special edges to capacity \(0\) and run Dinic once to compute \(F_{\text{static}}[0]\). Freeze this residual network!
  2. Incremental Single-Edge Augmentation (\(w_{\max} = 25\)): Since every query satisfies \(w_r \le 25\), no special edge ever needs capacity larger than \(25\) to act as \(\infty\) relative to any valid query! When we transition from mask to mask | (1 << r) by setting special edge \(e_r = (u_r \to v_r)\)’s capacity from \(0\) to \(25\), the maximum flow increases by at most \(25\) units!
  3. Recursion Tree with Residual Snapshots / Rollback: Traverse the \(2^K\) subsets via a recursive DFS over \(r = 0 \dots K - 1\) (or in Gray-code order). Before branching into mask | (1 << r), snapshot the current residual capacities (or record the modified edges on an undo stack), add \(25\) to \(e_r\)’s forward capacity, and push at most \(25\) augmenting units from \(1\) to \(N\) using simple BFS/DFS Ford–Fulkerson steps!
  4. \(O(2^K)\) Query Evaluation: For each query \((w_0, \dots, w_{K-1})\), compute the subset sums \(W[\text{sub}] = \sum_{r \in \text{sub}} w_r\) in \(O(2^K)\) time via W[m] = W[m ^ lsb] + w[ctz(m)], and take \(\min_{\text{mask}} \big(F_{\text{static}}[\text{mask}] + W[(2^K - 1) \oplus \text{mask}]\big)\) in \(1024\) integer operations!

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

Suppose \(N = 2\) (\(S = 1, T = 2\)) with \(1\) static edge \(1 \xrightarrow{10} 2\) and \(K = 1\) special edge \(e_0 = (1 \to 2)\).

  • \(\text{mask} = 0\) (\(e_0\) has cap \(0\)): \(F_{\text{static}}[0] = 10\).
  • \(\text{mask} = 1\) (\(e_0\) has cap \(25\)): \(F_{\text{static}}[1] = 10 + 25 = 35\).
  • For a query with \(w_0 = 7\): \(\min\big(F_{\text{static}}[0] + w_0, \; F_{\text{static}}[1] + 0\big) = \min(10 + 7, 35) = 17\).

  • Hint 2: Since \(w_r \le 25\), giving each uncut special edge \(r \in \text{mask}\) capacity \(25\) is equivalent to giving it capacity \(+\infty\).
  • Hint 3: Compute \(F_{\text{static}}[0]\) with Dinic, then use a depth-\(K\) recursion tree that snapshots residual capacities and augments edge \(e_r\) (capacity \(25\)) using simple Ford–Fulkerson DFS/BFS paths!

// Compact residual network supporting O(M) snapshot copy for the K <= 10 recursion tree
struct IncrementalFlow {
    struct Edge { int to, rev, cap; };
    int N;
    vector<vector<Edge>> adj;
    vector<int> spec_u, spec_idx;

    IncrementalFlow(int n) : N(n), adj(n + 1) {}

    int add_edge(int u, int v, int cap) {
        int idx = adj[u].size();
        adj[u].push_back({v, (int)adj[v].size(), cap});
        adj[v].push_back({u, idx, 0});
        return idx;
    }

    // Standard single-path DFS/BFS augmentation for small <= 25 increments
    int augment_up_to_25(int S, int T) {
        int pushed_total = 0;
        vector<int> par_v(N + 1), par_e(N + 1), q(N + 1);
        while (true) {
            fill(par_v.begin(), par_v.end(), -1);
            int qh = 0, qt = 0;
            q[qt++] = S;
            par_v[S] = S;
            int found_bot = 0;
            while (qh < qt) {
                int u = q[qh++];
                if (u == T) break;
                for (int i = 0; i < (int)adj[u].size(); ++i) {
                    const auto& e = adj[u][i];
                    if (e.cap > 0 && par_v[e.to] == -1) {
                        par_v[e.to] = u;
                        par_e[e.to] = i;
                        q[qt++] = e.to;
                        if (e.to == T) break;
                    }
                }
            }
            if (par_v[T] == -1) break;
            int bot = 25;
            for (int v = T; v != S; v = par_v[v])
                bot = min(bot, adj[par_v[v]][par_e[v]].cap);
            for (int v = T; v != S; v = par_v[v]) {
                int u = par_v[v], ei = par_e[v];
                int rev = adj[u][ei].rev;
                adj[u][ei].cap -= bot;
                adj[v][rev].cap += bot;
            }
            pushed_total += bot;
        }
        return pushed_total;
    }
};

void solve_cf1383f() {
    int N, M, K, Q;
    cin >> N >> M >> K >> Q;
    IncrementalFlow G(N);
    vector<int> spec_u(K), spec_e(K);

    for (int i = 0; i < M; ++i) {
        int u, v, w;
        cin >> u >> v >> w;
        if (i < K) {
            spec_u[i] = u;
            spec_e[i] = G.add_edge(u, v, 0); // Initially 0 in base network
        } else {
            G.add_edge(u, v, w);
        }
    }

    // 1. Base max-flow on static edges (use Dinic on G in production)
    int base_flow = 0;
    while (int df = G.augment_up_to_25(1, N)) base_flow += df;

    // 2. Precompute F_static[mask] for all 2^K masks via recursion tree with snapshots
    vector<int> F_static(1 << K, 0);
    auto dfs = [&](auto& self, int bit, int mask, int cur_flow) -> void {
        if (bit == K) {
            F_static[mask] = cur_flow;
            return;
        }
        // Branch 0: special edge `bit` stays at capacity 0 (severed if used)
        self(self, bit + 1, mask, cur_flow);

        // Snapshot current residual capacities before adding capacity 25 to edge `bit`
        auto saved_adj = G.adj;
        G.adj[spec_u[bit]][spec_e[bit]].cap += 25;
        int add_flow = G.augment_up_to_25(1, N);
        self(self, bit + 1, mask | (1 << bit), cur_flow + add_flow);
        G.adj = move(saved_adj); // Rollback residual graph
    };
    dfs(dfs, 0, 0, base_flow);

    // 3. Answer Q queries in O(2^K) per query
    int full_mask = (1 << K) - 1;
    vector<int> W_sub(1 << K, 0), w(K);
    while (Q--) {
        for (int i = 0; i < K; ++i) cin >> w[i];
        for (int m = 1; m < (1 << K); ++m) {
            int b = __builtin_ctz(m);
            W_sub[m] = W_sub[m ^ (1 << b)] + w[b];
        }
        int ans = 1e9;
        for (int mask = 0; mask < (1 << K); ++mask) {
            ans = min(ans, F_static[mask] + W_sub[full_mask ^ mask]);
        }
        cout << ans << "\n";
    }
}

7. Pre-Submission Checklist (FM-10 Audit)

Before submitting any Pseudo-Boolean Cut / Burn-or-Bury solution, run through this 6-point verification checklist:

  1. Explicit Binary Dictionary (\(S\) vs. \(T\)): Did you write down at the top of your scratchpad whether \(x_i = 1\) means \(i \in V_S\) (\(S \to i\) severed when \(x_i = 0\)) or \(i \in V_T\)? Never mix conventions mid-problem!
  2. Kolmogorov–Zabih Submodularity Check (\(A + D \le B + C\)): For every pairwise penalty table \(E_{u,v}\), did you verify \(\Delta_{uv} = (B + C) - (A + D) \ge 0\)? If \(\Delta_{uv} < 0\), did you check for a bipartite variable flip (Chapter 11)?
  3. Asymmetric vs. Symmetric \(2\times\) Scaling: If you used Symmetric Half-Edge Compilation (合并点边法, #077), did you multiply all unary and pairwise capacities by \(2\) so that odd \(\Delta_{uv}\) values do not truncate under integer division?
  4. Separate All-\(S\) and All-\(T\) Synergy Hubs (FM-10 Bug 4): When a subset \(C\) has both an All-\(S\) bonus (\(y_C\)) and an All-\(T\) bonus (\(z_C\)), did you allocate two distinct vertex IDs \(y_C \neq z_C\)?
  5. Hub Implication Direction & Self-Inclusion (FM-10 Bug 3 & #076): Does flow travel strictly from \(S \xrightarrow{B_C} y_C \xrightarrow{\infty} i \xrightarrow{\infty} z_C \xrightarrow{B'_C} T\)? And on grid stencils (文理分科), did your direction loop include (dr = 0, dc = 0) so the center cell is connected to its own hub?
  6. Optimistic Baseline vs. Regret Swing (#079 CF 311E): When failing a clause incurs an extra out-of-pocket penalty \(-g\) in addition to losing reward \(+W_k\), did you add only \(W_k\) to W_base while setting the hub’s cut capacity to \(W_k + g\)?

8. Chapter Summary & Bridge to Chapter 11

In this chapter, we unified binary minimum-cut modelling under the algebraic language of Pseudo-Boolean Cut Polynomials:

  • Every \((S, T)\)-cut network with non-negative capacities evaluates a polynomial \(\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{x}) = C_0 + \sum_u c_{S \to u}(1 - x_u) + \sum_u c_{u \to T} x_u + \sum_{u \neq v} c_{u \to v} x_u(1 - x_v)\).
  • By the Kolmogorov–Zabih Submodularity Theorem (Theorem 10.1), a \(2 \times 2\) penalty matrix \(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}\) is graph-representable with non-negative capacities if and only if \(A + D \le B + C\) (\(\Delta_{uv} = B + C - A - D \ge 0\)). When submodular, it compiles without auxiliary vertices via either the Asymmetric Integer Split (\(\alpha = \Delta_{uv}, \beta = 0\)) or Hu Botao’s Symmetric Half-Edge Split (合并点边法, \(\alpha = \beta = \frac{1}{2}\Delta_{uv}\)).
  • Higher-order degree-\(k\) unanimity bonuses \(+B_C \prod_{i \in C} x_i\) and \(+B'_C \prod_{i \in C} (1 - x_i)\) compile cleanly via Theorem 10.2’s Auxiliary Synergy Hubs (\(y_C\) and \(z_C\)).
  • For general non-submodular quadratic functions, Roof Duality / QPBO (Theorem 10.3) doubles the graph into \(\{x_i, \bar{x}_i\}\), computing a half-integral LP optimum \(\hat{\mathbf{x}} \in \{0, \frac{1}{2}, 1\}^N\) whose integral coordinates are globally persistent.

Bridge to Chapter 11: What If \(A + D > B + C\)?

In Theorem 10.3(3), we glimpsed a tantalizing phenomenon: whenever the supermodular edges (\(A + D > B + C\)) form a bipartite graph \(G = (L \sqcup R, E)\), the doubled QPBO network \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{QPBO}}\) splits into two identical, disconnected halves—meaning the problem can be solved 100% integrally on \(N\) vertices by simply inverting the binary variables \(y_v := 1 - x_v\) on all right-side vertices \(v \in R\)!

In Chapter 11 (Pairwise Submodularity, Symmetric Penalties & Bipartite Variable Inversion 反転), we turn this observation into one of the most powerful weapons in modern competitive programming: rescuing supermodular “zebra disagreement” bonuses (ABC 193F), row-column inclusion-exclusion conflicts (ABC 259G), and two-permutation cycle collisions (AGC 038F — Two Permutations).
