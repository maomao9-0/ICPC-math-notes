1. Motivation: When Edges Have Minimum Quotas and Vertices Have Mandatory Balances

In Chapters 1 through 3, every capacity constraint had the one-sided box form \(0 \le f(e) \le u(e)\) and every interior vertex obeyed homogeneous conservation \(\text{div}_f(v) = 0\). Crucially, the all-zero flow \(f \equiv 0\) was always a valid starting point in the feasible polytope, allowing Dinic’s algorithm to start at \(f = 0\) and greedily augment towards optimality.

In many contest and operations-research models, however, \(f \equiv 0\) is strictly infeasible:

  1. Pressurized Cooling Pipes & Supply Contracts (ZOJ 2314): Every pipe \(e = (u \to v)\) in a closed nuclear reactor loop must carry at least \(l(e) > 0\) liters/sec of coolant to prevent overheating, while never exceeding its burst limit \(u(e)\).
  2. Mandatory Edge/Vertex Coverage on a DAG (Luogu P4843 清理雪道): A fleet of snowplows must traverse a mountain trail network so that every ski trail \(e\) is cleared at least once (\(1 \le f(e) \le \infty\)), using the minimum possible number of snowplow runs from the summit \(s\) to the base lodge \(t\).
  3. Matrix & Statistical Table Rounding (POJ 2396, BZOJ 3698): Each cell \(X_{i,j}\) of an integer matrix must lie in a prescribed interval \([L_{i,j}, R_{i,j}]\) (or \(\{\lfloor A_{i,j} \rfloor, \lceil A_{i,j} \rceil\}\)), while row and column sums must fall into target intervals \([\underline{r}_i, \overline{r}_i]\) and \([\underline{c}_j, \overline{c}_j]\).
  4. Daily and Cumulative Quotas ([ZJOI2010] Shoot the Bullet): On day \(i\), a photographer takes between \(L_{i,j}\) and \(R_{i,j}\) photos of subject \(j\), capped at \(D_i\) total photos on day \(i\), while guaranteeing at least \(G_j\) cumulative photos of subject \(j\) across the entire season.

Whenever decision variables are bounded from both below and above (\(l(e) \le f(e) \le u(e)\) with \(l(e) > 0\)) or vertices have mandatory net demands \(b(v) \neq 0\), a single call to standard dinic(s, t) cannot even start: the zero flow violates every positive lower bound. Nor can we simply run standard Max-Flow with capacities \(u(e)\) and “hope” the flow on \(e\) exceeds \(l(e)\)—maximizing total throughput frequently starves side branches that have mandatory minimum quotas.

To compile lower-bound constraints into a standard Dinic solver, we need an algebraic transformation that shifts the origin of the flow polytope from \(0\) to \(l(e)\).

2. The Affine Slack Shift \(f(e) = l(e) + \tilde{f}(e)\) and the Four Lower-Bound Flow Variants (上下界网络流)

2.1 Step 1 & Step 2: Algebraic Derivation of Pre-Injected Nodal Excess \(M(v)\)

Consider a directed network \(\mathcal{N} = (V, E, l, u, b)\) where:

  • Each directed edge \(e = (u \to v) \in E\) has an integer lower bound \(l(e)\) and upper bound \(u(e)\) satisfying \(0 \le l(e) \le u(e)\). (If \(l(e) > u(e)\) for any edge, the system is immediately infeasible.)
  • Each vertex \(v \in V\) has a prescribed net demand/divergence \(b(v) \in \mathbb{Z}\), defined as mandatory net outflow minus inflow: \[\text{div}_f(v) \triangleq \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} f(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f(e) = b(v) \qquad \forall v \in V\] In a pure sourceless circulation, \(b(v) = 0\) for all \(v \in V\). When \(b(v) > 0\), vertex \(v\) is a net producer (must inject \(b(v)\) units into the network); when \(b(v) < 0\), vertex \(v\) is a net consumer (must absorb \(-b(v)\) units from the network). Since every edge \(e = (u \to v)\) contributes \(+f(e)\) to \(\text{div}_f(u)\) and \(-f(e)\) to \(\text{div}_f(v)\), summing over all \(v \in V\) yields the necessary global conservation check: \[\sum_{v \in V} b(v) = \sum_{v \in V} \text{div}_f(v) = 0.\]

The Affine Slack Shift

To restore standard zero-lower-bound box constraints, we decompose the physical flow \(f(e)\) on each edge \(e \in E\) into its mandatory baseline quota \(l(e)\) plus a non-negative free slack flow \(\tilde{f}(e)\): \[f(e) = l(e) + \tilde{f}(e), \qquad 0 \le \tilde{f}(e) \le u(e) - l(e) \triangleq \tilde{c}(e).\]

Now substitute \(f(e) = l(e) + \tilde{f}(e)\) directly into the vertex balance equation \(\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} f(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f(e) = b(v)\): \[\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} \big(l(e) + \tilde{f}(e)\big) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} \big(l(e) + \tilde{f}(e)\big) = b(v).\]

Separate the unknown slack variables \(\tilde{f}(e)\) on the left-hand side and move all known constants \(l(e)\) and \(b(v)\) to the right-hand side: \[\underbrace{\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} \tilde{f}(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} \tilde{f}(e)}_{\text{div}_{\tilde{f}}(v) \text{ (Net Slack Outflow from } v\text{)}} = \underbrace{\sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} l(e) - b(v)}_{M(v) \text{ (Pre-Injected Net Excess at } v\text{)}}.\]

We call \(M(v)\) the Pre-Injected Net Excess (or Mandatory Baseline Imbalance) at vertex \(v\): \[M(v) \triangleq \text{in}_l(v) - \text{out}_l(v) - b(v) = \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} l(e) - b(v).\]

Notice that as long as \(\sum_{v \in V} b(v) = 0\), the sum of \(M(v)\) across all vertices identically vanishes: \[\sum_{v \in V} M(v) = \sum_{e \in E} l(e) - \sum_{e \in E} l(e) - \sum_{v \in V} b(v) = 0.\]

2.2 Step 3: Mechanical Graph Compilation into Super-Terminals \(S^*\) and \(T^*\)

How do we enforce \(\text{div}_{\tilde{f}}(v) = M(v)\) for every \(v \in V\) using a standard zero-divergence max-flow solver? Let us trace the physical meaning of \(\text{div}_{\tilde{f}}(v) = M(v)\):

  1. Case 1: \(M(v) > 0\) (Pre-Injected Surplus at \(v\)): Pre-sending \(l(e)\) along every edge delivers \(M(v)\) more mandatory units into \(v\) than are required to leave \(v\). To balance the books at \(v\), the slack flow \(\tilde{f}\) must satisfy \(\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} \tilde{f}(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} \tilde{f}(e) = M(v) > 0\)—that is, \(v\) must emit a net surplus of \(M(v)\) units of slack flow out into the rest of the network.
    • Gadget Compilation: Create an auxiliary Super-Source \(S^*\) and add a directed companion arc \(S^* \to v\) with capacity \(M(v)\). If this arc is saturated (\(\tilde{f}(S^* \to v) = M(v)\)), standard conservation at \(v\) in the augmented network forces \(\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} \tilde{f}(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} \tilde{f}(e) = \tilde{f}(S^* \to v) = M(v)\)!
  2. Case 2: \(M(v) < 0\) (Pre-Injected Deficit at \(v\)): Pre-sending \(l(e)\) along every edge drains \(|M(v)| = -M(v)\) more mandatory units out of \(v\) than arrive at \(v\). To replenish this deficit, the slack flow \(\tilde{f}\) must satisfy \(\sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} \tilde{f}(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} \tilde{f}(e) = -M(v) > 0\)—that is, \(v\) must absorb a net inflow of \(-M(v)\) units of slack flow from the rest of the network.
    • Gadget Compilation: Create an auxiliary Super-Sink \(T^*\) and add a directed companion arc \(v \to T^*\) with capacity \(-M(v) = |M(v)|\). If this arc is saturated (\(\tilde{f}(v \to T^*) = -M(v)\)), standard conservation at \(v\) forces \(\sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} \tilde{f}(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} \tilde{f}(e) = \tilde{f}(v \to T^*) = -M(v)\)!
  3. Case 3: \(M(v) = 0\) (Pre-Balanced Vertex): The mandatory baseline flows already balance at \(v\); no companion arc to \(S^*\) or \(T^*\) is needed.

Define the Total Mandatory Positive Imbalance: \[\Sigma^+ \triangleq \sum_{v \in V : M(v) > 0} M(v) = \sum_{v \in V : M(v) < 0} \big(-M(v)\big).\]

Since no companion arc can carry flow exceeding its capacity, \(\tilde{f}\) saturates every companion arc out of \(S^*\) (and consequently every companion arc into \(T^*\)) if and only if the maximum \(S^*\)-\(T^*\) flow in the auxiliary network \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\) equals \(\Sigma^+\).

[!CAUTION] Broken-Gadget Counterexample — Reversing the Sign of \(M(v)\): The single most frequent error in 上下界网络流 is defining \(M(v) = \text{out}_l(v) - \text{in}_l(v)\) and wiring \(S^* \to v\) when \(\text{out}_l(v) > \text{in}_l(v)\).

1-Second Mnemonic Check on a 2-Node Loop: Suppose \(u \to v\) has mandatory bounds \([1, 1]\) (slack capacity \(1 - 1 = 0\)) and \(v \to u\) has free bounds \([0, 1]\) (slack capacity \(1\)).

  • Because \(1\) mandatory unit is pre-sent along \(u \to v\), vertex \(v\) receives \(1\) unit of baseline flow and must push \(1\) unit of slack flow back from \(v\) to \(u\) along the free return pipe \(v \to u\).
  • For slack flow in \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\) to travel from \(v\) to \(u\), \(S^*\) must inject flow into \(v\) (\(S^* \to v\), where \(\text{in}_l(v) - \text{out}_l(v) = 1 - 0 = +1 > 0\)) and \(u\) must drain into \(T^*\) (\(u \to T^*\), where \(\text{in}_l(u) - \text{out}_l(u) = 0 - 1 = -1 < 0\)).

2.3 Complete 2-Phase Protocols for the Four Lower-Bound Flow Variants

When the original network has a designated source \(s\) and sink \(t\) (Variants 2, 3, 4), conservation holds at all interior vertices \(v \in V \setminus \{s, t\}\), while \(s\) emits net flow \(|f|\) and \(t\) absorbs net flow \(|f|\). How do we reduce an \(s\)-\(t\) flow to a sourceless circulation (Variant 1)?

The Feedback Arc Reduction: Add a single virtual feedback arc \(e_{\text{fb}} = (t \to s)\) with lower bound \(l(e_{\text{fb}}) = 0\) and upper bound \(u(e_{\text{fb}}) = \infty\). Any flow \(f_0\) arriving at \(t\) from the physical network can now return from \(t\) to \(s\) along \(e_{\text{fb}}\), restoring strict conservation \(\text{div}_f(v) = 0\) at every vertex \(v \in V\) (including \(s\) and \(t\))! Moreover, the flow carried by the feedback arc is equal to the net \(s\)-\(t\) throughput of the physical network: \[|f| = f(e_{\text{fb}}) = \tilde{f}(t \to s) = c_{\tilde{f}}(s \to t)\] where \(c_{\tilde{f}}(s \to t) = \text{cap}[e_{\text{fb}} \oplus 1]\) is the residual capacity of the reverse half-edge of \(e_{\text{fb}}\) after Phase 1.

If \(F_1 < \Sigma^+\), return INFEASIBLE. Read \(f_0 = \text{cap}[e_{\text{fb}} \oplus 1]\). Zero out both half-edges of \(e_{\text{fb}}\) (cap[e_fb] = cap[e_fb ^ 1] = 0). Push flow backward from \(t\) to \(s\): \(f_{\text{sub}} = \text{MaxFlow}_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}}(t, s)\). Return \(F_{\min} = f_0 - f_{\text{sub}}\).

Proving the Three Structural Invariants of Phase 2

  1. Why does Phase 2 preserve every lower bound and interior conservation without touching \(S^*\) and \(T^*\)? When Phase 1 succeeds (\(F_1 = \Sigma^+\)), every companion arc \(S^* \to v\) is completely saturated (forward residual capacity \(0\), and no edge enters \(S^*\)), and every companion arc \(v \to T^*\) is completely saturated (forward residual capacity \(0\), and no edge leaves \(T^*\)). Thus in the residual network \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}\), no directed path from \(s\) to \(t\) (or from \(t\) to \(s\)) can ever visit \(S^*\) or \(T^*\)! Furthermore, every physical edge \(e\) has forward residual capacity \(u(e) - f(e)\) and reverse residual capacity \(f(e) - l(e)\), so augmenting in \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}\) keeps \(f(e) \in [l(e), u(e)]\) invariant.
  2. Why MUST we zero out BOTH half-edges of \(e_{\text{fb}} = (t \to s)\) before Phase 2? After Phase 1, the forward arc \(t \to s\) has residual capacity \(\infty\), and the reverse arc \(s \to t\) has residual capacity \(f_0\).
    • If we leave cap[e_fb] = inf during Variant 4 (MaxFlow(t, s)), Dinic will immediately push \(\infty\) units directly along \(t \to s\), yielding \(F_{\min} = -\infty\)!
    • If we leave cap[e_fb ^ 1] = f_0 during Variant 3 (MaxFlow(s, t)), Dinic will push \(f_0\) units along the reverse arc \(s \to t\), double-counting \(f_0\) if we compute \(f_0 + \text{MaxFlow}(s, t)\). Zeroing out both cap[e_fb] = 0 and cap[e_fb ^ 1] = 0 locks \(f_0\) in place so \(F_{\max} = f_0 + \text{MaxFlow}(s, t)\) and \(F_{\min} = f_0 - \text{MaxFlow}(t, s)\) hold symmetrically.
  3. Why does \(F_{\min} = f_0 - \text{MaxFlow}_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}}(t, s)\) find the exact minimum feasible \(s\)-\(t\) flow? Any augmenting path from \(t\) to \(s\) in the residual network \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}\) reduces the net \(s \to t\) flow by \(1\) unit per unit pushed while keeping \(l(e) \le f(e) \le u(e)\) and \(\text{div}_f(v) = 0\) (\(\forall v \notin \{s, t\}\)). By applying the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem to \((t, s)\) on \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}\), no further reduction is possible once \(t \not\leadsto s\) in \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}\), reaching the global minimum: \[F_{\min}(s, t) = \max_{(S, T) \text{ an } s\text{-}t \text{ cut}} \left( \sum_{e \in \delta^+(S)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(S)} u(e) \right).\]

2.4 Step 4: Worked Numerical Trace (Variants 2, 3, and 4) & FM-05 Trap

Let us trace all three \(s\)-\(t\) variants by hand on a 4-vertex network \(V = \{s, a, b, t\}\) with 5 bounded edges:

  • \(e_1 = (s \to a)\) with \([l_1, u_1] = [2, 5]\) (slack capacity \(\tilde{c}_1 = 3\))
  • \(e_2 = (s \to b)\) with \([l_2, u_2] = [1, 4]\) (slack capacity \(\tilde{c}_2 = 3\))

  • \(e_4 = (a \to t)\) with \([l_4, u_4] = [1, 4]\) (slack capacity \(\tilde{c}_4 = 3\))
  • \(e_5 = (b \to t)\) with \([l_5, u_5] = [2, 6]\) (slack capacity \(\tilde{c}_5 = 4\))

Add feedback arc \(e_{\text{fb}} = (t \to s)\) with \([0, \infty)\). Compute \(M(v) = \text{in}_l(v) - \text{out}_l(v)\) from \(l = (2, 1, 1, 1, 2, 0)\):

  • \(M(s) = 0 - (2 + 1) = -3 < 0 \implies\) add companion arc \(s \to T^*\) with capacity \(3\).
  • \(M(a) = 2 - (1 + 1) = 0 \implies\) balanced (\(0\)).
  • \(M(b) = (1 + 1) - 2 = 0 \implies\) balanced (\(0\)).
  • \(M(t) = (1 + 2) - 0 = +3 > 0 \implies\) add companion arc \(S^* \to t\) with capacity \(3\).
  • Total positive imbalance: \(\Sigma^+ = 3\).

Suppose during MaxFlow(S*, T*), Dinic first explores an interior path \(S^* \xrightarrow{2} t \xrightarrow{e_{\text{fb}}} s \xrightarrow{e_1} a \xrightarrow{e_4} t\) (Wait—actually \(t\) is already near \(S^*\), or suppose \(M(a)\) had surplus, or suppose we had an extra edge). Here, MaxFlow(S*, T*) pushes \(3\) units along \(S^* \xrightarrow{3} t \xrightarrow{e_{\text{fb}}} s \xrightarrow{3} T^*\), achieving \(F_1 = 3 = \Sigma^+\).

  • Feasible \(s\)-\(t\) Flow (Variant 2): \(f_0 = \tilde{f}(e_{\text{fb}}) = 3\), with physical flows \(f^{(0)} = l + \tilde{f} = (2, 1, 1, 1, 2)\).

From \(f^{(0)} = (2, 1, 1, 1, 2)\), the remaining forward slack capacities are \(\tilde{c} = (3, 3, 2, 3, 4)\). Running MaxFlow(s, t) on \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}\) pushes:

  • \(3\) units along \(s \xrightarrow{e_1} a \xrightarrow{e_4} t\), and
  • \(3\) units along \(s \xrightarrow{e_2} b \xrightarrow{e_5} t\),

giving \(f_{\text{add}} = 6\) and \(F_{\max} = f_0 + f_{\text{add}} = 3 + 6 = 9\), with physical flows \(f^{\max} = l + \tilde{f} = (2+3, 1+3, 1+0, 1+3, 2+3) = (5, 4, 1, 4, 5)\).

3. Hoffman’s Circulation Theorem & Extracting Infeasibility Certificates from \(S_{\min}^*\)

When does a sourceless network \(\mathcal{N} = (V, E, l, u)\) with \(0 \le l(e) \le u(e)\) admit a feasible circulation (\(\text{div}_f(v) = 0 \;\;\forall v \in V\))? And when Phase 1 fails (\(F_1 < \Sigma^+\)), can we extract a concrete combinatorial witness proving to the user why no circulation exists?

3.1 Theorem 3.1 (Hoffman’s Circulation Theorem, 1960)

A network \(\mathcal{N} = (V, E, l, u)\) with \(0 \le l(e) \le u(e)\) admits a feasible circulation \(f: E \to \mathbb{R}\) satisfying \(l(e) \le f(e) \le u(e)\) and \(\text{div}_f(v) = 0\) (\(\forall v \in V\)) if and only if for every vertex subset \(X \subseteq V\), the total mandatory lower bound entering \(X\) does not exceed the total upper-bound capacity leaving \(X\): \[\sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) \le \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} u(e) \qquad \forall X \subseteq V\] (or equivalently, replacing \(X\) by its complement \(V \setminus X\), \(\sum_{e \in \delta^+(Y)} l(e) \le \sum_{e \in \delta^-(Y)} u(e)\) for all \(Y \subseteq V\)). Moreover, if \(l\) and \(u\) are integral and the condition holds, an integral circulation \(f: E \to \mathbb{Z}\) exists.

Proof via Max-Flow Min-Cut Duality on \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\)

  1. Necessity (\(\implies\)): Suppose \(f\) is a valid circulation. Summing vertex conservation \(\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} f(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f(e) = 0\) over all \(v \in X\) cancels all internal edges with both endpoints in \(X\), leaving boundary conservation: \[\sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) \le \sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} f(e) = \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} f(e) \le \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} u(e).\]

  2. Sufficiency (\(\impliedby\)) & Exact Cut Identity: By Section 2.2, a feasible circulation exists if and only if \(\text{MaxFlow}_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^*, T^*) = \Sigma^+\). By the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem (Chapter 1), this holds if and only if every \(S^*\)-\(T^*\) cut \((S^* \sqcup X, T^* \sqcup \bar{X})\) in \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\) (where \(\bar{X} = V \setminus X\)) has cut capacity \(\kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X) \ge \Sigma^+\). Let us expand \(\kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X)\) explicitly. An arc of \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\) crosses from \(S^* \sqcup X\) to \(T^* \sqcup \bar{X}\) in one of three ways:

    • From \(S^*\) to a vertex \(v \in \bar{X}\) with \(M(v) > 0\), contributing \(M(v)\);
    • From a vertex \(v \in X\) with \(M(v) < 0\) to \(T^*\), contributing \(-M(v)\);
    • An original network edge \(e \in \delta^+(X)\) crossing from \(X\) to \(\bar{X}\), contributing its slack capacity \(u(e) - l(e)\).

    Summing these three contributions gives: \[\kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X) = \sum_{v \in \bar{X} : M(v) > 0} M(v) + \sum_{v \in X : M(v) < 0} \big(-M(v)\big) + \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} \big(u(e) - l(e)\big).\] Subtracting \(\kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X)\) from \(\Sigma^+ = \sum_{v \in X : M(v) > 0} M(v) + \sum_{v \in \bar{X} : M(v) > 0} M(v)\) cancels the \(\bar{X}\) sum and combines the two \(X\) sums into \(\sum_{v \in X} M(v)\): \[\Sigma^+ - \kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X) = \sum_{v \in X} M(v) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} \big(u(e) - l(e)\big).\] Now substitute \(\sum_{v \in X} M(v) = \sum_{v \in X} \big(\text{in}_l(v) - \text{out}_l(v)\big) = \sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} l(e)\). The \(-\sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} l(e)\) terms cancel identically, revealing the Fundamental Duality Identity of Lower-Bound Flows: \[\boxed{\Sigma^+ - \kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X) = \sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} u(e) \qquad \forall X \subseteq V.}\] Therefore, \(\kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X) \ge \Sigma^+\) holds for all \(X \subseteq V\) if and only if \(\sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) \le \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} u(e)\) holds for all \(X \subseteq V\)! Calling \(Y = \bar{X} = V \setminus X\) reverses \(\delta^-(X) = \delta^+(Y)\) and \(\delta^+(X) = \delta^-(Y)\), giving the dual form \(\sum_{e \in \delta^-(Y)} u(e) \ge \sum_{e \in \delta^+(Y)} l(e)\) for all \(Y \subseteq V\). \(\blacksquare\)

3.2 Theorem 3.2 (Constructive Infeasibility Certificate from \(S_{\min}^*\))

Suppose Phase 1 fails with \(\text{MaxFlow}_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^*, T^*) = F_1 < \Sigma^+\). Let \(S_{\min}^*\) be the set of vertices reachable from \(S^*\) in the final residual network \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}^*}\) (found by a single forward BFS/DFS from \(S^*\), as in Chapter 2). Define: \[X^* \triangleq S_{\min}^* \setminus \{S^*\} \subseteq V, \qquad Y^* \triangleq V \setminus X^*.\]

Then:

  1. Exact Certificate of Violation: The subset \(X^*\) violates Hoffman’s inflow-lower-bound inequality (and \(Y^*\) violates Hoffman’s outflow-lower-bound inequality) by strictly \(\Sigma^+ - F_1 > 0\): \[\sum_{e \in \delta^-(X^*)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X^*)} u(e) = \sum_{e \in \delta^+(Y^*)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(Y^*)} u(e) = \Sigma^+ - F_1 > 0.\]
  2. Maximum-Violation Optimality: Among all \(2^{|V|}\) subsets \(X \subseteq V\), \(X^*\) maximizes the Hoffman violation \(\sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} u(e)\).

Worked Numerical Trace: Extracting \(X^*\) When Phase 1 Fails

Suppose in our 4-vertex network \(V = \{s, a, b, t\}\) from Section 2.4, we raise the lower bound of \(e_1 = (s \to a)\) to \([l_1, u_1] = [4, 5]\), tighten \(e_3 = (a \to b)\) to \([1, 2]\), and tighten \(e_4 = (a \to t)\) to \([1, 1]\), keeping \(e_2 = (s \to b, [1, 4])\), \(e_5 = (b \to t, [2, 6])\), and \(e_{\text{fb}} = (t \to s, [0, \infty))\).

  1. Pre-Injected Imbalances \(M(v)\):
    • \(M(s) = 0 - (4 + 1) = -5 \implies s \to T^*\) (cap \(5\)).
    • \(M(a) = 4 - (1 + 1) = +2 \implies S^* \to a\) (cap \(2\)).
    • \(M(b) = (1 + 1) - 2 = 0\).
    • \(M(t) = (1 + 2) - 0 = +3 \implies S^* \to t\) (cap \(3\)).
    • Total positive imbalance: \(\Sigma^+ = 2 + 3 = 5\).
  2. Slack Capacities Leaving \(a\):
    • \(\tilde{c}(a \to b) = u_3 - l_3 = 2 - 1 = 1\), and \(\tilde{c}(a \to t) = u_4 - l_4 = 1 - 1 = 0\).
  3. Phase 1 Execution (MaxFlow(S*, T*)):
    • All \(3\) units from \(S^* \to t\) reach \(T^*\) via \(t \xrightarrow{e_{\text{fb}}} s \to T^*\).
    • Only \(1\) unit from \(S^* \to a\) reaches \(T^*\) via \(a \xrightarrow{e_3} b \xrightarrow{e_5} t \xrightarrow{e_{\text{fb}}} s \to T^*\).
    • Total Phase 1 flow is \(F_1 = 4 < \Sigma^+ = 5\) (INFEASIBLE).
  4. Extracting \(X^* = S_{\min}^* \setminus \{S^*\}\):
    • In the final residual network \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}^*}\), the arc \(S^* \to a\) still has residual capacity \(2 - 1 = 1 > 0\), whereas all arcs leaving \(a\) (\(a \to b\) and \(a \to t\)) have residual capacity \(0\).
    • A forward BFS from \(S^*\) visits \(S_{\min}^* = \{S^*, a\}\), yielding the witness subset: \[X^* = S_{\min}^* \setminus \{S^*\} = \{a\}.\]
    • Checking Hoffman’s inequality on \(X^* = \{a\}\): mandatory inflow into \(\{a\}\) is \(l(s \to a) = 4\), whereas maximum possible outflow from \(\{a\}\) is \(u(a \to b) + u(a \to t) = 2 + 1 = 3\), giving an exact violation of \(4 - 3 = 1 = \Sigma^+ - F_1\)!

4. Three Non-Graph Archetypes That Compile to Lower-Bound Flows

Following our Zero Magic Gadgets Mandate, we now derive the complete 4-step compilation for the three most important non-graph problem families that compile to lower-bound circulations.

4.1 Archetype A: Matrix & Contingency-Table Rounding (Baranyai's Rounding Lemma, POJ 2396, BZOJ 3698)

  • Step 1 (Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal): You are given an \(R \times C\) matrix where each cell \(X_{i,j}\) has box bounds \([L_{i,j}, U_{i,j}]\), each row \(i\) has row-sum bounds \([\underline{r}_i, \overline{r}_i]\), and each column \(j\) has column-sum bounds \([\underline{c}_j, \overline{c}_j]\). (In Real-Valued Matrix Rounding, you are given real numbers \(A_{i,j} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) with row sums \(r_i = \sum_j A_{i,j}\) and column sums \(c_j = \sum_i A_{i,j}\), and must round every entry and margin to either \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) or \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) while preserving all row and column sums.)
  • Step 2 (Intermediate Algebraic Representation): The unknown integer matrix \(X_{i,j} \in \mathbb{Z}\) must satisfy: \[\underline{r}_i \le \sum_{j=1}^C X_{i,j} \le \overline{r}_i \quad (\forall i), \qquad \underline{c}_j \le \sum_{i=1}^R X_{i,j} \le \overline{c}_j \quad (\forall j), \qquad L_{i,j} \le X_{i,j} \le U_{i,j} \quad (\forall i, j).\] Introduce slack row-sum variables \(R_i \triangleq \sum_{j=1}^C X_{i,j}\) and column-sum variables \(C_j \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^R X_{i,j}\). Then every row \(i\) satisfies homogeneous conservation \(R_i - \sum_{j=1}^C X_{i,j} = 0\), and every column \(j\) satisfies \(\sum_{i=1}^R X_{i,j} - C_j = 0\)!
  • Step 3 (Mechanical Graph Compilation): By the Rank-2 Factorization Principle (Chapter 3.3), create source \(s\), sink \(t\), row vertices \(\text{Row}_1, \dots, \text{Row}_R\), and column vertices \(\text{Col}_1, \dots, \text{Col}_C\):
    1. For each row \(i\), add directed edge \(s \to \text{Row}_i\) with bounds \([\underline{r}_i, \overline{r}_i]\) (carrying flow \(R_i\)).
    2. For each cell \((i, j)\), add directed edge \(\text{Row}_i \to \text{Col}_j\) with bounds \([L_{i,j}, U_{i,j}]\) (carrying flow \(X_{i,j}\)).
    3. For each column \(j\), add directed edge \(\text{Col}_j \to t\) with bounds \([\underline{c}_j, \overline{c}_j]\) (carrying flow \(C_j\)).
    4. Add feedback arc \(t \to s\) with bounds \([\underline{\Sigma}, \overline{\Sigma}]\) (or \([0, \infty)\) if total matrix sum is unconstrained).

[!TIP] Theorem 4.1 (Baranyai’s Consistent Matrix Rounding Theorem): For any real matrix \(A \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{R \times C}\) with row sums \(r_i = \sum_j A_{i,j}\), column sums \(c_j = \sum_i A_{i,j}\), and grand total \(S = \sum_{i,j} A_{i,j}\), setting \([l, u] = [\lfloor x \rfloor, \lceil x \rceil]\) on every row arc, column arc, cell arc, and feedback arc \(t \to s\) is always feasible!

Proof: The original real numbers \(f(s \to \text{Row}_i) = r_i\), \(f(\text{Row}_i \to \text{Col}_j) = A_{i,j}\), \(f(\text{Col}_j \to t) = c_j\), and \(f(t \to s) = S\) already form a valid real-valued circulation satisfying all integer box bounds \(\lfloor x \rfloor \le x \le \lceil x \rceil\). By the Integrality Theorem on the slack network \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\), because a real-valued feasible circulation exists and all bounds \(\lfloor x \rfloor, \lceil x \rceil\) are integers, an integral circulation \(X_{i,j} \in \{\lfloor A_{i,j} \rfloor, \lceil A_{i,j} \rceil\}\) is guaranteed to exist!

4.2 Archetype B: Mixed-Graph Eulerian Circuit Orientation (POJ 1637) & FM-06 Parity Trap

  • Step 1 (Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal): A connected mixed graph \(G = (V, E_D \cup E_U)\) contains both fixed directed edges \(E_D\) and reversible undirected edges \(E_U\). We must orient every undirected edge \(e \in E_U\) so that the resulting fully directed graph has an Eulerian circuit (\(\text{in}(v) = \text{out}(v)\) for every \(v \in V\)).
  • Step 2 (Intermediate Algebraic Representation): Assign every undirected edge \(e \in E_U\) an arbitrary initial orientation \(u \to v\). Now let \(\text{in}_0(v)\) and \(\text{out}_0(v)\) denote the initial in-degree and out-degree of vertex \(v\) across all edges \(E_D \cup E_U\). For each undirected edge \(e = (u \to v) \in E_U\), introduce a binary decision variable \(x_e \in \{0, 1\}\), where \(x_e = 1\) means we reverse \(e\) to \((v \to u)\) and \(x_e = 0\) means we keep \((u \to v)\). How does setting \(x_e = 1\) change the net degree discrepancy \(\text{out}(v) - \text{in}(v)\)?
    • At the tail \(u\), reversing \(u \to v\) into \(v \to u\) decreases \(\text{out}(u)\) by \(1\) and increases \(\text{in}(u)\) by \(1\), reducing \(\text{out}(u) - \text{in}(u)\) by \(2\)!
    • At the head \(v\), it increases \(\text{out}(v) - \text{in}(v)\) by \(2\)! Therefore, achieving \(\text{out}(v) - \text{in}(v) = 0\) at every vertex \(v \in V\) is equivalent to: \[\big(\text{out}_0(v) - \text{in}_0(v)\big) - 2 \sum_{e \in \delta^+_U(v)} x_e + 2 \sum_{e \in \delta^-_U(v)} x_e = 0 \qquad \forall v \in V.\] Divide the entire equation by \(2\): \[\underbrace{\sum_{e \in \delta^+_U(v)} x_e - \sum_{e \in \delta^-_U(v)} x_e}_{\text{div}_x(v)} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{out}_0(v) - \text{in}_0(v)}{2}}_{D(v)} \qquad \forall v \in V.\]
  • Step 3 (Mechanical Graph Compilation):
    1. Parity Pre-Check: If \(\text{out}_0(v) - \text{in}_0(v)\) is odd for any \(v \in V\), then \(D(v) \notin \mathbb{Z}\), so the equation has no solution—return impossible immediately!
    2. Flow Network: Build a standard \([0, 1]\) flow network with source \(S^*\) and sink \(T^*\):
      • For each undirected edge \(e \in E_U\) (initially oriented \(u \to v\)), add directed arc \(u \to v\) with capacity \(1\) (representing \(x_e \in [0, 1]\)). Do not add fixed directed edges \(e \in E_D\) to the flow network!
      • If \(D(v) > 0\) (\(\text{out}_0(v) > \text{in}_0(v)\)), vertex \(v\) must emit \(D(v)\) units of reversal flow: add \(S^* \to v\) with capacity \(D(v)\).
      • If \(D(v) < 0\) (\(\text{in}_0(v) > \text{out}_0(v)\)), vertex \(v\) must absorb \(-D(v)\) units of reversal flow: add \(v \to T^*\) with capacity \(-D(v)\).
    3. Existence Criterion: An Eulerian orientation exists if and only if \(\text{MaxFlow}(S^*, T^*) = \sum_{v : D(v) > 0} D(v)\).

[!WARNING] Broken-Gadget Counterexample FM-06 — Mixed Eulerian Parity Omission & Integer Division Truncation: In C++, writing int D = (out[v] - in[v]) / 2; without first checking (out[v] - in[v]) % 2 != 0 silently truncates odd discrepancies toward zero (e.g., 1 / 2 == 0 and -1 / 2 == 0)!

Counterexample: Consider \(V = \{1, 2\}\) connected by a single fixed directed edge \(1 \to 2\) (\(E_U = \emptyset\)). Here \(\text{out}_0(1) - \text{in}_0(1) = +1\) and \(\text{out}_0(2) - \text{in}_0(2) = -1\). If you omit the parity check, C++ integer division computes D[1] = 1 / 2 = 0 and D[2] = -1 / 2 = 0, builds an empty flow network with \(\Sigma^+ = 0\), and falsely reports that a single directed edge \(1 \to 2\) has an Eulerian circuit! Always verify (out[v] - in[v]) & 1 for every vertex before dividing by \(2\).

4.3 Archetype C: DAG Minimum Path / Fleet Cover with Mandatory Coverage (Luogu P4843 清理雪道)

  • Step 1 (Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal): Given a directed acyclic graph (or general directed network) \(G = (V, E)\), find the minimum number of paths (starting at any valid start vertices and ending at any valid end vertices) such that every required edge \(e \in E_{\text{req}}\) (or every required vertex \(v \in V_{\text{req}}\)) is traversed at least once.
    • Why Ch 01 Bipartite Path Cover Fails: In Chapter 1’s Minimum Path Cover on a DAG (|V| - MaxMatching), vertices could be visited at most once (\([0, 1]\)) and edges could not be reused. When paths are allowed to share vertices and edges freely (At Least Once, \([1, \infty)\)), either transitive closure + Dilworth (Ch 08) or Variant 4 (有源汇上下界最小流) is required—and when edges have finite upper capacities \([l(e), u(e)]\) or costs, Variant 4 is the only method that generalizes!
  • Step 2 & Step 3 (Algebraic & Graph Compilation): Let \(f(e) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) count how many paths traverse edge \(e\).
    1. Create a virtual source \(s\) (fleet depot) and virtual sink \(t\) (fleet terminal). For every vertex \(v\) where a path may start, add \(s \to v\) with \([0, \infty)\); for every vertex \(v\) where a path may terminate, add \(v \to t\) with \([0, \infty)\).
    2. For every required edge \(e \in E_{\text{req}}\), set lower bound \(l(e) = 1\) and upper bound \(u(e) = \infty\). (If a vertex \(v\) must be visited at least \(k_v\) times, split \(v \mapsto v_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{[k_v, \infty)} v_{\text{out}}\) as in Chapter 3.1.)
    3. Solve Variant 4 (Minimum \(s\)-\(t\) Flow with Lower Bounds): add feedback arc \(t \to s\) with \([0, \infty)\), run Phase 1 MaxFlow(S*, T*) to get initial flow \(f_0 = \tilde{f}(t \to s)\), zero out both half-edges of \(t \to s\), and return \(F_{\min} = f_0 - \text{MaxFlow}_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\tilde{f}}}(t, s)\).

Problem #025 — [ZOJ 2314 / ASC 1] Reactor Cooling ([EN])

  • Judge URL: ZOJ 2314 / SGU 194
  • Constraints: \(N \le 200\) cooling nodes, \(M \le 20000\) directed pipes. Each pipe \(e_k = (u_k \to v_k)\) has mandatory minimum flow \(l_k \ge 0\) and maximum capacity \(u_k \ge l_k\) (\(0 \le l_k \le u_k \le 10^5\)). NoteHint 1 — Why Standard Max-Flow Fails & How to Shift the Origin

There is no source \(s\) or sink \(t\) in the reactor—every node must have total inflow equal to total outflow, and \(f \equiv 0\) violates \(l_k > 0\). Substitute \(f(e_k) = l_k + \tilde{f}(e_k)\) where \(0 \le \tilde{f}(e_k) \le u_k - l_k\) into \(\sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f(e) = \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} f(e)\) and isolate the known constants \(l_k\) on one side.

NoteHint 2 — Wiring the Nodal Imbalances to Super-Terminals

Define \(M(v) = \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} l(e)\). If \(M(v) > 0\), node \(v\) receives \(M(v)\) more baseline flow than it sends out, so the free slack flow \(\tilde{f}\) must carry \(M(v)\) units away from \(v\): wire \(S^* \to v\) with capacity \(M(v)\). If \(M(v) < 0\), wire \(v \to T^*\) with capacity \(-M(v)\). What must \(\text{MaxFlow}(S^*, T^*)\) equal for all nodes to balance?

TipFull 4-Step Compilation & Solution — ZOJ 2314
Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

Every pipe has a two-sided box constraint \(l_k \le f(e_k) \le u_k\) and every vertex satisfies strict sourceless conservation \(\text{div}_f(v) = 0\). This is the canonical Variant 1 (无源汇上下界可行流) problem.

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

Write \(f(e_k) = l_k + \tilde{f}(e_k)\) with \(0 \le \tilde{f}(e_k) \le u_k - l_k\). Conservation at vertex \(v\) becomes: \[\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} \tilde{f}(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} \tilde{f}(e) = \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} l(e) \triangleq M(v).\]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Graph Compilation)
  1. Create auxiliary network \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\) with vertices \(\{1, \dots, N\} \cup \{S^*, T^*\}\).
  2. For each pipe \(k = 1, \dots, M\) from \(u_k\) to \(v_k\):
    • Add directed edge \(u_k \to v_k\) with capacity \(u_k - l_k\), recording the index rev_idx[k] of its reverse residual half-edge.
    • Update \(M(u_k) \gets M(u_k) - l_k\) and \(M(v_k) \gets M(v_k) + l_k\).
  3. Accumulate \(\Sigma^+ = 0\). For each \(v \in \{1, \dots, N\}\):
    • If \(M(v) > 0\): add \(S^* \to v\) with capacity \(M(v)\), and add \(M(v)\) to \(\Sigma^+\).

TipFull 4-Step Compilation & Solution — POJ 2396
Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

We have a 2D table of integer variables \(X_{i,j}\) constrained by cell intervals \([L_{i,j}, U_{i,j}]\) and exact 1D marginal sums \(\sum_j X_{i,j} = r_i\), \(\sum_i X_{i,j} = c_j\). By Archetype A (Section 4.1), this compiles directly to Variant 2 (有源汇上下界可行流).

  2. For each constraint (p, q, op, v), iterate over \(i \in (p = 0 ? [1..R] : \{p\})\) and \(j \in (q = 0 ? [1..C] : \{q\})\):
    • If op == '>': \(L_{i,j} \gets \max(L_{i,j}, v + 1)\).
    • If op == '<': \(U_{i,j} \gets \min(U_{i,j}, v - 1)\).

  • Failure-Mode Guard: Notice that because \(s \to \text{Row}_i\) has \([r_i, r_i]\) and \(\text{Col}_j \to t\) has \([c_j, c_j]\), their slack capacities in \(\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\) are \(0\)! 7.1 Clean C++ Wrapper for All Four Lower-Bound Flow Variants (LOJ #115, #116, #117)

Assuming a standard Dinic struct (Chapter 1), the following lightweight wrapper compiles all four variants (Feasible Circulation, Feasible s-t Flow, Max s-t Flow, and Min s-t Flow) while enforcing every invariant and failure-mode guard automatically:

struct BoundedFlow {
    int n, S_star, T_star;
    Dinic G;
    vector<long long> M, low;
    vector<int> rev_idx;
    bool box_valid = true;

    BoundedFlow(int n_)
        : n(n_), S_star(n_ + 1), T_star(n_ + 2), G(n_ + 3), M(n_ + 3, 0) {}

    // Add directed edge u -> v with box bounds [l, r]
    int add_bounded_edge(int u, int v, long long l, long long r) {
        if (l > r) box_valid = false;
        M[u] -= l;
        M[v] += l;
        low.push_back(l);
        int e_id = G.add_edge(u, v, max(0LL, r - l));
        rev_idx.push_back(e_id ^ 1);
        return e_id;
    }

    // Variant 1: Sourceless Feasible Circulation (LOJ #115)
    bool solve_circulation() {
        if (!box_valid) return false;
        long long sigma_plus = 0;
        for (int v = 1; v <= n; ++v) {
            if (M[v] > 0) {
                G.add_edge(S_star, v, M[v]);
                sigma_plus += M[v];
            } else if (M[v] < 0) {
                G.add_edge(v, T_star, -M[v]);
            }
        }
        return G.max_flow(S_star, T_star) == sigma_plus;
    }

    // Variants 2, 3, 4: Bounded s-t Flow (mode: 0 = Feasible, +1 = Max, -1 = Min)
    // Returns {feasible, flow_value}
    pair<bool, long long> solve_st_flow(int s, int t, int mode) {
        int e_fb = G.add_edge(t, s, INF); // Feedback arc t -> s with [0, INF)
        if (!solve_circulation()) return {false, -1};
        long long f0 = G.edges[e_fb ^ 1].cap;
        // FM-04b Guard: Lock BOTH half-edges of feedback arc before Phase 2!
        G.edges[e_fb].cap = G.edges[e_fb ^ 1].cap = 0;
        if (mode == 0) return {true, f0};
        if (mode > 0)  return {true, f0 + G.max_flow(s, t)}; // Variant 3 (LOJ #116)
        return {true, f0 - G.max_flow(t, s)};                // Variant 4 (LOJ #117)
    }

    // FM-05 Guard: Reconstruct physical flow f(e_k) = l(e_k) + slack_flow(e_k)
    long long get_edge_flow(int k) const {
        return low[k] + G.edges[rev_idx[k]].cap;
    }

    // Theorem 3.2: Extract Hoffman Violation Certificate X* = S_min* \ {S*}
    vector<int> get_hoffman_violation_subset() const {
        vector<int> X_star;
        for (int v = 1; v <= n; ++v) {
            if (G.level[v] != -1) X_star.push_back(v); // Reachable from S* in G_f*
        }
        return X_star;
    }
};

8.2 Chapter Revision Summary

  • The Affine Slack Shift: Substituting \(f(e) = l(e) + \tilde{f}(e)\) converts \(l(e) \le f(e) \le u(e)\) and \(\text{div}_f(v) = b(v)\) into standard box bounds \(0 \le \tilde{f}(e) \le u(e) - l(e)\) with nodal divergence \(\text{div}_{\tilde{f}}(v) = M(v) \triangleq \text{in}_l(v) - \text{out}_l(v) - b(v)\).
  • The Four Variants of 上下界网络流:
    • Variant 1 (Feasible Circulation): Saturate \(S^* \to T^*\) (\(\text{MaxFlow}(S^*, T^*) = \Sigma^+\)).
    • Variant 2 (Feasible \(s\)-\(t\) Flow): Add feedback arc \(t \xrightarrow{[0, \infty)} s\) and run Variant 1; initial flow is \(f_0 = \tilde{f}(t \to s)\).
    • Variant 3 (Maximum \(s\)-\(t\) Flow): Run Variant 2, zero out \(t \to s\) and \(s \to t\), and compute \(F_{\max} = f_0 + \text{MaxFlow}(s, t)\).
    • Variant 4 (Minimum \(s\)-\(t\) Flow): Run Variant 2, zero out \(t \to s\) and \(s \to t\), and peel back excess flow via \(F_{\min} = f_0 - \text{MaxFlow}(t, s)\).
  • Hoffman’s Circulation Theorem (1960): A circulation exists \(\iff \sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) \le \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} u(e)\) for all \(X \subseteq V\), proved via the exact cut identity \(\Sigma^+ - \kappa_{\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}(S^* \sqcup X) = \sum_{e \in \delta^-(X)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(X)} u(e)\). When Phase 1 fails, \(X^* = S_{\min}^* \setminus \{S^*\}\) is the maximum-violation witness subset.
  • Canonical Non-Graph Compilations: Contingency-table / real-matrix rounding (Baranyai's Theorem), mixed-graph Eulerian circuit orientation (parity + half-discrepancy flow), and minimum-fleet mandatory DAG edge/vertex covers (Variant 4).
