  • Binary Project Selection & Pseudo-Boolean Cut Compilation (Chapters 9–10): Translating binary choices \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\) with implication constraints \(x_u = 1 \implies x_v = 1\) into infinite-capacity directed arcs \(u \xrightarrow{\infty} v\) corresponding to penalty terms \(\infty \cdot x_u(1 - x_v)\).
  • Bipartite Variable Inversion & Parity Cuts (Chapter 11): Reversing the source/sink convention (\(y_v = 1 - x_v\)) on one side of a bipartite conflict graph to convert supermodular agreement or disjunction penalties into standard submodular \(S\)-\(T\) cut terms.

  2.   3. 1. Beyond Binary Variables — Unary Threshold Encoding \(z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k]\) (K値燃やす埋める / 切糕模型)

In Chapters 9 through 11, every decision entity \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) chose between two states: \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\). What happens when each entity must choose from \(K \ge 3\) ordered discrete states \(x_i \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}\)?

A first instinct is to assign one binary indicator variable \(e_{i,k} = [x_i = k] \in \{0, 1\}\) to each state \(k \in \{1, \dots, K\}\). However, this “one-hot” encoding fails immediately in minimum-cut modelling: enforcing the exact-one constraint \(\sum_{k=1}^K e_{i,k} = 1\) requires both \(\sum_{k=1}^K e_{i,k} \le 1\) and \(\sum_{k=1}^K e_{i,k} \ge 1\), which induces non-submodular multi-way exclusions across all \(K\) states.

The key breakthrough of the Japanese K値燃やす埋める (\(K\)-valued Burn-or-Bury, formalized by noshi91 and maspy) and Chinese 切糕模型 (Cake-Cutting Model, popularized by [HNOI2013] 切糕) is to replace point-value indicators \([x_i = k]\) with unary threshold indicators \(z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k]\). Because the propositions \([x_i \ge 1], [x_i \ge 2], \dots, [x_i \ge K]\) are naturally nested, they form a totally ordered implication chain whose monotonicity is purely submodular.

1.1 Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

How do you recognize from an English, Chinese, or Japanese problem statement that a problem compiles into a Multi-State Ordered Vertex Chain (\(K\)-ary Min-Cut)?

  1. Discrete Multi-Valued Choice per Entity: Each entity \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) must be assigned exactly one integer state \(x_i \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}\) (such as a cutting depth \(x_{r,c} \in \{1, \dots, R\}\) in [HNOI2013] 切糕, a power-plant level \(x_i \in [l_i, r_i]\) in CF 434 D, a shortest-path distance \(d_u \in \{1, \dots, N-1\}\) in FoxAndCity, or a 3-state sign/deletion choice \(\sigma_v \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\) in ARC 107 F).
  2. Arbitrary (Possibly Non-Convex) Unary Cost Table: Assigning \(x_i = k\) incurs an individual cost \(c_i(k)\) (or yields reward \(r_i(k)\)) given by an arbitrary \(N \times K\) table or non-concave formula (such as a quadratic polynomial \(a_i x_i^2 + b_i x_i + d_i\) with positive leading coefficient \(a_i > 0\) in CF 434 D).
  3. Pairwise Ordering, Smoothness, or Step-Implication Constraints: Pairs of variables \((u, v) \in E\) must satisfy a bounded-difference inequality \(x_u - x_v \le D\) (such as Lipschitz smoothness \(|x_u - x_v| \le D\) in 切糕, triangle inequality \(|d_u - d_v| \le 1\) in FoxAndCity, or general threshold implications \(x_u \ge a \implies x_v \ge b\)).
  4. Constraint Fingerprint: The total state-space size \(\sum_{i=1}^N K_i \le 6.5 \times 10^4\) is small enough to materialize \(O(NK)\) vertices in a flow network, whereas the interaction graph \(G = (V, E)\) has large treewidth (e.g., a \(40 \times 40\) 2D grid or a general 300-vertex graph).

Why Naive DP and Greedy Fail:

  • Dynamic Programming: On a \(P \times Q\) grid with \(P, Q, R \le 40\), a broken-profile (plug) DP must remember the chosen height \(x_{r,c} \in \{1, \dots, R\}\) for all \(Q\) columns on the frontier, requiring \(O(R^{\min(P, Q)}) = 40^{40}\) states. On general graphs with cycles (ARC 107 F, CF 434 D), treewidth can be \(\Omega(N)\).
  • Greedy / Local Descent: Because the unary cost tables \(c_i(k)\) are arbitrary and non-convex, a local minimum can be arbitrarily far from the global optimum. Raising or lowering a single variable \(x_i\) propagates through a cascade of difference constraints \(x_u - x_v \le D\), requiring a simultaneous global shift across a connected cluster of variables—precisely what an \(S\)-\(T\) cut computes.

1.2 Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation & The Dual-Indexed Rosetta Stone

Before drawing any vertices or edges, we translate the integer variable \(x_i \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}\) into binary level-set propositions.

Because the Japanese (燃やす埋める) and Chinese (切糕模型) competitive programming literatures use two slightly shifted indexing conventions for unary threshold bits, we present both side-by-side in a unified Rosetta Stone so you can read editorials in either language without off-by-one confusion.

ImportantDefinition 1.1: Reconciled Unary Threshold Indicator Variables

For each integer variable \(x_i \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}\), introduce \(K + 1\) binary indicator variables:

  • Convention A (Weak Inequality [JA]): \(z_{i,1}, z_{i,2}, \dots, z_{i,K+1} \in \{0, 1\}\), where \(z_{i,k} := [x_i \ge k]\).
  • Convention B (Strict Inequality [ZH]): \(w_{i,0}, w_{i,1}, \dots, w_{i,K} \in \{0, 1\}\), where \(w_{i,k} := [x_i > k]\).

Both conventions describe the exact same \(K + 1\) boolean propositions via the index shift \(z_{i,k} \equiv w_{i,k-1}\):

\[z_{i,k} := [x_i \ge k] \equiv w_{i,k-1} := [x_i > k - 1] = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x_i \ge k \quad (\text{vertex } v_{i,k} \in S), \\ 0 & \text{if } x_i < k \quad (\text{vertex } v_{i,k} \in T), \end{cases} \qquad \text{for } k = 1, 2, \dots, K + 1.\]

These unary threshold bits satisfy four foundational algebraic laws that mechanically dictate the entire flow network:

  1. Boundary Pinning to Source \(S\) and Sink \(T\): Because every valid assignment \(x_i \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}\) trivially satisfies \(x_i \ge 1\) (\(x_i > 0\)) and never satisfies \(x_i \ge K + 1\) (\(x_i > K\)), the first indicator bit is pinned to \(1\) (identified with the source \(S\)) and the last indicator bit is pinned to \(0\) (identified with the sink \(T\)):

    \[z_{i,1} = w_{i,0} = 1 \quad (v_{i,1} \equiv S), \qquad\qquad z_{i,K+1} = w_{i,K} = 0 \quad (v_{i,K+1} \equiv T).\]

    Thus only the \(K - 1\) internal threshold propositions \(z_{i,2}, z_{i,3}, \dots, z_{i,K}\) (equivalently \(w_{i,1}, w_{i,2}, \dots, w_{i,K-1}\)) require dedicated internal vertices in the graph.

  2. The Unary Monotonicity Invariant (Prefix-of-Ones Law): For every threshold \(k \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}\), if an integer satisfies \(x_i \ge k + 1\), it must also satisfy \(x_i \ge k\). In boolean logic, \(z_{i,k+1} = 1 \implies z_{i,k} = 1\), which forces the sequence of indicator bits to be monotonically non-increasing:

    \[1 = z_{i,1} \ge z_{i,2} \ge z_{i,3} \ge \dots \ge z_{i,K} \ge z_{i,K+1} = 0.\]

    In our pseudo-Boolean cut polynomial framework—where \(z_\alpha(1 - z_\beta) = 1\) if and only if \(\alpha \in S\) (\(z_\alpha = 1\)) and \(\beta \in T\) (\(z_\beta = 0\))—forbidding the non-monotonic violation \((z_{i,k+1} = 1 \land z_{i,k} = 0)\) adds the infinite penalty sum:

    \[\text{MonotonicityPenalty}_i(z_i) = \sum_{k=2}^{K-1} \infty \cdot z_{i,k+1}(1 - z_{i,k}) = 0.\]

  3. Order-Isomorphic Bijection with \(\{1, \dots, K\}\) & Point-Value Indicator \([x_i = k]\): Every binary vector \((z_{i,1}, z_{i,2}, \dots, z_{i,K+1}) \in \{0, 1\}^{K+1}\) satisfying the monotonicity law \(1 = z_{i,1} \ge z_{i,2} \ge \dots \ge z_{i,K+1} = 0\) consists of a contiguous prefix of \(k\) ones followed by \(K + 1 - k\) zeros for a unique integer \(k \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}\):

    \[(z_{i,1}, \dots, z_{i,k}, z_{i,k+1}, \dots, z_{i,K+1}) = (\underbrace{1, 1, \dots, 1}_{k \text{ ones (in } S\text{)}}, \underbrace{0, 0, \dots, 0}_{K + 1 - k \text{ zeros (in } T\text{)}}), \qquad x_i = k = \sum_{t=1}^{K} z_{i,t}.\]

    Crucially, under the monotonicity invariant \(z_{i,t} \ge z_{i,t+1}\), the difference \(z_{i,t} - z_{i,t+1}\) can never be \(-1\) (\(z_{i,t} = 0, z_{i,t+1} = 1\) is forbidden!). Therefore, the linear difference \(z_{i,k} - z_{i,k+1}\) and the quadratic cut indicator \(z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1})\) are algebraically identical on all valid chains, and both equal \(1\) at the unique transition index \(t = k\) where the chain steps across the cut from \(S\) (\(1\)) to \(T\) (\(0\)):

    \[\boxed{[x_i = k] = z_{i,k} - z_{i,k+1} = z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1}) \equiv w_{i,k-1}(1 - w_{i,k}).}\]

  4. Bounded-Difference Constraint Compilation (\(x_u - x_v \le D\)): Suppose two variables \(x_u, x_v \in \{1, \dots, K\}\) must satisfy the relative difference bound \(x_u - x_v \le D\) (i.e., \(x_u \le x_v + D\)) for some integer offset \(D \in \mathbb{Z}\). In terms of unary thresholds, \(x_u \le x_v + D\) holds if and only if for every level \(k \in \{2, \dots, K\}\), reaching \(x_u \ge k\) forces \(x_v \ge k - D\):

    \[x_u - x_v \le D \iff \forall k \in \{2, \dots, K\}, \; \big(z_{u,k} = 1 \implies z_{v,k-D} = 1\big) \iff \sum_{k=2}^{K} \infty \cdot z_{u,k}\big(1 - z_{v,\text{clamp}(k-D)}\big) = 0,\]

    where \(\text{clamp}(k - D) = \max(1, \min(K + 1, k - D))\) handles boundary clamping automatically:

    • If \(k - D \le 1\), then \(z_{v,1} = 1\) holds unconditionally (\(1 - z_{v,1} = 0\)), so the implication \(x_u \ge k \implies x_v \ge 1\) is vacuously true and no edge is needed.
    • If \(k - D \ge K + 1\), then \(z_{v,K+1} = 0\) holds unconditionally (\(1 - z_{v,K+1} = 1\)), so \(z_{u,k}\) is forced to \(0\) via an \(\infty\)-arc \(v_{u,k} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{v,K+1} \equiv T\).

1.3 Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad & The Series-vs.-Parallel Abel Equivalence Theorem

Summing the unary costs, intra-chain monotonicity penalties, and cross-chain difference constraints over all variables yields our master pseudo-Boolean cut polynomial:

\[\mathcal{P}(z) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{k=1}^{K} c_i(k) \cdot z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1}) + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{k=2}^{K-1} \infty \cdot z_{i,k+1}(1 - z_{i,k}) + \sum_{(u, v, D) \in \mathcal{C}} \sum_{k=2}^{K} \infty \cdot z_{u,k}\big(1 - z_{v,\text{clamp}(k-D)}\big).\]

Every quadratic term \(\text{cap} \cdot z_\alpha(1 - z_\beta)\) in \(\mathcal{P}(z)\) compiles directly into a single directed edge \(\alpha \xrightarrow{\text{cap}} \beta\). Furthermore, applying 1D discrete summation by parts (Abel transformation) reveals that the unary cost function \(c_i(x_i)\) can be wired in two dual ways on the exact same vertex chain:

NoteTheorem 1.2: Series Chain vs. Parallel Closure Equivalence via 1D Abel Summation

Let \(z_{i,1}, \dots, z_{i,K+1} \in \{0, 1\}\) satisfy the unary monotonicity invariant \(1 = z_{i,1} \ge z_{i,2} \ge \dots \ge z_{i,K} \ge z_{i,K+1} = 0\). Then the unary cost function \(c_i(x_i)\) admits two algebraically identical graph compilations on the internal vertex set \(\{v_{i,2}, \dots, v_{i,K}\}\) and reverse \(\infty\)-backbone \(v_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{i,k}\):

  1. Form A — Series Chain Compilation ([HNOI2013] 切糕 Form): Using \([x_i = k] = z_{i,k} - z_{i,k+1} = z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1})\) (valid whenever \(z_{i,k} \ge z_{i,k+1}\)):

    \[c_i(x_i) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} c_i(k) \cdot z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1}).\]

    Graph Compilation: Place capacity \(c_i(k) \ge 0\) on the forward series edge \(v_{i,k} \xrightarrow{c_i(k)} v_{i,k+1}\) for \(k = 1, \dots, K\) (where \(v_{i,1} \equiv S\) and \(v_{i,K+1} \equiv T\)). Because a monotonic chain transitions from \(1\) (\(S\)) to \(0\) (\(T\)) at exactly one index \(k = x_i\), the cut severs the unique forward series edge \(v_{i,k} \to v_{i,k+1}\) and pays \(c_i(k)\).

    (Pedestal Shift for Negative Costs or Maximization Objectives): Because \(\sum_{k=1}^K z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1}) = z_{i,1} - z_{i,K+1} = 1 - 0 = 1\) on every valid monotonic chain, adding a constant pedestal \(M_i \ge \max_k (-c_i(k))\) to all \(K\) forward edges of chain \(i\) (\(c'_i(k) = c_i(k) + M_i \ge 0\)) increases the capacity of every valid cut by exactly \(M_i\)! When maximizing a reward \(f_i(x_i)\), simply set \(c_i(k) = M_i - f_i(k) \ge 0\) with \(M_i > \max_k |f_i(k)|\), and recover the maximum reward as \(\sum_{i=1}^N M_i - \text{MinCut}(S, T)\).

  2. Form B — Parallel Closure Compilation (燃やす埋める Marginal Form): Applying 1D Abel summation by parts to \(\sum_{k=1}^K c_i(k)(z_{i,k} - z_{i,k+1})\) with \(z_{i,1} = 1\) and \(z_{i,K+1} = 0\):

    \[c_i(x_i) = c_i(1) z_{i,1} + \sum_{k=2}^{K} \underbrace{\big(c_i(k) - c_i(k-1)\big)}_{\delta_i(k)} z_{i,k} - c_i(K) z_{i,K+1} = c_i(1) + \sum_{\substack{k=2 \\ \delta_i(k) > 0}}^{K} \delta_i(k) \cdot z_{i,k}(1 - z_T) - \sum_{\substack{k=2 \\ \delta_i(k) < 0}}^{K} |\delta_i(k)| + \sum_{\substack{k=2 \\ \delta_i(k) < 0}}^{K} |\delta_i(k)| \cdot z_S(1 - z_{i,k}).\]

    Graph Compilation: Keep the reverse \(\infty\)-backbone \(v_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{i,k}\) (\(2 \le k \le K-1\)), and attach each threshold vertex \(v_{i,k}\) (\(2 \le k \le K\)) directly in parallel to \(T\) or \(S\) according to the sign of the marginal step cost \(\delta_i(k) = c_i(k) - c_i(k-1)\):

    • If \(\delta_i(k) > 0\) (upgrading from \(k-1\) to \(k\) costs \(\delta_i(k)\)): wire \(v_{i,k} \xrightarrow{\delta_i(k)} T\).
    • If \(\delta_i(k) < 0\) (upgrading from \(k-1\) to \(k\) earns \(|\delta_i(k)|\)): wire \(S \xrightarrow{|\delta_i(k)|} v_{i,k}\) and subtract \(|\delta_i(k)|\) from the base offset. \(\blacksquare\)

1.4 Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Broken-Gadget Counterexample FM-12

Why is the reverse \(\infty\)-edge \(v_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{i,k}\) mandatory in Form A (Series Chain)?

A classic misconception among competitive programmers meeting [HNOI2013] 切糕 for the first time is: “Since a minimum \(S\)-\(T\) cut minimizes capacity, and cutting a single forward edge \(v_{i,k} \to v_{i,k+1}\) already disconnects \(S\) from \(T\) along chain \(i\), why would a minimum cut ever sever TWO forward edges in the same chain?”

WarningBroken-Gadget Counterexample FM-12: The Hallucinated Double-State Failure Mode

When cross-chain edges connect chain \(i\) to neighboring chains, omitting the reverse lock \(v_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{i,k}\) allows a finite \(S\)-\(T\) cut to place an earlier threshold vertex \(v_{i,a} \in T\) (\(z_{i,a} = 0\), pretending \(x_i < a\) to satisfy one neighbor) while simultaneously placing a later threshold vertex \(v_{i,b} \in S\) (\(z_{i,b} = 1\) for \(b > a\), pretending \(x_i \ge b\) to satisfy another neighbor)!

In the forward series chain \(S \to v_{i,2} \to \dots \to v_{i,K} \to T\), any middle edge \(v_{i,t} \to v_{i,t+1}\) located between \(v_{i,a} \in T\) and \(v_{i,b} \in S\) now points from \(T\) to \(S\). Because directed cut capacity only charges edges pointing from \(S\) to \(T\), the expensive middle edge from \(T\) to \(S\) contributes \(0\) to the cut, allowing the solver to dodge a heavy middle penalty by cutting two cheap outer edges instead!

Concrete Numerical Counterexample (2 Coupled Chains / 3-State Trace)

Let us trace this failure mode with exact numbers on a 3-state system (\(K = 3\), states \(\{1, 2, 3\}\)), where each variable \(i\) has two internal threshold vertices \(v_{i,2}\) (\(z_{i,2} = [x_i \ge 2]\)) and \(v_{i,3}\) (\(z_{i,3} = [x_i \ge 3]\)) along the series chain

\[S \xrightarrow{c_i(1)} v_{i,2} \xrightarrow{c_i(2)} v_{i,3} \xrightarrow{c_i(3)} T.\]

Suppose we have \(N = 2\) variables \(x_1, x_2 \in \{1, 2, 3\}\) (or equivalently a middle column \(x_1\) coupled to boundary anchors) with unary cost tables:

  • Variable 1: \(c_1 = (1, \; 100, \; 1)\) — very cheap (\(1\)) at the extreme states \(x_1 = 1\) and \(x_1 = 3\), but very expensive (\(100\)) at the middle state \(x_1 = 2\).
  • Variable 2: \(c_2 = (100, \; 0, \; 100)\) — strongly anchored at the middle state \(x_2 = 2\) (cost \(0\)), with cost \(100\) at \(x_2 = 1\) or \(x_2 = 3\).

Suppose Variable 1 and Variable 2 must satisfy the smoothness constraint \(|x_1 - x_2| \le 0\) (i.e., \(x_1 - x_2 \le 0\) and \(x_2 - x_1 \le 0\), forcing \(x_1 = x_2\)). By Section 1.2, this compiles to the cross-chain \(\infty\)-edges:

  • \(x_1 \le x_2 \implies (v_{1,2} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{2,2}) \text{ and } (v_{1,3} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{2,3})\).
  • \(x_2 \le x_1 \implies (v_{2,2} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{1,2}) \text{ and } (v_{2,3} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{1,3})\).

(Equivalently, even if cross-chain edges are strictly one-way—say \(v_{1,2} \xrightarrow{\infty} T\) from a low neighbor \(x_{\text{low}} = 1\) and \(S \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{1,3}\) from a high neighbor \(x_{\text{high}} = 3\) with \(D = 0\)—the exact same phenomenon occurs on Chain 1!)   1. True Feasible Optimum (With Reverse \(\infty\)-Lock \(v_{1,3} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{1,2}\)):

    • Thus Chain 1 is forced to pick a valid monotonic state \((z_{1,2}, z_{1,3}) \in \{(0, 0), (1, 0), (1, 1)\}\). 2. Flagship Deep-Dive Case Study 1 — 3-State Sign/Deletion Cuts & AtCoder ARC 107 F — Sum of Abs (#095)

Problem Statement & Constraints

  • Problem: AtCoder ARC 107 F — Sum of Abs (#095)
  • Constraints: \(N \le 300\) vertices, \(M \le 300\) undirected edges, \(|A_v| \le 10^6\), \(|B_v| \le 10^6\) (crucially, both \(A_v\) and \(B_v\) can be negative!), Time Limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
  • Task: You are given an undirected graph \(G = (V, E)\) where each vertex \(v \in V\) has a deletion cost \(A_v \in \mathbb{Z}\) and a signed weight \(B_v \in \mathbb{Z}\). You choose a subset of vertices \(D \subseteq V\) to delete, paying \(\sum_{v \in D} A_v\). Let \(\text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])\) be the set of connected components of the surviving induced subgraph \(G[V \setminus D]\). Each surviving connected component \(C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])\) contributes the absolute value of the sum of \(B_v\) over its vertices. Maximize the total score:

\[\text{Score}(D) = \sum_{C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])} \left| \sum_{v \in C} B_v \right| - \sum_{v \in D} A_v.\]

2.1 Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal & Linearizing \(|X| = \max(+X, -X)\)

At first glance, ARC 107 F looks intractable for network flow: the objective depends on connected components \(C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])\), which dynamically split and merge as vertices in \(D\) are deleted, and the absolute value \(\left|\sum_{v \in C} B_v\right|\) is a non-linear global function of an entire component.

Why do standard approaches fail, and what diagnostic signal points to a 3-state ordered chain?

  1. Why DP Fails: \(G\) is an arbitrary undirected graph with \(N, M \le 300\) and cycles, so tree DP is impossible and treewidth can be \(\Omega(N)\).

  2. Why Binary (2-State) Cuts Fail:

    • A 2-state cut separating “Keep \(v\)” from “Delete \(v\)” cannot tell whether a surviving vertex \(v \notin D\) contributes \(+B_v\) (in a positive-sum component) or \(-B_v\) (in a negative-sum component).
    • Conversely, a 2-state cut separating “Sign \(+1\)” from “Sign \(-1\)” cannot model deleting a vertex \(v \in D\) to act as an insulator between a \(+1\) neighbor and a \(-1\) neighbor.

  3. Linearizing \(|X| = \max(+X, -X)\) via Component Sign Choice: For any real number \(X\), \(|X| = \max(+X, -X) = \max_{s \in \{-1, +1\}} s \cdot X\). Crucially, because we are maximizing the sum of component absolute values, we can replace each component’s absolute value with a free choice of component sign \(s_C \in \{-1, +1\}\):

    \[\max_{D \subseteq V} \left( \sum_{C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])} \left| \sum_{v \in C} B_v \right| - \sum_{v \in D} A_v \right) = \max_{D \subseteq V} \; \max_{\substack{s_C \in \{-1, +1\} \\ \forall C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])}} \left( \sum_{C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])} \sum_{v \in C} s_C B_v - \sum_{v \in D} A_v \right).\]

    Why does replacing \(|X|\) by \(\max_{s_C \in \{-1, +1\}} s_C X\) require no constraint forcing \(s_C\) to match \(\operatorname{sgn}\left(\sum_{v \in C} B_v\right)\)? Because in a maximization problem, if the solver picks the “wrong” sign \(s_C = -\operatorname{sgn}(X)\), it gets \(-|X| \le +|X|\), which is suboptimal and automatically dominated by the true sign choice!

  4. Lifting Component Signs to 3 Vertex States: Once each surviving component \(C\) has a sign \(s_C \in \{-1, +1\}\), every individual vertex \(v \in V\) is in exactly one of three states \(\sigma_v \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\):

    • \(\sigma_v = -1\): vertex \(v\) survives (\(v \notin D\)) inside a component with \(s_C = -1\), contributing \(-B_v\) to the score.
    • \(\sigma_v = 0\): vertex \(v\) is deleted (\(v \in D\)), contributing \(-A_v\) to the score.
    • \(\sigma_v = +1\): vertex \(v\) survives (\(v \notin D\)) inside a component with \(s_C = +1\), contributing \(+B_v\) to the score.

2.2 Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation & The 1-Step Jump Prohibition

When does a vertex-level assignment \(\sigma = (\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_N) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^V\) correspond to a valid deletion set \(D\) and a uniform sign \(s_C\) on each surviving connected component \(C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])\)?

ImportantLemma 2.1: Component-Sign to 3-State Smoothness Bijection

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be an undirected graph. A 3-state vertex assignment \(\sigma \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^V\) arises from a deletion set \(D = \{v \in V : \sigma_v = 0\}\) and a uniform sign choice \(s_C \in \{-1, +1\}\) on every connected component \(C \in \text{CC}(G[V \setminus D])\) if and only if no undirected edge \((u, v) \in E\) directly connects a vertex in state \(+1\) to a vertex in state \(-1\):

\[\forall (u, v) \in E, \qquad |\sigma_u - \sigma_v| \le 1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \neg\big((\sigma_u = +1 \land \sigma_v = -1) \lor (\sigma_u = -1 \land \sigma_v = +1)\big).\]

Proof:

  • \((\Rightarrow)\) Suppose \(D = \{v : \sigma_v = 0\}\) and \(\sigma_v = s_C \in \{-1, +1\}\) on each component \(C\) of \(G[V \setminus D]\). For any edge \((u, v) \in E\), either at least one endpoint is deleted (\(\sigma_u = 0\) or \(\sigma_v = 0\), which immediately gives \(|\sigma_u - \sigma_v| \le 1\)) or both \(u, v \notin D\) survive. In the latter case, edge \((u, v)\) survives in \(G[V \setminus D]\), forcing \(u\) and \(v\) into the same connected component \(C\), so \(\sigma_u = \sigma_v = s_C\) and \(|\sigma_u - \sigma_v| = 0 \le 1\).
  • \((\Leftarrow)\) Conversely, suppose \(|\sigma_u - \sigma_v| \le 1\) holds for every \((u, v) \in E\), and define \(D = \{v \in V : \sigma_v = 0\}\). For any surviving edge \((u, v)\) in \(G[V \setminus D]\), both endpoints have \(\sigma_u, \sigma_v \in \{-1, +1\}\) and satisfy \(|\sigma_u - \sigma_v| \le 1\), which forces \(\sigma_u = \sigma_v\). By induction along paths, \(\sigma_v\) is constant across every connected component \(C\) of \(G[V \setminus D]\). \(\blacksquare\)

Look closely at Lemma 2.1: why must the deleted state \(\sigma_v = 0\) sit in the middle of the ordering \(-1 \prec 0 \prec +1\) (FM-12)?

Because deleting a vertex (\(\sigma_v = 0\)) acts as an insulator between \(\sigma_u = -1\) and \(\sigma_w = +1\)! When we order the three states as \(\sigma_v \in \{-1 \prec 0 \prec +1\}\), the condition “no \(+1\) vertex is adjacent to a \(-1\) vertex” becomes the bounded-difference inequality \(|\sigma_u - \sigma_v| \le 1\)—literally the [HNOI2013] 切糕 Lipschitz constraint of Section 1.2 with \(K = 3\) states and \(D = 1\)! If you instead ordered the states as \((-1, +1, 0)\), forbidding \((-1, +1)\) while allowing \((-1, 0)\) and \((+1, 0)\) would not be a monotone threshold constraint and could not be represented by a chain cut.

Converting Vertex Gains into Non-Negative Series Cut Capacities

Under state \(\sigma_v \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\), vertex \(v\)’s individual gain in the maximization objective is:

\[\text{Gain}_v(\sigma_v) = \begin{cases} -B_v, & \text{if } \sigma_v = -1 \quad (\text{survives with } - \text{ sign}), \\ -A_v, & \text{if } \sigma_v = 0 \quad (\text{deleted at cost } A_v), \\ +B_v, & \text{if } \sigma_v = +1 \quad (\text{survives with } + \text{ sign}). \end{cases}\]

To turn \(\max_\sigma \sum_{v \in V} \text{Gain}_v(\sigma_v)\) into a minimum cut \(\sum_{v \in V} M_v - \min_\sigma \sum_{v \in V} c_v(\sigma_v)\) with non-negative edge capacities \(c_v(-1), c_v(0), c_v(+1) \ge 0\), we subtract \(\text{Gain}_v(\sigma_v)\) from a per-vertex pedestal constant \(M_v\):

\[c_v^- := c_v(-1) = M_v + B_v, \qquad c_v^{\text{mid}} := c_v(0) = M_v + A_v, \qquad c_v^+ := c_v(+1) = M_v - B_v.\]

There are two standard ways to choose the pedestal \(M_v\):

  1. Choice A — The Universal Pedestal \(M_v = \max(|B_v|, -A_v)\) (Recommended for Arbitrary \(A_v \in \mathbb{Z}\)): Since ARC 107 F allows \(A_v < 0\) (deleting \(v\) can actually grant a positive bonus \(-A_v > 0\)!), setting

    \[M_v = \max\big(|B_v|, \; -A_v\big)\]

    guarantees \(c_v^- = M_v + B_v \ge 0\), \(c_v^{\text{mid}} = M_v + A_v \ge 0\), and \(c_v^+ = M_v - B_v \ge 0\) unconditionally without any if (A[v] < 0) branches! When \(A_v \ge 0\), this simplifies to \(M_v = |B_v|\), giving:

    \[c_v^- = |B_v| + B_v, \qquad c_v^{\text{mid}} = A_v + |B_v|, \qquad c_v^+ = |B_v| - B_v \qquad (\text{with baseline } \Phi = \sum_{v \in V} |B_v|).\]

  2. Choice B — The Shifted-by-\(A_v + |B_v|\) Pedestal \(M_v = A_v + |B_v|\) (When \(A_v \ge 0\)): Equivalently, if we add a further constant shift so that \(M_v = A_v + |B_v|\) (or view every vertex as paying \(A_v\) unless saved), every forward capacity shifts by—wait: remember from Theorem 1.2 that any constant added to \(M_v\) must be added to all three capacities \(c_v^-, c_v^{\text{mid}}, c_v^+\) simultaneously so that the differences \(c_v(\sigma) - c_v(\sigma') = \text{Gain}_v(\sigma') - \text{Gain}_v(\sigma)\) remain invariant!

WarningPedagogical Audit: Do Not Swap the \(+A_v\) Offset onto \(c_v^-\) and \(c_v^+\)!

A frequent algebraic slip when writing down ARC 107 F from memory is writing “\(c_v^- = A_v + |B_v| + B_v, \; c_v^{\text{mid}} = A_v, \; c_v^+ = A_v + |B_v| - B_v\)”. Let us check why that formula has the \(+A_v\) on the wrong edges: if \(c_v^- = A_v + |B_v| + B_v\) and \(c_v^{\text{mid}} = A_v\), then switching from \(\sigma_v = -1\) (kept) to \(\sigma_v = 0\) (deleted) changes the cut cost by \(c_v^{\text{mid}} - c_v^- = -|B_v| - B_v\), completely independent of the deletion cost \(A_v\)!

In truth, deleting \(v\) (\(\sigma_v = 0\)) forfeits \(|B_v|\) AND pays \(A_v\), so the loss relative to \(M_v = |B_v|\) is:

\[c_v^- = |B_v| + B_v, \qquad c_v^{\text{mid}} = A_v + |B_v|, \qquad c_v^+ = |B_v| - B_v,\]

or universally for all \(A_v, B_v \in \mathbb{Z}\) with \(M_v = \max(|B_v|, -A_v)\):

\[\boxed{c_v^- = M_v + B_v, \qquad c_v^{\text{mid}} = M_v + A_v, \qquad c_v^+ = M_v - B_v.}\]

Complete Model Implementation (ARC 107 F)

// AtCoder ARC 107 F — Sum of Abs (#095)
// Assumes standard Dinic library: Dinic flow(num_nodes, S, T); flow.add_edge(u, v, cap);
long long solve_arc107f(int N, int M, const vector<long long>& A,
                        const vector<long long>& B,
                        const vector<pair<int, int>>& edges) {
    const long long INF = 1e15;
    int S = 0, T = 2 * N + 1;
    Dinic flow(2 * N + 2, S, T);

    auto v_in  = [&](int v) { return 2 * v - 1; }; // 1-indexed v in [1..N]
    auto v_out = [&](int v) { return 2 * v; };

    long long total_pedestal = 0;
    for (int v = 1; v <= N; ++v) {
        long long M_v = max(abs(B[v]), -A[v]);
        total_pedestal += M_v;

        long long c_neg = M_v + B[v]; // cut if sigma_v = -1: (v_in, v_out) = (T, T)
        long long c_mid = M_v + A[v]; // cut if sigma_v =  0: (v_in, v_out) = (S, T)
        long long c_pos = M_v - B[v]; // cut if sigma_v = +1: (v_in, v_out) = (S, S)

        flow.add_edge(S, v_in(v), c_neg);
        flow.add_edge(v_in(v), v_out(v), c_mid);
        flow.add_edge(v_out(v), T, c_pos);

        // FM-12 Guard: Reverse infinity lock forbidding (v_in in T, v_out in S)
        flow.add_edge(v_out(v), v_in(v), INF);
    }

    for (auto [u, v] : edges) {
        // Forbid (sigma_u = +1 and sigma_v = -1) and vice versa
        flow.add_edge(v_out(u), v_in(v), INF);
        flow.add_edge(v_out(v), v_in(u), INF);
    }

    return total_pedestal - flow.max_flow();
}

  1. Every forbidden configuration in \(G\) is a \((K-1)\)-step walk \(v_1 - v_2 - \dots - v_K\) visiting layers \(V_1 \to V_2 \to \dots \to V_K\) in strict order; and
  2. Then deleting a minimum-weight vertex subset \(D \subseteq V\) (where deleting \(v \in V_k\) costs \(w_v \ge 0\)) to eliminate all forbidden configurations compiles into a \(K\)-layer vertex-split DAG:

  • Split each vertex \(v \in V_k\) into \(v_{\text{in}} \xrightarrow{w_v} v_{\text{out}}\).
  • Connect \(S \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{\text{in}}\) for all \(v \in V_1\), and \(v_{\text{out}} \xrightarrow{\infty} T\) for all \(v \in V_K\).
  • For every edge \((u, v) \in E\) with \(u \in V_k\) and \(v \in V_{k+1}\) (\(1 \le k \le K-1\)), wire \(u_{\text{out}} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{\text{in}}\).

By LP duality, any finite \(S\)-\(T\) cut partitions the surviving vertices \(V \setminus D\) into a monotonic \((K+1)\)-state assignment where no edge transitions from an \(S\)-side layer-\(k\) vertex to a \(T\)-side layer-\((k+1)\) vertex. \(\blacksquare\)

Problem Statement & Constraints

  • Problem: AtCoder ARC 142 E — Pairing Wizards (#096)
  • Constraints: \(N \le 100\) wizards, \(M \le \binom{N}{2}\) pairs, initial strengths \(1 \le a_i \le 100\), target strengths \(1 \le b_i \le 100\), Time Limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).

\[(x_u \ge b_u \land x_v \ge b_v) \;\lor\; (x_u \ge b_v \land x_v \ge b_u).\]

Find the minimum total cost \(\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - a_i)\) required to satisfy all \(M\) pairs.

4.1 Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal & The Mandatory Pre-Promotion Lemma

Read the English problem statement of ARC 142 E again: there is no mention of flows, cuts, or bipartite graphs anywhere. Worse still, the input graph \(G = (V, E)\) is an arbitrary undirected graph that can contain triangles \(K_3\) and odd cycles of every length!

Let us see how our 4-Step Compilation Protocol uncovers the hidden network structure from pure algebra.

First, for any two pairs of numbers \((x_u, x_v)\) and \((b_u, b_v)\), saying that \((x_u, x_v)\) dominates \((b_u, b_v)\) in either the identity matching or the swapped matching is algebraically equivalent to saying that the sorted pair \((\min(x_u, x_v), \max(x_u, x_v))\) dominates \((\min(b_u, b_v), \max(b_u, b_v))\):

\[(x_u \ge b_u \land x_v \ge b_v) \;\lor\; (x_u \ge b_v \land x_v \ge b_u) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \begin{cases} \text{(1) Conjunctive Floor:} & \min(x_u, x_v) \ge \min(b_u, b_v), \\ \text{(2) Disjunctive Ceiling:} & \max(x_u, x_v) \ge \max(b_u, b_v). \end{cases}\]

Expand Condition (1) and Condition (2) into elementary inequalities:

  1. Condition (1) is a Pure Conjunction (AND): \[\min(x_u, x_v) \ge \min(b_u, b_v) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad x_u \ge \min(b_u, b_v) \;\land\; x_v \ge \min(b_u, b_v).\] There is no choice here! Every feasible solution \((x_1, \dots, x_N)\) must satisfy \(x_u \ge \min(b_u, b_v)\) for every neighbor \(v \in N(u)\).
  2. Condition (2) is a Pure Disjunction (OR): \[\max(x_u, x_v) \ge \max(b_u, b_v) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad x_u \ge \max(b_u, b_v) \;\lor\; x_v \ge \max(b_u, b_v).\] Why is a disjunctive lower bound \(x_u \ge B \lor x_v \ge B\) terrifying? Because in boolean indicator variables \(z_u = [x_u \ge B] \in \{0, 1\}\), the disjunction \(z_u = 1 \lor z_v = 1\) forbids \((z_u = 0 \land z_v = 0)\). On a general graph \(G = (V, E)\), choosing a minimum-cost set of vertices with \(z_u = 1\) so that every edge has at least one endpoint with \(z = 1\) is Minimum Weight Vertex Cover, which is NP-hard on non-bipartite graphs!

Why, then, is ARC 142 E solvable in polynomial time even when \(G\) contains triangles \(K_3\)? Because enforcing Condition (1) first via Mandatory Pre-Promotion destroys every odd cycle in the remaining conflict graph!

ImportantLemma 4.1: The Mandatory Pre-Promotion Lemma

In any feasible assignment \((x_1, \dots, x_N)\), every wizard \(u \in V\) must satisfy

\[x_u \ge a_u^{\text{new}} := \max\left(a_u, \; \max_{v \in N(u)} \min(b_u, b_v)\right).\]

Therefore, we may immediately promote \(a_u \gets a_u^{\text{new}}\) for every \(u \in V\) and add the mandatory upfront cost

\[C_{\text{base}} = \sum_{u=1}^{N} \big(a_u^{\text{new}} - a_u^{\text{old}}\big)\]

to the answer. After this pre-promotion, Condition (1) (\(\min(x_u, x_v) \ge \min(b_u, b_v)\)) is universally satisfied for all \((u, v) \in E\) by any \(x \ge a\). \(\blacksquare\)

4.2 Step 2: The Low-Role vs. High-Role Bipartite Structural Dichotomy Lemma

Henceforth, let \(a_u\) denote the post-promotion strength of wizard \(u\) after Lemma 4.1 has been applied. Partition all wizards \(V\) into two disjoint sets according to whether their post-promotion strength \(a_u\) is still strictly below their own target \(b_u\):

\[V_{\text{low}} := \big\{ u \in V : a_u < b_u \big\} \quad (\textbf{Low-Role / Deficient Wizards}), \qquad V_{\text{high}} := \big\{ v \in V : a_v \ge b_v \big\} \quad (\textbf{High-Role / Sufficient Wizards}).\]

(Terminology Note: A wizard \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) currently has low strength \(a_u < b_u\) relative to its own target \(b_u\); a wizard \(v \in V_{\text{high}}\) has already met its own target \(a_v \ge b_v\) and only ever needs to climb to higher thresholds \(k \in (a_v, 100]\) to cover a neighbor’s target!)

Let us prove the central structural theorem of this section: after pre-promotion, the remaining unsatisfied edges of \(G\) are 100% bipartite between \(V_{\text{low}}\) and \(V_{\text{high}}\), and every wizard in \(V_{\text{low}}\) makes a pure binary jump decision!

ImportantTheorem 4.2: The Low-Role vs. High-Role Bipartite Structural Dichotomy Lemma

Assume \(a_u \gets \max_{v \in N(u)}(a_u, \min(b_u, b_v))\) has been applied for all \(u \in V\). Then:

  1. Bipartite Dichotomy of Unsatisfied Edges: For every edge \((u, v) \in E\) whose disjunctive condition \(\max(x_u, x_v) \ge \max(b_u, b_v)\) is not yet satisfied by \((a_u, a_v)\) (i.e., \(\max(a_u, a_v) < \max(b_u, b_v)\)), exactly one endpoint belongs to \(V_{\text{low}}\) (\(a_u < b_u\)) and the other endpoint belongs to \(V_{\text{high}}\) (\(a_v \ge b_v\)), with \(\max(b_u, b_v) = b_u > a_u\). In particular, \(V_{\text{low}} \cap V_{\text{high}} = \emptyset\), and no unsatisfied edge ever connects two vertices in \(V_{\text{low}}\) or two vertices in \(V_{\text{high}}\).
  2. Binary Jump Property on \(V_{\text{low}}\): For every wizard \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) (\(a_u < b_u\)), every incident edge \((u, w) \in E\) has \(\max(b_u, b_w) = b_u\). Consequently, in an optimal solution, \(x_u\) only ever takes one of two values: \(x_u \in \{a_u, b_u\}\).

Proof:

  1. Let \((u, v) \in E\) be any edge with \(\max(a_u, a_v) < \max(b_u, b_v)\). Without loss of generality, assume \(b_v \le b_u\), so that \(\min(b_u, b_v) = b_v\) and \(\max(b_u, b_v) = b_u\).

    • By Lemma 4.1 (Mandatory Pre-Promotion on edge \((u, v)\)), we have

      \[a_v \ge \min(b_u, b_v) = b_v \quad \Longrightarrow \quad v \in V_{\text{high}}!\]

    • Simultaneously, since \(\max(a_u, a_v) < \max(b_u, b_v) = b_u\), we must have

      \[a_u < b_u \quad \text{and} \quad a_v < b_u \quad \Longrightarrow \quad u \in V_{\text{low}}!\]

    Thus every unsatisfied edge has one endpoint \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) and one endpoint \(v \in V_{\text{high}}\), and requires \(\max(x_u, x_v) \ge b_u\).
  2. Now take any \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) (\(a_u < b_u\)) and consider any neighbor \(w \in N(u)\). By Lemma 4.1, \(\min(b_u, b_w) \le a_u < b_u\). Since \(\min(b_u, b_w) < b_u\), we must have \(b_w \le a_u < b_u\), which implies \(\max(b_u, b_w) = b_u\) on every edge \((u, w)\) incident to \(u\)! Therefore, raising \(x_u\) to \(b_u\) satisfies all edges incident to \(u\) simultaneously; raising \(x_u\) to any intermediate integer in \((a_u, b_u)\) satisfies none of them (since \(a_w\) or \(x_w\) would still need to reach \(b_u\)); and raising \(x_u > b_u\) is strictly wasteful. Thus \(x_u \in \{a_u, b_u\}\) is purely binary! \(\blacksquare\)

4.3 Step 3: Intermediate Algebraic Representation (Binary Jump vs. Unary Threshold Chain)

By Theorem 4.2, the optimization problem reduces to a bipartite system between \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) and \(v \in V_{\text{high}}\):

  1. Binary Indicator \(y_u \in \{0, 1\}\) for Low-Role Wizards \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) (\(a_u < b_u\)): Since \(x_u \in \{a_u, b_u\}\), define the binary jump indicator:

    \[y_u := [x_u \ge b_u] = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x_u = a_u \quad (\text{cost } 0), \\ 1 & \text{if } x_u = b_u \quad (\text{cost } b_u - a_u). \end{cases}\]

  2. Unary Threshold Chain \(z_{v,k} \in \{0, 1\}\) for High-Role Wizards \(v \in V_{\text{high}}\) (\(a_v \ge b_v\)): A High-Role wizard \(v \in V_{\text{high}}\) may be adjacent to several Low-Role wizards \(u_1, u_2, \dots \in V_{\text{low}}\) with distinct targets \(b_{u_1}, b_{u_2}, \dots \in (a_v, 100]\). Because each \(+1\) increment of \(x_v\) above \(a_v\) costs \(1\), we encode \(x_v \in \{a_v, a_v + 1, \dots, 100\}\) using the Form B (Parallel Closure) unary threshold indicators from Theorem 1.2:

    \[z_{v,k} := [x_v \ge k] \in \{0, 1\} \qquad \text{for } k \in \{a_v + 1, a_v + 2, \dots, 100\},\]

    subject to the decreasing monotonicity chain \(z_{v, a_v+1} \ge z_{v, a_v+2} \ge \dots \ge z_{v, 100}\) and linear upgrade cost:

    \[x_v - a_v = \sum_{k = a_v + 1}^{100} 1 \cdot z_{v,k}.\]

  3. Disjunctive OR Constraint on Active Edges \((u, v) \in E\) (\(u \in V_{\text{low}}, v \in V_{\text{high}}, a_v < b_u\)): Edge \((u, v)\) requires \(x_u \ge b_u \lor x_v \ge b_u\), which in our boolean indicators is the disjunction:

    \[y_u = 1 \;\lor\; z_{v, b_u} = 1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \neg\big(y_u = 0 \;\land\; z_{v, b_u} = 0\big).\]

4.4 Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation via Bipartite Variable Inversion

To compile the forbidden conjunction \((y_u = 0 \land z_{v, b_u} = 0)\) into a directed \(S \to T\) cut penalty \(\infty \cdot [\text{tail} \in S][\text{head} \in T]\), we apply Bipartite Variable Inversion (Chapter 11) across the bipartition \((V_{\text{low}}, V_{\text{high}})\):

  • On \(V_{\text{low}}\) (Inverted Source Convention): Place vertex \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) in \(S\) when \(y_u = 0\) (\(x_u = a_u\), not promoted) and in \(T\) when \(y_u = 1\) (\(x_u = b_u\), promoted at cost \(b_u - a_u\)).

    • When \(u \in T\) (\(y_u = 1\)), we must pay \(b_u - a_u\), which compiles to the source edge:

      \[S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } = \; b_u - a_u} u.\]

  • On \(V_{\text{high}}\) (Standard Unary Chain Convention): For each \(v \in V_{\text{high}}\) and each threshold \(k \in (a_v, 100]\), place vertex \((v, k)\) in \(S\) when \(z_{v,k} = 1\) (\(x_v \ge k\), paying \(+1\)) and in \(T\) when \(z_{v,k} = 0\) (\(x_v < k\), paying \(0\)).

    • Paying \(+1\) when \((v, k) \in S\) (\(z_{v,k} = 1\)) compiles to the parallel unit sink edge:

      \[(v, k) \xrightarrow{\text{cap } = \; 1} T \qquad \forall k \in \{a_v + 1, \dots, 100\}.\]

    • Enforcing unary monotonicity \(z_{v,k} = 1 \implies z_{v,k-1} = 1\) (\((v, k) \in S \implies (v, k-1) \in S\)) compiles to the reverse \(\infty\)-lock:

      \[(v, k) \xrightarrow{\text{cap } = \; \infty} (v, k - 1) \qquad \forall k \in \{a_v + 2, \dots, 100\}.\]

  • Cross-Bipartition OR Constraint (\(y_u = 1 \lor z_{v, b_u} = 1\)): The forbidden state \((y_u = 0 \land z_{v, b_u} = 0)\) is now literally \(u \in S \land (v, b_u) \in T\)! Forbidding \(u \in S\) and \((v, b_u) \in T\) compiles to the single directed \(\infty\)-arc:

    \[u \xrightarrow{\text{cap } = \; \infty} (v, b_u).\]

Let us check the logic of this gadget: for any active edge \((u, v)\) with \(u \in V_{\text{low}}, v \in V_{\text{high}}, a_v < b_u\), either \(u \in T\) (severing \(S \xrightarrow{b_u - a_u} u\) and paying \(b_u - a_u\) to set \(x_u = b_u\)), or \(u \in S\), which forces \((v, b_u) \in S\) via \(u \xrightarrow{\infty} (v, b_u)\), which in turn forces \((v, b_u - 1), \dots, (v, a_v + 1) \in S\) via the reverse \(\infty\)-locks, severing \(b_u - a_v\) unit edges to \(T\) and paying \(b_u - a_v\) to raise \(x_v\) to \(b_u\)!

\[\boxed{\text{Minimum Total Cost} = C_{\text{base}} + \text{MinCut}(S, T).}\]

Concrete Numerical Trace (\(N = 3\) Star Graph \(2 - 1 - 3\))

Suppose \(N = 3\) wizards have initial and target strengths \((a_1, b_1) = (3, 2)\), \((a_2, b_2) = (1, 5)\), \((a_3, b_3) = (2, 6)\), connected by edges \((1, 2)\) and \((1, 3)\):

  1. Step 1 (Mandatory Pre-Promotion):
    • On edge \((1, 2)\), \(\min(b_1, b_2) = \min(2, 5) = 2\). On edge \((1, 3)\), \(\min(b_1, b_3) = \min(2, 6) = 2\).
    • New floors: \(a_1 \gets \max(3, 2, 2) = 3\) (cost \(0\)); \(a_2 \gets \max(1, 2) = 2\) (cost \(2 - 1 = 1\)); \(a_3 \gets \max(2, 2) = 2\) (cost \(0\)).
    • Base cost \(C_{\text{base}} = 1\), post-promotion strengths \(a = (3, 2, 2)\).
  2. Step 2 (Low-Role vs. High-Role Bipartition):
    • Wizard 1 has \(a_1 = 3 \ge b_1 = 2 \implies 1 \in V_{\text{high}}\), with unary chain nodes \((1, 4), (1, 5), (1, 6)\) wired \((1, 6) \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 5) \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 4)\) and each \((1, k) \xrightarrow{1} T\).
    • Wizard 2 has \(a_2 = 2 < b_2 = 5 \implies 2 \in V_{\text{low}}\) (\(S \xrightarrow{5 - 2 = 3} 2\), OR-edge \(2 \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 5)\)).
    • Wizard 3 has \(a_3 = 2 < b_3 = 6 \implies 3 \in V_{\text{low}}\) (\(S \xrightarrow{6 - 2 = 4} 3\), OR-edge \(3 \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 6)\)).
  3. Step 3 (Evaluating Candidate Cuts):
    • Cut A (Promote Wizard 1 to \(6\)): Keep \(2, 3 \in S\) (\(y_2 = y_3 = 0\)). Then \((1, 6) \in S \implies (1, 5), (1, 4) \in S\), severing three unit edges to \(T\) for cut cost \(3\).
    • Cut B (Promote Wizard 1 to \(5\), Jump Wizard 3 to \(6\)): Keep \(2 \in S \implies (1, 5), (1, 4) \in S\) (cost \(2\)), and place \(3 \in T\) (severing \(S \xrightarrow{4} 3\), cost \(4\)), total cut cost \(2 + 4 = 6\).
    • Cut C (Jump Wizards 2 and 3): Place \(2, 3 \in T\) and keep \((1, 4), (1, 5), (1, 6) \in T\), severing \(S \xrightarrow{3} 2\) and \(S \xrightarrow{4} 3\) for cut cost \(3 + 4 = 7\).
    • The minimum cut is \(3\) (Cut A), giving total cost \(C_{\text{base}} + \text{MinCut}(S, T) = 1 + 3 = \mathbf{4}\) at optimal strengths \(x^* = (6, 2, 2)\).

Broken-Gadget Counterexamples

  1. What Breaks If You Skip Step 1 (Mandatory Pre-Promotion) on a Triangle \(K_3\)?
    • Suppose \(G\) is a triangle \(K_3\) on wizards \(\{1, 2, 3\}\) with \((a_1, b_1) = (0, 2)\), \((a_2, b_2) = (0, 4)\), and \((a_3, b_3) = (0, 6)\).
    • Without pre-promotion, all three wizards satisfy \(a_i = 0 < b_i\), so all three appear to belong to \(V_{\text{low}}\) on an odd cycle \(K_3\)! Worse yet, Wizard 2 acts as the higher-target endpoint on edge \((1, 2)\) (\(\max(b_1, b_2) = 4\)) and as the lower-target endpoint on edge \((2, 3)\) (\(\max(b_2, b_3) = 6\)), destroying the bipartition!
    • With pre-promotion (Lemma 4.1): \(a_1 \gets \max(0, \min(2,4), \min(2,6)) = 2 \ge b_1\), \(a_2 \gets \max(0, \min(4,2), \min(4,6)) = 4 \ge b_2\), and \(a_3 \gets \max(0, \min(6,2), \min(6,4)) = 4 < b_3 = 6\). Pre-promotion automatically moves Wizards 1 and 2 into \(V_{\text{high}}\), completely satisfies edge \((1, 2)\) at cost \(0\), and leaves only Wizard 3 in \(V_{\text{low}}\)!
  2. What Breaks If You Omit the Reverse Monotonicity Locks \((v, k) \xrightarrow{\infty} (v, k-1)\) on \(v \in V_{\text{high}}\)?
    • In our star-graph trace above, the OR-edges \(2 \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 5)\) and \(3 \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 6)\) enter \((1, 5)\) and \((1, 6)\), but nothing directly enters \((1, 4)\)!
    • If \((1, 6) \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 5) \xrightarrow{\infty} (1, 4)\) were omitted, Cut A would place \((1, 6) \in S\) and \((1, 5) \in S\) while leaving \((1, 4) \in T\), paying only \(2\) instead of \(3\) to raise Wizard 1 from \(a_1 = 3\) to \(6\)!

Complete Model Implementation (ARC 142 E)

// AtCoder ARC 142 E — Pairing Wizards (#096)
// Assumes standard Dinic library: Dinic flow(num_nodes, S, T); flow.add_edge(u, v, cap);
long long solve_arc142e(int N, vector<int> a, const vector<int>& b,
                        const vector<pair<int, int>>& edges) {
    const int INF = 1e9;
    const int B_MAX = 100;

    // Step 1: Mandatory Pre-Promotion (Lemma 4.1)
    long long base_cost = 0;
    vector<int> a_new = a;
    for (auto [u, v] : edges) {
        int floor_uv = min(b[u], b[v]);
        a_new[u] = max(a_new[u], floor_uv);
        a_new[v] = max(a_new[v], floor_uv);
    }
    for (int i = 0; i < N; ++i) {
        base_cost += a_new[i] - a[i];
        a[i] = a_new[i];
    }

    // Step 2: Bipartition into V_low (a_i < b_i) and V_high (a_i >= b_i)
    // Node indexing:
    //   S = 0, T = 1
    //   wizard i binary node (for i in V_low): 2 + i
    //   wizard i unary chain node (i, k) for k in [1..B_MAX]: 2 + N + i * (B_MAX + 1) + k
    int S = 0, T = 1;
    int total_nodes = 2 + N + N * (B_MAX + 1);
    Dinic flow(total_nodes, S, T);

    auto low_node  = [&](int u) { return 2 + u; };
    auto high_node = [&](int v, int k) { return 2 + N + v * (B_MAX + 1) + k; };

    for (int i = 0; i < N; ++i) {
        if (a[i] < b[i]) {
            // Low-Role wizard: cutting S -> u pays (b_i - a_i) to jump x_i to b_i
            flow.add_edge(S, low_node(i), b[i] - a[i]);
        } else {
            // High-Role wizard: unary chain for k in (a_i, B_MAX]
            for (int k = a[i] + 1; k <= B_MAX; ++k) {
                flow.add_edge(high_node(i, k), T, 1);
                if (k > a[i] + 1) {
                    flow.add_edge(high_node(i, k), high_node(i, k - 1), INF);
                }
            }
        }
    }

    // Step 4: Wire Disjunctive OR edges u -> (v, b_u) for unsatisfied pairs
    for (auto [u, v] : edges) {
        if (max(a[u], a[v]) >= max(b[u], b[v])) continue; // already satisfied
        if (a[u] >= b[u]) swap(u, v); // ensure u in V_low (a_u < b_u) and v in V_high
        flow.add_edge(low_node(u), high_node(v, b[u]), INF);
    }

    return base_cost + flow.max_flow();
}

Complexity: The network has at most \(N + N \cdot B_{\max} \le 10,100\) active vertices and \(O(N B_{\max} + M)\) edges. Dinic’s algorithm runs in \(< 5\text{ ms}\).

5. The 4-Step Multi-State Chain Compilation Protocol

Whenever you encounter a multi-state assignment, sign/deletion optimization, or threshold-upgrade problem in a contest, follow this 4-Step Compilation Protocol to build a bug-free network:

  1. Step 1 — Identify the Ordered State Space & Check \(N \cdot K\): Determine the discrete state set \(\{1, 2, \dots, K\}\) of each entity \(i\). If the states are not initially numbers (e.g., \(\{-, \text{del}, +\}\) in ARC 107 F or 4 coordinate parities in CF 1517 G), order them so that all forbidden pairwise transitions jump across intermediate insulator states (\(|x_u - x_v| \le D\)).
  2. Step 2 — Choose Form A (Series Chain) vs. Form B (Parallel Closure):
    • Use Form A (Series Chain) \(S \xrightarrow{c_i(1)} v_{i,2} \xrightarrow{c_i(2)} \dots \xrightarrow{c_i(K)} T\) when arbitrary point-value costs \(c_i(k)\) are given ([HNOI2013] 切糕, ARC 107 F, CF 434 D). If maximizing \(f_i(k)\) or if \(c_i(k) < 0\), shift every forward edge on chain \(i\) by \(+M_i\) where \(M_i \ge \max_k |f_i(k)|\).
    • Use Form B (Parallel Closure) \(v_{i,k} \xrightarrow{\delta_i(k)} T\) when marginal upgrade costs \(\delta_i(k) = c_i(k) - c_i(k-1) > 0\) are given (ARC 142 E, ABC 326 G).
  3. Step 3 — Wire the Intra-Chain Reverse \(\infty\)-Locks (FM-12 Guard): In both Form A and Form B, unconditionally wire \(v_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{i,k}\) for all \(2 \le k \le K-1\) so that \(z_{i,k+1} \le z_{i,k}\) holds and no chain can ever be severed twice.
  4. Step 4 — Compile Cross-Chain Implications & Contract Inactive Sub-Chains:
    • Difference Bound \(x_u - x_v \le D\): Wire \(v_{u,k} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{v,\text{clamp}(k-D)}\).
    • Conjunctive Milestone (ABC 326 G): Wire \(S \xrightarrow{A_j} m_j\) and \(m_j \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{i, L_{j,i}}\).
    • Bipartite Disjunctive OR (ARC 142 E): Invert \(u \in V_{\text{low}}\) (\(S \xrightarrow{\text{cost}} u\)) and wire \(u \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{v, \text{req}}\).
    • Active-Event Chain Contraction (When \(K \le 10^9\) is Sparse): If \(K\) is huge but only \(k_i \ll K\) threshold levels \(1 = t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_{k_i} \le K\) on chain \(i\) have incident cross-edges, contract each unbranched segment \([t_r, t_{r+1})\) into a single forward series edge of capacity \(\min_{t_r \le k < t_{r+1}} c_i(k)\) (in Form A) or a single parallel edge of capacity \(c_i(t_r) - c_i(t_{r-1})\) (in Form B)!

#091 (Int-1) — AtCoder ARC 125 E: Snack

  • Judge URL: AtCoder ARC 125 E (Companion 3-layer hex-parity cut: Luogu P5458 [BJOI2016] 水晶)
  • Constraints: \(N, M \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(1 \le A_i \le 10^{12}\), \(1 \le B_j, C_j \le 10^{12}\), Time Limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
  • Statement: There are \(N\) types of snacks (type \(i\) has \(A_i\) pieces) and \(M\) children. Child \(j\) can receive at most \(B_j\) pieces of any single snack type \(i\), and at most \(C_j\) pieces in total across all snack types. Find the maximum total number of snack pieces that can be distributed.

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

Distributing snacks from \(N\) supply types to \(M\) children subject to per-type caps \(A_i\), per-child caps \(C_j\), and pairwise \((i, j)\) caps \(B_j\) is a textbook bipartite maximum flow on \(S \xrightarrow{A_i} u_i \xrightarrow{B_j} v_j \xrightarrow{C_j} T\). Because \(N, M \le 2 \times 10^5\), materializing \(NM = 4 \times 10^{10}\) edges is impossible. However, notice that every cross-edge \((u_i \to v_j)\) has capacity \(B_j\) depending only on \(j\), not on \(i\)! This symmetry allows us to dualize to Minimum \(S\)-\(T\) Cut and parameterize the cut by the unary count \(k = |S \cap U| \in \{0, 1, \dots, N\}\) of snack vertices kept on the \(S\)-side.

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

Let \(x_i \in \{0, 1\}\) (\(1\) if snack \(i \in S\), \(0\) if \(i \in T\)) and \(y_j \in \{0, 1\}\) (\(1\) if child \(j \in S\), \(0\) if \(j \in T\)). The cut capacity is:

\[\text{Cut}(x, y) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} A_i (1 - x_i) + \sum_{j=1}^{M} C_j y_j + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{M} B_j x_i (1 - y_j).\]

Suppose we fix the number of snack types in \(S\) to be \(k = \sum_{i=1}^N x_i \in \{0, 1, \dots, N\}\). Then \(\sum_{i=1}^N B_j x_i (1 - y_j) = k \cdot B_j (1 - y_j)\), so each child \(j\)’s optimal choice \(y_j \in \{0, 1\}\) decouples completely and pays \(\min(C_j, \; k \cdot B_j)\)! Meanwhile, to minimize \(\sum_{i=1}^N A_i (1 - x_i)\) subject to \(\sum_{i=1}^N x_i = k\), we greedily set \(x_i = 1\) for the \(k\) snack types with the largest \(A_i\):

\[\text{MinCut}(S, T) = \min_{k=0}^{N} \left( \sum_{i=k+1}^{N} A_{(i)} + \sum_{j=1}^{M} \min\big(C_j, \; k \cdot B_j\big) \right),\]

where \(A_{(1)} \ge A_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge A_{(N)}\) are the snack supplies sorted in descending order.

  • Constraints: \(N \le 500\), \(0 \le B_{ij}, C_i \le 10^3\), Time Limit \(1.0\text{ s}\).
  • Statement: Given an \(N \times N\) non-negative matrix \(B\) and a \(1 \times N\) vector \(C\), choose a binary row vector \(A = (x_1, \dots, x_N) \in \{0, 1\}^N\) to maximize \(D(x) = (A B - C) A^T = A B A^T - C A^T\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

Expanding the matrix product reveals a quadratic pseudo-Boolean maximization objective:

\[D(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} B_{ij} x_i x_j - \sum_{i=1}^{N} C_i x_i, \qquad x_i \in \{0, 1\}.\]

With \(N = 500\), creating \(N^2 = 250,000\) auxiliary project nodes \(p_{ij}\) for every pair \((i, j)\) builds a large graph. Instead, applying the 1D/2D Abel identity \(x_i x_j = x_i - x_i(1 - x_j)\) eliminates all \(N^2\) auxiliary vertices and compiles the entire matrix game onto just \(N\) vertices!

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

Substitute \(x_i x_j = x_i - x_i(1 - x_j)\) into \(D(x)\):

\[D(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{N} B_{ij}\right) x_i - \sum_{i=1}^{N} C_i x_i - \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} B_{ij} x_i(1 - x_j).\]

Let \(R_i = \sum_{j=1}^N B_{ij}\) be the \(i\)-th row sum of \(B\). Subtracting from the baseline \(\Phi = \sum_{i=1}^N R_i\) using \(x_i = 1 - (1 - x_i)\) gives the exact cut polynomial:

\[\Phi - D(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} R_i (1 - x_i) + \sum_{i=1}^{N} C_i x_i + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j \ne i} B_{ij} x_i(1 - x_j).\]

8. Chapter Summary & Bridge to Chapter 13

In this chapter, we lifted binary project selection into the multi-state domain by encoding each \(K\)-valued variable \(x_i \in \{1, \dots, K\}\) as a monotonic chain of \(K - 1\) unary threshold indicators \(z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k]\) locked together by reverse \(\infty\)-arcs \(v_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} v_{i,k}\). We saw how 1D Abel summation bridges Form A (Series Chains for point-value tables like [HNOI2013] 切糕 and ARC 107 F) and Form B (Parallel Chains for marginal upgrades like ARC 142 E and ABC 326 G).

What happens when the pairwise interaction between \(x_u\) and \(x_v\) is not a hard \(\infty\)-cliff \([x_u - x_v > D]\infty\), but a soft convex penalty such as \((x_u - x_v)^2\) (ABC 347 G) or \(w_{uv}|h_u - h_v|\) over \(K = 10^9\) shades of grey (CF 1427 G)?

In Chapter 13 (The Coarea Formula, Level-Set Cuts, Ishikawa’s Convex Chains & Monge Arrays), we apply 2D Abel Summation by Parts to prove Ishikawa’s Theorem—compiling any submodular/Monge penalty \(\Phi(x_u, x_v)\) into cross-chain capacities \(-\Delta_a \Delta_b \Phi(a, b) \ge 0\)—and derive the Discrete Coarea Formula, which decouples \(10^9\)-state \(\ell_1\) smoothing into nested level-set cuts solved in \(O(N^3)\) total time via incremental residual rollback!
