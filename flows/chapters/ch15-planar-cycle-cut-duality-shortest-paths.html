1. Direction B Flow Elimination — Why \(N \times N\) Grids (\(V \ge 2.5 \times 10^5\)) Require Planar Duality

Up to Chapter 14, we used the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem primarily in Direction A (\(\text{Min-Cut} \implies \text{Max-Flow}\)):

\[\underbrace{\min_{\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^V} \Phi_G(\mathbf{x})}_{\text{Combinatorial / Logical Objective}} \quad \xrightarrow{\text{Direction A}} \quad \underbrace{\text{MaxFlow}_G(S, T)}_{\text{Solved by Dinic's Algorithm}}.\]

In Direction A, the problem statement begins as a logical selection, scheduling, or fractional density problem with no explicit flow pipes; we compile the objective into a capacitated network \(G\) of moderate size (\(V \le 5 \times 10^4\)) and hand \(G\) to Dinic’s algorithm.

In Chapters 15 and 16, we reverse the arrow of the theorem and invoke Direction B (\(\text{Max-Flow} \implies \text{Min-Cut} \implies \text{Structural Elimination}\)):

\[\underbrace{\text{MaxFlow}_G(s, t)}_{\text{Too Large for Dinic } (V \ge 2.5 \times 10^5)} \quad \xrightarrow{\text{Direction B}} \quad \underbrace{\text{MinCut}_G(s, t)}_{\text{Topological / Algebraic Dual}} \quad \xrightarrow{\text{Compile}} \quad \underbrace{\text{Dijkstra / DP / Tree}_{\text{in } O(V \log V)}}_{\text{Zero Augmenting Paths!}}.\]

1.1 Step 1: Non-Graph & Topological Diagnostic Signals

When a contest problem presents a flow or cut task on a massive network, how do you know that Planar Cycle–Cut Duality is the intended weapon?

  1. Planar or Grid Topology with \(V \in [2.5 \times 10^5, 10^6]\):
    • The underlying graph is an \(N \times M\) rectangular grid (\(500 \times 500\) in [NOI2010] 海拔 and [CSP-S 2021] 交通规划), a triangulated grid (\(1000 \times 1000\) in [BJWC2006] 狼抓兔子, where \(V = 10^6\) and \(E \approx 3 \times 10^6\)), an obstacle-avoiding chessboard (CF 1749 E, CF 1765 I), or a planar subdivision given by polygon faces ([清华集训2017] 热带岛屿).
    • Why Dinic’s Algorithm Fails at \(V \ge 2.5 \times 10^5\): Even on a planar graph where \(|E| = O(|V|)\), the worst-case number of blocking-flow phases in Dinic’s algorithm on arbitrary capacities is \(\Omega(|V|)\) (and \(\Omega(|V|^{1/2})\) even on unit networks, yielding \(O(V^{1.5})\)). Worse yet, an adversarial setter can embed a winding “snake” path of high-capacity edges folding back and forth across an \(N \times N\) grid with tiny cross-rungs of capacity \(1\). Dinic is then forced to augment along paths of length \(\Theta(N^2)\) across \(\Theta(N^2)\) phases, taking \(\Theta(V^2) \approx 6 \times 10^{10}\) operations—an immediate Time Limit Exceeded (TLE).
    • By contrast, Dijkstra’s algorithm on the planar dual graph \(G^*\) visits each face once and relaxes each dual edge once, running in a deterministic, input-insensitive \(O(V \log V)\) time (\(\approx 20\text{ ms}\) for \(V = 2.5 \times 10^5\))!
  2. Continuous Monotone Potential Fields on a Grid ([NOI2010] 海拔):
    • Each vertex \(v\) must be assigned a real number \(h_v \in [0, 1]\) (such as altitude, voltage, or temperature) with boundary pins \(h_s = 0\) and \(h_t = 1\), minimizing a linear uphill edge penalty \(\sum_{(u \to v) \in E} c_{uv} \max(0, h_v - h_u)\).
    • Naive continuous optimization (LP or gradient descent) is hopeless for \(2.5 \times 10^5\) variables, and dynamic programming across grid columns fails because altitudes can zig-zag non-monotonically across both rows and columns.
  3. Multi-Query Boundary Terminal Cuts ([CSP-S 2021] 交通规划):
    • An \(N \times M\) grid (\(N, M \le 500\)) is queried \(Q \le 50\) times; each query attaches \(k \le 50\) colored sources and sinks around the outer boundary of the grid. Running Dinic \(50\) times on \(2.5 \times 10^5\) vertices is \(50\times\) beyond the time limit, whereas \(k\) runs of Dual Dijkstra exploit the small boundary terminal count \(k \ll V\).
  4. 4-Connectivity vs. 8-Connectivity Grid Barrier Problems (CF 1749 E):
    • The problem asks for the minimum number of cells to block all 4-connected (orthogonal) paths from top to bottom (or left to right). By planar grid topology, a minimal cut of 4-connected primal paths is a simple 8-connected (diagonal-capable) dual barrier from the left boundary to the right boundary.

1.2 Step 2: Euler’s Planar Formula & The Dual Graph \(G^* = (F, E^*)\)

Before introducing cuts and orientations, let us establish the exact combinatorial contract of a plane graph and its geometric dual.

A graph \(G = (V, E)\) is planar if it can be drawn in the plane \(\mathbb{R}^2\) (or on the sphere \(\mathbb{S}^2\)) such that edges intersect only at their endpoints. A concrete such drawing is called a planar embedding (or plane graph). Removing the curves of the edges and vertices of \(G\) from \(\mathbb{R}^2\) partitions the remaining open plane into connected regions called faces, denoted by the set \(F\). Exactly one face is unbounded; we call it the outer (unbounded) face \(f_\infty \in F\), while all other faces \(f \in F \setminus \{f_\infty\}\) are interior (bounded) faces.

ImportantTheorem 1.1 (Euler’s Polyhedral Formula & Sparsity of Planar Graphs)

For any connected plane graph \(G = (V, E)\) with face set \(F\):

\[|V| - |E| + |F| = 2.\]

More generally, if \(G\) has \(c \ge 1\) connected components, then \(|V| - |E| + |F| = 1 + c\). If \(G\) is simple (no self-loops or parallel edges) and \(|V| \ge 3\), every face boundary contains at least \(3\) edge sides, so \(2|E| \ge 3|F|\), which immediately implies:

\[|E| \le 3|V| - 6 \qquad \text{and} \qquad |F| \le 2|V| - 4.\]

Proof: Proceed by induction on \(|E|\) for connected \(G\). - Base case (\(|E| = |V| - 1\)): \(G\) is a tree. A tree contains no cycles, so the plane is not divided and there is only the single unbounded face \(|F| = 1\). Indeed, \(|V| - (|V| - 1) + 1 = 2\). - Inductive step (\(|E| \ge |V|\)): Since \(|E| \ge |V|\), \(G\) contains at least one simple cycle \(C\). By the Jordan Curve Theorem, the simple closed curve \(C\) divides the plane into an interior and an exterior, so any edge \(e \in C\) separates two distinct faces \(f_1 \neq f_2 \in F\). Deleting \(e\) merges \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) into a single face while keeping \(G \setminus \{e\}\) connected. Thus \(|V|\) is unchanged, \(|E|\) decreases by \(1\), and \(|F|\) decreases by \(1\), preserving \(|V| - |E| + |F| = 2\). \(\blacksquare\)

Constructing the Geometric Dual \(G^* = (F, E^*)\)

Given a connected plane graph \(G = (V, E)\) with face set \(F\), its planar dual multigraph \(G^* = (F, E^*)\) is defined mechanically as follows:

  1. Dual Vertices: Place one dual vertex \(f^* \in F\) inside each face \(f\) of \(G\) (including one vertex \(f_\infty^*\) for the unbounded outer face \(f_\infty\)).
  2. Dual Edges: For every primal edge \(e \in E\) that separates face \(f_1\) on one side from face \(f_2\) on the other side, draw a dual edge \(e^* \in E^*\) between \(f_1^*\) and \(f_2^*\) that crosses \(e\) exactly once and crosses no other primal edge. (If \(e\) is a bridge in \(G\) with the same face \(f_1 = f_2\) on both sides, then \(e^*\) is a self-loop at \(f_1^*\).)
  3. Edge Weights: Each dual edge \(e^*\) inherits the exact capacity/weight of its primal counterpart: \(w(e^*) = c(e)\).

Notice the exact dimensions on our two benchmark competitive programming grids:

  • \(N \times M\) Rectangular Grid (\(|V| = NM\) vertices):
    • Horizontal edges: \(N(M - 1)\); Vertical edges: \((N - 1)M\); Total \(|E| = 2NM - N - M\).
    • Interior unit squares: \((N - 1)(M - 1)\) bounded faces, plus \(1\) outer face \(f_\infty\), giving \(|F| = (N - 1)(M - 1) + 1\).
    • Euler check: \(NM - (2NM - N - M) + (NM - N - M + 2) = 2\).
  • \(N \times M\) Triangulated Grid ([BJWC2006] 狼抓兔子, where each unit square is split by a \(\searrow\) diagonal):
    • Each of the \((N - 1)(M - 1)\) unit squares is divided into two triangular faces (lower-left \(F_{r,c,0}\) and upper-right \(F_{r,c,1}\)), giving \(2(N - 1)(M - 1)\) interior dual vertices plus the outer face!

2. Planar Cycle–Cut Duality & Directed \((s, t)\)-Planar Dual Construction

Why does a minimal edge cut in \(G\) become a simple cycle in \(G^*\), and how does that cycle collapse further into a shortest path when \(s\) and \(t\) both lie on the outer boundary? Let us derive both results from first principles, first for undirected graphs and then with full orientation rigor for directed networks.

2.1 Whitney’s Cycle–Cut Orthogonality & Hassin’s \((s, t)\)-Planar Bijection

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be a connected plane graph. A non-empty edge set \(C \subseteq E\) is called a bond (or minimal edge-cut) of \(G\) if removing \(C\) increases the number of connected components of \(G\), but removing any proper subset \(C' \subsetneq C\) does not. Equivalently, \(C = \delta(V_S, V_T)\) where both induced subgraphs \(G[V_S]\) and \(G[V_T]\) are connected.

ImportantTheorem 2.1 (Whitney 1932 — Planar Bond \(\leftrightarrow\) Simple Dual Cycle Bijection)

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be a connected plane graph with dual \(G^* = (F, E^*)\). A subset of edges \(C \subseteq E\) is a minimal edge-cut (bond) of \(G\) if and only if the corresponding dual edges \(C^* = \{e^* : e \in C\} \subseteq E^*\) form a simple cycle in \(G^*\).

Proof: 1. (\(\Longleftarrow\) Simple Dual Cycle \(\implies\) Minimal Primal Cut): Let \(C^*\) be a simple cycle in \(G^*\). Geometrically, attaching the curves of the dual edges \(e^* \in C^*\) forms a simple closed Jordan curve \(\Gamma\) in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) that passes only through faces of \(G\) and crosses exclusively the primal edges \(e \in C\). By the Jordan Curve Theorem, \(\Gamma\) partitions \(\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \Gamma\) into an interior open region \(\Omega_{\text{in}}\) and an exterior open region \(\Omega_{\text{out}}\), both containing at least one primal vertex (since \(C^*\) is a simple cycle in \(G^*\) and every dual face of \(G^*\) contains a primal vertex of \(G\)). No path in \(G \setminus C\) can cross \(\Gamma\), so \(C\) disconnects \(V \cap \Omega_{\text{in}}\) from \(V \cap \Omega_{\text{out}}\). If we restore any single edge \(e \in C\), its dual edge \(e^*\) opens a gap in \(\Gamma\), reconnecting \(\Omega_{\text{in}}\) and \(\Omega_{\text{out}}\) into a single connected region; hence \(C\) is a minimal cut (bond). 2. (\(\Longrightarrow\) Minimal Primal Cut \(\implies\) Simple Dual Cycle): Let \(C = \delta(V_S, V_T)\) be a minimal cut in \(G\), so both \(G[V_S]\) and \(G[V_T]\) are connected. Draw a small neighbourhood around the connected planar drawing of \(G[V_S]\); its topological boundary \(\partial \text{Nbhd}(G[V_S])\) is a single simple closed curve (because both \(G[V_S]\) and its complement \(G[V_T]\) are connected!) that crosses precisely the edges of \(\delta(V_S, V_T) = C\). Translating this closed boundary curve into sequences of faces visited and primal edges crossed produces a simple closed walk (cycle) \(C^*\) in \(G^*\). \(\blacksquare\)

From Dual Cycle to Dual Path: Hassin’s Reference Arc \(e_\infty = (t \to s)\)

Now suppose we want the minimum \((s, t)\)-cut in \(G\), and both \(s\) and \(t\) lie on the boundary of the unbounded outer face \(f_\infty\). (Such a network is called an \((s, t)\)-planar network.)

How do we tell which dual cycles \(C^*\) separate \(s\) from \(t\), and how do we avoid searching over cycles altogether? We use Hassin’s (1981) Reference Arc Trick:

  1. Draw the External Reference Arc \(e_\infty = (t \to s)\): Inside the unbounded outer face \(f_\infty\), draw an artificial directed arc \(e_\infty = (t \to s)\) of capacity \(+\infty\) connecting \(t\) back to \(s\) without crossing any edges of \(G\).
  2. Splitting the Outer Face \(f_\infty \mapsto (S^*, T^*)\): Together with the boundary of \(G\), the new arc \(e_\infty\) closes a cycle that splits the old outer face \(f_\infty\) into two distinct faces:
    • One face lies on the left of \(e_\infty\) (bordering one half of the outer perimeter of \(G\) between \(s\) and \(t\));
    • The other face lies on the right of \(e_\infty\) (bordering the complementary half of the outer perimeter of \(G\) between \(s\) and \(t\)). We designate these two new boundary faces as our dual source \(S^*\) and dual sink \(T^*\)!
  3. Why \(s\)-\(t\) Cuts Become \(S^*\)-\(T^*\) Paths: In the augmented plane graph \(\widetilde{G} = G \cup \{e_\infty\}\), a subset of edges \(C \subseteq E\) separates \(s\) from \(t\) in \(G\) if and only if \(C \cup \{e_\infty\}\) disconnects \(s\) from \(t\) in \(\widetilde{G}\). By Theorem 2.1 applied to \(\widetilde{G}\): \[\text{Minimal } s\text{-}t \text{ cut } C \text{ in } G \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad C^* \cup \{e_\infty^*\} \text{ is a simple cycle in } \widetilde{G}^* \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad C^* \text{ is a simple } S^*\text{-}T^* \text{ path in } G^*!\] Deleting the single artificial dual edge \(e_\infty^* = (T^*, S^*)\) from the cycle \(C^* \cup \{e_\infty^*\}\) leaves a simple path \(P^* = C^*\) between \(S^*\) and \(T^*\) in \(G^*\) whose total edge weight is \(\sum_{e^* \in P^*} w(e^*) = \sum_{e \in C} c(e)\)!

2.2 Step 3: Mechanical Compilation of Directed Planar Duals & Reconciling the \(90^\circ\) Rotation Conventions

When the primal graph \(G\) is undirected (as in #113 [BJWC2006] 狼抓兔子), every primal edge \(e = \{u, v\}\) can be crossed in either direction with the same capacity \(c_e\), so every dual edge \(e^* = \{f_1^*, f_2^*\}\) is simply an undirected edge of weight \(c_e\).

What happens when the primal graph \(G\) is directed (as in #114 [SCOI2010] 平面图 and #117 [NOI2010] 海拔), where edge \((u \to v)\) has capacity \(c(u \to v)\) while the reverse edge \((v \to u)\) has a completely different capacity \(c(v \to u) \neq c(u \to v)\)?

In a directed \((s, t)\)-cut \((V_S, V_T)\) with \(s \in V_S\) and \(t \in V_T\):

  • A directed primal arc \(e = (u \to v)\) pays \(c(u \to v)\) if and only if \(u \in V_S\) and \(v \in V_T\).
  • If \(u \in V_T\) and \(v \in V_S\), then \(e = (u \to v)\) points backward from \(V_T\) to \(V_S\) and pays \(0\) (instead, the reverse primal arc \(v \to u\) points from \(V_S\) to \(V_T\) and pays \(c(v \to u)\)!).

Therefore, each directed primal arc \(e = (u \to v)\) must compile to a directed dual arc \(e^*\) of weight \(c(u \to v)\). Should we rotate \(e = (u \to v)\) by \(90^\circ\) Counter-Clockwise (CCW) or \(90^\circ\) Clockwise (CW) to obtain \(e^*\)?

In the competitive programming literature, some editorials state “always rotate \(90^\circ\) CCW”, while others state “always rotate \(90^\circ\) CW”. Memorizing one rotation angle without understanding how it depends on the placement of \(S^*\) and \(T^*\) is a guaranteed recipe for a wrong answer! Let us state the Universal Left/Right Bank Orientation Invariant that makes both conventions 100% transparent and bug-proof.

ImportantTheorem 2.2 (The Universal Left/Right Bank Orientation Invariant for Directed Planar Duals)

Imagine walking in the plane along the directed dual path \(P^*\) from the dual source \(S^*\) to the dual sink \(T^*\). As you walk forward along \(P^*\), the curve of \(P^*\) slices the primal graph \(G\) into two banks: your Left-Hand Bank and your Right-Hand Bank.

Because \(P^*\) separates \(s \in V_S\) from \(t \in V_T\), the entire source side \(V_S\) lies on one bank of \(P^*\) and the entire sink side \(V_T\) lies on the opposite bank:

  1. Convention I (\(V_S\) on Your Left Bank, \(V_T\) on Your Right Bank):
    • On a standard grid with \(s = (1, 1)\) at Top-Left and \(t = (N, M)\) at Bottom-Right, place the dual source \(S^*\) at the Bottom-Left outer face (bordering the Left column and Bottom row) and the dual sink \(T^*\) at the Top-Right outer face (bordering the Top row and Right column).
    • As you walk from \(S^*\) (Bottom-Left) to \(T^*\) (Top-Right), the Top-Left corner \(s\) lies on your LEFT, and the Bottom-Right corner \(t\) lies on your RIGHT.
    • Therefore, a primal directed edge \(e = (u \to v)\) goes from \(V_S\) (Left Bank) to \(V_T\) (Right Bank) if and only if \(e\) crosses your dual step \(e^*\) from Left to Right—which means rotating the primal arrow \(e = (u \to v)\) by \(90^\circ\) Counter-Clockwise (CCW) gives the directed dual arrow \(e^*\): \[\text{East } (\rightarrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CCW}} \text{North } (\uparrow), \quad \text{South } (\downarrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CCW}} \text{East } (\rightarrow), \quad \text{West } (\leftarrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CCW}} \text{South } (\downarrow), \quad \text{North } (\uparrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CCW}} \text{West } (\leftarrow).\]
  2. Convention II (\(V_S\) on Your Right Bank, \(V_T\) on Your Left Bank):
    • Keep \(s = (1, 1)\) at Top-Left and \(t = (N, M)\) at Bottom-Right, but place \(S^*\) at the Top-Right outer face and \(T^*\) at the Bottom-Left outer face.
    • Now, as you walk from \(S^*\) (Top-Right) to \(T^*\) (Bottom-Left), \(s\) (Top-Left) lies on your RIGHT, and \(t\) (Bottom-Right) lies on your LEFT.
    • Consequently, a primal edge \(e = (u \to v)\) from \(V_S\) (Right Bank) to \(V_T\) (Left Bank) crosses your dual step from Right to Left—which means rotating the primal arrow \(e = (u \to v)\) by \(90^\circ\) Clockwise (CW) gives \(e^*\): \[\text{East } (\rightarrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CW}} \text{South } (\downarrow), \quad \text{South } (\downarrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CW}} \text{West } (\leftarrow), \quad \text{West } (\leftarrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CW}} \text{North } (\uparrow), \quad \text{North } (\uparrow) \xrightarrow{90^\circ\text{ CW}} \text{East } (\rightarrow).\]

In both conventions, the length of the shortest directed path from \(S^*\) to \(T^*\) in \(G^*\) equals the exact capacity of the minimum directed \((s, t)\)-cut in \(G\):

\[\text{MaxFlow}_G(s, t) = \text{MinCut}_G(s, t) = \text{ShortestPath}_{G^*}(S^* \leadsto T^*).\]

Notice the exact algebraic symmetry between Convention I and Convention II: swapping \(S^* \leftrightarrow T^*\) reverses the direction in which we traverse the dual path \(P^*\), which reverses every dual arrow \(e^*\) by \(180^\circ\)—precisely transforming a \(90^\circ\) CCW rotation into a \(90^\circ\) CW rotation!

3. Continuous-to-Binary Potential Reduction on Planar Grids ([NOI2010] 海拔)

One of the most striking moments in competitive programming occurs when a problem asks you to optimize over continuous real variables \(h_v \in \mathbb{R}\), yet the optimal solution is secretly a discrete binary \(\{0, 1\}\) minimum cut—which, on a planar grid, collapses further into a single run of Dijkstra’s algorithm.

The canonical flagship of this two-stage reduction is #117 Luogu P2046 — [NOI2010] 海拔 (Altitude).

3.1 Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

In [NOI2010] 海拔, an \((N + 1) \times (N + 1)\) grid of city intersections (\(N \le 500\), so \(|V| = (N+1)^2 \le 251,001\)) has fixed altitude \(h_{0,0} = 0\) at the Top-Left corner \(s = (0, 0)\) and fixed altitude \(h_{N,N} = 1\) at the Bottom-Right corner \(t = (N, N)\). Every pair of adjacent intersections \((u, v)\) is connected by two directed one-way streets \(u \to v\) and \(v \to u\) with asymmetric daily traffic volumes \(w(u \to v) \ge 0\) and \(w(v \to u) \ge 0\).

  • Traveling downhill or flat (\(h_v \le h_u\)) consumes \(0\) mechanical energy.
  • Traveling uphill (\(h_v > h_u\)) along \(u \to v\) consumes energy proportional to the altitude gain: \(w(u \to v) \cdot (h_v - h_u)\).
  • Task: Assign a real (or integer) altitude \(h_v \in \mathbb{R}\) to every intersection \(v \in V\) (with \(h_{0,0} = 0, h_{N,N} = 1\)) to minimize the total uphill energy cost:

\[\Phi(\mathbf{h}) = \sum_{(u \to v) \in E} w(u \to v) \cdot \max(0, h_v - h_u).\]

What diagnostic signals tell us how to attack \(\Phi(\mathbf{h})\)?

  1. Piecewise-Linear Convexity of \(\max(0, h_v - h_u)\): Each term \(\max(0, h_v - h_u)\) is a positively homogeneous of degree \(1\), convex function of the difference \(h_v - h_u\). Whenever an objective on a graph is a positive linear combination of \(\max(0, h_v - h_u)\) with boundary pins \(h_s = 0\) and \(h_t = 1\), we are looking at the Lovász Extension of the directed minimum-cut function!
  2. Why Continuous / Profile DP Fails: Because traffic flows in all four compass directions (North, South, East, West), optimal altitudes do not necessarily increase monotonically with row \(r\) or column \(c\) if we try to sweep a profile, and \((N+1)^2 = 2.5 \times 10^5\) variables rule out both general LP solvers and Dinic’s algorithm.

3.2 Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic & Variational Representation (The Coarea Formula)

Let us prove from first principles why allowing fractional altitudes \(h_v \in \mathbb{R}\) never beats restricting every intersection to the binary set \(h_v \in \{0, 1\}\).

ImportantTheorem 3.1 (Coarea / Layer-Cake Continuous-to-Binary Potential Reduction)

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be any directed graph with non-negative edge weights \(w(u \to v) \ge 0\) and two distinct terminals \(s, t \in V\). Then the continuous variational minimum over \(\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^V\) equals the discrete binary minimum \((s, t)\)-cut:

\[\min_{\substack{\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^V \\ h_s = 0, \, h_t = 1}} \sum_{(u \to v) \in E} w(u \to v) \max(0, h_v - h_u) \;=\; \min_{\substack{(V_S, V_T) \\ s \in V_S, \, t \in V_T}} \sum_{\substack{(u \to v) \in E \\ u \in V_S, \, v \in V_T}} w(u \to v) \;=\; \text{MinCut}_G(s, t).\]

Proof: We proceed in three elementary steps:

  1. Step A — Clamping \(\mathbb{R}^V\) to the Unit Cube \([0, 1]^V\): For any \(\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^V\) with \(h_s = 0\) and \(h_t = 1\), define the clamped vector \(\tilde{h}_v = \min(1, \max(0, h_v)) \in [0, 1]\). Since the map \(z \mapsto \min(1, \max(0, z))\) is a \(1\)-Lipschitz non-decreasing contraction on \(\mathbb{R}\), for every directed edge \((u \to v) \in E\) we have: \[0 \le \max(0, \tilde{h}_v - \tilde{h}_u) \le \max(0, h_v - h_u).\] Since \(w(u \to v) \ge 0\), clamping never increases \(\Phi(\mathbf{h})\) and preserves \(\tilde{h}_s = 0, \tilde{h}_t = 1\). Thus we may restrict \(\mathbf{h} \in [0, 1]^V\).

  2. Step B — The Coarea (Layer-Cake) Integral Decomposition: For any threshold \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), define the binary level-set indicator vector \(\mathbf{x}(\lambda) \in \{0, 1\}^V\) by: \[x_v(\lambda) = [h_v < \lambda] = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } h_v < \lambda \quad (v \in V_S(\lambda)), \\ 0 & \text{if } h_v \ge \lambda \quad (v \in V_T(\lambda)). \end{cases}\] Because \(h_s = 0 < \lambda \le 1 = h_t\) for every \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\), the partition \(\big(V_S(\lambda), V_T(\lambda)\big)\) is always a valid \((s, t)\)-cut with \(s \in V_S(\lambda)\) and \(t \in V_T(\lambda)\)! Now look at a single directed edge \((u \to v) \in E\). For any real numbers \(h_u, h_v \in [0, 1]\):

    • If \(h_v \le h_u\), then for no \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\) do we have \(h_u < \lambda \le h_v\), so \(\int_0^1 [h_u < \lambda \le h_v]\,d\lambda = 0 = \max(0, h_v - h_u)\).
    • If \(h_v > h_u\), then \([h_u < \lambda \le h_v] = 1\) precisely on the half-open interval \(\lambda \in (h_u, h_v]\) of length \(h_v - h_u\), and \(0\) elsewhere! Therefore, in both cases, we have the exact Coarea Identity: \[\max(0, h_v - h_u) = \int_0^1 \big[h_u < \lambda \le h_v\big]\,d\lambda = \int_0^1 x_u(\lambda)\big(1 - x_v(\lambda)\big)\,d\lambda.\]

  3. Step C — Exchanging Sum and Integral (Convex Averaging over Level Cuts): Multiply by \(w(u \to v)\), sum over all \((u \to v) \in E\), and interchange the finite sum with the integral: \[\begin{aligned} \Phi(\mathbf{h}) &= \sum_{(u \to v) \in E} w(u \to v) \int_0^1 x_u(\lambda)\big(1 - x_v(\lambda)\big)\,d\lambda \\ &= \int_0^1 \underbrace{\left( \sum_{(u \to v) \in E} w(u \to v)\, x_u(\lambda)\big(1 - x_v(\lambda)\big) \right)}_{\text{cap}_G(V_S(\lambda), V_T(\lambda)) \;\ge\; \text{MinCut}_G(s, t)} d\lambda \\ &\ge \int_0^1 \text{MinCut}_G(s, t)\,d\lambda = \text{MinCut}_G(s, t). \end{aligned}\] Conversely, if \((V_S^*, V_T^*)\) is a minimum binary \((s, t)\)-cut in \(G\), setting \(h_v^* = 0\) for all \(v \in V_S^*\) and \(h_v^* = 1\) for all \(v \in V_T^*\) belongs to \([0, 1]^V\) and achieves \(\Phi(\mathbf{h}^*) = \text{MinCut}_G(s, t)\)! \(\blacksquare\)

NoteConnection to Lovász Extension & LP Total Unimodularity

Theorem 3.1 can be viewed through two complementary lenses that recur throughout Part IV and Part V:

  • Submodular Convexity (Lovász 1983): For any set function \(f: 2^V \to \mathbb{R}\) with \(f(\emptyset) = f(V) = 0\), its Lovász extension \(\hat{f}(\mathbf{y}) = \int_0^1 f(\{v : y_v \ge \lambda\})\,d\lambda\) is convex on \([0, 1]^V\) if and only if \(f\) is submodular, and its minimum over \([0, 1]^V\) always coincides with its minimum over \(\{0, 1\}^V\).
  • LP Duality: Minimizing \(\sum_{e} w_e \eta_e\) subject to \(\eta_{uv} \ge h_v - h_u\), \(\eta_{uv} \ge 0\), \(h_s = 0, h_t = 1\) is the exact linear programming dual of the Maximum \((s, t)\)-Flow LP! Since the constraint matrix is the node-arc incidence matrix of \(G\) (which is Totally Unimodular, Chapter 21), every basic optimal solution \((h^*, \eta^*)\) is integral: \(h_v^* \in \{0, 1\}\).

3.3 Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Two-Stage Compilation to Dual Dijkstra)

Combining Theorem 3.1 (Section 3.2) with Theorem 2.2 (Section 2.2), our compilation pipeline for [NOI2010] 海拔 is completely mechanical:

\[\underbrace{\min_{\mathbf{h} \in [0, 1]^V} \sum_{(u \to v)} w_{uv} \max(0, h_v - h_u)}_{\text{Continuous Potential Problem}} \;\xrightarrow{\text{Thm 3.1 (Coarea)}}\; \underbrace{\text{MinCut}_G\big((0,0), (N,N)\big)}_{\text{Directed }(s,t)\text{-Planar Cut}} \;\xrightarrow{\text{Thm 2.2 (90}^\circ\text{ CCW)}}\; \underbrace{\text{ShortestPath}_{G^*}(S^* \leadsto T^*)}_{O(N^2 \log N)\text{ Dijkstra}}.\]

Specifically, on the \((N+1) \times (N+1)\) vertex grid (\(0 \le r, c \le N\)):

  • When \(u \in V_S\) has altitude \(h_u = 0\) and \(v \in V_T\) has altitude \(h_v = 1\), a traveler walking along the directed street \(u \to v\) climbs from altitude \(0\) to altitude \(1\) (\(\max(0, h_v - h_u) = 1\)) and pays \(w(u \to v)\).
  • A traveler walking along the reverse street \(v \to u\) descends from altitude \(1\) to altitude \(0\) (\(\max(0, h_u - h_v) = 0\)) and pays \(0\).
  • Thus the directed capacity of \(u \to v\) in the cut network is literally the input traffic volume \(w(u \to v)\)! We apply Convention I (\(S^*\) at Bottom-Left, \(T^*\) at Top-Right, \(90^\circ\) CCW rotation) on the \(N^2\) interior square faces and run a single Dijkstra in \(O(N^2 \log N)\) time.

3.4 Step 4: Hand-Checkable Numerical Trace of Level-Set Decomposition

Let us verify the Coarea Formula (Theorem 3.1) by hand on a \(4\)-vertex graph \(V = \{s, a, b, t\}\) with edges \(s \xrightarrow{5} a\), \(a \xrightarrow{2} b\), \(b \xrightarrow{6} t\), \(s \xrightarrow{4} b\), and \(a \xrightarrow{3} t\).

Suppose a contestant proposes the fractional altitude assignment:

\[h_s = 0.0, \qquad h_a = 0.4, \qquad h_b = 0.7, \qquad h_t = 1.0.\]

  2. Coarea Level-Set Decomposition Across \(\lambda \in (0, 1]\): As \(\lambda\) sweeps from \(0\) to \(1\), the level set \(V_S(\lambda) = \{v : h_v < \lambda\}\) takes three constant binary cut configurations on the intervals \((0.0, 0.4]\), \((0.4, 0.7]\), and \((0.7, 1.0]\):
    • Slice 1: \(\lambda \in (0.0, 0.4]\) (width \(\Delta \lambda_1 = 0.4\)): \(V_S(\lambda) = \{s\}\), \(V_T(\lambda) = \{a, b, t\}\). Cut capacity \(= w(s \to a) + w(s \to b) = 5 + 4 = 9\).
    • Slice 2: \(\lambda \in (0.4, 0.7]\) (width \(\Delta \lambda_2 = 0.3\)): \(V_S(\lambda) = \{s, a\}\), \(V_T(\lambda) = \{b, t\}\). Cut capacity \(= w(a \to b) + w(a \to t) + w(s \to b) = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9\).
    • Slice 3: \(\lambda \in (0.7, 1.0]\) (width \(\Delta \lambda_3 = 0.3\)): \(V_S(\lambda) = \{s, a, b\}\), \(V_T(\lambda) = \{t\}\). Cut capacity \(= w(b \to t) + w(a \to t) = 6 + 3 = 9\).
    • Coarea Integral Sum: \[\int_0^1 \text{cap}_G\big(V_S(\lambda), V_T(\lambda)\big)\,d\lambda = 0.4 \times 9 + 0.3 \times 9 + 0.3 \times 9 = \mathbf{9.0} = \Phi(\mathbf{h})!\] And since \(\Delta \lambda_1 + \Delta \lambda_2 + \Delta \lambda_3 = 1\), this weighted average of binary cuts can never be strictly smaller than the best single binary cut!

4. Multi-Terminal Planar Boundary Cuts ([CSP-S 2021] 交通规划) — Dual Boundary Splits & Non-Crossing Interval DP

In Sections 2 and 3, our planar network had only one source \(s\) and one sink \(t\) on the outer boundary, splitting the outer face \(f_\infty\) into \(2\) boundary dual vertices \((S^*, T^*)\) connected by a single dual shortest path.

What happens when \(k\) sources and sinks (with \(k \le 50\)) sit around the outer perimeter of an \(N \times M\) planar grid (\(N, M \le 500\)), as in #119 Luogu P7916 — [CSP-S 2021] 交通规划 (Traffic Planning)?

If we wire all White (\(S\)) boundary terminals to a super-source \(S_{\text{sup}}\) and all Black (\(T\)) boundary terminals to a super-sink \(T_{\text{sup}}\), the resulting graph is no longer planar whenever White and Black terminals alternate (W - B - W - B) around the perimeter (by Kuratowski’s \(K_{3,3} / K_5\) obstruction, connecting alternating perimeter vertices to two external hubs forces crossings!). Yet the underlying grid is planar, and all terminals still lie on the boundary of \(f_\infty\). Let us see how planar duality transforms this multi-terminal cut into a Non-Crossing Circular Matching on the dual boundary.

4.1 Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

  1. Multiple Boundary Terminals (\(k \le 50\)) on a Massive Grid (\(N, M \le 500\)):
    • The interior graph is a static \(N \times M\) planar grid (\(V = 2.5 \times 10^5\)), and \(Q \le 50\) queries each attach \(k \le 50\) colored degree-\(1\) terminals around the outer boundary.
    • Because \(k \le 50\) is small while \(NM = 2.5 \times 10^5\) is large, any algorithm whose runtime scales as \(O(k \cdot NM \log(NM) + k^3)\) per query will easily pass!
  2. Color Alternation Around the Perimeter Cycle:
    • Walking clockwise around the perimeter of the grid, terminals are colored \(c_r \in \{0 (\text{White}/S), 1 (\text{Black}/T)\}\). Whenever two adjacent terminals have opposite colors (\(c_r \neq c_{r+1}\)), any valid cut separating White from Black must drive a cut boundary into the grid between terminal \(r\) and terminal \(r+1\).

4.2 Step 2: Intermediate Topological & Algebraic Representation

Let the \(k\) boundary terminals be ordered clockwise around the perimeter of the grid as \(T_1, T_2, \dots, T_k\) (with \(T_{k+1} \equiv T_1\)), where terminal \(T_r\) is attached to a perimeter grid vertex by a stem edge \(e_r\) of capacity \(w(e_r)\) and has color \(c_r \in \{0, 1\}\).

1. Splitting the Outer Face \(f_\infty\) into \(k\) Boundary Intervals \(b_1^*, \dots, b_k^*\)

Extend the \(k\) terminal stem edges \(e_1, \dots, e_k\) outward to infinity across the unbounded face \(f_\infty\). These \(k\) rays partition the outer face \(f_\infty\) into \(k\) clockwise boundary interval faces \(b_1^*, b_2^*, \dots, b_k^*\), where:

  • Boundary interval \(b_r^*\) lies on the perimeter between terminal \(T_r\) and terminal \(T_{r+1}\).
  • In the dual graph \(G^*\), the adjacent boundary intervals \(b_{r-1}^*\) and \(b_r^*\) are separated by the stem edge \(e_r\) of terminal \(T_r\), so they are connected by a dual edge \(e_r^* = (b_{r-1}^*, b_r^*)\) of weight \(w(e_r)\).
  • Every perimeter edge \(e\) of the \(N \times M\) grid that lies between \(T_r\) and \(T_{r+1}\) separates an interior grid face \(F_{\text{in}}\) from the boundary interval \(b_r^*\), creating a dual edge \((F_{\text{in}}, b_r^*)\) of weight \(w(e)\).

2. Defect Terminals (\(c_r \neq c_{r+1}\)) vs. Monochromatic Stepping Stones (\(c_r = c_{r+1}\))

Now consider any binary cut assignment \(x_v \in \{0, 1\}\) on the grid vertices and terminals where every terminal \(T_r\) is fixed to its color \(x_{T_r} = c_r \in \{0, 1\}\) (with the option to cut the stem edge \(e_r\) if the grid attachment vertex of \(T_r\) chooses the opposite color \(1 - c_r\)).

Around each face \(f^*\) of the augmented plane graph:

  • For every interior grid face \(F_{\text{in}}\), as we walk around the boundary cycle of \(F_{\text{in}}\), the vertex colors \(x_v \in \{0, 1\}\) must change an even number of times (\(0, 2,\) or \(4\) times). Thus every interior face \(F_{\text{in}}\) has even degree in the dual cut edge set \(C^*\)!
  • What about the \(k\) boundary intervals \(b_1^*, \dots, b_k^*\)? As we walk along the grid perimeter of interval \(b_r^*\) from terminal \(T_r\) (color \(c_r\)) to terminal \(T_{r+1}\) (color \(c_{r+1}\)):
    • If \(c_r \neq c_{r+1}\) (Bichromatic / Defect Interval): The colors at the two ends of interval \(b_r^*\) are opposite (\(0\) and \(1\)). Therefore, the vertex colors along the boundary of \(b_r^*\) must switch an odd number of times! In the dual graph \(G^*\), this means the cut edge set \(C^*\) has odd degree at \(b_r^*\) (\(b_r^*\) is an endpoint of a dual cut path!).
    • If \(c_r = c_{r+1}\) (Monochromatic Interval): The colors at the two ends of \(b_r^*\) are identical (\(0\) and \(0\), or \(1\) and \(1\)). Thus the vertex colors along \(b_r^*\) switch an even number of times (\(0\) or \(2\)), so \(b_r^*\) has even degree in \(C^*\) (it is not an endpoint of a dual path, though a dual path may pass through \(b_r^*\): \(1\) edge in, \(1\) edge out!).

Since colors \(c_1, \dots, c_k \in \{0, 1\}\) sit on a closed clockwise cycle, the number of color transitions \(c_r \neq c_{r+1}\) around the perimeter is always an even integer \(2m \le k\). Let the \(2m\) defect boundary intervals in clockwise order be:

\[u_1^*, u_2^*, \dots, u_{2m}^* \in \{b_1^*, \dots, b_k^*\}.\]

ImportantTheorem 4.1 (Multi-Terminal Outerplanar Cut \(\leftrightarrow\) Non-Crossing Dual Matching)

In an outer-boundary multi-terminal planar network with \(2m\) clockwise color-transition defect intervals \(u_1^*, \dots, u_{2m}^*\):

  1. Every minimal multi-terminal cut \(C^*\) in \(G\) decomposes in the dual graph \(G^*\) into \(m\) simple shortest paths connecting the \(2m\) defect vertices \(u_1^*, \dots, u_{2m}^*\) in pairs.
  2. By the Planar Uncrossing Property, there exists an optimal pairing of \(\{u_1^*, \dots, u_{2m}^*\}\) in which no two dual paths cross each other. Consequently, the pairing forms a non-crossing circular matching of \((u_1^*, \dots, u_{2m}^*)\)!

Proof of the Planar Uncrossing Property: Suppose two dual shortest paths \(P_1^*\) (connecting \(u_a^*\) and \(u_c^*\)) and \(P_2^*\) (connecting \(u_b^*\) and \(u_d^*\), with clockwise order \(a < b < c < d\)) cross at some dual vertex \(w^* \in F\). Notice that around the perimeter cycle, the defects alternate in orientation (\(0 \to 1\) at odd indices, \(1 \to 0\) at even indices), so any valid primal \((V_S, V_T)\)-partition induces a boundary of the planar region \(V_S\) whose boundary curves in \(G^*\) are pairwise disjoint (non-crossing) Jordan arcs pairing an odd defect with an even defect! Even algebraically, if \(P_1^*\) and \(P_2^*\) intersect at \(w^*\), we can uncross them into two paths connecting \((u_a^*, u_b^*)\) and \((u_c^*, u_d^*)\) (or \((u_a^*, u_d^*)\) and \((u_b^*, u_c^*)\)) of total weight \(\le w(P_1^*) + w(P_2^*)\) by the triangle inequality! \(\blacksquare\)

4.3 Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (All-Pairs Defect Dijkstra + \(O(k^3)\) Circular Interval DP)

How do we compile Theorem 4.1 into an \(O\big(Q(k NM \log(NM) + k^3)\big)\) algorithm for [CSP-S 2021] 交通规划?

  1. Step 1 — Build the Static Interior Dual Graph Once: The \((N - 1) \times (M - 1)\) interior square faces of the \(N \times M\) grid and the internal dual edges between adjacent interior faces never change across the \(Q\) queries. Build this base graph once before reading the queries.

  2. Step 2 — Per Query: Attach All \(k\) Boundary Intervals \(b_1^*, \dots, b_k^*\): Sort the \(k\) query terminals clockwise around the \(2(N + M)\) perimeter slots. Create \(k\) boundary dual vertices \(b_1^*, \dots, b_k^*\) (even when \(c_r = c_{r+1}\)!):

    • Connect \(b_{r-1}^* \leftrightarrow b_r^*\) with undirected weight \(w(e_r)\) (the capacity of terminal \(T_r\)’s stem edge).
    • Connect every perimeter grid edge between \(T_r\) and \(T_{r+1}\) from its adjacent interior grid face to \(b_r^*\) with weight equal to that perimeter edge’s capacity.

  3. Step 3 — Collect the \(2m\) Defect Vertices & Run \(2m\) Dijkstras: Identify the clockwise subsequence \(u_1^*, \dots, u_{2m}^*\) of boundary vertices \(b_r^*\) where \(c_r \neq c_{r+1}\).

    • If \(m = 0\) (all \(k\) terminals have the same color!), output 0 immediately.
    • Otherwise, run Dijkstra \(2m\) times (once from each \(u_a^*\)) on \(G^*\) to populate the \(2m \times 2m\) shortest-path distance matrix \(D[a][b] = \text{dist}_{G^*}(u_a^*, u_b^*)\).

  4. Step 4 — \(O(m^3)\) Circular Non-Crossing Interval DP: Duplicate the defect array to length \(4m\): \(u_{a + 2m}^* = u_a^*\) for \(1 \le a \le 2m\), with \(D[a][b] = D[(a-1)\bmod 2m + 1][(b-1)\bmod 2m + 1]\). For every even interval length \(L = 2, 4, \dots, 2m\) and every starting index \(l \in [1, 2m]\) (setting \(r = l + L - 1\)):

    \[dp[l][r] = \min\left( D[l][r] + dp[l + 1][r - 1], \;\; \min_{\substack{p = l + 1 \\ (p - l)\text{ odd}}}^{r - 2} \big( dp[l][p] + dp[p + 1][r] \big) \right).\]

    Finally, the minimum multi-terminal cut is the best full-circle window of length \(2m\):

    \[\text{MinCut} = \min_{1 \le l \le 2m} dp[l][l + 2m - 1].\]

4.4 Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & The Monochromatic Stepping-Stone Trap

Why did we emphasize in Step 2 that monochromatic boundary intervals \(b_r^*\) (\(c_r = c_{r+1}\)) must remain as vertices in \(G^*\) even though they are not endpoints in the Interval DP? Let us trace a concrete \(1 \times 2\) grid counterexample where merging same-colored terminals or deleting monochromatic intervals gives a Wrong Answer.

          Terminal 1 (White, cap=100)      Terminal 2 (White, cap=1)
                    |                                |

  • \(T_1\): White (\(c_1 = 0\)), stem capacity \(100\).
  • \(T_2\): White (\(c_2 = 0\)), stem capacity \(1\).
  • \(T_3\): Black (\(c_3 = 1\)), stem capacity \(100\).
  • \(T_4\): Black (\(c_4 = 1\)), stem capacity \(100\).

What is the true minimum cut separating White \(\{T_1, T_2\}\) from Black \(\{T_3, T_4\}\)?

How does the dual graph \(G^*\) find this cut of weight \(51\)?

  • The \(4\) boundary intervals are:
    • \(b_1^*\) (between \(T_1\) and \(T_2\), \(W\)-\(W\)): Monochromatic stepping stone.
    • \(b_2^*\) (between \(T_2\) and \(T_3\), \(W\)-\(B\)): Defect \(u_1^*\).
    • \(b_3^*\) (between \(T_3\) and \(T_4\), \(B\)-\(B\)): Monochromatic stepping stone.
    • \(b_4^*\) (between \(T_4\) and \(T_1\), \(B\)-\(W\)): Defect \(u_2^*\).
  • Because \(b_1^*\) is kept as a vertex in \(G^*\) connected to \(b_2^*\) via \(T_2\)’s stem edge of weight \(1\), the shortest path in \(G^*\) from defect \(u_1^* = b_2^*\) to defect \(u_2^* = b_4^*\) takes the route: \[u_1^* (b_2^*) \xrightarrow{\text{stem } T_2 \text{ (cost } 1\text{)}} b_1^* \xrightarrow{\text{enter } F_1 \text{ & exit to } b_4^* \text{ (cost } 50\text{)}} u_2^* (b_4^*),\] achieving the exact optimal weight \(1 + 50 = 51\)!

Without \(b_1^*\), the stem edge \(e_2^*\) of weight \(1\) disappears from \(G^*\), forcing the path \(u_1^* \leadsto u_2^*\) to travel through the interior of \(F_2\) and \(F_1\) at cost \(50 + 50 = \mathbf{100} \neq 51\)!

5. General Non-\((s, t)\)-Planar Cuts — Reif’s Cut-Path Duality (\(O(V \log^2 V)\)) & \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-Homology Double Covers

In Sections 2–4, every source and sink lay on the boundary of the outer face \(f_\infty\). What happens when \(s\) and \(t\) lie strictly in the interior of a general planar graph \(G = (V, E)\) (\(V \le 10^5\)), and no single face contains both \(s\) and \(t\) on its boundary?

If we attempt Hassin’s trick of adding a reference arc \(e_\infty = (t \to s)\), the new arc \(e_\infty\) must cross existing edges of \(G\), destroying planarity! Nevertheless, Whitney’s theorem (Theorem 2.1) still holds: a minimal \((s, t)\)-cut \(C \subseteq E\) in \(G\) corresponds to a minimum-weight simple cycle \(C^*\) in \(G^*\) that topologically separates the face-neighborhood of \(s\) from the face-neighborhood of \(t\) (a separating dual cycle). How do we find a minimum-weight \(s\)-\(t\) separating cycle in \(G^*\) without Dinic’s algorithm?

5.1 Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

  1. General Planar Graph with Interior \((s, t)\) and \(V, E \in [3 \cdot 10^4, 2 \cdot 10^5]\):
    • The problem either gives a planar embedding directly (faces or clockwise adjacency lists) or a Concentric / Radial / Triangulated mesh where \(s\) is near the center (e.g., an island capital or inner ring) and \(t\) is on the outer ring (or another interior node).
    • Because \(s\) and \(t\) do not share a face, there is no single \((S^*, T^*)\) pair of split boundary faces. Instead, any cut separating \(s\) and \(t\) is a closed Jordan curve in the dual graph enclosing \(s\) and excluding \(t\).

5.2 Step 2: Intermediate Topological & Algebraic Representation

Let \(P = (s = v_0 \xrightarrow{e_1} v_1 \xrightarrow{e_2} \dots \xrightarrow{e_L} v_L = t)\) be any fixed simple path from \(s\) to \(t\) in the primal graph \(G\) (for instance, the unweighted BFS shortest path, computable in \(O(V)\) time).

1. The \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-Intersection Number Characterization of Separating Dual Cycles

When does a simple cycle \(C^*\) in the dual graph \(G^*\) separate \(s\) from \(t\) in the plane \(\mathbb{R}^2\)?

By the Jordan Curve Theorem, a closed curve \(C^*\) in \(\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{s, t\}\) separates \(s\) (inside \(C^*\)) from \(t\) (outside \(C^*\)) if and only if any continuous curve \(P\) from \(s\) to \(t\) crosses \(C^*\) an odd number of times:

\[\big| C^* \cap P^* \big| \equiv 1 \pmod 2,\]

where \(P^* = \{e_1^*, e_2^*, \dots, e_L^*\}\) is the set of dual edges crossing the primal path \(P\).

2. Reif’s Minimum-Path Uncrossing Lemma (1983)

Can a minimum-weight \(s\)-\(t\) separating cycle \(C^*\) cross the shortest primal path \(P\) multiple times (say, \(3\) or \(5\) times)? Reif (1983) proved that if \(P\) is chosen to be a minimum-weight (or shortest) \((s, t)\)-path in the dual/primal sense—or even more simply, if we slice \(G^*\) open along any simple primal \((s, t)\)-path \(P\) for undirected planar graphs—there exists an optimal minimum \((s, t)\)-cut \(C^*\) that crosses \(P\) at most once (and since \(|C^* \cap P^*|\) is odd, exactly once)!

Let us verify why: suppose an undirected minimal cut \(C\) induces a vertex partition \((V_S, V_T)\) with \(s \in V_S\) and \(t \in V_T\). If \(P\) is a simple primal path from \(s\) to \(t\), what happens if \(P\) crosses the cut \(C\) three times (\(s \in V_S \to V_T \to V_S \to V_T \ni t\))? Wait—a path \(P\) in \(G\) can cross the cut \(C\) more than once if \(V_S\) wraps around a “peninsula” of \(P\). However, if we inspect the \(L\) faces adjacent to \(P\) and slice the dual graph \(G^*\) along the primal path \(P\), every dual edge \(e_i^* \in P^*\) (\(1 \le i \le L\)) is split into two copies:

  • a Left-Bank terminal \(e_{i, L}^*\) on the left side of \(P\) (walking \(s \to t\)), and
  • a Right-Bank terminal \(e_{i, R}^*\) on the right side of \(P\).

If a separating dual cycle \(C^*\) crosses \(P\) at some edge \(e_i^*\), cutting \(C^*\) open at \(e_i^*\) yields a dual path in the sliced graph \(G^* \setminus P^*\) from \(e_{i, L}^*\) to \(e_{i, R}^*\)! Better still: in the sliced graph \(G^* \setminus P^*\), what is the shortest path between \(e_{i, L}^*\) and \(e_{i, R}^*\)?

ImportantTheorem 5.1 (Reif’s Cut-Path Duality & Non-Crossing Property, 1983)

Let \(G = (V, E, w)\) be an undirected planar network, let \(P = (e_1, \dots, e_L)\) be a simple \((s, t)\)-path in \(G\), and let \(G_{\text{slice}}^*\) be the planar dual graph obtained by cutting \(G^*\) open along \(P^*\) so that each dual edge \(e_i^* \in P^*\) has a Left endpoint \(e_{i, L}^*\) and a Right endpoint \(e_{i, R}^*\) (including the weight \(w(e_i)\) once on the transition across \(P\)). Then:

  1. Exact Reduction to Same-Index Bank-to-Bank Shortest Paths: \[\text{MinCut}_G(s, t) = \min_{1 \le i \le L} \text{dist}_{G_{\text{slice}}^*}\big(e_{i, L}^*, e_{i, R}^*\big).\] (Even if the global shortest cycle \(C^*\) crosses \(P^*\) multiple times, say at \(i_1, i_2, i_3\), its segments between consecutive crossings plus the \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) parity of crossings guarantee that at least one single-index cycle through \(e_i^*\) closes with weight \(\le w(C^*)\)!)
  2. Monotonicity / Non-Crossing of Optimal Cycles (\(i < j\)): There exists a family of shortest paths \(C_i^* = \text{ShortestPath}_{G_{\text{slice}}^*}(e_{i, L}^*, e_{i, R}^*)\) for \(i = 1, \dots, L\) such that for all \(1 \le i < j \le L\), \(C_i^*\) and \(C_j^*\) do not cross in \(G_{\text{slice}}^*\). Consequently, \(C_{\text{mid}}^*\) splits \(G_{\text{slice}}^*\) into two independent subgraphs containing \(\{1, \dots, \text{mid}-1\}\) and \(\{\text{mid}+1, \dots, L\}\).

5.3 Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Two Algorithmic Compilations)

Depending on whether you want the simplest possible implementation using a \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-Parity Double Cover or the guaranteed \(O(V \log^2 V)\) Divide-and-Conquer bound, Theorem 5.1 gives two concrete compilations:

Compilation A: Reif’s \(O(V \log^2 V)\) Divide-and-Conquer Dijkstra (Guaranteed General Undirected Planar)

  1. Find any simple \((s, t)\)-path \(P = (e_1, \dots, e_L)\) in \(G\) via BFS in \(O(V)\) time.
  2. Construct the sliced dual graph \(G_{\text{slice}}^*\) by cutting along the primal path \(P\): each dual edge \(e_i^*\) (\(1 \le i \le L\)) becomes a pair of portal vertices \((e_{i, L}^*, e_{i, R}^*)\).
  3. Execute Solve(l, r, H^*) on the active range \([l, r]\) of indices and active subgraph \(H^* \subseteq G_{\text{slice}}^*\):
    • Let \(m = \lfloor (l + r) / 2 \rfloor\).
    • Run Dijkstra from \(e_{m, L}^*\) to \(e_{m, R}^*\) inside \(H^*\) in \(O(|H^*| \log |H^*|)\) time, recording \(\text{ans} = \min(\text{ans}, \text{dist}_{H^*}(e_{m, L}^*, e_{m, R}^*))\).
    • Extract the shortest path \(C_m^*\) between \(e_{m, L}^*\) and \(e_{m, R}^*\). Because \(H^*\) is planar and \(C_m^*\) connects the left and right banks at index \(m\), slicing \(H^*\) along \(C_m^*\) partitions \(H^*\) into two subgraphs \(H_{\text{left}}^*\) (containing portals \(l, \dots, m - 1\)) and \(H_{\text{right}}^*\) (containing portals \(m + 1, \dots, r\)) that share only the vertices on \(C_m^*\).
    • Recurse on Solve(l, m - 1, H_left^*) and Solve(m + 1, r, H_right^*).
  4. Complexity: Because each vertex of \(C_m^*\) is duplicated at most once into both halves (and we can contract or prune redundant bank portals), the total graph size across each level of the recursion tree of depth \(\lceil \log_2 L \rceil\) is \(O(V)\), giving \(O(V \log^2 V)\) total time!

Compilation B: The \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-Homology Double Cover \(G^* \times \{0, 1\}\) (When \(P\) is a Geodesic Dual Cut)

There is an even simpler 10-line trick that contestants often ask about:

“If a separating cycle \(C^*\) is just a closed walk in \(G^*\) that crosses \(P^*\) an odd number of times, why not create a 2-layer parity graph \(G^* \times \{0, 1\}\) where every dual edge \(e^* \notin P^*\) connects \((u^*, p) \leftrightarrow (v^*, p)\) on the same layer \(p \in \{0, 1\}\), and every dual edge \(e_i^* \in P^*\) crosses layers \((u^*, p) \leftrightarrow (v^*, p \oplus 1)\)?”

Let us analyze what a path from \((f^*, 0)\) to \((f^*, 1)\) in \(G^* \times \{0, 1\}\) represents:

  • It is a closed walk in \(G^*\) starting and ending at face \(f^*\) that crosses \(P^*\) an odd number of times!
  • Wait—what is the catch? If \(f^*\) does not lie on the optimal separating cycle \(C^*\), then a walk from \((f^*, 0)\) to \((f^*, 1)\) can still reach \(C^*\) via a “stem” path \(Q^*\) from \(f^*\) to \(C^*\), traverse \(C^*\) once (flipping parity from \(0\) to \(1\)), and walk back along \(Q^*\) to \(f^*\)—which pays \(2 \cdot w(Q^*) + w(C^*)\) instead of \(w(C^*)\)!
  • Therefore, \((f^*, 0) \leadsto (f^*, 1)\) in \(G^* \times \{0, 1\}\) equals \(w(C^*)\) if and only if \(f^*\) lies on \(C^*\)!
  • Since \(C^*\) must cross \(P^*\) at least once, \(C^*\) is guaranteed to contain at least one portal vertex \(e_{i, L}^*\) on \(P^*\). Thus minimizing \(\text{dist}_{G^* \times \{0, 1\}}\big((e_{i, L}^*, 0), (e_{i, L}^*, 1)\big)\) over \(i \in \{1, \dots, L\}\) (using Reif’s divide-and-conquer across \(i \in [1, L]\), or Chalermsook–Fakcharoenphol–Nanongkai’s 2004 negative-cycle binary search where \(P^*\) is chosen as a shortest path in \(G^*\) from a face incident to \(s\) to a face incident to \(t\)) recovers the exact minimum cut!

5.4 Step 4: Hand-Checkable Concentric-Ring Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

Consider a small concentric planar graph where \(s\) is the center vertex of a 3-spoke wheel and \(t\) is the outer ring vertex:

  • Inner ring of 3 vertices \(a, b, c\) around \(s\): edges \((s, a)=5, (s, b)=6, (s, c)=7\), and triangle edges \((a, b)=1, (b, c)=1, (c, a)=1\).
  • Outer spokes from \(a, b, c\) to \(t\): edges \((a, t)=8, (b, t)=2, (c, t)=9\).
  • Primal path \(P\): Choose the 2-edge path \(s \xrightarrow{e_1=(s,b), w=6} b \xrightarrow{e_2=(b,t), w=2} t\).
  • Dual portals along \(P^*\):
    • \(e_1^* = (s, b)^*\) has weight \(6\). A separating cycle using \(e_1^*\) and not \(e_2^*\) must cut \((s, a)=5\) and \((s, c)=7\), paying \(6 + 5 + 7 = 18\), OR cut \((s, a)=5, (a, b)=1\), and \((b, t)=2\) (which crosses both \(e_1^*\) and \(e_2^*\), Wait: cutting \((s, a), (a, b), (b, c), (s, c)\) leaves \(b\) with \(s\) and cuts \(5 + 1 + 1 + 7 = 14\)).
    • \(e_2^* = (b, t)^*\) has weight \(2\). A separating cycle through \(e_2^*\) can cut \((b, t)=2, (b, a)=1, (s, a)=5, (s, c)=7, (c, b)=1\) (cost \(16\)), or cut \((b, t)=2, (a, t)=8, (c, t)=9\) (cost \(19\)).
    • Thus \(\min_{i \in \{1, 2\}} \text{dist}_{G_{\text{slice}}^*}(e_{i, L}^*, e_{i, R}^*) = \min(14, 16) = \mathbf{14}\), which matches the true minimum \((s, t)\)-cut \(\{ (s, a), (a, b), (b, c), (s, c) \}\) of capacity \(5 + 1 + 1 + 7 = 14\)!

6.1 [Stepping Stone #113] Luogu P4001 / BZOJ 1001 — [BJWC2006 / ICPC-Beijing 2006] 狼抓兔子 (Wolves Catching Rabbits)

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P4001
  • Constraints: \(N, M \le 1000\), undirected edge weights \(w_e \in [1, 10^6]\). Time Limit: \(1.0\text{ s}\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

We are given an \(N \times M\) grid (\(V \le 10^6\)) with horizontal, vertical, and main-diagonal (\ top-left to bottom-right) edges, totaling \(E \approx 3 \times 10^6\) edges. We must find the minimum number of wolves (minimum edge cut capacity) needed to block all paths of rabbits from the top-left corner \(s = (1, 1)\) to the bottom-right corner \(t = (N, M)\).

Because \(V = 10^6\) and \(E = 3 \times 10^6\), running Dinic’s algorithm directly risks TLE on strict judges, whereas the graph is \((s, t)\)-planar with \(2(N-1)(M-1)\) triangular interior faces!

  1. Degenerate Guard (\(N = 1\) or \(M = 1\)): When \(N = 1\) or \(M = 1\), there are \(0\) interior faces (\(2(N-1)(M-1) = 0\)); the primal graph is just a single path of \(\max(N, M) - 1\) edges, and the minimum cut is simply the minimum edge weight along that path!
  2. Triangulated Dual Graph (\(N, M \ge 2\)): Each grid square \((r, c)\) (\(1 \le r < N, 1 \le c < M\)) is split by its diagonal edge (r, c) -- (r+1, c+1) into two triangular faces:
    • Lower-Left Triangle \(F_{r, c, 0}\): bounded by the left vertical edge, the bottom horizontal edge, and the diagonal edge of cell \((r, c)\).
    • Upper-Right Triangle \(F_{r, c, 1}\): bounded by the top horizontal edge, the right vertical edge, and the diagonal edge of cell \((r, c)\).

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical Graph Compilation)

Using Convention I (\(S^*\) on the Bottom-Left boundary, \(T^*\) on the Top-Right boundary):

  • Super-Source \(S^*\) (id = 0): connected to the left vertical edge of every leftmost lower-left triangle \(F_{r, 1, 0}\) (\(1 \le r < N\)) and the bottom horizontal edge of every bottommost lower-left triangle \(F_{N-1, c, 0}\) (\(1 \le c < M\)).
  • Super-Sink \(T^*\) (id = 2*(N-1)*(M-1) + 1): connected to the top horizontal edge of every topmost upper-right triangle \(F_{1, c, 1}\) (\(1 \le c < M\)) and the right vertical edge of every rightmost upper-right triangle \(F_{r, M-1, 1}\) (\(1 \le r < N\)).

    • Horizontal edge (r, c) -- (r, c+1) (\(2 \le r \le N-1\)): connects \(F_{r-1, c, 0} \leftrightarrow F_{r, c, 1}\).
    • Vertical edge (r, c) -- (r+1, c) (\(2 \le c \le M-1\)): connects \(F_{r, c-1, 1} \leftrightarrow F_{r, c, 0}\).
    • Diagonal edge (r, c) -- (r+1, c+1): connects \(F_{r, c, 0} \leftrightarrow F_{r, c, 1}\).

  • On a \(2 \times 2\) grid (\(N = 2, M = 2\)), there are \(2(1)(1) = 2\) interior triangles: \(F_{1,1,0}\) (connected to \(S^*\) via left vertical and bottom horizontal edges) and \(F_{1,1,1}\) (connected to \(T^*\) via top horizontal and right vertical edges), linked to each other by the diagonal edge of \((1, 1)\).

#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

const long long INF = 1e18;

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);

    int N, M;
    if (!(cin >> N >> M)) return 0;

    // Guard against degenerate 1D grid (N == 1 or M == 1)
    if (N == 1 || M == 1) {
        long long ans = INF, w;
        int cnt = max(N, M) - 1;
        for (int i = 0; i < cnt; ++i) {
            cin >> w;
            ans = min(ans, w);
        }
        cout << (cnt == 0 ? 0 : ans) << "\n";
        return 0;
    }

    int R = N - 1, C = M - 1;
    int S = 0, T = 2 * R * C + 1;
    auto id0 = [&](int r, int c) { return 2 * (r * C + c) + 1; }; // Lower-Left (0-indexed r,c)
    auto id1 = [&](int r, int c) { return 2 * (r * C + c) + 2; }; // Upper-Right

    vector<vector<pair<int, int>>> adj(T + 1);
    auto add_edge = [&](int u, int v, int w) {
        adj[u].push_back({v, w});
        adj[v].push_back({u, w});
    };

    // 1. Horizontal edges: N rows, M-1 per row
    for (int r = 0; r < N; ++r) {
        for (int c = 0; c < C; ++c) {
            int w;
            cin >> w;
            if (r == 0) add_edge(id1(0, c), T, w);
            else if (r == N - 1) add_edge(S, id0(R - 1, c), w);
            else add_edge(id0(r - 1, c), id1(r, c), w);
        }
    }

    // 2. Vertical edges: N-1 rows, M per row
    for (int r = 0; r < R; ++r) {
        for (int c = 0; c < M; ++c) {
            int w;
            cin >> w;
            if (c == 0) add_edge(S, id0(r, 0), w);
            else if (c == M - 1) add_edge(id1(r, C - 1), T, w);
            else add_edge(id1(r, c - 1), id0(r, c), w);
        }
    }

    // 3. Diagonal edges: N-1 rows, M-1 per row
    for (int r = 0; r < R; ++r) {
        for (int c = 0; c < C; ++c) {
            int w;
            cin >> w;
            add_edge(id0(r, c), id1(r, c), w);
        }
    }

    vector<long long> dist(T + 1, INF);
    priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
    dist[S] = 0;
    pq.push({0, S});

    while (!pq.empty()) {
        auto [d, u] = pq.top();
        pq.pop();
        if (d > dist[u]) continue;
        if (u == T) break;
        for (auto [v, w] : adj[u]) {
            if (dist[u] + w < dist[v]) {
                dist[v] = dist[u] + w;
                pq.push({dist[v], v});
            }
        }
    }

    cout << dist[T] << "\n";
    return 0;
}

6.2 [Stepping Stone #114] BZOJ 2007 / Luogu P2046 Prelim — [SCOI2010] 平面图 (Directed Grid Dual) & HDU 3870 (Catch the Theves)

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P2046 (Directed Grid Core) / HDU 3870 (Undirected Square Grid \(N \le 400\))
  • Constraints: \((N+1) \times (N+1)\) vertices (\(N \le 500\)), \(4 N (N+1)\) directed edges (East, West, South, North). Time Limit: \(1.0\text{ s}\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Unlike #113 (where edges are undirected and triangulated), here we have an \((N+1) \times (N+1)\) square grid where every grid segment has two independent directed capacities (East \(w_E\) vs. West \(w_W\), and South \(w_S\) vs. North \(w_N\)). We need the minimum directed \((s, t)\)-cut from top-left \(s = (0, 0)\) to bottom-right \(t = (N, N)\).

(Note: Its undirected cousin HDU 3870 — Catch the Theves is the special case where \(w_E = w_W\) and \(w_S = w_N\) on an \(N \times N\) square grid.)

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic / Topological Representation

Apply Theorem 2.2 (Convention I: \(S^*\) at Bottom-Left, \(T^*\) at Top-Right, \(90^\circ\) Counter-Clockwise rotation). For each interior square cell \(F_{r, c}\) (\(1 \le r, c \le N\)), a directed primal edge \(e = (u \to v)\) compiles to a directed dual arc \(e^* = (f_L \to f_R)\) from the face on the left of \(u \to v\) to the face on the right of \(u \to v\).

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical 4-Direction Compilation)

Using the exact 4-direction table derived in Section 2.3:

  1. East Primal Edge \((r, c-1) \to (r, c)\): Left is above (\(F_{r, c}\) or \(T^*\)), Right is below (\(F_{r+1, c}\) or \(S^*\)) \(\implies\) Dual arc South \(F_{r, c} \to F_{r+1, c}\)? Wait—let us check carefully!
    • Stand at \((r, c-1)\) and look East toward \((r, c)\):
    • Your Left hand points North (into cell \(F_{r, c}\), or \(T^*\) if \(r = 0\)).
    • Your Right hand points South (into cell \(F_{r+1, c}\), or \(S^*\) if \(r = N\)).
    • Wait: if \(u \in V_S\) and \(v \in V_T\), then \(V_S\) is at \((r, c-1)\) (Left of the cut) and \(V_T\) is at \((r, c)\) (Right of the cut). As our dual path walks from \(S^*\) (Bottom-Left) to \(T^*\) (Top-Right), \(V_S\) is on its Left and \(V_T\) is on its Right!
    • Therefore, when the dual path walks North from \(F_{r+1, c}\) (South) to \(F_{r, c}\) (North), its Left hand is \((r, c-1) \in V_S\) and its Right hand is \((r, c) \in V_T\)!
    • Thus an East primal edge \((r, c-1) \to (r, c)\) is forward-cut when the dual path steps North: \(F_{r+1, c} \to F_{r, c}\) (\(90^\circ\) CCW rotation of East is North!).
  2. Summary of \(90^\circ\) CCW Dual Arcs (\(S^* \leadsto T^*\)):
    • East primal edge \((r, c-1) \to (r, c)\) \(\xrightarrow{90^\circ \text{ CCW}}\) North dual arc \(F_{r+1, c} \to F_{r, c}\) (with \(F_{N+1, c} \equiv S^*\) and \(F_{0, c} \equiv T^*\)).
    • South primal edge \((r-1, c) \to (r, c)\) \(\xrightarrow{90^\circ \text{ CCW}}\) East dual arc \(F_{r, c} \to F_{r, c+1}\) (with \(F_{r, 0} \equiv S^*\) and \(F_{r, N+1} \equiv T^*\)).
    • West primal edge \((r, c) \to (r, c-1)\) \(\xrightarrow{90^\circ \text{ CCW}}\) South dual arc \(F_{r, c} \to F_{r+1, c}\).
    • North primal edge \((r, c) \to (r-1, c)\) \(\xrightarrow{90^\circ \text{ CCW}}\) West dual arc \(F_{r, c+1} \to F_{r, c}\).

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

Look at how clean the \(90^\circ\) CCW rule is:

  • Every East primal edge becomes a North dual edge (\(S^* \to \dots \to T^*\)).
  • Every South primal edge becomes an East dual edge (\(S^* \to \dots \to T^*\)).
  • Since \(s = (0, 0)\) is Top-Left and \(t = (N, N)\) is Bottom-Right, all forward primal flow goes East and South, so all forward-cut dual edges go North and East—straight from \(S^*\) (Bottom-Left) to \(T^*\) (Top-Right)! If you ever see an East primal edge pointing away from \(T^*\) toward \(S^*\), your rotation is backwards (FM-15A)!

6.3 [Intermediate #115] Codeforces 1749E — Cactus Wall

  • Judge URL: https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/1749/E
  • Constraints: \(\sum N \cdot M \le 2 \cdot 10^5\). Grid of . (empty) and # (existing cactus). Time Limit: \(2.0\text{ s}\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

We want to place the minimum number of new cacti # on empty cells so that no 4-connected path of . cells exists from the top row (\(r = 1\)) to the bottom row (\(r = N\)), subject to the independent-set constraint that no two cacti may share a side (no 4-adjacent # cells!).

Notice that this is a minimum vertex cut on an \(N \times M\) grid where selected cut vertices must form an independent set! Vertex-splitting + Dinic is completely impossible because the independent-set constraint between cut vertices is non-submodular on a general cut. Why does planar duality solve it in \(O(NM)\) time?

Step 2: Intermediate Topological & Algebraic Representation

  1. 4-Connected Primal Path \(\Longleftrightarrow\) 8-Connected Dual Barrier (Digital Topology Duality): By discrete Jordan curve duality on a 2D pixel grid, blocking all 4-connected (orthogonal) paths of . from row \(1\) to row \(N\) is equivalent to building an 8-connected (orthogonal + diagonal King’s move) barrier of # cells from column \(1\) (\(c = 1\)) to column \(M\) (\(c = M\))!
  2. How the Independent-Set Rule Simplifies 8-Connectivity to Pure Diagonal Moves:
    • In our barrier of # cells from column \(1\) to column \(M\), no two # cells on the grid may be 4-adjacent (orthogonally adjacent).
    • Therefore, our 8-connected barrier cannot take orthogonal steps! Every step along the barrier from (r, c) to the next cactus must be one of the 4 diagonal moves (r ± 1, c ± 1).
  3. The Chessboard Parity Invariant (\((r + c) \bmod 2\)): Wait: if we find a shortest purely diagonal path of valid cells (cells not orthogonally adjacent to any pre-existing #) from column \(1\) to column \(M\), could two non-consecutive vertices on this diagonal path accidentally be 4-adjacent to each other?
    • No! Every diagonal move \((r \pm 1, c \pm 1)\) changes both \(r\) and \(c\) by \(\pm 1\), which preserves the chessboard color parity \((r + c) \bmod 2\)!
    • Thus every cell on a diagonal path has the exact same parity \((r + c) \bmod 2\), whereas any two 4-adjacent cells on a grid have opposite parities!
    • Consequently, any simple diagonal path of valid cells from column \(1\) to column \(M\) is automatically free of internal 4-adjacencies!

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical 0-1 BFS Compilation)

  1. Mark a cell \((r, c)\) as valid (valid[r][c] = true) if neither \((r, c)\) nor any of its 4 orthogonal neighbors \((r \pm 1, c), (r, c \pm 1)\) contains a pre-existing # (wait: \((r, c)\) itself can contain a pre-existing #—since the input guarantees no two pre-existing # are 4-adjacent, a pre-existing # has no orthogonal # neighbors and costs \(0\) to use!).
    • Specifically: \((r, c)\) is forbidden if and only if at least one of its 4 orthogonal neighbors \((r \pm 1, c), (r, c \pm 1)\) already has a #.
  2. For each non-forbidden cell \((r, c)\):
    • Vertex cost \(w(r, c) = 0\) if grid[r][c] == '#', else \(1\) if grid[r][c] == '.'.
  3. Run 0-1 BFS from all non-forbidden cells in column \(1\) (\(c = 0\) in 0-indexed) using only the 4 diagonal transitions \((r \pm 1, c \pm 1)\) to find the minimum-cost path to column \(M - 1\), and reconstruct the path via parent pointers!

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

Suppose \(N = 2, M = 3\) with # at \((0, 1)\).

  • Orthogonal neighbors of \((0, 1)\) are \((0, 0), (0, 2), (1, 1)\)—all three are forbidden.
  • Valid cells: \((1, 0)\) (cost \(1\)), \((0, 1)\) (existing #, cost \(0\)), and \((1, 2)\) (cost \(1\)).
  • Diagonal path from column \(0\) to column \(2\): \((1, 0) \to (0, 1) \to (1, 2)\) has total cost \(1 + 0 + 1 = 2\), and all three cells have \((r + c) \bmod 2 = 1\), so no two are 4-adjacent!

    for (int i = 0; i < n; ++i) cin >> s[i];

        for (int d = 0; d < 4; ++d) {

            if (nr >= 0 && nr < n && nc >= 0 && nc < m && s[nr][nc] == '#')
                return false;
        }
        return true;
    };

    vector<vector<pair<int, int>>> par(n, vector<pair<int, int>>(m, {-1, -1}));

            int w = (s[r][0] == '.' ? 1 : 0);
            dist[r][0] = w;
            if (w == 0) dq.push_front({r, 0});
            else dq.push_back({r, 0});

        auto [r, c] = dq.front();
        dq.pop_front();

            if (!valid(nr, nc)) continue;
            int w = (s[nr][nc] == '.' ? 1 : 0);
            if (dist[r][c] + w < dist[nr][nc]) {
                dist[nr][nc] = dist[r][c] + w;
                par[nr][nc] = {r, c};
                if (w == 0) dq.push_front({nr, nc});
                else dq.push_back({nr, nc});
            }
        }
    }

    int best_r = -1;
    for (int r = 0; r < n; ++r) {
        if (dist[r][m - 1] < INF && (best_r == -1 || dist[r][m - 1] < dist[best_r][m - 1])) {
            best_r = r;
        }
    }

    if (best_r == -1) {
        cout << "NO\n";
        return;
    }

    cout << "YES\n";
    int cr = best_r, cc = m - 1;
    while (cr != -1) {
        s[cr][cc] = '#';
        tie(cr, cc) = par[cr][cc];
    }
    for (int i = 0; i < n; ++i) cout << s[i] << "\n";
}

    int T;
    if (cin >> T) {
        while (T--) solve();
    }

6.4 [Intermediate #116] Codeforces 1765I — Infinite Chess

  • Judge URL: https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/1765/I
  • Constraints: Board of \(8\) rows (\(y \in [1, 8]\)) and \(10^8\) columns (\(x \in [1, 10^8]\)). \(N \le 15\) static white pieces (K, Q, R, B, N). Time Limit: \(2.0\text{ s}\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

A Black King moves (8-connected King moves, 1 step per turn) from \((x_S, y_S)\) to \((x_T, y_T)\) on an \(8 \times 10^8\) chessboard without stepping onto any square attacked by \(N \le 15\) static white pieces (the Black King may capture a white piece if that square is not defended by another white piece).

Because \(X_{\max} = 10^8\), an explicit BFS over all \(8 \times 10^8\) squares will MLE/TLE. However, from the planar dual perspective, an obstacle blocking the King from reaching \((x_T, y_T)\) is a vertical/diagonal barrier of attacked squares spanning from \(y = 1\) to \(y = 8\), and because \(N \le 15\) and the board height is only \(8\), the attack geometry only changes within a radius of \(\pm 8\) columns around each piece!

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic / Topological Representation

Let us analyze the board columns \(x \in [1, 10^8]\) around the \(N + 2 \le 17\) special \(x\)-coordinates (the \(N\) white pieces plus \(x_S\) and \(x_T\)):

  • Any diagonal attack ray from a Queen or Bishop at \((x_i, y_i)\) starts at \(y_i \in [1, 8]\) and leaves the 8-row board within \(|x - x_i| \le 7\) columns!
  • Outside the window \([x_i - 8, x_i + 8]\) around each special \(x\)-coordinate, only horizontal Rook/Queen rays exist, and those horizontal rays are completely invariant across the entire open column interval \((X_k, X_{k+1})\) between two consecutive retained columns!

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Coordinate Compression + Weighted Dijkstra)

  1. Collect all columns \(x \in [1, 10^8]\) such that \(|x - x^*| \le 8\) for at least one \(x^* \in \{x_S, x_T, x_1, \dots, x_N\}\).
  2. Sort and deduplicate these columns into \(X_0 < X_1 < \dots < X_{K-1}\) (where \(K \le 17 \times 17 = 289\)).
  3. For each retained column \(X_k\) and row \(y \in [1, 8]\), compute safe(X_k, y) by simulating the attacks of the \(N\) white pieces on the original board (remembering that pieces block Queen/Rook/Bishop rays!).
  4. Build a weighted graph on the \(8 K \le 2312\) safe states \((k, y)\):
    • If \(X_{k+1} = X_k + 1\) (adjacent board columns): add standard 1-step King edges of weight \(1\) between \((k, y)\) and \((k \pm 1, y + \Delta y)\) for \(\Delta y \in \{-1, 0, 1\}\).
    • If \(X_{k+1} > X_k + 1\) (a compressed empty gap of width \(\Delta X = X_{k+1} - X_k\)): because every column inside \([X_k, X_{k+1}]\) has the exact same safe rows (no piece or diagonal ray exists in the gap!), a King at a safe row \((k, y)\) can walk straight horizontally across the gap to \((k+1, y)\) in \(\Delta X = X_{k+1} - X_k\) steps! (Within \(X_k\) and \(X_{k+1}\) themselves, the King can still move vertically \((k, y) \leftrightarrow (k, y \pm 1)\) at cost \(1\).)
  5. Run Dijkstra from \((k_S, y_S)\) to \((k_T, y_T)\) in \(O(K \log K)\) time!

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

  • Failure Mode: What if a Queen at \((50, 4)\) is blocked on its right by a Knight at \((55, 4)\)? When checking whether a gap column \(x \in (X_k, X_{k+1})\) is attacked horizontally by a Rook/Queen, you must check whether any white piece sits strictly between the Rook/Queen and column \(x\) on the same row \(y\)! Since our retained columns include \(x^* \pm 1\) for every piece, simply testing attacks at the real board coordinate \((X_k, y)\) automatically respects ray blocking by all \(N\) pieces!

        for (const auto& p : pcs) {
            if (p.x == x && p.y == y) continue; // Capturing the piece itself is allowed if undefended
            long long dx = x - p.x, dy = y - p.y;
            long long adx = abs(dx), ady = abs(dy);
            if (p.type == 'K') {
                if (max(adx, ady) <= 1) return false;
            } else if (p.type == 'N') {
                if ((adx == 1 && ady == 2) || (adx == 2 && ady == 1)) return false;
            } else {
                bool ortho = (dx == 0 || dy == 0);
                bool diag = (adx == ady);
                if ((p.type == 'R' && ortho) || (p.type == 'B' && diag) || (p.type == 'Q' && (ortho || diag))) {
                    long long sx = (dx == 0 ? 0 : (dx > 0 ? 1 : -1));
                    int sy = (dy == 0 ? 0 : (dy > 0 ? 1 : -1));
                    bool blocked = false;
                    for (const auto& q : pcs) {
                        if (q.x == p.x && q.y == p.y) continue;
                        if (q.x == x && q.y == y) continue;
                        long long qdx = q.x - p.x, qdy = q.y - p.y;
                        // Check if q lies strictly on the ray segment from p to (x, y)
                        if (sx == 0 && qdx == 0 && qdy * sy > 0 && abs(qdy) < ady) blocked = true;
                        else if (sy == 0 && qdy == 0 && qdx * sx > 0 && abs(qdx) < adx) blocked = true;
                        else if (sx != 0 && sy != 0 && qdx * sx > 0 && qdy * sy > 0 && abs(qdx) == abs(qdy) && abs(qdx) < adx)
                            blocked = true;
                    }
                    if (!blocked) return false;
                }
            }
        }
        return true;
    };

    vector<vector<bool>> safe(K, vector<bool>(9, false));
    for (int k = 0; k < K; ++k)
        for (int y = 1; y <= 8; ++y)
            safe[k][y] = is_safe(xs[k], y);

    auto id = [&](int k, int y) { return k * 8 + (y - 1); };
    vector<vector<pair<int, long long>>> adj(K * 8);

    for (int k = 0; k < K; ++k) {
        for (int y = 1; y <= 8; ++y) {
            if (!safe[k][y]) continue;

            for (int ny : {y - 1, y + 1}) {
                if (ny >= 1 && ny <= 8 && safe[k][ny])
                    adj[id(k, y)].push_back({id(k, ny), 1});
            }
            // Moves to next column k + 1
            if (k + 1 < K) {
                long long gap = xs[k + 1] - xs[k];
                if (gap == 1) {
                    for (int ny = max(1, y - 1); ny <= min(8, y + 1); ++ny) {
                        if (safe[k + 1][ny]) {
                            adj[id(k, y)].push_back({id(k + 1, ny), 1});
                            adj[id(k + 1, ny)].push_back({id(k, y), 1});
                        }
                    }
                } else {
                    if (safe[k + 1][y]) {
                        adj[id(k, y)].push_back({id(k + 1, y), gap});
                        adj[id(k + 1, y)].push_back({id(k, y), gap});
                    }
                }
            }
        }
    }

    int kS = lower_bound(xs.begin(), xs.end(), xS) - xs.begin();
    int kT = lower_bound(xs.begin(), xs.end(), xT) - xs.begin();
    int start = id(kS, yS), target = id(kT, yT);

    vector<long long> dist(K * 8, INF);
    priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
    dist[start] = 0;
    pq.push({0, start});

    while (!pq.empty()) {
        auto [d, u] = pq.top();
        pq.pop();
        if (d > dist[u]) continue;
        if (u == target) break;
        for (auto [v, w] : adj[u]) {
            if (dist[u] + w < dist[v]) {
                dist[v] = dist[u] + w;
                pq.push({dist[v], v});
            }
        }
    }

    cout << (dist[target] == INF ? -1 : dist[target]) << "\n";
    return 0;
}

Each intersection \(v \in \{0, \dots, N\}^2\) must be assigned a real altitude \(h_v \in [0, 1]\) with \(h_{(0,0)} = 0\) and \(h_{(N,N)} = 1\) to minimize total uphill energy \(\sum_{(u \to v)} w_{uv} \max(0, h_v - h_u)\). \[\min_{\substack{\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^V \\ h_s = 0, \, h_t = 1}} \sum_{(u \to v) \in E} w_{uv} \max(0, h_v - h_u) = \min_{\substack{x \in \{0, 1\}^V \\ x_s = 1, \, x_t = 0}} \sum_{(u \to v) \in E} w_{uv}\, x_u(1 - x_v) = \text{MinCut}_G(s, t).\]

Here \(x_u = 1\) corresponds to altitude \(h_u = 0\) (the \(V_S\) bank containing \(s = (0, 0)\)) and \(x_v = 0\) corresponds to altitude \(h_v = 1\) (the \(V_T\) bank containing \(t = (N, N)\)).

6.6 [Advanced #118] LOJ / ICPC Reif Benchmark — Interior \(s\)-\(t\) Planar Minimum Cut

  • Problem Archetype: Given an undirected planar graph \(G = (V, E, w)\) (\(V \le 5 \cdot 10^4\)) embedded in the plane (for instance, an \(R \times C\) cylindrical/annular grid or general planar mesh where \(s\) is an interior vertex on the inner ring and \(t\) is on the outer ring), compute \(\text{MinCut}_G(s, t)\) in \(O(V \log^2 V)\) time using Reif’s Cut-Path Duality (Theorem 5.1).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Neither \(s\) nor \(t\) shares a common face, so the graph is not \((s, t)\)-planar. Any minimum \((s, t)\)-cut in \(G\) is a minimum-weight separating cycle \(C^*\) in \(G^*\) that encloses \(s\) and excludes \(t\).

Step 2: Intermediate Topological & Algebraic Representation

Let \(P = (e_1, e_2, \dots, e_L)\) be a shortest unweighted \((s, t)\)-path in \(G\). Slicing the dual graph \(G^*\) along \(P^*\) splits each crossed dual edge \(e_i^*\) into a Left-bank portal \(e_{i, L}^*\) and a Right-bank portal \(e_{i, R}^*\). By Theorem 5.1:

\[\text{MinCut}_G(s, t) = \min_{1 \le i \le L} \text{dist}_{G_{\text{slice}}^*}\big(e_{i, L}^*, e_{i, R}^*\big),\]

and the optimal cycles \(C_i^* = \text{ShortestPath}_{G_{\text{slice}}^*}(e_{i, L}^*, e_{i, R}^*)\) are pairwise non-crossing.

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Divide-and-Conquer Dijkstra on Annular / Sliced Dual Graphs)

When \(G^*\) is sliced along \(P^*\), how do we implement Reif’s divide-and-conquer cleanly without complex planar subgraph splitting?

  • Notice that on an annular / concentric radial grid (where \(P\) is a radial ray of length \(L\) from the inner ring \(s\) to the outer ring \(t\)), slicing \(G^*\) along \(P^*\) unrolls the annulus into a planar strip \([0, L-1] \times [0, K]\) where Left-bank portals \((i, 0)\) sit on the top boundary of the strip and Right-bank portals \((i, K)\) sit on the bottom boundary of the strip!
  • In this unrolled planar strip, when we compute the shortest path \(C_{\text{mid}}^*\) from \((\text{mid}, 0)\) to \((\text{mid}, K)\), every vertex on \(C_{\text{mid}}^*\) separates indices \(i < \text{mid}\) from indices \(i > \text{mid}\). Even more generally, on any planar graph where we run Divide-and-Conquer Dijkstra over portal pairs \((e_{i, L}^*, e_{i, R}^*)\), we can pass the active vertex list V_active to each recursive call and partition V_active along the shortest path tree / cycle \(C_{\text{mid}}^*\)!

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

  • Failure-Mode Guard: When cutting \(G^*\) along \(P^* = (e_1^*, \dots, e_L^*)\), where do we place the weight \(w(e_i)\) of the crossed primal edge \(e_i\)?
    • If \(e_i\) separates dual face \(f_{i, L}\) (on the left of \(P\)) from \(f_{i, R}\) (on the right of \(P\)), then in \(G^* \setminus P^*\) we simply delete the edges \(e_1^*, \dots, e_L^*\) from \(G^*\), and for each \(i \in \{1, \dots, L\}\) we query: \[w(e_i) + \text{dist}_{G^* \setminus P^*}\big(f_{i, L}, f_{i, R}\big)!\]
    • Look how clean that is: deleting \(P^*\) from \(G^*\) requires zero vertex duplication, and \(w(e_i) + \text{dist}_{G^* \setminus P^*}(f_{i, L}, f_{i, R})\) is the exact weight of the shortest cycle in \(G^*\) that crosses \(P^*\) solely at \(e_i^*\)! (And if a cycle crosses \(P^*\) at multiple edges \(e_{i_1}^*, e_{i_2}^*, \dots\), we also include shortcut edges \((f_{i, L}, f_{i, R})\) of weight \(w(e_i)\) for \(i \neq m\), or use the 2-layer parity graph \(G^* \times \{0, 1\}\)!)
NoteProgressive Hints for #118 (Reif’s Interior Planar Cut)
  • Hint 1: Choose a simple primal \((s, t)\)-path \(P = (e_1, \dots, e_L)\) where each primal edge \(e_i\) separates dual face \(f_{i, L}\) (Left of \(P\)) from \(f_{i, R}\) (Right of \(P\)).
  • Hint 2: Build the \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-Parity Double Cover \(G^* \times \{0, 1\}\): every dual edge \(e^* \notin P^*\) connects \((u^*, p) \leftrightarrow (v^*, p)\) on the same parity layer \(p \in \{0, 1\}\), while every dual edge \(e_i^* \in P^*\) crosses layers \((f_{i, L}, p) \leftrightarrow (f_{i, R}, p \oplus 1)\) with weight \(w(e_i)\).
  • Hint 3: Then the shortest \(s\)-\(t\) separating cycle through \(f_{i, L}\) has weight \(\text{dist}_{G^* \times \{0, 1\}}\big((f_{i, L}, 0), (f_{i, L}, 1)\big)\), and by Reif’s non-crossing property on the unrolled strip, Divide-and-Conquer Dijkstra finds \(\min_{1 \le i \le L}\) in \(O(V \log^2 V)\) time!

        adj[v].push_back({u, w});

        }
    }
    // 2. Dual edges between adjacent rings (radial steps in G* \ P*)
    for (int r = 0; r + 1 < R; ++r) {
        for (int c = 0; c <= C; ++c) {
            long long w;
            cin >> w;
            add_edge(id(r, c), id(r + 1, c), w);
        }
    }
    // 3. Weights of the L = R primal edges e_r along the s-t path P
    vector<long long> radial_w(R);
    for (int r = 0; r < R; ++r) cin >> radial_w[r];

    long long ans = INF;
    vector<long long> dist(V, INF);
    vector<int> par(V, -1), region_tag(V, -1);
    int timer = 0;

    // Reif's Divide-and-Conquer over portal range [l, r_hi] inside active vertex set `nodes`
    auto solve = [&](auto& self, int l, int r_hi, const vector<int>& nodes) -> void {
        if (l > r_hi || nodes.empty()) return;
        int mid = (l + r_hi) / 2;
        int src = id(mid, 0), dst = id(mid, C);

        ++timer;
        for (int u : nodes) {
            dist[u] = INF;
            par[u] = -1;
            region_tag[u] = timer;
        }

        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
        if (region_tag[src] == timer) {
            dist[src] = 0;
            pq.push({0, src});
        }

        while (!pq.empty()) {
            auto [d, u] = pq.top();
            pq.pop();
            if (d > dist[u]) continue;
            for (auto [v, w] : adj[u]) {
                if (region_tag[v] != timer) continue;
                if (dist[u] + w < dist[v]) {
                    dist[v] = dist[u] + w;
                    par[v] = u;
                    pq.push({dist[v], v});
                }
            }
        }

        if (region_tag[dst] == timer && dist[dst] < INF) {
            ans = min(ans, dist[dst] + radial_w[mid]);
        }

        if (l == r_hi) return;

        // Partition active nodes along the shortest path separator (or ring coordinate relative to mid)
        vector<int> left_nodes, right_nodes;
        for (int u : nodes) {
            int r_coord = u / (C + 1);
            if (r_coord <= mid) left_nodes.push_back(u);
            if (r_coord >= mid) right_nodes.push_back(u);
        }
        self(self, l, mid - 1, left_nodes);
        self(self, mid + 1, r_hi, right_nodes);
    };

    vector<int> all_nodes(V);
    iota(all_nodes.begin(), all_nodes.end(), 0);
    solve(solve, 0, R - 1, all_nodes);

    cout << ans << "\n";
    return 0;
}

6.7 [Mastery #119] Luogu P7916 — [CSP-S 2021] 交通规划 (Traffic Planning)

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P7916
  • Constraints: \(N, M \le 500\), \(T \le 50\) queries, \(\sum k \le 50\) (up to \(k \le 50\) colored perimeter terminals per query). Time Limit: \(3.0\text{ s}\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Each query places \(k \le 50\) colored terminals (\(t_r \in \{0, 1\}\)) around the outer perimeter slot \(p_r \in [1, 2N + 2M]\) of a static \(N \times M\) undirected planar grid. As derived in Section 4, this is a Multi-Terminal Outer-Boundary Planar Cut, solvable in \(O\big(k \cdot NM \log(NM) + k^3\big)\) per query via Non-Crossing Dual Interval DP.

Step 2: Intermediate Topological & Algebraic Representation

Let us map the \(2N + 2M\) clockwise perimeter slots \(p \in [1, 2N + 2M]\) of [CSP-S 2021] 交通规划 to their adjacent interior dual face adj_face(p) and perimeter edge weight perim_w(p):

  • Top border (\(1 \le p \le M\)): column \(c = p\) (\(1 \le c \le M\)).
    • For \(1 \le p \le M - 1\), the perimeter grid edge between \((1, p)\) and \((1, p+1)\) borders interior face \(F_{1, p}\).
    • For \(p = M\) (the top-right corner \((1, M)\)), the corner turns clockwise without an interior face (adjacent face is 0, weight 0).
  • Right border (\(M + 1 \le p \le M + N\)): row \(r = p - M\) (\(1 \le r \le N\)).
    • For \(M + 1 \le p \le M + N - 1\), the perimeter grid edge between \((r, M)\) and \((r+1, M)\) borders interior face \(F_{r, M - 1}\).
    • For \(p = M + N\) (bottom-right corner \((N, M)\)), corner turn (weight 0).
  • Bottom border (\(M + N + 1 \le p \le 2M + N\)): column \(c = M - (p - (M + N)) + 1\) (\(M \ge c \ge 1\)).
    • For \(M + N + 1 \le p \le 2M + N - 1\), the perimeter grid edge between \((N, c-1)\) and \((N, c)\) borders interior face \(F_{N - 1, c - 1}\).
    • For \(p = 2M + N\) (bottom-left corner \((N, 1)\)), corner turn (weight 0).
  • Left border (\(2M + N + 1 \le p \le 2M + 2N\)): row \(r = N - (p - (2M + N)) + 1\) (\(N \ge r \ge 1\)).
    • For \(2M + N + 1 \le p \le 2M + 2N - 1\), the perimeter grid edge between \((r-1, 1)\) and \((r, 1)\) borders interior face \(F_{r - 1, 1}\).
    • For \(p = 2M + 2N\) (top-left corner \((1, 1)\)), corner turn (weight 0).

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Query-by-Query Graph Restoration + Interval DP)

  1. Sort the \(k\) terminals by their clockwise perimeter slot \(p_0 < p_1 < \dots < p_{k-1}\).
  2. Create \(k\) boundary interval vertices \(b_0^*, b_1^*, \dots, b_{k-1}^*\), where \(b_i^*\) covers the clockwise perimeter slots from \(p_i\) to \(p_{(i+1)\bmod k} - 1\):
    • Connect \(b_{(i-1+k)\bmod k}^* \leftrightarrow b_i^*\) with weight \(x_i\) (the stem edge capacity of terminal \(i\) at slot \(p_i\)).
    • For every perimeter slot \(s\) in the clockwise interval from \(p_i\) to \(p_{(i+1)\bmod k} - 1\) that has an interior face adj_face(s) (i.e., non-corner slots), connect adj_face(s) \(\leftrightarrow b_i^*\) with weight perim_w(s).
  3. Collect the \(2m\) defect vertices \(u_0^*, \dots, u_{2m-1}^*\) among \(\{b_0^*, \dots, b_{k-1}^*\}\) where \(t_i \neq t_{(i+1)\bmod k}\).
  4. Run \(2m\) Dijkstras from \(u_0^*, \dots, u_{2m-1}^*\) on the \((N-1)(M-1) + k\) vertices, then run the \(O(m^3)\) circular non-crossing interval DP (Section 4.3), and finally pop the temporary query edges from the interior faces so the base graph is ready for the next query in \(O(N + M + k)\) time!

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

  • Corner Slot Guard: Notice that in [CSP-S 2021] 交通规划, slots \(p = M, M + N, 2M + N, 2M + 2N\) are corners of the \(N \times M\) vertex grid: both slot \(p = M\) (top-right, going East) and slot \(p = M + 1\) (top-right, going South) attach to the same corner grid vertex \((1, M)\)! Because there is no grid perimeter edge between a corner’s East ray and its South ray, the transition from slot \(M\) to slot \(M + 1\) adds zero edges to the interior faces—only the boundary interval vertex \(b_i^*\) spans across the corner! Our adj_face(s) table handles this automatically by assigning adj_face(corner) = -1.

using namespace std;

const long long INF = 1e18;

struct Terminal {
    long long w;
    int p, col;
    bool operator<(const Terminal& o) const { return p < o.p; }
};

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);

    int n, m, T;
    if (!(cin >> n >> m >> T)) return 0;

    vector<vector<long long>> h_edge(n - 1, vector<long long>(m));
    for (int r = 0; r < n - 1; ++r)
        for (int c = 0; c < m; ++c)
            cin >> h_edge[r][c]; // vertical primal edge (r, c) -- (r+1, c)

    vector<vector<long long>> w_edge(n, vector<long long>(m - 1));
    for (int r = 0; r < n; ++r)
        for (int c = 0; c < m - 1; ++c)
            cin >> w_edge[r][c]; // horizontal primal edge (r, c) -- (r, c+1)

    int R = n - 1, C = m - 1;
    int num_in = R * C;
    auto fid = [&](int r, int c) { return r * C + c; }; // 0 <= r < R, 0 <= c < C

    // Max vertices per query: num_in + 55
    vector<vector<pair<int, long long>>> adj(num_in + 60);
    auto add_base_edge = [&](int u, int v, long long w) {
        adj[u].push_back({v, w});
        adj[v].push_back({u, w});
    };

    // Build static interior dual edges
    for (int r = 0; r < R; ++r) {
        for (int c = 0; c < C; ++c) {
            if (r + 1 < R) add_base_edge(fid(r, c), fid(r + 1, c), w_edge[r + 1][c]);
            if (c + 1 < C) add_base_edge(fid(r, c), fid(r, c + 1), h_edge[r][c + 1]);
        }
    }

    // Map each clockwise perimeter slot p in [1, 2n + 2m] to its perimeter grid edge
    int P_MAX = 2 * n + 2 * m;
    vector<int> slot_face(P_MAX + 1, -1);
    vector<long long> slot_w(P_MAX + 1, 0);

    // 1. Top border: p in [1, m-1] -> horizontal edge (0, p-1)--(0, p)
    for (int p = 1; p <= m - 1; ++p) {
        slot_face[p] = (R > 0 ? fid(0, p - 1) : -1);
        slot_w[p] = w_edge[0][p - 1];
    }
    // 2. Right border: p in [m+1, m+n-1] -> vertical edge (r, m-1)--(r+1, m-1)
    for (int p = m + 1; p <= m + n - 1; ++p) {
        int r = p - m - 1;
        slot_face[p] = (C > 0 ? fid(r, C - 1) : -1);
        slot_w[p] = h_edge[r][m - 1];
    }
    // 3. Bottom border: p in [m+n+1, 2m+n-1] -> horizontal edge (n-1, c)--(n-1, c+1)
    for (int p = m + n + 1; p <= 2 * m + n - 1; ++p) {
        int c = m - 1 - (p - (m + n));
        slot_face[p] = (R > 0 ? fid(R - 1, c) : -1);
        slot_w[p] = w_edge[n - 1][c];
    }
    // 4. Left border: p in [2m+n+1, 2m+2n-1] -> vertical edge (r, 0)--(r+1, 0)
    for (int p = 2 * m + n + 1; p <= 2 * m + 2 * n - 1; ++p) {
        int r = n - 1 - (p - (2 * m + n));
        slot_face[p] = (C > 0 ? fid(r, 0) : -1);
        slot_w[p] = h_edge[r][0];
    }

    // Record base degree of interior faces for O(1) rollback after each query
    vector<size_t> base_deg(num_in);
    for (int i = 0; i < num_in; ++i) base_deg[i] = adj[i].size();

    while (T--) {
        int k;
        cin >> k;
        vector<Terminal> terms(k);
        for (int i = 0; i < k; ++i) {
            cin >> terms[i].w >> terms[i].p >> terms[i].col;
        }
        sort(terms.begin(), terms.end());

        int V_tot = num_in + k;
        for (int i = 0; i < k; ++i) adj[num_in + i].clear();

        auto add_temp_edge = [&](int u, int v, long long w) {
            adj[u].push_back({v, w});
            adj[v].push_back({u, w});
        };

        for (int i = 0; i < k; ++i) {
            int nxt = (i + 1) % k;
            int b_cur = num_in + i;
            int b_nxt = num_in + nxt;
            // Stem edge of terminal nxt separates boundary interval i and nxt
            add_temp_edge(b_cur, b_nxt, terms[nxt].w);

            // Attach all perimeter grid edges between terms[i].p and terms[nxt].p
            int s = terms[i].p;
            while (s != terms[nxt].p) {
                if (slot_face[s] != -1) {
                    add_temp_edge(slot_face[s], b_cur, slot_w[s]);
                } else if ((R == 0 || C == 0) && slot_w[s] > 0) {
                    // Degenerate 1D grid guard if needed
                }
                s = (s % P_MAX) + 1;
            }
        }

        vector<int> defects;
        for (int i = 0; i < k; ++i) {
            if (terms[i].col != terms[(i + 1) % k].col) {
                defects.push_back(num_in + i);
            }
        }

        int D = defects.size(); // D = 2m is guaranteed even
        if (D == 0) {
            cout << 0 << "\n";
            for (int i = 0; i < num_in; ++i) adj[i].resize(base_deg[i]);
            continue;
        }

        vector<vector<long long>> dist_mat(D, vector<long long>(D, INF));
        vector<long long> dist(V_tot, INF);
        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;

        for (int a = 0; a < D; ++a) {
            fill(dist.begin(), dist.end(), INF);
            int src = defects[a];
            dist[src] = 0;
            pq.push({0, src});
            while (!pq.empty()) {
                auto [d, u] = pq.top();
                pq.pop();
                if (d > dist[u]) continue;
                for (auto [v, w] : adj[u]) {
                    if (dist[u] + w < dist[v]) {
                        dist[v] = dist[u] + w;
                        pq.push({dist[v], v});
                    }
                }
            }
            for (int b = 0; b < D; ++b) {
                dist_mat[a][b] = dist[defects[b]];
            }
        }

        // Circular Non-Crossing Interval DP over 2*D duplicated array
        int M2 = 2 * D;
        vector<vector<long long>> dp(M2, vector<long long>(M2, 0));
        for (int len = 2; len <= D; len += 2) {
            for (int l = 0; l + len - 1 < M2; ++l) {
                int r = l + len - 1;
                long long best = dist_mat[l % D][r % D] + (l + 1 <= r - 1 ? dp[l + 1][r - 1] : 0);
                for (int p = l + 1; p < r; p += 2) {
                    best = min(best, dp[l][p] + dp[p + 1][r]);
                }
                dp[l][r] = best;
            }
        }

        long long ans = INF;
        for (int l = 0; l < D; ++l) {
            ans = min(ans, dp[l][l + D - 1]);
        }
        cout << ans << "\n";

        // Rollback temporary edges from interior faces
        for (int i = 0; i < num_in; ++i) adj[i].resize(base_deg[i]);
    }

    return 0;
}

6.8 [Mastery #120] LOJ #2320 — [清华集训2017] 热带岛屿 (Tropical Island)

  • Judge URL: https://loj.ac/p/2320
  • Constraints: Planar / grid terrain with height barriers \(h_e\), \(N, M \le 1000\) (or general planar \(V, E \le 10^5\)). Time Limit: \(2.0\text{ s}\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

Unlike additive minimum cuts (\(\sum_{e \in C} w_e\)), tropical island / flood-retention problems ask: how much water can each cell/face \(f^*\) retain before water spills out to the infinite ocean \(O^* = f_\infty\), or what is the minimum-bottleneck enclosing dike around a region?

By Planar Bottleneck Cycle–Cut Duality, water in face \(f^*\) spills to the ocean \(O^*\) as soon as the water level reaches the height of the lowest maximum-barrier path (minimax path) from \(f^*\) to \(O^*\) in the dual graph \(G^*\)! And when dike upgrades have additive costs across multiple water levels, each level set \(\{f^* : \text{water}_{f^*} \ge \lambda\}\) is enclosed by a dual cut cycle around \(f^*\)!

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic / Topological Representation

  1. Minimax Path \(\Longleftrightarrow\) Minimum Spanning Tree / Kruskal Reconstruction Tree on \(G^*\): For any interior face \(f^* \in F \setminus \{O^*\}\), the equilibrium water level \(W(f^*)\) is: \[W(f^*) = \min_{P^*: f^* \leadsto O^*} \max_{e^* \in P^*} w(e^*).\]
  2. Tree Hierarchy of Nested Planar Flood Basins: Sort all dual edges \(e^* = (u^*, v^*)\) by barrier height \(w(e^*)\) in non-decreasing order and build the Kruskal Reconstruction Tree \(T_{\text{KRT}}\) (or run Multi-Source Minimax Dijkstra from \(O^*\)). Each internal node \(x \in T_{\text{KRT}}\) with weight \(w(x)\) represents a connected basin of faces in \(G^*\) that merges at water level \(w(x)\), and its subtree in \(T_{\text{KRT}}\) is the exact connected component of faces enclosed by a planar barrier cycle of height \(\ge w(x)\)!

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Minimax Dijkstra + Kruskal Reconstruction Tree on \(G^*\))

  1. Construct the planar dual graph \(G^* = (F, E^*)\) with ocean vertex \(O^* = 0\).
  2. To compute the equilibrium water volume \(\sum_{f^*} \max(0, W(f^*) - \text{ground}(f^*))\):
    • Initialize \(W(O^*) = 0\) and \(W(f^*) = \infty\) for \(f^* \neq O^*\).
    • Run Minimax Dijkstra from \(O^*\) using transition \(W(v^*) \gets \min\big(W(v^*), \max(W(u^*), w(u^*, v^*))\big)\).
  3. If queries ask for subtree/basin aggregations or dike reinforcements across water levels, build the Kruskal Reconstruction Tree by merging DSU components in increasing order of \(w(e^*)\) (always rooting the component containing \(O^*\) as the “drained” ocean component), converting 2D planar cycle enclosure into 1D subtree queries!

Step 4: Hand-Checkable Toy Trace & Failure-Mode Guard

  • Suppose a \(3 \times 3\) island has a ring of 8 perimeter cells of height \(10\) surrounding a central cell \((2, 2)\) of ground height \(2\), except one perimeter cell has a gap of height \(6\).
  • In \(G^*\), the central cell \((2, 2)\) is connected to the ocean \(O^*\) through the 4 orthogonal perimeter cells of heights \(10, 10, 10, 6\).
  • Minimax Dijkstra from \(O^*\) visits the height-\(6\) perimeter cell at water level \(6\), and then floods the central cell \((2, 2)\) at water level \(\max(6, 2) = 6\), retaining \(6 - 2 = 4\) units of water!
NoteProgressive Hints for #120 (LOJ #2320 — Tropical Island)
  • Hint 1: Water flows from face to face across shared edges, and spills into the infinite outer face \(O^*\). What is the exact dual graph \(G^*\)?
  • Hint 2: A set of cells can hold water at level \(H\) if and only if every boundary edge of that cell set has height \(\ge H\)—which means in \(G^*\), the cells are disconnected from \(O^*\) when all dual edges with weight \(< H\) are removed!

#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

struct DualEdge {
    int u, v;
    long long w;
    bool operator<(const DualEdge& o) const { return w < o.w; }
};

struct DSU {
    vector<int> p;
    DSU(int n) : p(n) { iota(p.begin(), p.end(), 0); }
    int find(int x) { return p[x] == x ? x : p[x] = find(p[x]); }
};

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);

    int N, M;
    if (!(cin >> N >> M)) return 0;

    vector<vector<long long>> h(N, vector<long long>(M));
    for (int r = 0; r < N; ++r)
        for (int c = 0; c < M; ++c)
            cin >> h[r][c];

    int O_star = 0; // Ocean (outer face)
    auto id = [&](int r, int c) { return r * M + c + 1; };
    int V = N * M + 1;

    vector<DualEdge> edges;
    const int dr[4] = {-1, 1, 0, 0};
    const int dc[4] = {0, 0, -1, 1};

    for (int r = 0; r < N; ++r) {
        for (int c = 0; c < M; ++c) {
            int u = id(r, c);
            for (int d = 0; d < 4; ++d) {
                int nr = r + dr[d], nc = c + dc[d];
                if (nr < 0 || nr >= N || nc < 0 || nc >= M) {
                    edges.push_back({u, O_star, h[r][c]});
                } else if (u < id(nr, nc)) {
                    edges.push_back({u, id(nr, nc), max(h[r][c], h[nr][nc])});
                }
            }
        }
    }

    // Build Kruskal Reconstruction Tree (Laminar Basin Hierarchy) + Minimax Water Levels
    sort(edges.begin(), edges.end());
    DSU dsu(2 * V);
    vector<long long> water_level(V, 0);
    vector<vector<int>> comp_members(V);
    for (int i = 0; i < V; ++i) comp_members[i].push_back(i);

    for (const auto& e : edges) {
        int ru = dsu.find(e.u), rv = dsu.find(e.v);
        if (ru == rv) continue;
        int ro = dsu.find(O_star);
        if (ru == ro || rv == ro) {
            int flooded = (ru == ro ? rv : ru);
            for (int cell : comp_members[flooded]) {
                water_level[cell] = e.w;
            }
            comp_members[flooded].clear();
            dsu.p[flooded] = ro;
        } else {
            if (comp_members[ru].size() < comp_members[rv].size()) swap(ru, rv);
            for (int cell : comp_members[rv]) comp_members[ru].push_back(cell);
            comp_members[rv].clear();
            dsu.p[rv] = ru;
        }
    }

    long long total_retained = 0;
    for (int r = 0; r < N; ++r) {
        for (int c = 0; c < M; ++c) {
            total_retained += max(0LL, water_level[id(r, c)] - h[r][c]);
        }
    }

    cout << total_retained << "\n";
    return 0;
}

  1. Mystery Problem A: You are given a \(600 \times 600\) grid of city blocks. Every directed street segment \((u \to v)\) has a congestion toll \(c(u \to v) \ge 0\). You must assign a continuous potential \(\phi_v \in [0, 10]\) to every intersection with \(\phi_{(0,0)} = 0\) and \(\phi_{(600,600)} = 10\) to minimize \(\sum_{(u \to v)} c(u \to v) \max(0, \phi_v - \phi_u)\).
  2. Mystery Problem B: You are given a \(400 \times 400\) planar grid with \(12\) sources \(s_1, \dots, s_{12}\) and \(12\) sinks \(t_1, \dots, t_{12}\) placed at arbitrary positions around the outer border of the grid. Find the minimum capacity of edges to remove so that no source \(s_i\) can reach any sink \(t_j\).
NoteDiagnostic Answers
  • Mystery Problem A: Factor out the constant \(10\): by the Coarea Level-Set Collapse (Theorem 3.1), optimal potentials take values in \(\{0, 10\}\), reducing the objective to \(10 \times \text{MinCut}_G\big((0, 0), (600, 600)\big)\), which compiles via \(90^\circ\) CCW Directed Planar Dual to a single Dijkstra in \(O(N^2 \log N)\) time (#117).
  • Mystery Problem B: The \(24\) terminals all lie on the outer boundary \(f_\infty\), partitioning \(f_\infty\) into \(k = 24\) boundary intervals \(b_1^*, \dots, b_{24}^*\). Identify the \(2m \le 24\) color-transition defects (\(s \leftrightarrow t\)), run \(2m\) Dijkstras on \(G^*\) (keeping all \(24\) boundary intervals in \(G^*\)!), and run the \(O(m^3)\) Circular Non-Crossing Interval DP (#119).

Bridge to Chapter 16: What When the Graph Is Non-Planar, But Tree-Like?

In Chapter 15, we eliminated Dinic’s algorithm on \(N \times N\) grids by exploiting 2D surface topology (Euler’s formula \(|V| - |E| + |F| = 2\) and Jordan Cycle–Cut Duality). What happens when a network has \(V, E \le 2 \times 10^5\), is not embedded on a plane with boundary terminals, yet exhibits sparse tree-like structure—such as a Cactus graph (every edge belongs to at most one simple cycle), a Series-Parallel graph (\(\text{tw}(G) \le 2\)), or a graph with bounded Treewidth \(\text{tw}(G) \le k\) where a handful of extra edges spoil a tree?

In Chapter 16 (Flows and Cuts on Cactus, Series-Parallel, and Bounded-Treewidth Graphs — DP Elimination of Max-Flow), we will develop the algebraic counterpart of planar duality: replacing augmenting paths with Cycle Bottleneck Folding, Block-Cut Tree / DFS-Tree Cut DP, Dynamic DP (Matrix-Vector Segment Tree Maintainers), and \(O(2^{\text{tw}} \cdot N)\) Nice Tree Decomposition Cut DP.

  • [EN] Reif, J. H. (1983). Minimum \(s\)-\(t\) cut of a planar undirected network in \(O(n \log^2 n)\) time. SIAM Journal on Computing, 12(1):71–81. Introduced the primal shortest-path bank slicing and divide-and-conquer non-crossing cycle algorithm (Section 5).
