1. The Algebraic Equation-Difference Compiler (流守恒差分建模法: \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\))

1.1 Why Traditional Interval-Flow Editorials Violate the “Zero Magic Gadgets” Mandate

Open almost any traditional editorial for the classic [NOI2008 / Luogu P3980] 志愿者招募 (Volunteer Recruitment, #148) and you will be greeted by a baffling recipe presented out of thin air:

“Create \(n + 1\) vertices \(1, 2, \dots, n + 1\). Connect \(S \to i\) with capacity \(A_i - A_{i-1}\) if \(A_i - A_{i-1} > 0\), or connect \(i \to T\) with capacity \(A_{i-1} - A_i\) if \(A_i - A_{i-1} < 0\). Add a backward edge \(i + 1 \to i\) of capacity \(\infty\) and cost \(0\) for each \(i = 1, \dots, n\). For each volunteer type \([l_j, r_j]\), add a forward edge \(l_j \to r_j + 1\) of capacity \(\infty\) and cost \(c_j\). Run Min-Cost Max-Flow.”

A programmer meeting this construction for the first time asks four completely legitimate questions:

  1. Why are there \(n + 1\) vertices when the problem only has \(n\) days?
  2. Where did the finite difference \(A_i - A_{i-1}\) come from?
  3. Why does a volunteer working on days \([l_j, r_j]\) connect to \(r_j + 1\) instead of \(r_j\)?
  4. Why do the free spine edges point backward (\(i + 1 \to i\)) in lower-bound coverage, whereas they point forward (\(i \to i + 1\)) in upper-bound interval packing?

The answer is not a clever geometric picture of people walking along a corridor. It is pure linear algebra—specifically, the Algebraic Equation-Difference Method (流守恒差分建模法), formalized in Chinese competitive programming by Bushu Zhou (周冬) in the 2009 National Training Team Papers (国家集训队论文).

Once you understand this four-step algebraic compiler, you will never guess a vertex count, an arc orientation, or a right-hand-side supply again.

1.2 Step 1 — Starting from Pure Linear Inequalities (Zero Graphs Mentioned)

Suppose a problem gives us a discrete timeline of \(n\) positions (days, hours, or elementary segments) indexed by \(i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\), and \(m\) decision variables \(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m\). Each decision variable \(x_j \in [0, u_j]\) (with unit cost \(c_j \in \mathbb{R}\) and upper bound \(u_j \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1} \cup \{\infty\}\)) is active across a contiguous closed interval of positions \([l_j, r_j] \subseteq \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\) (\(1 \le l_j \le r_j \le n\)).

On each position \(i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\), the total number of active variables covering position \(i\) must be at least \(A_i\):

\[ \min_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{Z}^m} \sum_{j=1}^m c_j x_j \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{j \,:\, l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j \ge A_i \quad (i = 1, 2, \dots, n), \qquad 0 \le x_j \le u_j. \]

Why isn’t this linear program already a network flow problem?

Recall from Chapter 01 that in any directed network \(G = (V, E)\), the flow conservation equations at the vertices \(i \in V\):

\[ \text{OutFlow}(i) - \text{InFlow}(i) = \sum_{e \in \delta^+(i)} f_e - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(i)} f_e = b_i \]

have a very rigid matrix structure: each flow variable \(f_e\) (corresponding to a directed arc \(e = (u \to v)\)) appears in the entire system of equations at most twice—once with coefficient \(+1\) in the equation for its tail vertex \(u\), and once with coefficient \(-1\) in the equation for its head vertex \(v\).

In our interval covering system, by contrast, variable \(x_j\) appears with coefficient \(+1\) in every inequality \(i \in \{l_j, l_j + 1, \dots, r_j\}\). If we stack the \(n\) constraints as rows of an \(n \times m\) matrix \(M\), the \(j\)-th column of \(M\) is a contiguous block of \(r_j - l_j + 1\) ones:

\[ M_{*, j} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \cdots & 0 & \underbrace{1 \quad 1 \quad \cdots \quad 1}_{\text{rows } l_j \text{ through } r_j} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \end{bmatrix}^T. \]

A \(\{0, 1\}\)-matrix whose ones in every column form a single contiguous block is said to satisfy the Consecutive-Ones Property (连续 1 性质, C1P) on columns.

1.3 Step 2 — Slack Variables \(y_i \ge 0\) and Consecutive Row Differencing (\(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\))

How do we transform a column containing a contiguous run of ones \((0, \dots, 0, 1, 1, \dots, 1, 0, \dots, 0)^T\) into a column containing only a single \(+1\) and a single \(-1\)?

First, we cannot subtract two inequalities \(\ge\) from one another directly (if \(\alpha \ge A_2\) and \(\beta \ge A_1\), it does not follow that \(\alpha - \beta \ge A_2 - A_1\)). Therefore, we must first convert every inequality into an exact linear equation by introducing an explicit non-negative surplus (slack) variable \(y_i \ge 0\) for each position \(i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\):

\[ y_i := \left(\sum_{j \,:\, l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j\right) - A_i \ge 0 \qquad (i = 1, 2, \dots, n). \]

Physically, \(y_i \ge 0\) counts the excess coverage at position \(i\) above the required threshold \(A_i\), and incurs zero cost (\(0 \cdot y_i\)) in the objective function. Rewriting the \(n\) constraints as exact equalities \(E_1, E_2, \dots, E_n\):

\[ E_i : \quad \sum_{j \,:\, l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j - y_i = A_i \qquad (i = 1, 2, \dots, n, \quad y_i \ge 0). \]

Second, to ensure that intervals starting at \(l_j = 1\) and intervals ending at \(r_j = n\) both have a clean boundary when we take row differences, we pad the system at the top and bottom with two trivial dummy equations \(E_0\) and \(E_{n+1}\) (defining \(A_0 := 0\), \(A_{n+1} := 0\), \(y_0 \equiv 0\), and \(y_{n+1} \equiv 0\)):

\[ E_0 : \quad 0 = 0, \qquad\qquad E_{n+1} : \quad 0 = 0. \]

Now, replace the system \((E_1, E_2, \dots, E_n, E_{n+1})\) by the \(n + 1\) consecutive difference equations:

\[ \Delta E_i := E_i - E_{i-1} \qquad \text{for } i = 1, 2, \dots, n + 1. \]

Why is this replacement lossless? Because for any \(k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n+1\}\), the prefix sum of the first \(k\) difference equations telescopes back to \(E_k\):

\[ \sum_{i=1}^k \Delta E_i = \sum_{i=1}^k (E_i - E_{i-1}) = E_k - E_0 = E_k. \]

In matrix terms, premultiplying by the \((n+1) \times n\) first-difference operator \(D\) maps the solution space bijectively onto the subspace of \((\Delta E_1, \dots, \Delta E_{n+1})\) satisfying \(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} \Delta E_i = E_{n+1} - E_0 = 0\).

1.4 Step 3 — The Consecutive-Ones Telescoping Theorem (Why Every Column Becomes an Arc)

Let us compute the exact coefficient of each interval variable \(x_j\) and each slack variable \(y_k\) across the \(n + 1\) differenced equations \(\Delta E_1, \Delta E_2, \dots, \Delta E_{n+1}\).

ImportantTheorem 19.1 (The Consecutive-Ones Telescoping Law)

Let \(E_i : \sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j - y_i = A_i\) (\(i = 1, \dots, n\)) be a Consecutive-Ones linear system padded with \(E_0: 0 = 0\) and \(E_{n+1}: 0 = 0\). In the differenced system \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) for \(i = 1, 2, \dots, n + 1\):

  1. Interval Variable \(x_j\) (active on \([l_j, r_j] \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}\)):
    • For \(1 \le i < l_j\): \(x_j\) appears in neither \(E_i\) nor \(E_{i-1}\), so its coefficient in \(\Delta E_i\) is \(0 - 0 = 0\).
    • At \(i = l_j\): \(x_j\) appears in \(E_{l_j}\) (coefficient \(+1\)) and does not appear in \(E_{l_j - 1}\) (coefficient \(0\)), so its coefficient in \(\Delta E_{l_j}\) is \(+1 - 0 = +1\).
    • For \(l_j < i \le r_j\): \(x_j\) appears in both \(E_i\) (\(+1\)) and \(E_{i-1}\) (\(+1\)), so its coefficient in \(\Delta E_i\) cancels identically: \(+1 - 1 = 0\)!
    • At \(i = r_j + 1\): \(x_j\) does not appear in \(E_{r_j + 1}\) (\(0\)) and does appear in \(E_{r_j}\) (\(+1\)), so its coefficient in \(\Delta E_{r_j + 1}\) is \(0 - 1 = -1\).
    • For \(r_j + 1 < i \le n + 1\): \(x_j\) appears in neither equation, so its coefficient is \(0\).
  2. Surplus Slack Variable \(y_k\) (\(1 \le k \le n\), appearing only in \(E_k\) with coefficient \(-1\)):
    • At \(i = k\): in \(\Delta E_k = E_k - E_{k-1}\), \(y_k\) has coefficient \(-1 - 0 = -1\).
    • At \(i = k + 1\): in \(\Delta E_{k+1} = E_{k+1} - E_k\), \(y_k\) has coefficient \(0 - (-1) = +1\).
    • For all \(i \notin \{k, k + 1\}\): \(y_k\) has coefficient \(0\).
  3. Right-Hand Side Nodal Demand \(b_i\):
    • The right-hand side of \(\Delta E_i\) is \(b_i := A_i - A_{i-1}\) (with \(A_0 = A_{n+1} = 0\)), satisfying total balance \(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} b_i = A_{n+1} - A_0 = 0\).

Look at the column for \(x_j\) before and after differencing:

\[ \text{Before } (\text{rows } 1 \dots n): \begin{array}{c} 1 \\ \vdots \\ l_j - 1 \\ l_j \\ l_j + 1 \\ \vdots \\ r_j \\ r_j + 1 \\ \vdots \\ n \end{array} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ +1 \\ +1 \\ \vdots \\ +1 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \xrightarrow{\quad \Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1} \quad} \qquad \text{After } (\text{rows } 1 \dots n+1): \begin{array}{c} 1 \\ \vdots \\ l_j - 1 \\ \mathbf{l_j} \\ l_j + 1 \\ \vdots \\ r_j \\ \mathbf{r_j + 1} \\ \vdots \\ n + 1 \end{array} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ \mathbf{+1} \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ \mathbf{-1} \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Every single column in the entire differenced system \(\Delta E_1, \dots, \Delta E_{n+1}\) now has literally one \(+1\) and one \(-1\)!

1.5 Step 4 — Mechanical Graph Compilation from \(\Delta E_i\)

Writing out the \(i\)-th difference equation \(\Delta E_i\) (\(i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n+1\}\)) and grouping the \(+1\) terms (outflow) and \(-1\) terms (inflow) gives:

\[ \Delta E_i : \quad \underbrace{\left(\sum_{j \,:\, l_j = i} x_j + y_{i-1}\right)}_{\text{OutFlow}(i) \text{ (coeff } +1\text{)}} - \underbrace{\left(\sum_{j \,:\, r_j + 1 = i} x_j + y_i\right)}_{\text{InFlow}(i) \text{ (coeff } -1\text{)}} = A_i - A_{i-1} =: b_i, \]

where \(y_0 \equiv 0\) and \(y_{n+1} \equiv 0\).

Now create \(n + 1\) timeline vertices \(V_{\text{time}} = \{1, 2, \dots, n + 1\}\), plus a super-source \(S\) and super-sink \(T\). Under the standard nodal conservation law \(\text{OutFlow}(i) - \text{InFlow}(i) = b_i\):

  • Any non-negative variable \(z \in [0, U]\) with unit cost \(c\) that has \(+1\) in \(\Delta E_u\) (outflow from \(u\)) and \(-1\) in \(\Delta E_v\) (inflow to \(v\)) is compiled into a directed arc:

    \[ u \xrightarrow{\text{cap } U, \;\text{cost } c} v. \]

Applying this rule mechanically to every term in \(\Delta E_i\) produces the complete network specification:

Interval Variable \(x_j \in [0, u_j]\) (\([l_j, r_j]\)) \(\Delta E_{l_j}\) \(\Delta E_{r_j + 1}\) \(l_j \longrightarrow r_j + 1\) \(u_j\) \(c_j\)
Surplus Slack \(y_i \ge 0\) (\(\sum x_j - y_i = A_i\)) \(\Delta E_{i+1}\) \(\Delta E_i\) \(i + 1 \longrightarrow i\) (backward!) \(\infty\) \(0\)
Deficit Slack \(y_i \ge 0\) (\(\sum x_j + y_i = K_i\)) \(\Delta E_i\) \(\Delta E_{i+1}\) \(i \longrightarrow i + 1\) (forward!) \(\infty\) \(0\)

Every mystery from Section 1.1 is now completely resolved:

  1. Why \(n + 1\) vertices? Because an interval \([l_j, r_j]\) covering day \(r_j = n\) is still active in \(E_n\) and only drops out in \(E_{n+1}: 0 = 0\), placing its \(-1\) coefficient in \(\Delta E_{n+1}\) (vertex \(n + 1\)).
  2. Why \(A_i - A_{i-1}\)? Because the right-hand side of \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) is literally \(A_i - A_{i-1}\).
  3. Why \(l_j \to r_j + 1\)? Because \(x_j\) enters at row \(l_j\) (\(E_{l_j} - E_{l_j - 1}\)) and leaves at row \(r_j + 1\) (\(E_{r_j + 1} - E_{r_j}\)).
  4. Why does the slack edge point backward (\(i + 1 \to i\)) for \(\ge A_i\) but forward (\(i \to i + 1\)) for \(\le K_i\)? Because in \(\sum x_j - y_i = A_i\), the slack variable has sign \(-y_i\) in \(E_i\), giving \(-1\) in \(\Delta E_i\) and \(+1\) in \(\Delta E_{i+1}\) (\(i + 1 \to i\)); whereas in \(\sum x_j + y_i = K_i\), the slack variable has sign \(+y_i\) in \(E_i\), giving \(+1\) in \(\Delta E_i\) and \(-1\) in \(\Delta E_{i+1}\) (\(i \to i + 1\))!

Suppose \(n = 2\) days with requirements \(A_1 = 1, A_2 = 1\), and we have a volunteer type covering the closed interval \([l_1, r_1] = [1, 2]\) at cost \(c_1 = 5\), plus single-day volunteers \([1, 1]\) (cost \(4\)) and \([2, 2]\) (cost \(4\)).

  • What breaks if you wire \([l_j, r_j]\) as \(l_j \to r_j\) instead of \(l_j \to r_j + 1\)?
    • The single-day volunteers \([1, 1]\) and \([2, 2]\) become self-loops \(1 \to 1\) and \(2 \to 2\) that cannot carry any net flow between vertices!
    • Meanwhile, the two-day volunteer \([1, 2]\) becomes the arc \(1 \to 2\), which only has \(+1\) in \(\Delta E_1\) and \(-1\) in \(\Delta E_2\). Undifferencing \(+1\) at row \(1\) and \(-1\) at row \(2\) corresponds to covering only day \(1\) (\([1, 2 - 1] = [1, 1]\)), leaving day \(2\) completely uncovered!
  • What breaks if you wire \(l_j \to r_j + 1\) but only allocate \(n\) vertices (omitting \(\Delta E_{n+1}\))?
    • In \(E_1, \dots, E_n\), \(b_1 = A_1 - 0 = +1\) and \(b_2 = A_2 - A_1 = 0\), so \(\sum_{i=1}^n b_i = A_n = 1 \ne 0\)! There is no sink demand \(b_{n+1} = 0 - A_n = -1\) at vertex \(n + 1\), so the flow network has \(0\) sink capacity and outputs cost \(0\)!
  • Audit Rule (H-21): Whenever an interval \([l_j, r_j]\) is closed on integer indices \(1 \dots n\), its half-open span is \([l_j, r_j + 1)\), its arc is \(l_j \to r_j + 1\), and the timeline requires \(n + 1\) vertices.

2.2 Doubly-Bounded Sliding Windows ([NEERC 2016] Delight for a Cat #151)

Now suppose a problem imposes both a lower bound \(L_i\) and an upper bound \(R_i\) on each constraint row \(i\):

\[ L_i \le \sum_{j \,:\, l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j \le R_i \qquad (i = 1, 2, \dots, W). \]

This exact structure arises in [ICPC NEERC 2016 D / Luogu P6967] Delight for a Cat (#151) (and [ONTAK 2010] Vacation):

  • Across \(n\) hours \(j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\), a cat must choose either to sleep (gaining \(s_j\) happiness) or to eat (gaining \(e_j\) happiness) in each hour \(j\).
  • In every contiguous sliding window of \(k\) hours \([i, i + k - 1]\) (\(i = 1, 2, \dots, n - k + 1\)), the cat must sleep for at least \(m_s\) hours and eat for at least \(m_e\) hours (\(m_s + m_e \le k\)).
  • Maximize the cat’s total happiness across the \(n\) hours.

Step 1: Dualizing Point Decisions into Interval Columns

If the cat sleeps in all \(n\) hours, its baseline happiness is \(S_{\text{base}} = \sum_{j=1}^n s_j\). Let \(x_j \in \{0, 1\}\) indicate whether the cat switches hour \(j\) from sleeping to eating, which changes happiness by \(w_j = e_j - s_j\) (equivalent to minimizing cost \(c_j = -w_j = s_j - e_j\)).

Let \(W = n - k + 1\) be the number of sliding windows. In window \(i \in \{1, 2, \dots, W\}\) (spanning hours \(j \in [i, i + k - 1]\)):

  • Eating at least \(m_e\) hours means \(\sum_{j=i}^{i+k-1} x_j \ge m_e =: L\).
  • Sleeping at least \(m_s\) hours means eating at most \(k - m_s\) hours: \(\sum_{j=i}^{i+k-1} x_j \le k - m_s =: R\).

Now ask: Which window constraints \(E_i\) (\(1 \le i \le W\)) contain the decision variable \(x_j\)?

\[ x_j \text{ appears in } E_i \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad i \le j \le i + k - 1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad j - k + 1 \le i \le j. \]

Intersecting with \(1 \le i \le W\), variable \(x_j\) appears with coefficient \(+1\) in the single contiguous interval of window indices \([l_j, r_j]\) given by:

\[ l_j = \max(1, \; j - k + 1), \qquad r_j = \min(W, \; j) \qquad (1 \le l_j \le r_j \le W)! \]

Thus, the sliding-window constraint matrix has the Consecutive-Ones Property on columns!

Step 2: Turning Two-Sided Inequalities \(L_i \le \text{sum}_i \le R_i\) into a Single Bounded Slack Variable

At first glance, \(L_i \le \sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j \le R_i\) looks like two separate inequalities per window \(i\). If we wrote two separate rows per window, each variable \(x_j\) would appear in two non-adjacent blocks of rows, destroying the Consecutive-Ones Property!

Instead, observe that a two-sided inequality \(L_i \le S_i \le R_i\) is 100% equivalent to a single equality with a capacity-bounded slack variable of range \([0, R_i - L_i]\)! Moreover, we have two dual choices for how to define that slack variable:

Compilation Variant Slack Variable Definition Equality \(E_i\) (\(i = 1, \dots, W\)) Differenced Arc for \(x_j\) Differenced Arc for Slack Nodal Balance \(b_i\) (when \(L_i \equiv L, R_i \equiv R\))
Variant A: Upper-Slack Forward-DAG \(y_i := R_i - \sum x_j \in [0, R_i - L_i]\) (unused upper capacity) \(\sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j + y_i = R_i\) \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -w_j} r_j + 1\) \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } R_i - L_i, \text{cost } 0} i + 1\) (forward!) \(b_1 = +R\), \(b_{W+1} = -R\), \(b_2 = \dots = b_W = 0\)
Variant B: Lower-Slack Backward-Spine \(z_i := \sum x_j - L_i \in [0, R_i - L_i]\) (surplus above lower bound) \(\sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j - z_i = L_i\) \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -w_j} r_j + 1\) \(i + 1 \xrightarrow{\text{cap } R_i - L_i, \text{cost } 0} i\) (backward!) \(b_1 = +L\), \(b_{W+1} = -L\), \(b_2 = \dots = b_W = 0\)
TipWhy Both Compilations Are Algebraically Isomorphic — And When to Prefer Variant A vs. Variant B

Notice that \(y_i + z_i = R_i - L_i\): sending \(z_i\) units backward on \(i + 1 \to i\) is literally the residual reverse of sending \(y_i = (R_i - L_i) - z_i\) units forward on \(i \to i + 1\)!

  • Why Variant A (Upper-Slack Forward-DAG) is Magical: In Variant A, both the decision arcs \(l_j \to r_j + 1\) and the slack arcs \(i \to i + 1\) point strictly from smaller index to larger index (\(u < v\)). Therefore, the initial network on vertices \(1, 2, \dots, W + 1\) is a strictly topological DAG! Even though \(-w_j = s_j - e_j\) can be negative when \(e_j > s_j\), there are zero directed cycles at \(f = 0\), so we can compute initial Johnson potentials \(\pi_0\) in \(O(n + W)\) time via a single left-to-right DAG DP pass and run \(R = k - m_s\) steps of pure Dijkstra without SPFA or pre-saturation!
  • How Variant B (Lower-Slack Backward-Spine) Works: In Variant B, the total flow from \(1\) to \(W+1\) is only \(L = m_e\) units (which is smaller when \(m_e \ll k - m_s\)), and the negative-cost arcs \(l_j \to r_j + 1\) (\(e_j > s_j\)) can be handled in \(O(n)\) setup via Mandatory Initial Saturation (Ch 04 / Ch 17) so that \(\pi_0 \equiv 0\) works for Dijkstra!

3. \(K\)-Overlapping Interval Selection (最长k可重区间集) — Backbone Bypass vs. Equation Differencing

We now turn to the packing dual of NOI2008 志愿者招募: instead of covering each point \(i\) at least \(A_i\) times at minimum cost, we are given \(m\) weighted intervals \(I_j\) with profits \(w_j > 0\) and must select a subset of intervals of maximum total weight such that no point on the real line is covered by more than \(K\) selected intervals ([Luogu P3358] 最长k可重区间集问题 (#145), [POJ 3680] Intervals (#146), [CF 164C] Machine Programming).

3.1 The 4-Step Compilation of Maximum-Weight \(K\)-Interval Packing

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal (Recognition)

  • Why Greedy and Standard DP Fail for \(K \ge 2\):
    • When \(K = 1\) and \(w_j \equiv 1\), classic earliest-deadline-first greedy runs in \(O(m \log m)\) time.
    • When \(K = 1\) and weights \(w_j\) are arbitrary, sorting by right endpoint and running 1D DP with binary search runs in \(O(m \log m)\) time because the last chosen interval completely summarizes the suffix compatibility state.
    • When \(K \ge 2\) and weights \(w_j\) are arbitrary, a naive DP from left to right must remember the right endpoints of all \(K\) currently active intervals, blowing up the state space to \(O(m^K)\). Yet the constraint “at every coordinate \(x\), at most \(K\) chosen intervals cover \(x\)” is a system of linear upper-bound inequalities where each interval covers a contiguous range of coordinates!

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation & Half-Open Normalization

Before writing down linear equations, we must normalize the geometry of intervals so that endpoint touching semantics match the problem statement:

  • Open Intervals \(I_j = (L_j, R_j)\) (e.g., Luogu P3358, POJ 3680, CF 164C): Two intervals \((1, 3)\) and \((3, 5)\) do not overlap at \(x = 3\). After coordinate-compressing the distinct endpoint values into \(1 < 2 < \dots < n\), the real line between \(1\) and \(n\) is partitioned into \(n - 1\) elementary half-open segments \(S_i = [i, i + 1)\) for \(i = 1, 2, \dots, n - 1\). An open interval \((l_j, r_j)\) (in compressed indices \(1 \le l_j < r_j \le n\)) covers the contiguous range of elementary segments \(S_i\) for \(i \in \{l_j, l_j + 1, \dots, r_j - 1\}\).
  • Closed Intervals \(I_j = [L_j, R_j]\) (Integer Points): Two intervals \([1, 3]\) and \([3, 5]\) do overlap at the integer point \(x = 3\). By shifting the right endpoint \(R_j \gets R_j + 1\), every closed integer interval \([L_j, R_j]\) becomes the half-open interval \([L_j, R_j + 1)\), reducing immediately to the open-interval case!

Now let \(x_j \in \{0, 1\}\) indicate whether interval \(I_j = [l_j, r_j)\) is selected, and introduce unused-capacity slack variables \(y_i \ge 0\) for each elementary segment \(S_i = [i, i + 1)\) (\(i = 1, 2, \dots, n - 1\)):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Maximize} \quad & \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j x_j \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \text{Minimize} \quad \sum_{j=1}^{m} (-w_j) x_j \\ \text{subject to} \quad & E_i : \quad \sum_{j \,:\, l_j \le i \le r_j - 1} x_j + y_i = K, \qquad i = 1, 2, \dots, n - 1, \\ & x_j \in [0, 1], \quad y_i \in [0, \infty). \end{aligned} \]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Equation Differencing vs. Backbone Bypass)

Attach boundary equations \(E_0 : 0 = 0\) and \(E_n : 0 = 0\), and take consecutive differences \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) for \(i = 1, 2, \dots, n\):

  1. Decision Variable \(x_j \in [0, 1]\) (covering rows \(i \in \{l_j, \dots, r_j - 1\}\)):

    • Enters at \(\Delta E_{l_j}\) with coefficient \(+1\) and exits at \(\Delta E_{(r_j - 1) + 1} = \Delta E_{r_j}\) with coefficient \(-1\).

    • Compiles to a forward bypass arc:

      \[ e_{x_j} = (l_j \to r_j), \qquad \text{cap} = 1, \qquad \text{cost} = -w_j. \]

  2. Unused-Capacity Slack Variable \(y_i \in [0, \infty)\) (appearing in row \(E_i\) with coefficient \(+1\)):

    • Enters at \(\Delta E_i\) with coefficient \(+1\) and exits at \(\Delta E_{i+1}\) with coefficient \(-1\).

    • Compiles to a forward backbone arc:

      \[ e_{y_i} = (i \to i + 1), \qquad \text{cap} = \infty \text{ (or } K\text{)}, \qquad \text{cost} = 0. \]

  3. Right-Hand Side Supplies & Demands \(b_i = K_i - K_{i-1}\):

    • Since \(K_0 = 0\), \(K_1 = K_2 = \dots = K_{n-1} = K\), and \(K_n = 0\):

      \[ b_1 = K - 0 = +K, \qquad b_2 = b_3 = \dots = b_{n-1} = K - K = 0, \qquad b_n = 0 - K = -K. \]

    • Only vertex \(1\) receives supply \(+K\) from \(S\) (\(S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} 1\)), and only vertex \(n\) sends demand \(K\) to \(T\) (\(n \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} T\))!

ImportantTheorem 19.2: Exact Isomorphism Between the Intuitive ‘Backbone + Bypass’ Model and Equation Differencing

In competitive programming folklore, Luogu P3358 (最长k可重区间集) is often taught using two seemingly unrelated explanations:

  1. The Intuitive “Flow Conservation / Ticket Bypass” Story: Imagine \(K\) parallel tracks running from coordinate \(1\) to coordinate \(n\) along the zero-cost backbone \(1 \to 2 \to \dots \to n\). Selecting interval \([l_j, r_j)\) means pulling \(1\) unit of flow off the backbone at \(l_j\), routing it through the bypass arc \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -w_j} r_j\), and returning it to the backbone at \(r_j\). While that \(1\) unit of flow is in flight on the bypass \(l_j \to r_j\), only \(K - 1\) units remain on the backbone segments \(i \to i + 1\) for \(l_j \le i < r_j\). Because the backbone flow \(y_i\) on \(i \to i + 1\) satisfies \(y_i \ge 0\), at most \(K\) units can be in flight across cut \(( \{1, \dots, i\}, \{i+1, \dots, n\} )\) at any segment \(i\)!
  2. The Algebraic Equation-Difference Story: Write \(\sum_{l_j \le i < r_j} x_j + y_i = K\) and take \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\).

Theorem 19.2: These two models produce the exact same graph, vertex for vertex, edge for edge, and flow unit for flow unit. Specifically, the flow \(y_i\) on the backbone edge \(i \to i + 1\) in the intuitive story is literally the slack variable \(y_i = K - \sum_{l_j \le i < r_j} x_j\) in equation \(E_i\), and the cut conservation equation across \((\{1, \dots, i\}, \{i+1, \dots, n\})\) is literally the prefix sum \(\sum_{t=1}^i \Delta E_t = E_i\)!

3.2 \(O(N + M)\) DAG DP Potential Initialization (Anti-SPFA Guardrail)

Notice a crucial structural property of the Luogu P3358 / POJ 3680 / CF 164C network (and Variant A of NEERC 2016 Delight for a Cat):

  • Every backbone edge \(i \to i + 1\) satisfies \(i < i + 1\).
  • Every interval bypass edge \(l_j \to r_j\) satisfies \(l_j < r_j\).
  • Therefore, at initial flow \(f = 0\), the entire network is a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) in topological order \(1, 2, \dots, n\)!

Even though every interval bypass edge has a negative cost \(-w_j < 0\), there are zero negative cycles in the initial graph. Instead of running \(O(NM)\) Bellman-Ford / SPFA to initialize Johnson potentials \(\pi_0\), or even pre-saturating negative edges, we can compute the exact shortest-path potentials \(\pi_0(1), \pi_0(2), \dots, \pi_0(n)\) in \(O(n + m)\) linear time via a single left-to-right sweep:

\[ \pi_0(1) = 0, \qquad \pi_0(v) = \min\!\left( \pi_0(v - 1), \; \min_{j \,:\, r_j = v} \bigl(\pi_0(l_j) - w_j\bigr) \right) \quad \text{for } v = 2, 3, \dots, n. \]

After this \(O(n + m)\) initialization, we run at most \(K\) iterations of Dijkstra’s algorithm (Ch 17), giving an optimal total time complexity of:

\[ T(n, m, K) = O\bigl((n + m) + K (n + m) \log n\bigr). \]

When \(K \le 10\) and \(n, m \le 2000\) (as in CF 164C — Machine Programming), this \(K\)-step DAG-initialized Dijkstra finishes in under \(5\text{ ms}\), whereas naive SPFA-based MCMF risks TLE on adversarial anti-SPFA DAGs!

4. 2D Segment Projection & The Coordinate Doubling Trick \(x \mapsto (2x, 2x+1)\) (最长k可重线段集 #147)

What happens when the objects we are selecting are 2D line segments in the plane \(\mathbb{R}^2\), and the constraint forbids any vertical line \(x = x_0\) (\(x_0 \in \mathbb{R}\)) from intersecting more than \(K\) selected segments? This is the classic [Luogu P3357] 最长k可重线段集问题 (#147):

  • We are given \(m\) line segments in \(\mathbb{R}^2\), where segment \(j\) connects \((x_{j,1}, y_{j,1})\) to \((x_{j,2}, y_{j,2})\) with integer coordinates and weight \(w_j = \lfloor \sqrt{(x_{j,1} - x_{j,2})^2 + (y_{j,1} - y_{j,2})^2} \rfloor > 0\).
  • No vertical line \(x = x_0\) (\(x_0 \in \mathbb{R}\)) may intersect more than \(K\) selected segments.

4.1 Why Naive \(x\)-Projection Fails Catastrophically (FM-19b)

A tempting first thought is: “Since the constraint only cares about vertical lines \(x = x_0\), the \(y\)-coordinates only affect the weight \(w_j\)! Let’s project every 2D segment onto its \(x\)-interval \([\min(x_{j,1}, x_{j,2}), \max(x_{j,1}, x_{j,2})]\) and run Luogu P3358!”

If you do this naively by adding an edge \(x_{j,1} \to x_{j,2}\) (assuming \(x_{j,1} \le x_{j,2}\)), two fatal bugs occur simultaneously:

  1. The Vertical Segment Self-Loop Bug (\(x_{j,1} = x_{j,2}\)):
    • A vertical segment from \((x, y_1)\) to \((x, y_2)\) (\(y_1 \ne y_2\)) has positive length \(w_j = |y_1 - y_2| > 0\), yet its \(x\)-projection is the degenerate single point \([x, x]\) (\(x_{j,1} = x_{j,2} = x\)).
    • If you add an edge \(x_{j,1} \to x_{j,2}\), it becomes a self-loop \(x \to x\) with negative cost \(-w_j < 0\)!
    • A flow unit on \(S \to \dots \to x \to \dots \to T\) does not need to traverse a self-loop \(x \to x\) to satisfy flow conservation, so SSP Dijkstra ignores it; conversely, in a circulation solver, a negative self-loop is a negative cycle of length \(1\) whose capacity does not interact with the backbone capacity \(K\) at all! Thus \(K + 1\) vertical segments at the same \(x\) could all be selected simultaneously, or none of them could ever be selected along an \(S \to T\) path!
  2. The Closed-Endpoint Collision Bug (\(x_{a,2} = x_{b,1}\)):
    • Even if there are no vertical segments, if segments are closed (so \((1, 0)\text{--}(2, 0)\) and \((2, 1)\text{--}(3, 1)\) both intersect the vertical line \(x = 2\)), mapping them to \(1 \to 2\) and \(2 \to 3\) allows the same single unit of flow to traverse \(1 \to 2 \to 3\), counting them as only \(1\) segment at \(x = 2\) when in fact two segments intersect the vertical line \(x = 2\)!

4.2 The Coordinate Doubling Compiler: Splitting Points vs. Unit Intervals

To distinguish intersection with a vertical line \(x = c\) at an integer coordinate from intersection with a vertical line \(x = c + 0.5\) strictly between integers, we split every integer coordinate \(x \in \mathbb{Z}\) into two points:

\[ x \quad \longmapsto \quad \bigl(2x, \; 2x + 1\bigr), \]

where we think of \(2x\) as \(x^-\) (just to the left of \(x\)) and \(2x + 1\) as \(x^+\) (just to the right of \(x\)):

  • The elementary step \(2x \to 2x + 1\) represents crossing the exact integer vertical line \(x\) (the singleton closed interval \([x, x]\)).
  • The elementary step \(2x + 1 \to 2(x + 1)\) represents crossing the open unit interval \((x, x + 1)\) between integers \(x\) and \(x + 1\).

This gives a clean, mechanical compilation table for both endpoint conventions encountered on online judges:

Endpoint Convention Geometric Meaning of Segment \(j\) with \(x_1 \le x_2\) Vertical Segment (\(x_1 = x_2 = x\)) Non-Vertical Segment (\(x_1 < x_2\))
Convention 1: Closed Segments (every segment includes both endpoints \([x_1, x_2]\)) Covers all \(x_0 \in [x_1, x_2]\), including \(x_0 = x_1\) and \(x_0 = x_2\) \(\mathbf{2x \to 2x + 1}\) (covers \([x, x]\)) \(\mathbf{2x_1 \to 2x_2 + 1}\) (covers \([x_1, x_2]\))

WarningJudge Convention Alert for Luogu P3357 (最长k可重线段集问题)

In Luogu P3357 (from the classic Network Flow 24 Problems suite), the problem statement says “segments are open segments (开线段)”, which creates a famous logical paradox if taken literally: an open vertical segment \(((x, y_1), (x, y_2))\) still intersects the vertical line \(x_0 = x\) on the open \(y\)-interval \((y_1, y_2)\), whereas an open non-vertical segment \(((x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2))\) with \(x_1 < x_2\) intersects vertical lines \(x_0 \in (x_1, x_2)\) but not \(x_0 = x_1\) or \(x_0 = x_2\)!

Coordinate doubling \(x \mapsto (2x, 2x+1)\) resolves this paradox effortlessly:

  • Under Convention 2 (Open Segments, P3357):
    • A vertical segment at \(x_1 = x_2 = x\) intersects the vertical line \(x_0 = x\), so it spans \(\mathbf{2x \to 2x + 1}\).
    • An open non-vertical segment with \(x_1 < x_2\) intersects vertical lines \(x_0 \in (x_1, x_2)\) (strictly after \(x_1\) and strictly before \(x_2\)), so it starts at \(x_1^+ = \mathbf{2x_1 + 1}\) and ends at \(x_2^- = \mathbf{2x_2}\)!
    • Check: Can a vertical segment at \(x\) (\(2x \to 2x + 1\)) and an open non-vertical segment starting at \(x\) (\(2x + 1 \to 2x_2\)) share the same unit of flow? Yes, at node \(2x + 1\), because the open segment \((x, x_2)\) does not contain \(x\), so no single vertical line intersects both!
    • Can two vertical segments at \(x\) (both \(2x \to 2x + 1\)) share the same unit of flow? No, because both traverse the segment \([2x, 2x+1)\) in parallel!
    • Can a vertical segment at \(x\) (\(2x \to 2x + 1\)) and an open non-vertical segment \((x_1, x_2)\) that straddles \(x\) (\(x_1 < x < x_2\), i.e., \(2x_1 + 1 \to 2x_2\) with \(2x_1 + 1 < 2x < 2x + 1 < 2x_2\)) share the same unit of flow? No, because both intervals cover the elementary slice \([2x, 2x + 1)\)!

4.3 Concrete Toy Trace: Coordinate Doubling in Action

Suppose \(K = 1\) and we have three segments around \(x \in \{1, 2\}\):

  1. Segment 1 (Vertical at \(x = 1\)): \((1, 0)\text{--}(1, 5)\), weight \(w_1 = 5\).
  2. Segment 2 (Vertical at \(x = 1\)): \((1, 2)\text{--}(1, 6)\), weight \(w_2 = 4\).
  3. Segment 3 (Non-vertical from \(x = 1\) to \(x = 2\)): \((1, 0)\text{--}(2, 0)\), weight \(w_3 = 1\).

Compare how the two conventions compile these three segments onto the doubled coordinate spine \(2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 5\) (representing \(1^- \to 1^+ \to 2^- \to 2^+\)):

  • Under Convention 1 (Closed Segments — endpoints count as intersections):
    • Segment 1 (\(x = 1\)): arc \(2 \to 3\), cap \(1\), cost \(-5\).
    • Segment 2 (\(x = 1\)): arc \(2 \to 3\), cap \(1\), cost \(-4\).
    • Segment 3 (\([1, 2]\) closed): arc \(2 \to 5\), cap \(1\), cost \(-1\).
    • Notice that all three segments cover the elementary slice \([2, 3)\) (the vertical line \(x = 1\)). With \(K = 1\), at most one of \(\{1, 2, 3\}\) can be chosen, and the optimal flow of \(1\) unit chooses \(2 \xrightarrow{\text{Seg 1}} 3 \to 4 \to 5\) of weight \(5\).
  • Under Convention 2 (Open Segments — Luogu P3357):
    • Segment 1 (\(x = 1\)): arc \(2 \to 3\), cap \(1\), cost \(-5\).
    • Segment 2 (\(x = 1\)): arc \(2 \to 3\), cap \(1\), cost \(-4\).
    • Segment 3 (\((1, 2)\) open): arc \(3 \to 4\), cap \(1\), cost \(-1\).
    • Now Segments 1 and 2 still compete on slice \([2, 3)\) (line \(x = 1\)), so we cannot pick both. However, Segment 1 (\(2 \to 3\)) and Segment 3 (\(3 \to 4\)) touch at node \(3\) (\(x = 1^+\)) without overlapping on any slice! The single unit of flow routes \(2 \xrightarrow{\text{Seg 1}} 3 \xrightarrow{\text{Seg 3}} 4 \to 5\), achieving total weight \(5 + 1 = 6\).

5.2 LP Dual Transposition: When Intervals Live on Rows ([ZJOI2013 / Luogu P3337] 防守战线)

Suppose we are given \(n\) positions \(i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), where placing a defense tower at position \(i\) costs \(a_i\) per tower (up to \(C_i\) towers, or \(C_i = 1\)), and \(m\) interval requirements stating that the total number of towers in interval \([l_j, r_j]\) must be at least \(D_j\):

\[ \text{Minimize} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i d_i \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{i=l_j}^{r_j} d_i \ge D_j \quad (j = 1, \dots, m), \qquad 0 \le d_i \le C_i. \]

  • When \(n\) is small (\(n \le 1000\)) and \(m \le 1000\): Notice that variable \(d_i\) appears in constraint \(j\) iff \(l_j \le i \le r_j\). If \(m\) constraints have arbitrary intervals \([l_j, r_j]\), the columns \(d_i\) do not have consecutive ones unless the intervals \([l_j, r_j]\) have fixed length \(k\) (sliding windows)! Instead, the rows \(j\) have consecutive ones over the columns \(i \in [l_j, r_j]\)!
  • Can we use Prefix Sums \(P_i = \sum_{t=1}^i d_t\) (Difference Constraints)? Only when \(a_i\) is uniform! When each position \(i\) has an arbitrary unit cost \(a_i\), shortest-path difference constraints cannot express \(\min \sum_{i=1}^n a_i (P_i - P_{i-1})\).
  • The LP Dual Transposition Trick: Because a matrix \(A\) is Totally Unimodular if and only if its transpose \(A^T\) is Totally Unimodular (Ch 18), taking the Linear Programming Dual transposes the constraint matrix \(A \mapsto A^T\), moving the consecutive-ones intervals \([l_j, r_j]\) from the rows to the columns!

Specifically, associating dual variables \(y_j \ge 0\) with the interval constraints \(\sum_{i=l_j}^{r_j} d_i \ge D_j\) and dual variables \(z_i \ge 0\) with the upper-bound constraints \(-d_i \ge -C_i\), the LP Dual of the above problem is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Maximize} \quad & \sum_{j=1}^{m} D_j y_j - \sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i z_i \\ \text{subject to} \quad & \sum_{j \,:\, l_j \le i \le r_j} y_j - z_i \le a_i, \qquad i = 1, 2, \dots, n, \\ & y_j \ge 0, \quad z_i \ge 0. \end{aligned} \]

Look at the dual constraint for each position \(i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\): the dual variable \(y_j\) appears in row \(i\) iff \(i \in [l_j, r_j]\)—which is a contiguous range of rows! Adding an unused-slack variable \(s_i \ge 0\) gives \(\sum_{l_j \le i \le r_j} y_j - z_i + s_i = a_i\), which compiles via \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) directly into a \(n+1\)-vertex MCMF network!

5.3 Rooted Tree Equation Differencing (树上流守恒差分: \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_u = E_u - \sum_{v \in \text{child}(u)} E_v\))

The Consecutive-Ones equation-difference method is not restricted to 1D lines! Suppose we have a rooted tree \(T\) with root \(r\) (append a virtual super-root \(0 = \text{parent}(r)\) with equation \(E_0 : 0 = 0\)), where each node \(u \in V(T)\) has a coverage requirement \(E_u : \sum_{j : u \in P_j} x_j - y_u = A_u\), and each available operation \(j\) covers a vertical ancestor-descendant chain \(P_j = \text{path}(p_j, q_j)\) where \(p_j\) is an ancestor of \(q_j\) ([LOJ #6405] 无源汇有上下界最小流 / 树上志愿者招募 (#152)).

If we tried to order the tree nodes linearly and take \(E_i - E_{i-1}\), a vertical path \(p_j \to q_j\) would break into \(\Theta(n)\) disjoint pieces. Instead, what is the natural tree generalization of \(E_i - E_{i-1}\)?

ImportantTheorem 19.3: Rooted Tree Equation Differencing (树上流守恒差分)

For every node \(u \in V(T) \cup \{0\}\) in a rooted tree (with virtual super-root \(0 = \text{parent}(r)\) and \(E_0 : 0 = 0\)), define the tree-differenced equation at node \(u\) by subtracting the sum of the equations of all direct children of \(u\) from \(E_u\):

\[ \Delta^{\text{tree}} E_u \;:=\; E_u - \sum_{v \in \text{child}(u)} E_v \qquad \bigl(\forall u \in V(T) \cup \{0\}\bigr). \]

Then for any vertical ancestor-descendant chain \(P_j = \text{path}(p_j, q_j)\) (where \(p_j\) is an ancestor of \(q_j\)):

  1. If \(u = q_j\) (the bottom endpoint of the chain), \(x_j\) appears in \(E_{q_j}\) (\(+1\)) and in none of \(q_j\)’s children (\(0\)), so its net coefficient in \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_{q_j}\) is \(+1\).
  2. If \(u \in P_j \setminus \{q_j\}\) (any interior node or \(p_j\) on the chain), \(x_j\) appears in \(E_u\) (\(+1\)) and in exactly one child \(v \in \text{child}(u)\) on the chain (\(+1\)), so its net coefficient in \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_u\) is \(1 - 1 = \mathbf{0}\)!
  3. If \(u = \text{parent}(p_j)\) (the strict parent of the top endpoint), \(x_j\) does not appear in \(E_{\text{parent}(p_j)}\) (\(0\)) but appears in the single child \(p_j \in \text{child}(\text{parent}(p_j))\) (\(+1\)), so its net coefficient in \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_{\text{parent}(p_j)}\) is \(0 - 1 = \mathbf{-1}\)!
  4. For all other nodes \(u\), \(x_j\) has coefficient \(\mathbf{0}\).

Therefore, under \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_u = E_u - \sum_{v \in \text{child}(u)} E_v\):

  • Every vertical path variable \(x_j\) on \(\text{path}(p_j, q_j)\) compiles to a single directed arc from the bottom endpoint to the parent of the top endpoint:

    \[ e_{x_j} = \bigl(q_j \to \text{parent}(p_j)\bigr), \qquad \text{cap} = u_j, \qquad \text{cost} = c_j. \]

  • Every surplus slack variable \(-y_u\) (a chain of length \(1\) with \(p = q = u\), appearing as \(-y_u\) in \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_u\) and \(+y_u\) in \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_{\text{parent}(u)}\)) compiles to a downward tree arc:

    \[ e_{y_u} = \bigl(\text{parent}(u) \to u\bigr), \qquad \text{cap} = \infty, \qquad \text{cost} = 0. \]

  • Every node \(u \in V(T) \cup \{0\}\) has net supply/demand:

    \[ b_u = A_u - \sum_{v \in \text{child}(u)} A_v \qquad (A_0 = 0). \]

Look how clean Theorem 19.3 is: when the tree \(T\) is a 1D chain \(0 \gets 1 \gets 2 \gets \dots \gets n\) (where \(\text{parent}(i) = i - 1\) and \(\text{child}(i - 1) = \{i\}\)), Theorem 19.3 reduces immediately to \(\Delta E_{i-1} = E_{i-1} - E_i\) (the exact sign-flipped form of Theorem 19.1)!

5.4 The Tractability Boundary: Multi-Period Coupling ([ONTAK 2010] Vacation) vs. Equal-Flow NP-Hardness (FM-19d)

What happens if a decision variable \(x_j\) appears in two or more disjoint contiguous blocks of equations \([l_{j,1}, r_{j,1}] \cup [l_{j,2}, r_{j,2}]\)?

  • In General: NP-Hard (The Equal-Flow Boundary FM-19d): If \(x_j\) covers \([l_{j,1}, r_{j,1}]\) and \([l_{j,2}, r_{j,2}]\) (\(r_{j,1} + 1 < l_{j,2}\)), taking \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) leaves four non-zero coefficients for \(x_j\): \(+1\) at \(l_{j,1}\), \(-1\) at \(r_{j,1} + 1\), \(+1\) at \(l_{j,2}\), and \(-1\) at \(r_{j,2} + 1\). In graph terms, \(x_j\) would have to simultaneously send equal flow along two separate arcs \((l_{j,1} \to r_{j,1} + 1)\) and \((l_{j,2} \to r_{j,2} + 1)\). This is the Integer Equal-Flow Problem, which is strongly NP-hard (reducing from 3-SAT / Exact Cover).
  • The Remarkable Exception — Coupled Two-Scale Sliding Windows ([ONTAK 2010 / BZOJ 3550] Vacation #152): Suppose \(N = 3K\) days are divided into \(3\) consecutive blocks of length \(K\) (\([1, K]\), \([K+1, 2K]\), \([2K+1, 3K]\)), and we must satisfy both:
    1. Every sliding window of length \(K\) contains at most \(L_1\) chosen elements: \(\sum_{t=i}^{i+K-1} x_t \le L_1\) for \(i = 1, \dots, 2K + 1\).
    2. The entire \(3K\)-day horizon \([1, 3K]\) contains at most \(L_2\) chosen elements: \(\sum_{t=1}^{3K} x_t \le L_2\).
    Even though this looks like two different window sizes (\(K\) and \(3K\)), notice that for the middle block \(t \in [K+1, 2K]\), variable \(x_t\) affects windows \(i \in [t - K + 1, t]\), whereas for \(t \in [1, K]\) and \(t \in [2K+1, 3K]\), pairing \(x_t\) (\(t \le K\)), \(x_{t+K}\) (\(K < t+K \le 2K\)), and \(x_{t+2K}\) (\(2K < t+2K \le 3K\)) along residue classes modulo \(K\) allows the global constraint \(\sum_{t=1}^{3K} x_t \le L_2\) to act as a single \(L_2\)-capacity choke edge in the flow network! We will derive the exact algebraic compilation of BZOJ 3550 (#152) in Section 6.8 below.

6.1 Problem #145 (SS-1): [Luogu P3358] 最长k可重区间集问题 (\(K\)-Overlapping Intervals)

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P3358
  • Difficulty Tier: Stepping Stone (SS-1)
  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 500\) open intervals \(I_j = (L_j, R_j)\), \(1 \le K \le 3\), \(0 \le L_j < R_j \le 10^5\). Weight of interval \(j\) is its length \(w_j = R_j - L_j\). Maximize the total length of selected intervals such that no real point \(x \in \mathbb{R}\) is covered by more than \(K\) selected intervals.

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

  • Open intervals \((L_j, R_j)\) with weight \(w_j = R_j - L_j\) and point overlap cap \(K\). Since \(K > 1\), greedy fails, and since \(L_j, R_j \le 10^5\) with \(n \le 500\), coordinate compression reduces the active coordinates to \(V \le 2n \le 1000\) vertices.

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

  • Compress the distinct endpoints \(\{L_j, R_j\}\) into \(1 < 2 < \dots < M\) (\(M \le 2n\)).

  • For each elementary half-open segment \([i, i+1)\) (\(i = 1, \dots, M-1\)) and unused capacity slack \(y_i \ge 0\):

    \[ E_i : \quad \sum_{j \,:\, l_j \le i < r_j} x_j + y_i = K \qquad (i = 1, \dots, M-1), \qquad \min \sum_{j=1}^n (-w_j) x_j. \]

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Mechanical Compilation)

  • Taking \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) (\(i = 1, \dots, M\) with \(E_0 = E_M = 0\)):
    • Source \(S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} 1\) (since \(b_1 = +K\)) and \(M \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} T\) (since \(b_M = -K\)).
    • For each slack \(y_i\) (\(1 \le i < M\)): forward backbone edge \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } 0} i + 1\).
    • For each interval \(j\) (\(1 \le j \le n\)): forward bypass edge \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -w_j} r_j\).
  • Because all initial edges go from smaller index to larger index (\(u < v\)), we initialize \(\pi_0\) in \(O(M + n)\) time via topological DAG DP and run \(K\) steps of Dijkstra SSP.

Step 4: Toy Trace & Boundary Verification

  • Suppose \(K = 1\) and two intervals are \((1, 3)\) (weight \(2\)) and \((3, 5)\) (weight \(2\)). Compressed coordinates: \(1 \mapsto 1, 3 \mapsto 2, 5 \mapsto 3\). Arcs: \(1 \to 2\) (cost \(-2\)) and \(2 \to 3\) (cost \(-2\)). Because the intervals are open, they touch at vertex \(2\) (\(x = 3\)) and a single unit of flow traverses \(1 \to 2 \to 3\) for total weight \(4\).
NoteProgressive Hints for Luogu P3358 (#145)
  • Hint 1 (Coordinate Compression & Touching Endpoints): Because the intervals are open \((L_j, R_j)\), two intervals \((1, 3)\) and \((3, 5)\) can both be chosen by the same track of flow. Coordinate-compress all \(L_j\) and \(R_j\) values directly without shifting \(R_j\).
  • Hint 2 (Algebraic-to-Graph Compilation): Write \(E_i : \sum_{l_j \le i < r_j} x_j + y_i = K\) for each elementary segment \([i, i+1)\). Subtracting \(E_i - E_{i-1}\) gives \(l_j \to r_j\) (cap \(1\), cost \(-(R_j - L_j)\)) and backbone \(i \to i+1\) (cap \(K\), cost \(0\)).
  • Hint 3 (Anti-SPFA Potential Initialization): Since every edge satisfies \(u < v\), compute \(\pi_0[v] = \min(\pi_0[v-1], \min_{r_j = v} (\pi_0[l_j] - w_j))\) in a single loop for \(v = 1 \dots M\), then run \(K\) steps of Dijkstra.
NoteComplete C++ Solution — Luogu P3358 (#145) 
// Luogu P3358 — 最长k可重区间集问题 (#145)
// Method: Equation Differencing + O(N+M) DAG DP Potential Initialization + K-step Dijkstra MCMF
#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

const long long INF = 1e18;

struct Edge {
    int to, rev, cap;
    long long cost;
};

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);

    int n, K;
    if (!(cin >> n >> K)) return 0;

    vector<long long> L(n), R(n), W(n), coords;
    for (int i = 0; i < n; ++i) {
        cin >> L[i] >> R[i];
        if (L[i] > R[i]) swap(L[i], R[i]);
        W[i] = R[i] - L[i];
        coords.push_back(L[i]);
        coords.push_back(R[i]);
    }

    sort(coords.begin(), coords.end());
    coords.erase(unique(coords.begin(), coords.end()), coords.end());
    int M = (int)coords.size();

    // Vertices: 0 = S, 1..M = compressed coordinates, M+1 = T
    int S = 0, T = M + 1, V = M + 2;
    vector<vector<Edge>> adj(V);
    auto add_edge = [&](int u, int v, int cap, long long cost) {
        adj[u].push_back({v, (int)adj[v].size(), cap, cost});
        adj[v].push_back({u, (int)adj[u].size() - 1, 0, -cost});
    };

    // Source and Sink supplies/demands from b_1 = +K, b_M = -K
    add_edge(S, 1, K, 0);
    add_edge(M, T, K, 0);

    // Forward slack backbone arcs y_i: i -> i + 1
    for (int i = 1; i < M; ++i) {
        add_edge(i, i + 1, K, 0);
    }

    // Interval bypass arcs x_j: l_j -> r_j
    vector<long long> pi(V, INF);
    pi[S] = 0;
    pi[1] = 0;
    vector<vector<pair<int, long long>>> dag_edges(M + 1);
    for (int i = 0; i < n; ++i) {
        int l = (int)(lower_bound(coords.begin(), coords.end(), L[i]) - coords.begin()) + 1;
        int r = (int)(lower_bound(coords.begin(), coords.end(), R[i]) - coords.begin()) + 1;
        add_edge(l, r, 1, -W[i]);
        dag_edges[l].push_back({r, -W[i]});
    }

    // O(N + M) Topological DAG DP to initialize Johnson potentials pi[]
    for (int u = 1; u <= M; ++u) {
        if (u < M) pi[u + 1] = min(pi[u + 1], pi[u]);
        for (auto [v, c] : dag_edges[u]) {
            pi[v] = min(pi[v], pi[u] + c);
        }
    }
    pi[T] = pi[M];

    // Run at most K iterations of Dijkstra SSP
    long long min_cost = 0;
    vector<long long> dist(V);
    vector<int> pv(V), pe(V);

    while (true) {
        fill(dist.begin(), dist.end(), INF);
        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
        dist[S] = 0;
        pq.push({0, S});

        while (!pq.empty()) {
            auto [d, u] = pq.top();
            pq.pop();
            if (d != dist[u]) continue;
            for (int i = 0; i < (int)adj[u].size(); ++i) {
                const auto& e = adj[u][i];
                if (e.cap > 0) {
                    long long nd = d + e.cost + pi[u] - pi[e.to];
                    if (nd < dist[e.to]) {
                        dist[e.to] = nd;
                        pv[e.to] = u;
                        pe[e.to] = i;
                        pq.push({nd, e.to});
                    }
                }
            }
        }

        if (dist[T] == INF) break;
        for (int v = 0; v < V; ++v) {
            if (dist[v] < INF) pi[v] += dist[v];
        }
        // If shortest path has >= 0 true cost, we still push because S->1->...->M->T has 0 cost slack
        int pushed = K;
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            pushed = min(pushed, adj[pv[v]][pe[v]].cap);
        }
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            auto& e = adj[pv[v]][pe[v]];
            e.cap -= pushed;
            adj[v][e.rev].cap += pushed;
        }
        min_cost += 1LL * pushed * pi[T];
    }

    cout << -min_cost << "\n";
    return 0;
}

6.2 Problem #146 (SS-2): [POJ 3680] Intervals & [CF 164C] Machine Programming

  • Judge URL: http://poj.org/problem?id=3680 / https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/164/C
  • Difficulty Tier: Stepping Stone (SS-2)
  • Constraints:
    • POJ 3680: \(T_{\text{cases}} \le 200\), \(N \le 200\) open intervals \((a_j, b_j)\) with arbitrary positive weights \(w_j \le 10^5\), \(K \le 10^5\) (\(a_j < b_j \le 10^5\)). Maximize total weight of selected intervals with point overlap \(\le K\).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

  • Identical mathematical structure to #145, except the interval profits \(w_j\) are arbitrary (decoupled from interval length \(b_j - a_j\)), and in CF 164C we must also reconstruct the exact binary indicators \(x_j \in \{0, 1\}\) from the residual capacities of the bypass edges \(e_{x_j} = (l_j \to r_j)\).

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation

  • After coordinate-compressing the \(2N\) endpoints into \(1 < 2 < \dots < M\), each interval \(j\) covers elementary segments \(i \in \{l_j, \dots, r_j - 1\}\).
  • Introducing slack \(y_i \ge 0\) for each segment \(i \in \{1, \dots, M-1\}\) gives \(E_i : \sum_{l_j \le i < r_j} x_j + y_i = K\), with objective \(\min \sum_{j=1}^N (-w_j) x_j\).

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • Taking \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) produces:
    • \(S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} 1\) and \(M \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} T\).
    • Slack arcs \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} i + 1\) for \(1 \le i < M\).
    • Decision arcs \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -w_j} r_j\) for \(1 \le j \le N\).
  • Choice Reconstruction: After the MCMF terminates, interval \(j\) is selected (\(x_j = 1\)) if and only if the residual capacity of its forward bypass edge \(l_j \to r_j\) is \(0\) (equivalently, its reverse edge has residual capacity \(1\)).

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P3357
  • Difficulty Tier: Intermediate (Int-1)
  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 500\) open 2D segments \(((x_{j,1}, y_{j,1}), (x_{j,2}, y_{j,2}))\), \(1 \le K \le 10\), integer coordinates \(|x|, |y| \le 10^5\).   • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P3980
  • Difficulty Tier: Intermediate (Int-2, Chapter Flagship)
  • Constraints: \(n \le 1000\) days, \(m \le 10000\) volunteer types. Day \(i\) requires at least \(A_i\) volunteers (\(0 \le A_i \le 2^{31}-1\)). Volunteer type \(j\) works from day \(l_j\) to day \(r_j\) inclusive at cost \(c_j \ge 0\) per person (unlimited supply per type; in the bounded variant Luogu P4311 / BZOJ 1061, type \(j\) has an upper limit \(u_j\) on the number of volunteers). Summary of the 4-Step Compilation (Derived in Full in Sections 1 & 2.1)

  1. Equality System: \(\sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j - y_i = A_i\) (\(i = 1, \dots, n\)), with \(E_0 : 0 = 0\) and \(E_{n+1} : 0 = 0\).
  2. Consecutive Differences \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) (\(i = 1, \dots, n + 1\)):
    • Nodal balance \(b_i = A_i - A_{i-1}\) (\(A_0 = A_{n+1} = 0\)): if \(b_i > 0\), add \(S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } b_i, \text{cost } 0} i\); if \(b_i < 0\), add \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } -b_i, \text{cost } 0} T\).
    • Volunteer arc \(x_j\): \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } \infty \text{ (or } u_j\text{)}, \text{cost } c_j} r_j + 1\).
    • Backward surplus slack arc \(y_i\): \(i + 1 \xrightarrow{\text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } 0} i\) for \(i = 1, \dots, n\).
  3. Why \(\pi_0 \equiv 0\) Works Immediately for Dijkstra: Even though the network has directed cycles (\(l_j \to r_j + 1 \to \dots \to l_j\)), every initial edge has cost \(c_j \ge 0\) or \(0\), so \(\pi_0(v) = 0\) satisfies \(w(u, v) + \pi_0(u) - \pi_0(v) \ge 0\) everywhere!
NoteProgressive Hints for Luogu P3980 [NOI2008 志愿者招募] (#148)
  • Hint 1: Don’t forget node \(n + 1\)! Because \(E_{n+1} - E_n\) has right-hand side \(A_{n+1} - A_n = 0 - A_n = -A_n\), node \(n + 1\) always has an edge to \(T\) of capacity \(A_n\).
  • Hint 2: The slack variable \(y_i \ge 0\) has coefficient \(-1\) in \(E_i\), so in \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) it has coefficient \(-1\) (entering node \(i\)) and in \(\Delta E_{i+1} = E_{i+1} - E_i\) it has coefficient \(+1\) (leaving node \(i+1\)). Thus the slack edge goes backward from \(i + 1 \to i\).
  • Hint 3: Since all initial costs are non-negative, initialize pi to all zeros and run pure Dijkstra + Primal-Dual augmentations for fast execution.
NoteComplete C++ Solution — Luogu P3980 [NOI2008 志愿者招募] (#148) 
// Luogu P3980 [NOI2008] — 志愿者招募 (Volunteer Recruitment) (#148)
// Method: Consecutive Equation Differencing (\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}) + Primal-Dual Dijkstra MCMF
#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

const long long INF = 4e18;

struct Edge {
    int to, rev;
    long long cap, cost;
};

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);

    int n, m;
    if (!(cin >> n >> m)) return 0;

    vector<long long> A(n + 2, 0);
    for (int i = 1; i <= n; ++i) {
        cin >> A[i];
    }

    int S = 0, T = n + 2, V = n + 3;
    vector<vector<Edge>> adj(V);
    auto add_edge = [&](int u, int v, long long cap, long long cost) {
        adj[u].push_back({v, (int)adj[v].size(), cap, cost});
        adj[v].push_back({u, (int)adj[u].size() - 1, 0, -cost});
    };

    // 1. Supply/demand edges from b_i = A_i - A_{i-1} for i = 1..n+1
    for (int i = 1; i <= n + 1; ++i) {
        long long b = A[i] - A[i - 1];
        if (b > 0) {
            add_edge(S, i, b, 0);
        } else if (b < 0) {
            add_edge(i, T, -b, 0);
        }
    }

    // 2. Backward surplus slack spine y_i: (i + 1) -> i with cap INF, cost 0
    for (int i = 1; i <= n; ++i) {
        add_edge(i + 1, i, INF, 0);
    }

    // 3. Volunteer interval edges x_j: l_j -> r_j + 1 with cap INF, cost c_j
    for (int j = 0; j < m; ++j) {
        int l, r;
        long long c;
        cin >> l >> r >> c;
        add_edge(l, r + 1, INF, c);
    }

    // All initial edge costs are >= 0, so pi_0 == 0 is immediately valid!
    vector<long long> pi(V, 0), dist(V);
    vector<int> cur(V);
    vector<bool> vis(V);
    long long min_cost = 0;

    auto dijkstra = [&]() -> bool {
        fill(dist.begin(), dist.end(), INF);
        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
        dist[S] = 0;
        pq.push({0, S});
        while (!pq.empty()) {
            auto [d, u] = pq.top();
            pq.pop();
            if (d != dist[u]) continue;
            for (const auto& e : adj[u]) {
                if (e.cap > 0) {
                    long long nd = d + e.cost + pi[u] - pi[e.to];
                    if (nd < dist[e.to]) {
                        dist[e.to] = nd;
                        pq.push({nd, e.to});
                    }
                }
            }
        }
        if (dist[T] == INF) return false;
        for (int v = 0; v < V; ++v) {
            if (dist[v] < INF) pi[v] += dist[v];
        }
        return true;
    };

    auto dfs = [&](auto& self, int u, long long pushed) -> long long {
        if (u == T || pushed == 0) return pushed;
        vis[u] = true;
        long long total = 0;
        for (int& i = cur[u]; i < (int)adj[u].size(); ++i) {
            auto& e = adj[u][i];
            if (!vis[e.to] && e.cap > 0 && pi[u] + e.cost - pi[e.to] == 0) {
                long long tr = self(self, e.to, min(pushed, e.cap));
                if (tr > 0) {
                    e.cap -= tr;
                    adj[e.to][e.rev].cap += tr;
                    pushed -= tr;
                    total += tr;
                    min_cost += tr * e.cost;
                    if (pushed == 0) break;
                }
            }
        }
        vis[u] = false;
        return total;
    };

    while (dijkstra()) {
        fill(cur.begin(), cur.end(), 0);
        fill(vis.begin(), vis.end(), false);
        while (dfs(dfs, S, INF) > 0) {}
    }

    cout << min_cost << "\n";
    return 0;
}

  • Difficulty Tier: Intermediate (Int-3)
  • Constraints:
    • CF 717G (Underfail): A string \(S\) of length \(n \le 500\), \(m \le 100\) dictionary words \((p_d, w_d)\) with profit \(w_d\), and an integer \(x \le 100\). Each occurrence of \(p_d\) in \(S\) at \([i, i + |p_d| - 1]\) can be chosen at most once, and each character index \(t \in \{1, \dots, n\}\) can be covered by at most \(x\) chosen word occurrences. Maximize total profit.
    • CF 1288F (Red-Blue Graph): Bipartite graph \((N_1, N_2, M \le 200)\) where each edge \(e\) can be colored Red (\(x_e = 1\), cost \(R\)), Blue (\(y_e = 1\), cost \(B\)), or Uncolored (\(x_e = y_e = 0\), cost \(0\)), subject to vertex color-difference constraints \(|d_R(v) - d_B(v)| \ge 1\) for colored vertices (R requires \(d_R(v) - d_B(v) \ge 1\); B requires \(d_B(v) - d_R(v) \ge 1\)).

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

  • In CF 717G, finding all substring matches of each dictionary word \(p_d\) in \(S\) yields at most \(n \cdot m \le 5 \times 10^4\) closed character intervals \([l_j, r_j] = [i, i + |p_d| - 1]\) with weight \(w_d\), where no character index \(t \in [1, n]\) may be covered more than \(x\) times.
  • In CF 1288F, each edge \(e = (u, v)\) (\(u \in V_1, v \in V_2\)) contributes \(+1\) to \(d_R(u) - d_B(u)\) and \(d_R(v) - d_B(v)\) if colored Red, or \(-1\) if colored Blue. By shifting the baseline (say, pre-coloring or using lower-bound circulation [1, deg(v)] on nodal net flow), the two-sided inequality on each vertex’s net degree difference compiles into a single bounded-slack nodal conservation law (Ch 04 / Ch 17).

Step 2 & Step 3: Algebraic Compilation of Substring Interval Packing (CF 717G)

  • Each match of word \(p_d\) starting at \(1\)-based index \(i \in [1, n - |p_d| + 1]\) covers the closed character interval \([i, i + |p_d| - 1]\), which is the half-open interval \([i, i + |p_d|)\).
  • For each character \(t \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), \(\sum_{j : l_j \le t \le r_j} x_j + y_t = x\) (\(y_t \ge 0\)).
  • Taking \(\Delta E_t = E_t - E_{t-1}\) (\(t = 1, \dots, n + 1\)) compiles each match at \([i, i + |p_d| - 1]\) to the forward bypass arc \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -w_d} i + |p_d|\) and each slack \(y_t\) to \(t \xrightarrow{\text{cap } x, \text{cost } 0} t + 1\).
NoteProgressive Hints for CF 717G (#149)

// CF 717G — Underfail (#149)
// Method: Substring Match Interval Extraction + Equation Differencing + DAG DP Potentials + Dijkstra MCMF
#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

const long long INF = 1e18;

struct Edge {
    int to, rev, cap;
    long long cost;
};

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);

    int n;
    string s;
    if (!(cin >> n >> s)) return 0;

    int m;
    cin >> m;

    // Vertices: 0 = Source, 1..n+1 = string positions, n+2 = Sink
    int S = 0, T = n + 2, V = n + 3;
    vector<vector<Edge>> adj(V);
    auto add_edge = [&](int u, int v, int cap, long long cost) {
        adj[u].push_back({v, (int)adj[v].size(), cap, cost});
        adj[v].push_back({u, (int)adj[u].size() - 1, 0, -cost});
    };

    vector<vector<pair<int, long long>>> dag_edges(n + 2);
    for (int d = 0; d < m; ++d) {
        string p;
        long long w;
        cin >> p >> w;
        int len = (int)p.size();
        for (int i = 0; i + len <= n; ++i) {
            if (s.compare(i, len, p) == 0) {
                int u = i + 1, v = i + len + 1;
                add_edge(u, v, 1, -w);
                dag_edges[u].push_back({v, -w});
            }
        }
    }

    int X;
    cin >> X;

    add_edge(S, 1, X, 0);
    add_edge(n + 1, T, X, 0);
    for (int i = 1; i <= n; ++i) {
        add_edge(i, i + 1, X, 0);
    }

    vector<long long> pi(V, INF);
    pi[S] = pi[1] = 0;
    for (int u = 1; u <= n + 1; ++u) {
        if (u <= n) pi[u + 1] = min(pi[u + 1], pi[u]);
        for (auto [v, c] : dag_edges[u]) {
            pi[v] = min(pi[v], pi[u] + c);
        }
    }
    pi[T] = pi[n + 1];

    long long min_cost = 0;
    vector<long long> dist(V);
    vector<int> pv(V), pe(V);
    while (true) {
        fill(dist.begin(), dist.end(), INF);
        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
        dist[S] = 0;
        pq.push({0, S});
        while (!pq.empty()) {
            auto [d, u] = pq.top();
            pq.pop();
            if (d != dist[u]) continue;
            for (int i = 0; i < (int)adj[u].size(); ++i) {
                const auto& e = adj[u][i];
                if (e.cap > 0) {
                    long long nd = d + e.cost + pi[u] - pi[e.to];
                    if (nd < dist[e.to]) {
                        dist[e.to] = nd;
                        pv[e.to] = u;
                        pe[e.to] = i;
                        pq.push({nd, e.to});
                    }
                }
            }
        }
        if (dist[T] == INF) break;
        for (int v = 0; v < V; ++v) {
            if (dist[v] < INF) pi[v] += dist[v];
        }
        int pushed = X;
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            pushed = min(pushed, adj[pv[v]][pe[v]].cap);
        }
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            auto& e = adj[pv[v]][pe[v]];
            e.cap -= pushed;
            adj[v][e.rev].cap += pushed;
        }
        min_cost += 1LL * pushed * pi[T];
    }

    cout << -min_cost << "\n";
    return 0;
}

6.6 Problem #150 (Adv-1): [BZOJ 1283] 序列 (Sequence Sliding-Window Sum)

  • Judge URL: https://hydro.ac/d/bzoj/p/1283
  • Difficulty Tier: Advanced (Adv-1)
  • Constraints: Sequence of \(n \le 1000\) positive integers \(c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n\). Select a subset of indices \(x_j \in \{0, 1\}\) to maximize \(\sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j\) such that every contiguous subsequence of length \(m\) contains at most \(K\) selected elements.

Step 1: Non-Graph Diagnostic Signal

  • Selecting point variables \(x_j \in \{0, 1\}\) subject to sliding-window upper bounds \(\sum_{j=i}^{i+m-1} x_j \le K\) for \(i = 1, \dots, W\) (\(W = n - m + 1\)). Note that if \(n \le m\), there is at most \(1\) window and we simply pick the \(K\) largest elements; otherwise \(W = n - m + 1 \ge 1\).

Step 2: Intermediate Algebraic Representation (Dualizing Points into Window Intervals)

  • Decision variable \(x_j\) (\(1 \le j \le n\)) appears in window equation \(E_i\) (\(1 \le i \le W\)) iff \(i \le j \le i + m - 1 \iff \max(1, j - m + 1) \le i \le \min(W, j)\).
  • Thus \(x_j\) covers the contiguous range of window equations \([l_j, r_j]\) where \(l_j = \max(1, j - m + 1)\) and \(r_j = \min(W, j)\).
  • Adding unused-capacity slack \(y_i \ge 0\) gives \(E_i : \sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j + y_i = K\) for \(i = 1, \dots, W\).

Step 3: Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • Taking \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) for \(i = 1, \dots, W + 1\) (with \(E_0 = E_{W+1} = 0\)):
    • Supply \(b_1 = +K \implies S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} 1\); Demand \(b_{W+1} = -K \implies W + 1 \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} T\).
    • Slack \(y_i\) (\(1 \le i \le W\)): forward spine arc \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } K, \text{cost } 0} i + 1\).
    • Point decision \(x_j\) (\(1 \le j \le n\)): forward bypass arc \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -c_j} r_j + 1\).
    • (Equivalent \(n+1\)-vertex formulation): You can also write the equation on all \(n\) positions where \(x_j\) spans \(j \to \min(n + 1, j + m)\)! Both networks are isomorphic because windows \(i > n - m + 1\) are simply contracted into vertex \(W + 1\).
NoteProgressive Hints for BZOJ 1283 (#150)

6.7 Problem #151 (Adv-2): [Luogu P6967 / ICPC NEERC 2016 D] Delight for a Cat

  • Judge URL: https://www.luogu.com.cn/problem/P6967 / https://codeforces.com/gym/101190
  • Difficulty Tier: Advanced (Adv-2)
  • Constraints: \(1 \le k \le n \le 1000\), \(0 \le m_s, m_e \le k\) with \(m_s + m_e \le k\). Arrays \(s_1, \dots, s_n\) and \(e_1, \dots, e_n\) (\(0 \le s_j, e_j \le 10^9\)). In every sliding window of length \(k\), at least \(m_s\) hours must be S (sleep) and at least \(m_e\) hours must be E (eat). Output the maximum total happiness and the length-\(n\) string of S/E choices.

Summary of the 4-Step Compilation (Derived in Full in Section 2.2)

  1. Baseline & Decision Variable: Start with all hours sleeping (\(S_{\text{base}} = \sum_{j=1}^n s_j\)). Let \(x_j \in \{0, 1\}\) indicate switching hour \(j\) to eating (E), with cost \(c_j = s_j - e_j\).
  2. Two-Sided Window Inequality: For each window \(i \in \{1, \dots, W\}\) (\(W = n - k + 1\)), \(L \le \sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j \le R\), where \(L = m_e\), \(R = k - m_s\), \(l_j = \max(1, j - k + 1)\), and \(r_j = \min(W, j)\).
  3. Variant A (Upper-Slack Forward-DAG Compilation):
    • Introduce bounded slack \(y_i \in [0, R - L]\) so \(\sum_{j : l_j \le i \le r_j} x_j + y_i = R\).
    • Taking \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) (\(i = 1, \dots, W + 1\)) produces a pure topological DAG on vertices \(1, \dots, W + 1\):
      • Supply \(S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } R, \text{cost } 0} 1\) and demand \(W + 1 \xrightarrow{\text{cap } R, \text{cost } 0} T\).
      • Bounded slack spine \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } R - L, \text{cost } 0} i + 1\) for \(i = 1, \dots, W\).
      • Decision arc \(l_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } s_j - e_j} r_j + 1\) for \(j = 1, \dots, n\).
  4. Why Variant A Beats SPFA: Because every edge \((u \to v)\) has \(u < v\) and \(R = k - m_s\) is the exact required flow from \(S\) to \(T\) (every feasible schedule corresponds to a flow of value \(R\)), we initialize \(\pi_0\) in \(O(n + W)\) via DAG DP and run Primal-Dual Dijkstra until \(R\) units of flow reach \(T\)!
NoteProgressive Hints for NEERC 2016 Delight for a Cat (#151)
  • Hint 1: Notice that the upper-slack variable \(y_i = R - \sum_{j=i}^{i+k-1} x_j\) must satisfy \(0 \le y_i \le R - L = (k - m_s) - m_e\). If you forget the upper capacity \(R - L\) on the slack edge \(i \to i + 1\), the lower bound \(\sum x_j \ge m_e\) will be violated!
  • Hint 2: Unlike #145 where positive-cost paths can bypass \(T\), here we must push all \(R = k - m_s\) units of flow from \(S\) to \(T\) even if a shortest path has positive cost \(s_j - e_j > 0\), because the cat is forced to eat at least \(m_e\) hours per window (and the slack arcs \(i \to i + 1\) only have capacity \(R - L\), forcing at least \(L = m_e\) units onto the \(x_j\) arcs!).

// Luogu P6967 [ICPC NEERC 2016 D] — Delight for a Cat (#151)
// Method: Two-Sided Sliding Window Upper-Slack Forward-DAG + DAG DP Potentials + Primal-Dual Dijkstra
#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

const long long INF = 4e18;

struct Edge {
    int to, rev, cap;
    long long cost;
};

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);

    int n, k, ms, me;
    if (!(cin >> n >> k >> ms >> me)) return 0;

    vector<long long> s(n + 1), e(n + 1);
    long long base_sum = 0;
    for (int j = 1; j <= n; ++j) {
        cin >> s[j];
        base_sum += s[j];
    }
    for (int j = 1; j <= n; ++j) {
        cin >> e[j];
    }

    int W = n - k + 1;
    int L_bound = me, R_bound = k - ms;
    int S = 0, T = W + 2, V = W + 3;

    vector<vector<Edge>> adj(V);
    auto add_edge = [&](int u, int v, int cap, long long cost) -> pair<int, int> {
        int idx = (int)adj[u].size();
        adj[u].push_back({v, (int)adj[v].size(), cap, cost});
        adj[v].push_back({u, idx, 0, -cost});
        return {u, idx};
    };

    // Total required flow is R_bound = k - ms
    add_edge(S, 1, R_bound, 0);
    add_edge(W + 1, T, R_bound, 0);

    // Bounded forward slack arcs y_i in [0, R_bound - L_bound]: i -> i + 1
    vector<vector<pair<int, long long>>> dag_edges(W + 2);
    for (int i = 1; i <= W; ++i) {
        if (R_bound - L_bound > 0) {
            add_edge(i, i + 1, R_bound - L_bound, 0);
            dag_edges[i].push_back({i + 1, 0});
        }
    }

    // Hour decision arcs x_j: l_j -> r_j + 1 with cap 1, cost s_j - e_j
    vector<pair<int, int>> hour_edge(n + 1);
    for (int j = 1; j <= n; ++j) {
        int l = max(1, j - k + 1);
        int r = min(W, j);
        long long cost = s[j] - e[j];
        hour_edge[j] = add_edge(l, r + 1, 1, cost);
        dag_edges[l].push_back({r + 1, cost});
    }

    // O(n + W) Topological DAG DP for initial Johnson potentials pi[]
    vector<long long> pi(V, INF);
    pi[S] = pi[1] = 0;
    for (int u = 1; u <= W + 1; ++u) {
        if (pi[u] == INF) continue;
        for (auto [v, c] : dag_edges[u]) {
            pi[v] = min(pi[v], pi[u] + c);
        }
    }
    pi[T] = pi[W + 1];

    long long min_cost = 0;
    vector<long long> dist(V);
    vector<int> cur(V);
    vector<bool> vis(V);

    auto dijkstra = [&]() -> bool {
        fill(dist.begin(), dist.end(), INF);
        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
        dist[S] = 0;
        pq.push({0, S});
        while (!pq.empty()) {
            auto [d, u] = pq.top();
            pq.pop();
            if (d != dist[u]) continue;
            for (const auto& ed : adj[u]) {
                if (ed.cap > 0) {
                    long long nd = d + ed.cost + pi[u] - pi[ed.to];
                    if (nd < dist[ed.to]) {
                        dist[ed.to] = nd;
                        pq.push({nd, ed.to});
                    }
                }
            }
        }
        if (dist[T] == INF) return false;
        for (int v = 0; v < V; ++v) {
            if (dist[v] < INF) pi[v] += dist[v];
        }
        return true;
    };

    auto dfs = [&](auto& self, int u, int pushed) -> int {
        if (u == T || pushed == 0) return pushed;
        vis[u] = true;
        int total = 0;
        for (int& i = cur[u]; i < (int)adj[u].size(); ++i) {
            auto& ed = adj[u][i];
            if (!vis[ed.to] && ed.cap > 0 && pi[u] + ed.cost - pi[ed.to] == 0) {
                int tr = self(self, ed.to, min(pushed, ed.cap));
                if (tr > 0) {
                    ed.cap -= tr;
                    adj[ed.to][ed.rev].cap += tr;
                    pushed -= tr;
                    total += tr;
                    min_cost += 1LL * tr * ed.cost;
                    if (pushed == 0) break;
                }
            }
        }
        vis[u] = false;
        return total;
    };

    while (dijkstra()) {
        fill(cur.begin(), cur.end(), 0);
        fill(vis.begin(), vis.end(), false);
        while (dfs(dfs, S, R_bound) > 0) {}
    }

    cout << base_sum - min_cost << "\n";
    string ans(n, 'S');
    for (int j = 1; j <= n; ++j) {
        auto [u, idx] = hour_edge[j];
        if (adj[u][idx].cap == 0) ans[j - 1] = 'E';
    }
    cout << ans << "\n";
    return 0;
}

Part A: 4-Step Compilation of BZOJ 3550 [ONTAK 2010] Vacation

  1. Diagnostic Signal: We are given \(N = 3K\) vacation days with profits \(c_1, \dots, c_{3K}\) and must choose \(x_j \in \{0, 1\}\) to maximize \(\sum_{j=1}^{3K} c_j x_j\) subject to the sliding-window upper bound \(\sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1} x_j \le L\) for every contiguous \(K\)-day window \(i = 1, 2, \dots, 2K + 1\).
  2. Intermediate Algebraic System:

    • Let \(W = 2K + 1\) be the number of sliding windows of length \(K\). Introducing unused-capacity slack variables \(y_i \ge 0\) for each window \(i \in \{1, \dots, 2K + 1\}\) yields:

      \[ E_i : \quad \sum_{j=i}^{i+K-1} x_j + y_i = L \qquad (i = 1, 2, \dots, 2K + 1), \qquad E_0 : 0 = 0, \quad E_{2K+2} : 0 = 0. \]

    • Notice how the \(3K\) decision variables partition cleanly into three consecutive blocks of length \(K\):

      • Period 1 (\(1 \le j \le K\)): \(x_j\) appears in windows \(E_1, \dots, E_j\) (entering at \(\Delta E_1\) and exiting at \(\Delta E_{j+1}\)).
      • Period 2 (\(K + 1 \le j \le 2K\)): \(x_j\) appears in windows \(E_{j-K+1}, \dots, E_j\) (entering at \(\Delta E_{j-K+1}\) and exiting at \(\Delta E_{j+1}\)).
      • Period 3 (\(2K + 1 \le j \le 3K\)): \(x_j\) appears in windows \(E_{j-K+1}, \dots, E_{2K+1}\) (entering at \(\Delta E_{j-K+1}\) and exiting at \(\Delta E_{2K+2}\)).
  3. Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad:
    • Taking \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) for \(i = 1, \dots, 2K + 2\) produces a 3-layer topological DAG on vertices \(1, \dots, 2K + 2\):
      • Supply \(b_1 = +L \implies S \xrightarrow{\text{cap } L, \text{cost } 0} 1\); Demand \(b_{2K+2} = -L \implies 2K + 2 \xrightarrow{\text{cap } L, \text{cost } 0} T\).
      • Slack spine \(y_i\) (\(1 \le i \le 2K + 1\)): \(i \xrightarrow{\text{cap } L, \text{cost } 0} i + 1\).
      • Day decision \(x_j\) (\(1 \le j \le 3K\)): \(\max(1, j - K + 1) \xrightarrow{\text{cap } 1, \text{cost } -c_j} \min(2K + 1, j) + 1\).

Part B: 4-Step Compilation of LOJ #6405 (Rooted Tree Equation Differencing 树上流守恒差分)

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Each vertex \(u \in \{2, \dots, n\}\) in a rooted tree (root \(1\)) has a coverage demand \(A_u \ge 0\) on the tree edge \((\text{parent}(u), u)\), and we are given \(m\) vertical ancestor-descendant paths \(P_j = \text{path}(p_j, q_j)\) (\(p_j\) is an ancestor of \(q_j\)) with cost \(c_j\) (plus the ability to add extra flow along any root-to-vertex or downward tree edge at cost \(d_u\)).
  2. Intermediate Algebraic Representation:
    • For each node \(u \in \{2, \dots, n\}\), let \(E_u : \sum_{j : u \in \text{path}(p_j, q_j)} x_j - y_u = A_u\) (\(y_u \ge 0\)), with root equation \(E_1 : 0 = 0\).
  3. Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad (Applying Theorem 19.3 \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_u = E_u - \sum_{v \in \text{child}(u)} E_v\)):
    • Nodal balance at \(u \in \{1, \dots, n\}\): \(b_u = A_u - \sum_{v \in \text{child}(u)} A_v\) (\(A_1 = 0\)).
    • Every vertical path variable \(x_j\) covering \(u \in \text{path}(p_j, q_j) \setminus \{p_j\}\) (all tree edges on the path from \(p_j\) down to \(q_j\)) enters at \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_{q_j}\) with \(+1\) and exits at \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_{p_j}\) with \(-1\), compiling to the upward arc \(q_j \xrightarrow{\text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } c_j} p_j\)!
    • Every surplus slack \(y_u\) on edge \((\text{parent}(u), u)\) compiles to the downward tree arc \(\text{parent}(u) \xrightarrow{\text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } d_u} u\)!

// Solution 1: BZOJ 3550 [ONTAK 2010] — Vacation (#152A)
// Method: Consecutive Equation Differencing on 3K Horizon + DAG DP Potentials + Dijkstra MCMF
#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

const long long INF = 1e18;

struct Edge {
    int to, rev, cap;
    long long cost;
};

void solve_bzoj3550() {
    int K, L;
    if (!(cin >> K >> L)) return;
    int n = 3 * K;
    vector<long long> c(n + 1);
    for (int j = 1; j <= n; ++j) cin >> c[j];

    int W = 2 * K + 1;
    int S = 0, T = W + 2, V = W + 3;
    vector<vector<Edge>> adj(V);
    auto add_edge = [&](int u, int v, int cap, long long cost) {
        adj[u].push_back({v, (int)adj[v].size(), cap, cost});
        adj[v].push_back({u, (int)adj[u].size() - 1, 0, -cost});
    };

    add_edge(S, 1, L, 0);
    add_edge(W + 1, T, L, 0);
    for (int i = 1; i <= W; ++i) {
        add_edge(i, i + 1, L, 0);
    }

    vector<vector<pair<int, long long>>> dag_edges(W + 2);
    for (int j = 1; j <= n; ++j) {
        int l = max(1, j - K + 1);
        int r = min(W, j);
        add_edge(l, r + 1, 1, -c[j]);
        dag_edges[l].push_back({r + 1, -c[j]});
    }

    vector<long long> pi(V, INF);
    pi[S] = pi[1] = 0;
    for (int u = 1; u <= W + 1; ++u) {
        if (u <= W) pi[u + 1] = min(pi[u + 1], pi[u]);
        for (auto [v, cost] : dag_edges[u]) {
            pi[v] = min(pi[v], pi[u] + cost);
        }
    }
    pi[T] = pi[W + 1];

    long long min_cost = 0;
    vector<long long> dist(V);
    vector<int> pv(V), pe(V);
    while (true) {
        fill(dist.begin(), dist.end(), INF);
        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
        dist[S] = 0;
        pq.push({0, S});
        while (!pq.empty()) {
            auto [d, u] = pq.top();
            pq.pop();
            if (d != dist[u]) continue;
            for (int i = 0; i < (int)adj[u].size(); ++i) {
                const auto& e = adj[u][i];
                if (e.cap > 0) {
                    long long nd = d + e.cost + pi[u] - pi[e.to];
                    if (nd < dist[e.to]) {
                        dist[e.to] = nd;
                        pv[e.to] = u;
                        pe[e.to] = i;
                        pq.push({nd, e.to});
                    }
                }
            }
        }
        if (dist[T] == INF) break;
        for (int v = 0; v < V; ++v) {
            if (dist[v] < INF) pi[v] += dist[v];
        }
        int pushed = L;
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            pushed = min(pushed, adj[pv[v]][pe[v]].cap);
        }
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            auto& e = adj[pv[v]][pe[v]];
            e.cap -= pushed;
            adj[v][e.rev].cap += pushed;
        }
        min_cost += 1LL * pushed * pi[T];
    }

    cout << -min_cost << "\n";
}

// Solution 2: Rooted Tree Equation Differencing (\Delta^{tree} E_u = E_u - \sum_{v \in child(u)} E_v) (#152B)
// Compiles vertical ancestor-descendant path covering on a rooted tree into O(N + M) MCMF without HLD.
long long solve_tree_volunteer_recruitment(
    int n,
    const vector<int>& parent,          // parent[u] for u = 2..n (root is 1, parent[1] = 0)
    const vector<long long>& A,         // A[u] = minimum coverage required at node u (u = 1..n)
    const vector<tuple<int, int, long long>>& chains // (p_j, q_j, cost_j) covering path(p_j, q_j)
) {
    int S = n + 1, T = n + 2, V = n + 3; // Node 0 is virtual super-root parent(1)
    vector<vector<Edge>> adj(V);
    auto add_edge = [&](int u, int v, int cap, long long cost) {
        adj[u].push_back({v, (int)adj[v].size(), cap, cost});
        adj[v].push_back({u, (int)adj[u].size() - 1, 0, -cost});
    };

    // 1. Compute tree-differenced nodal balance b_u = A_u - \sum_{v \in child(u)} A_v
    vector<long long> b(n + 1, 0);
    for (int u = 1; u <= n; ++u) {
        b[u] += A[u];
        b[parent[u]] -= A[u];
    }
    for (int u = 0; u <= n; ++u) {
        if (b[u] > 0) add_edge(S, u, (int)b[u], 0);
        else if (b[u] < 0) add_edge(u, T, (int)(-b[u]), 0);
    }

    // 2. Downward surplus slack arcs y_u: parent(u) -> u with cap INF, cost 0
    const int INF_CAP = 1e9;
    for (int u = 1; u <= n; ++u) {
        add_edge(parent[u], u, INF_CAP, 0);
    }

    // 3. Vertical chain arcs x_j: q_j -> parent(p_j) with cap INF, cost c_j
    for (auto [p, q, cost] : chains) {
        add_edge(q, parent[p], INF_CAP, cost);
    }

    // All initial costs are >= 0, so pi_0 == 0 is valid for Primal-Dual Dijkstra!
    vector<long long> pi(V, 0), dist(V);
    vector<int> pv(V), pe(V);
    long long min_cost = 0;
    while (true) {
        fill(dist.begin(), dist.end(), INF);
        priority_queue<pair<long long, int>, vector<pair<long long, int>>, greater<>> pq;
        dist[S] = 0;
        pq.push({0, S});
        while (!pq.empty()) {
            auto [d, u] = pq.top();
            pq.pop();
            if (d != dist[u]) continue;
            for (int i = 0; i < (int)adj[u].size(); ++i) {
                const auto& e = adj[u][i];
                if (e.cap > 0) {
                    long long nd = d + e.cost + pi[u] - pi[e.to];
                    if (nd < dist[e.to]) {
                        dist[e.to] = nd;
                        pv[e.to] = u;
                        pe[e.to] = i;
                        pq.push({nd, e.to});
                    }
                }
            }
        }
        if (dist[T] == INF) break;
        for (int v = 0; v < V; ++v) {
            if (dist[v] < INF) pi[v] += dist[v];
        }
        int pushed = INF_CAP;
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            pushed = min(pushed, adj[pv[v]][pe[v]].cap);
        }
        for (int v = T; v != S; v = pv[v]) {
            auto& e = adj[pv[v]][pe[v]];
            e.cap -= pushed;
            adj[v][e.rev].cap += pushed;
        }
        min_cost += 1LL * pushed * pi[T];
    }
    return min_cost;
}

int main() {
    ios::sync_with_stdio(false);
    cin.tie(nullptr);
    solve_bzoj3550();
    return 0;
}

7. Pre-Submission Checklist (FM-19 Audit) & Quarantined Template Problems

Before submitting any interval-covering, interval-packing, or equation-differencing MCMF solution, run through this 6-point verification checklist:

  1. Off-by-One Endpoint Audit (r_j + 1 vs. r_j, FM-19a):
    • Did you write out the discrete equation indices \(E_i\) (\(i = 1, \dots, n\)) that contain \(x_j\)?
    • If \(x_j\) appears in equations \(i \in \{l_j, l_j + 1, \dots, r_j\}\) (inclusive), then in \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) the \(-1\) coefficient appears at row \(\mathbf{r_j + 1}\), so the arc is \(l_j \to \mathbf{r_j + 1}\) and the graph has \(\mathbf{n + 1}\) equation nodes (\(1, \dots, n + 1\)) with \(A_0 = A_{n+1} = 0\).
    • If \(x_j\) is an open interval \((L_j, R_j)\) over compressed coordinates \(1 \dots M\), it covers elementary segments \(i \in \{l_j, \dots, r_j - 1\}\), so the \(-1\) coefficient appears at \((r_j - 1) + 1 = \mathbf{r_j}\).
  2. Slack Arc Orientation Audit (FM-19, H-21):
    • Lower-bound covering (\(\sum x_j \ge A_i \iff \sum x_j - y_i = A_i\)): slack \(-y_i\) has coefficient \(-1\) in \(\Delta E_i\) and \(+1\) in \(\Delta E_{i+1}\), so the slack arc points backward (\(i + 1 \to i\)).
    • Upper-bound packing (\(\sum x_j \le K_i \iff \sum x_j + y_i = K_i\)): slack \(+y_i\) has coefficient \(+1\) in \(\Delta E_i\) and \(-1\) in \(\Delta E_{i+1}\), so the slack arc points forward (\(i \to i + 1\)).
  3. Two-Sided Sliding Window Capacity Audit (FM-19c):
    • For \(L_i \le \sum x_j \le R_i\), did you bound the slack arc capacity by \(\mathbf{R_i - L_i}\)? Leaving the slack capacity as \(\infty\) drops the lower bound \(L_i\) (in Variant A) or drops the upper bound \(R_i\) (in Variant B).
  4. 2D Vertical Segment Self-Loop Audit (FM-19b):
    • In 2D segment projection problems (Luogu P3357), did you double coordinates \(x \mapsto (2x, 2x + 1)\) so vertical segments \(x_1 = x_2 = x\) map to \(2x \to 2x + 1\) instead of zero-length self-loops \(x \to x\)?
  5. DAG DP vs. \(\pi_0 \equiv 0\) Potential Initialization Audit:
    • In lower-bound covering (NOI2008 志愿者招募), all initial edge costs are \(\ge 0\), so \(\pi_0 \equiv 0\) is immediately valid for Dijkstra despite directed cycles.
    • In upper-bound packing (P3358, POJ 3680, CF 164C, NEERC 2016 Delight for a Cat Variant A), initial edge costs \(-w_j < 0\) are negative, but all edges satisfy \(u < v\) (a topological DAG), so a single \(O(N + M)\) left-to-right DP pass computes exact initial Johnson potentials \(\pi_0\) without SPFA!
  6. Consecutive-Ones Contiguity Audit (FM-19d):
    • Does every variable \(x_j\) appear in a single contiguous interval of equations? If a variable spans two disjoint intervals of equations, stop—that is the NP-hard Equal-Flow problem unless a global block sum telescopes onto slack variables.

8. Chapter Summary, Bridge to Chapter 20, and Multilingual Bibliography

Key Takeaways of Chapter 19

  • The Consecutive-Ones Property is the Soul of Interval Flow: Any linear program whose constraint matrix \(A \in \{0, 1\}^{n \times m}\) has contiguous \(1\)s in every column reduces mechanically via consecutive row subtraction \(\Delta E_i = E_i - E_{i-1}\) (流守恒差分建模法) to a node-arc incidence matrix of a directed flow network on \(n + 1\) vertices.
  • No More Guessing Edge Directions: Slack sign dictates arc direction—surplus slack \(-y_i\) in \(\ge A_i\) constraints compiles to backward arcs \(i + 1 \to i\), unused slack \(+y_i\) in \(\le K_i\) constraints compiles to forward arcs \(i \to i + 1\), and two-sided bounds \(L_i \le \text{sum}_i \le R_i\) compile to capacity-bounded slack arcs of capacity \(R_i - L_i\).
  • Coordinate Doubling & Tree Differencing: Coordinate doubling \(x \mapsto (2x, 2x+1)\) resolves 2D vertical segment self-loops (Luogu P3357), and subtree equation differencing \(\Delta^{\text{tree}} E_u = E_u - \sum_{v \in \text{child}(u)} E_v\) extends the compiler from 1D intervals to vertical ancestor-descendant chains on rooted trees.

Bridge to Chapter 20: Convex Cost Scaling, Incremental Slope Arrays, and Non-Linear MCMF

Throughout Chapters 17–19, every decision variable \(x_j\) had a linear unit cost \(c_j x_j\). What happens when the cost of routing \(x_j\) units of flow along an edge—or the penalty of a degree imbalance, project tardiness, or worker fatigue—is a nonlinear convex function \(\Phi_j(x_j)\) such as \(a x_j^2 + b x_j\), \(x_j \ln x_j\), or an arbitrary convex piecewise-linear curve? In Chapter 20, we open the Convex Cost & Non-Linear MCMF toolkit:

  • The Incremental Finite-Difference / Parallel-Edge Gadget (拆边费用流): How discrete convexity \(\Delta^2 \Phi(k) = \Phi(k+1) - 2\Phi(k) + \Phi(k-1) \ge 0\) guarantees that SSP Dijkstra greedily saturates parallel unit-capacity edges in exact order \(k = 1, 2, \dots, u\).
  • Dynamic On-the-Fly Edge Generation (动态加边): How to avoid \(O(U)\) parallel edges by inserting the \((k+1)\)-th marginal edge \(\Delta \Phi(k+1) = \Phi(k+1) - \Phi(k)\) only when the \(k\)-th edge becomes saturated.
  • Capacity-Scaling MCMF (\(O(M^2 \log U \log V)\)): How binary bit-scaling handles huge capacities \(U \le 10^9\) when explicit parallel edges are impossible.
