Network Flows, Minimum Cuts, Matching, Duality & Non-Graph Modelling

A Modelling-First Competitive Programming Textbook on Recognizing, Compiling, Dualizing, and Eliminating Flow Networks

Welcome to the Network Flows, Minimum Cuts, Matching, Duality & Non-Graph Modelling textbook.

Unlike domains such as Formal Power Series (where dozens of distinct algebraic algorithms must be mastered), network flows in competitive programming rely on a remarkably small algorithmic kernel: blocking-flow maximum flow (Dinic), Successive Shortest Path / primal–dual Min-Cost Max-Flow (MCMF), and Edmonds’ blossom algorithm for general matching. Consistent with the repository’s editorial policy, this textbook assumes your competitive programming library already contains tested implementations of Dinic, Hopcroft–Karp, and MCMF.

Why, then, are flow and minimum-cut problems among the hardest tasks on AtCoder (ARC/AGC), Codeforces (Div. 1), ICPC World Finals, and the Chinese NOI/集训队 circuit? Because over 90% of the difficulty lies in recognition and graph construction (modelling):

  1. Direction A (\(\text{Non-Graph Statement} \to \text{Flow / Cut / Matching / MCMF}\)): A problem statement mentions permutations, matrices, absolute-value sums, intervals, wizard skill pairs, or grid heights—with no graph in sight—and you must recognize the hidden conservation law, bipartite parity, submodular cut polynomial (燃やす埋める), multi-state unary threshold chain (切糕模型), discrete coarea level-set integral, consecutive equation difference, or LP dual that compiles the English specification into an exact flow network.
  2. Direction B (\(\text{Flow / Cut / Matching} \to \text{Fast } O(N \log N) \text{ Non-Flow Algorithm}\)): Conversely, when \(N \le 2 \times 10^5\) makes explicit graph construction too slow, you must use the structure of the flow/cut/MCMF graph to eliminate the solver entirely—collapsing Hall’s Marriage Theorem into a segment tree, planar \(s\)-\(t\) min-cut into dual Dijkstra, convex flow DP into Slope Trick or \((\min,+)\) tropical matrix multiplication (CF 724 E, CF 1919 F2), or augmenting paths into a priority-queue regret greedy (模拟费用流).

Every chapter in this textbook enforces the 4-Step “Zero Magic Gadgets” Compilation Standard:

\[\text{1. English Constraint} \longrightarrow \text{2. Algebraic Intermediate Model} \longrightarrow \text{3. Normal-Form Graph Gadget} \longrightarrow \text{4. Broken-Gadget Counterexample}\]

No graph edge or capacity is ever introduced “by magic”: every vertex, directed arc, \(\infty\)-constraint, and constant offset is derived algebraically from first principles, accompanied by a concrete counterexample proving how the model fails when a hypothesis (such as bipartite parity, submodularity \(A + D \le B + C\), or cost convexity) is violated.

Entry Contract & Prerequisites

Before starting Chapter 1, we assume you have:

  • Tested Black-Box Solvers:
    • A blocking-flow maximum-flow solver (Dinic(V, E) supporting residual graph inspection e.cap and s-reachability queries after termination).
    • A Min-Cost Max-Flow solver (MCMF(V, E) supporting Johnson potentials \(\pi(v)\), Successive Shortest Path with Dijkstra, and slope inspection \((f_k, C(f_k))\)).
  • Foundational Graph & Data-Structure Fluency:
    • Breadth-First Search (BFS), Depth-First Search (DFS), topological sorting, and strongly connected components (Tarjan / Kosaraju).
    • Dijkstra’s algorithm, Bellman–Ford / SPFA on small DAGs or negative-weight initialization, and segment trees with lazy propagation.
  • No Prior University LP or Combinatorial Optimization Required:
    • Every mathematical tool—from residual skew-symmetry, Hoffman’s circulation theorem, König–Hall–Dilworth duality, and submodular pseudo-Boolean polynomials to LP primal–dual compilation, totally unimodular (TU) matrices, and \(L_p\) isotonic regression—is developed from scratch with self-contained proofs and small hand-worked traces.

Start with Notation & Conventions for canonical symbols and the 24-item Failure Mode taxonomy (FM-01–FM-24), and consult the Study Plan & Diagnostic Protocol to choose your track.
