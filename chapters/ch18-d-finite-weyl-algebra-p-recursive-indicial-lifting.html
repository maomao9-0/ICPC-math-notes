In Chapter 16, we saw that extracting the diagonal \(\operatorname{Diag}_{x,y} R(x, y) = \sum_{n \ge 0} r_{n, n} t^n\) of a bivariate rational generating function produces an algebraic series—such as \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 6t + t^2}}\) for the Central Delannoy numbers—and that in three or more variables, rational diagonals escape the algebraic world altogether while remaining inside the class of D-finite (differentiably finite) or holonomic series.

From an algorithmic standpoint, moving from the algebraic presentation \(P(x, F(x)) = 0\) to the D-finite presentation \(\sum_{j=0}^r q_j(x) F^{(j)}(x) = 0\) is one of the most profitable compilation steps in competitive programming. Evaluating an algebraic radical like \((1 - 2ax + bx^2)^{-1/2} \bmod x^{N+1}\) via polynomial Newton iteration (sqrt and inv) costs \(\Theta(N \log N)\) time with a substantial NTT constant factor, which easily exceeds the time limit when \(N = 10^7\). By contrast, a D-finite linear ODE with polynomial coefficients translates directly into a P-recursive (polynomial-recursive) recurrence relation for the coefficients \(f_n = [x^n] F(x)\). Unrolling this recurrence computes \(f_0, f_1, \dots, f_N\) in \(O(N)\) time with a single modular inverse per step and zero FFTs.

This chapter builds the complete algebraic and algorithmic machinery of D-finite generating functions: the Euler operator dictionary in the Weyl algebra (§18.1), the Abel–Cockle–Harley–Comtet compilation recipes from algebraic equations to linear ODEs (§18.2), the 7-archetype substitution table for nonlinear and rational-coefficient ODEs (§18.3), and the closure theorems together with Elegia’s \(p\)-adic indicial lifting algorithm for bypassing modular division-by-zero singularities (§18.4).

18.1 D-Finite (Holonomic) Series \(\leftrightarrow\) P-Recursive Sequences & The Euler Operator Dictionary

Plain-Language Motivation: From Constant Coefficients to Polynomial Coefficients

In Chapter 5, we learned that a formal power series \(F(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} f_n x^n\) is rational (\(F(x) = P(x)/Q(x)\)) if and only if its coefficients satisfy a linear recurrence with constant coefficients: \[ q_0 f_n + q_1 f_{n-1} + \dots + q_d f_{n-d} = 0 \qquad (n \ge \deg P + 1). \] However, even the simplest factorials \(f_n = n!\) or Catalan numbers \(C_n = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}\) grow too fast or have asymptotic factors like \(n^{-3/2}\) that no constant-coefficient recurrence can produce. Yet both sequences obey short recurrences whose coefficients are polynomials in the index \(n\): \[ f_n - n f_{n-1} = 0 \quad (f_n = n!), \qquad (n + 1) C_n - (4n - 2) C_{n-1} = 0 \quad (\text{Catalan}). \] Multiplying \(f_n\) by \(n\) in coefficient space corresponds to applying the differential operator \(x \frac{d}{dx}\) in series space. Therefore, allowing polynomial coefficients in \(n\) inside the recurrence is equivalent to allowing derivatives \(\frac{d^j}{dx^j}\) with polynomial coefficients in \(x\) inside the functional equation for \(F(x)\).

Definitions: D-Finite Series, P-Recursive Sequences, and the Weyl Algebra

Let \(K\) be a field of characteristic \(0\) (or a finite field \(\mathbb{F}_p\) with prime \(p > N\), where we extract coefficients up to degree \(N\)).

  1. D-Finite (Differentiably Finite / Holonomic) Series: A formal power series \(F(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty f_n x^n \in K[[x]]\) is called D-finite (or holonomic) over \(K(x)\) if the infinite sequence of derivatives \(F(x), F'(x), F''(x), \dots\) spans a finite-dimensional vector space over the rational function field \(K(x)\). By clearing denominators in \(K(x)\), this is equivalent to the existence of polynomials \(q_0(x), q_1(x), \dots, q_r(x) \in K[x]\) (not all zero) such that: \[ q_r(x) F^{(r)}(x) + q_{r-1}(x) F^{(r-1)}(x) + \dots + q_1(x) F'(x) + q_0(x) F(x) = 0. \] The smallest integer \(r \ge 0\) for which such a nontrivial relation holds is the order of the D-finite ODE, and \(d = \max_{0 \le j \le r} \deg(q_j)\) is its polynomial degree.

  2. The Weyl Algebra \(W_1(K) = K[x]\langle D_x \rangle\): Linear differential operators with polynomial coefficients form a non-commutative ring generated by the multiplication-by-\(x\) operator and the formal differentiation operator \(D_x = \frac{d}{dx}\). Because the product rule gives \(D_x(x F(x)) = F(x) + x D_x F(x)\), the generators obey the fundamental Weyl commutation relation: \[ D_x x - x D_x = 1 \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad [D_x, x] = 1. \] A series \(F(x)\) is D-finite if and only if it is annihilated by a nonzero operator \(L \in W_1(K)\), written \(L \cdot F(x) = 0\).

  3. P-Recursive (Polynomially Recursive) Sequence: A sequence \((f_n)_{n \ge 0}\) in \(K\) is called P-recursive of order (or shift width) \(d\) if there exist polynomials \(P_0(n), P_1(n), \dots, P_d(n) \in K[n]\), with \(P_0(n)\) not identically zero, such that for all sufficiently large \(n \ge n_0\): \[ P_0(n) f_n + P_1(n) f_{n-1} + \dots + P_d(n) f_{n-d} = 0. \]

The Euler Homogeneous Operator \(\vartheta = x D_x\) & Conversion Dictionary

When converting a standard ODE \(\sum_{j=0}^r q_j(x) D_x^j F(x) = 0\) into a recurrence for \(f_n\), the ordinary derivative \(D_x^j\) shifts the exponent of \(x^n\) down by \(j\) (\(D_x^j(x^n) = n^{\underline{j}} x^{n-j}\)), while multiplying by \(x^i\) in \(q_j(x)\) shifts the exponent up by \(i\). Tracking the net shift \(i - j\) by hand across high-order derivatives is a frequent source of off-by-one bugs.

We eliminate all degree-shift mismatches by rewriting the differential operator in terms of the Euler (homogeneous / theta) operator: \[ \vartheta = x D_x = x \frac{d}{dx}. \] Because \(\vartheta(x^n) = x (n x^{n-1}) = n x^n\), the monomials \(x^n\) are eigenfunctions of \(\vartheta\) with eigenvalue \(n\). Consequently, for any polynomial \(P(\vartheta) = \sum_{k=0}^r c_k \vartheta^k \in K[\vartheta]\), applying \(P(\vartheta)\) to \(x^n\) simply evaluates the polynomial \(P\) at the integer \(n\) without changing the power of \(x\): \[ P(\vartheta)(x^n) = P(n) x^n. \]

ImportantTheorem 18.1 (The Weyl–Euler Operator Conversion Dictionary)

The operators \(D_x\) and \(\vartheta = x D_x\) in the Weyl algebra \(K[x]\langle D_x \rangle\) obey the following exact identities for all integers \(j, k \ge 0\), \(s \in \mathbb{Z}\), and formal power series \(F(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} f_n x^n\):

  1. Falling-Factorial Conversion (\(x^j D_x^j \leftrightarrow \vartheta\)): \[ x^j D_x^j = \vartheta^{\underline{j}} = \vartheta(\vartheta - 1)(\vartheta - 2)\cdots(\vartheta - j + 1), \qquad \vartheta^k = \sum_{j=0}^k \left\{ \begin{matrix} k \\ j \end{matrix} \right\} x^j D_x^j, \] where \(\left\{ \begin{matrix} k \\ j \end{matrix} \right\}\) are the Stirling numbers of the second kind.
  2. Shift Commutation Rule: Moving \(x^s\) across any polynomial \(P(\vartheta)\) shifts the operator argument by \(+s\): \[ P(\vartheta)\!\left(x^s F(x)\right) = x^s P(\vartheta + s) F(x). \]
  3. Canonical \(\vartheta\)-Form to P-Recursion Bijection: Every linear ODE \(\sum_{j=0}^r q_j(x) D_x^j F(x) = 0\) can be multiplied by \(x^r\) (or by \(x^m\) where \(m = \max_j(j - \operatorname{val}(q_j))\)) and grouped by powers of \(x\) into the canonical \(\vartheta\)-form: \[ \sum_{i=0}^d x^i P_i(\vartheta) F(x) = 0. \] Extracting \([x^n]\) from this canonical form yields the exact P-recursive relation: \[ \sum_{i=0}^d P_i(n - i) f_{n-i} = 0 \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad P_0(n) f_n = -\sum_{i=1}^d P_i(n - i) f_{n-i}. \] Equivalently, if the operator is written with \(x^i\) to the right of the \(\vartheta\)-polynomials as \(\sum_{i=0}^d \widetilde{P}_i(\vartheta)\!\left(x^i F(x)\right) = 0\) (where \(\widetilde{P}_i(\vartheta) = P_i(\vartheta - i)\) by the Shift Commutation Rule), extracting \([x^n]\) evaluates every polynomial at the same index \(n\): \[ \sum_{i=0}^d \widetilde{P}_i(n) f_{n-i} = 0. \]

Single-Step Verbalized Proof of Theorem 18.1

  1. Identity 1 (\(x^j D_x^j = \vartheta^{\underline{j}}\)): On each basis monomial \(x^n\), applying \(D_x^j\) differentiates \(j\) times to produce the falling factorial \(n^{\underline{j}} x^{n-j}\), and multiplying by \(x^j\) restores the exponent to \(x^n\): \[ (x^j D_x^j)(x^n) = n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1) x^n = \vartheta(\vartheta - 1)\cdots(\vartheta - j + 1)(x^n) = \vartheta^{\underline{j}}(x^n). \] By linearity over \(K[[x]]\), \(x^j D_x^j = \vartheta^{\underline{j}}\). The inverse relation \(\vartheta^k = \sum_{j=0}^k \left\{\begin{matrix} k \\ j \end{matrix}\right\} x^j D_x^j\) follows from the standard polynomial identity \(n^k = \sum_{j=0}^k \left\{\begin{matrix} k \\ j \end{matrix}\right\} n^{\underline{j}}\).
  2. Identity 2 (Shift Commutation): On a single term \(f_n x^n\) of \(F(x)\), multiplying by \(x^s\) yields \(f_n x^{n+s}\). Applying \(P(\vartheta)\) multiplies by the eigenvalue \(P(n + s)\): \[ P(\vartheta)(f_n x^{n+s}) = P(n + s) f_n x^{n+s} = x^s \cdot P(n + s) f_n x^n = x^s P(\vartheta + s)(f_n x^n). \]
  3. Identity 3 (ODE to P-Recursion): In the term \(x^i P_i(\vartheta) F(x)\), the operator \(P_i(\vartheta)\) maps \(f_{n-i} x^{n-i}\) to \(P_i(n-i) f_{n-i} x^{n-i}\), and multiplying on the left by \(x^i\) shifts the monomial to \(P_i(n-i) f_{n-i} x^n\). Summing over \(i = 0, \dots, d\) and setting the coefficient of \(x^n\) to zero gives \(\sum_{i=0}^d P_i(n-i) f_{n-i} = 0\).

The Indicial Polynomial \(I(n) = P_0(n)\) and \(O(d \cdot N)\) Coefficient Unrolling

TipDiagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: The Euler Operator \(\vartheta = x D_x\) & Indicial Unrolling
  • The Diagnostic Signal: You have a linear ODE \(\sum_{j=0}^r q_j(x) D_x^j F(x) = 0\) (or a recurrence with polynomial coefficients in \(n\)) and want to extract \([x^n]F(x)\) for \(0 \le n \le N\) in \(O(N)\) time without off-by-one shift errors.
  • The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: Multiply the ODE by \(x^m\) so every derivative appears as \(x^j D_x^j = \vartheta^{\underline{j}}\), then group terms by powers of \(x\) into \(\sum_{i=0}^d x^i P_i(\vartheta) F(x) = 0\). Because \(\vartheta(x^k) = k x^k\) is degree-preserving, \([x^n]\big(x^i P_i(\vartheta) F(x)\big)\) is immediately \(P_i(n-i) f_{n-i}\), and isolating the \(i = 0\) term \(I(n) f_n = P_0(n) f_n\) unrolls \(f_n\) in \(O(d)\) operations per step!

In the recurrence \(P_0(n) f_n = -\sum_{i=1}^d P_i(n-i) f_{n-i}\), the leading polynomial coefficient \(I(n) = P_0(n)\) multiplying \(f_n\) is called the indicial polynomial of the ODE at \(x = 0\).

To compute \(f_n\) from \(f_{n-1}, \dots, f_{n-d}\), we must divide by \(I(n)\): \[ f_n = -\frac{1}{I(n)} \sum_{i=1}^d P_i(n - i) f_{n-i} \qquad (\text{provided } I(n) \in K^\times). \] Let \(n_0 = \max\{n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} : I(n) = 0 \text{ in } \mathbb{Z}\}\) be the largest non-negative integer root of \(I(n)\) in characteristic \(0\). Once the initial values \(f_0, f_1, \dots, f_{\max(n_0, d-1)}\) are known (from the combinatorial base cases of the problem), every subsequent coefficient \(f_n\) up to \(n = N\) is uniquely determined and can be unrolled in \(O(d)\) arithmetic operations per step. Using linear-time batch modular inversion (or precomputed inverses when \(I(n)\) factors into linear forms \(a n + b\)), the entire array \(f_0, \dots, f_N\) is computed in \(O(d \cdot N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space (or \(O(d)\) streaming space) with zero NTTs.

Concrete Hand-Checkable Trace (\(N \le 4\)): 2-Regular Simple Graphs

Consider the exponential generating function \(F(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty f_n \frac{x^n}{n!}\) of 2-regular simple labelled graphs (collections of vertex-disjoint undirected simple cycles of length \(\ge 3\)). A single undirected cycle of length \(k \ge 3\) on \(k\) labelled vertices can be oriented in \(\frac{(k-1)!}{2}\) ways, so the connected EGF is \(C(x) = \sum_{k=3}^\infty \frac{x^k}{2k} = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1}{1-x} - \frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{4}\), and by the Exponential Formula: \[ F(x) = \exp(C(x)) = \frac{\exp\!\left(-\frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{4}\right)}{\sqrt{1 - x}}. \] Let us compile \(F(x)\) into a P-recursive recurrence and trace \(f_0, f_1, f_2, f_3, f_4\) by hand:

  1. Logarithmic Derivative: Differentiating \(\ln F(x) = -\frac{1}{2}\ln(1-x) - \frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{4}\) gives: \[ \frac{F'(x)}{F(x)} = \frac{1}{2(1 - x)} - \frac{1 + x}{2} = \frac{1 - (1 - x^2)}{2(1 - x)} = \frac{x^2}{2(1 - x)}. \]
  2. Clear Denominators & Convert to \(\vartheta\)-Form: Cross-multiplying by \(2(1-x)\) and multiplying by \(x\) to replace \(x F'(x)\) with \(\vartheta F(x)\) gives: \[ 2(1 - x) F'(x) - x^2 F(x) = 0 \implies 2(1 - x)\vartheta F(x) - x^3 F(x) = 0 \implies 2\vartheta F(x) - 2 x \vartheta F(x) - x^3 F(x) = 0. \] Here \(P_0(\vartheta) = 2\vartheta\), \(P_1(\vartheta) = -2\vartheta\), \(P_2(\vartheta) = 0\), and \(P_3(\vartheta) = -1\). The indicial polynomial is \(I(n) = 2n\), whose only integer root is \(n = 0\) (fixed by \(f_0 = 1\)).
  3. Extract the Recurrence: For the EGF coefficients \(a_n = [x^n] F(x) = \frac{f_n}{n!}\), Theorem 18.1 gives \(2n a_n - 2(n-1) a_{n-1} - a_{n-3} = 0\). Substituting \(a_n = \frac{f_n}{n!}\) and multiplying by \(\frac{(n-1)!}{2}\) clears the factorials completely: \[ f_n = (n - 1) f_{n-1} + \frac{(n - 1)(n - 2)}{2} f_{n-3} \qquad (n \ge 1). \]
  4. Trace for \(N = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\):
    • \(n = 0\): \(f_0 = 1\) (empty graph).
    • \(n = 1\): \(f_1 = 0 \cdot f_0 + 0 = 0\) (no 1-cycles allowed).
    • \(n = 2\): \(f_2 = 1 \cdot f_1 + 0 = 0\) (no 2-cycles allowed).
    • \(n = 3\): \(f_3 = 2 \cdot f_2 + \binom{2}{2} f_0 = 0 + 1 \cdot 1 = 1\) (the single triangle on \(\{1, 2, 3\}\)).
    • \(n = 4\): \(f_4 = 3 \cdot f_3 + \binom{3}{2} f_1 = 3 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 0 = 3\) (the \(3\) distinct 4-cycles on \(\{1, 2, 3, 4\}\)).

18.2 The Abel–Cockle–Harley–Comtet Theorem (Algebraic \(\implies\) D-Finite) & Recipes A / B

Every algebraic formal power series is D-finite. This theorem—discovered in progressively broader generality by Abel (1827), Cockle (1860), Harley (1862), and popularized in enumerative combinatorics by Comtet (1964) and Stanley (1980)—is the bridge that turns \(\Theta(N \log N)\) algebraic Newton iterations (sqrt, inv, pow) into \(O(N)\) linear recurrences.

ImportantTheorem 18.2 (Abel–Cockle–Harley–Comtet: Algebraic \(\implies\) D-Finite)

Let \(F(x) \in K[[x]]\) be an algebraic formal power series over \(K(x)\), satisfying \(P(x, F(x)) = 0\) where \(P(x, y) \in K[x, y]\) is a square-free polynomial of degree \(d = \deg_y P \ge 1\) in \(y\). Then:

  1. Every higher derivative \(F^{(k)}(x)\) belongs to the \(d\)-dimensional \(K(x)\)-vector space \(V = K(x) \oplus K(x) F(x) \oplus \dots \oplus K(x) F(x)^{d-1}\).
  2. Consequently, \(F(x)\) satisfies a non-trivial linear ODE with polynomial coefficients of order at most \(d\) (and of order at most \(d - 1\) whenever the trace term \([y^{d-1}]P(x, y) = 0\)).

Constructive Proof via Extended GCD in \(K(x)[y]\)

  1. Differentiating \(P(x, F(x)) = 0\) with respect to \(x\) by the multivariable chain rule yields: \[ P_x(x, F(x)) + P_y(x, F(x)) F'(x) = 0 \implies F'(x) = -\frac{P_x(x, F(x))}{P_y(x, F(x))}, \] where \(P_x = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\) and \(P_y = \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\).
  2. Because \(P(x, y)\) is square-free as a polynomial in \(y\) over the field \(K(x)\), \(\gcd_{K(x)[y]}(P(x, y), P_y(x, y)) = 1\). By the Extended Euclidean Algorithm in the univariate polynomial ring \(K(x)[y]\), there exist polynomials \(U(x, y), V(x, y) \in K(x)[y]\) with \(\deg_y U < d\) such that: \[ U(x, y) P_y(x, y) + V(x, y) P(x, y) = 1. \]
  3. Substituting \(y = F(x)\) makes \(P(x, F(x)) = 0\), so \(P_y(x, F(x))^{-1} = U(x, F(x))\). Multiplying by \(-P_x(x, F(x))\) and taking the remainder modulo \(P(x, F(x))\) in \(K(x)[y]\) expresses \(F'(x)\) as a unique polynomial in \(F(x)\) of degree \(\le d - 1\): \[ F'(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{d-1} c_{1, i}(x) F(x)^i, \qquad c_{1, i}(x) \in K(x). \]
  4. Differentiating inductively and replacing every occurrence of \(F'(x)\) by \(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1} c_{1,i}(x) F(x)^i\) modulo \(P(x, F(x))\) expresses each \(F^{(k)}(x)\) (\(0 \le k \le d\)) as a \(K(x)\)-linear combination of \(\{1, F(x), \dots, F(x)^{d-1}\}\). Since \(d + 1\) vectors in a \(d\)-dimensional vector space over \(K(x)\) are linearly dependent, clearing denominators produces a polynomial-coefficient ODE of order \(\le d\).

In contest problems, we rarely need to run a full \(d \times d\) Gaussian elimination because \(99\%\) of algebraic and elementary generating functions fall into two explicit compilation recipes: Recipe A (rational powers and rational exponentials) and Recipe B (quadratic radicals).

Recipe A: Rational Powers \(A(x) B(x)^\alpha\) and Rational Exponentials \(A(x) \exp(R(x)/S(x))\)

  • The Diagnostic Signal: You need coefficients of a product \(F(x) = A(x) B(x)^\alpha\) (with \(\alpha \in K\), such as \(\alpha = -1/2\) or \(\alpha = n\)) or \(F(x) = A(x) \exp\!\left(\frac{R(x)}{S(x)}\right)\) where \(A, B, R, S \in K[x]\) are sparse polynomials of small degree.
  • The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: Taking the logarithmic derivative \(\frac{F'(x)}{F(x)}\) turns products into sums, turns the power \(B(x)^\alpha\) into \(\alpha \frac{B'(x)}{B(x)}\), and turns \(\exp(R/S)\) into \((R/S)'\). Clearing the polynomial denominator immediately produces a first-order D-finite ODE!
  1. Case A1 (\(F(x) = A(x) B(x)^\alpha\)): \[ \frac{F'(x)}{F(x)} = \frac{A'(x)}{A(x)} + \alpha \frac{B'(x)}{B(x)} \implies A(x) B(x) F'(x) - \Big( A'(x) B(x) + \alpha A(x) B'(x) \Big) F(x) = 0. \]
  2. Case A2 (\(F(x) = A(x) \exp\!\left(\frac{R(x)}{S(x)}\right)\)): \[ \frac{F'(x)}{F(x)} = \frac{A'(x)}{A(x)} + \frac{R'(x) S(x) - R(x) S'(x)}{S(x)^2} \implies A(x) S(x)^2 F'(x) - \Big( A'(x) S(x)^2 + A(x)\big(R'(x)S(x) - R(x)S'(x)\big) \Big) F(x) = 0. \]

Canonical Derivation: Central Delannoy & Legendre Radical \(F(x) = (1 - 2ax + bx^2)^{-1/2}\)

Applying Case A1 with \(A(x) = 1\), \(B(x) = 1 - 2ax + bx^2\), and \(\alpha = -1/2\) gives: \[ (1 - 2ax + bx^2) F'(x) = -\frac{1}{2}(-2a + 2bx) F(x) = (a - bx) F(x). \] Multiplying by \(x\) to convert \(x F'(x) = \vartheta F(x)\) and shifting \(F'(x) = x^{-1} \vartheta F(x)\) (or extracting \([x^{n-1}]\) directly from \((1 - 2ax + bx^2) F'(x) - (a - bx) F(x) = 0\)) gives: \[ [x^{n-1}]: \quad n f_n - 2a(n - 1) f_{n-1} + b(n - 2) f_{n-2} - a f_{n-1} + b f_{n-2} = 0, \] which simplifies to the classic 3-term \(O(N)\) recurrence: \[ n f_n = a(2n - 1) f_{n-1} - b(n - 1) f_{n-2} \qquad (n \ge 1, \quad f_0 = 1, \; f_{-1} = 0). \] Setting \(a = 3, b = 1\) (so \(B(x) = 1 - 6x + x^2\)) recovers the Central Delannoy numbers \(D_n = [x^n](1 - 6x + x^2)^{-1/2}\): \[ n D_n = 3(2n - 1) D_{n-1} - (n - 1) D_{n-2} \implies D_0 = 1, \; D_1 = 3, \; D_2 = 13, \; D_3 = 63, \; D_4 = 321. \]

Stepping Stone 1 (#151) — Spanning Tree Block Sum (Luogu P4002 — [清华集训2017] 生成树计数)

  • Tier: Stepping Stone 1 | Canonical ID: #151
  • Source: Luogu P4002 — [清华集训2017] 生成树计数
  • Prerequisite Tags: Prüfer Sequence EGF, Euler Operator \(\vartheta = x D_x\) (§18.1), Logarithmic Derivative Power Sums (§18.2)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given \(N\) vertex-disjoint connected components of sizes \(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_N\) and a positive integer exponent \(m\). You wish to add \(N - 1\) edges between the components to connect all \(\sum_{i=1}^N a_i\) vertices into a single spanning tree. Whenever an added edge connects component \(i\) and component \(j\), there are \(a_i a_j\) choices for its endpoints; thus, a contracted spanning tree \(T\) on the \(N\) components in which component \(i\) has degree \(d_i \ge 1\) corresponds to \(\prod_{i=1}^N a_i^{d_i}\) vertex-level spanning trees.

For each vertex-level spanning tree with component degrees \((d_1, \dots, d_N)\), its weight is defined as: \[ \left( \sum_{i=1}^N d_i^m \right) \prod_{i=1}^N d_i^m. \] Compute the sum of weights over all valid vertex-level spanning trees modulo \(998244353\): \[ \text{Ans} = \sum_{T} \left( \sum_{i=1}^N d_i^m \right) \left( \prod_{i=1}^N d_i^m a_i^{d_i} \right) \pmod{998244353}. \]

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 30000\), \(1 \le m \le 30000\), \(1 \le a_i < 998244353\), \(\sum_{i=1}^N a_i \not\equiv 0 \pmod{998244353}\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

Concrete Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\))

  • Instance 1 (\(N = 2, m = 1, a = (2, 3)\)): There is only \(1\) contracted tree on \(2\) components, with degrees \(d_1 = 1, d_2 = 1\). There are \(a_1^{d_1} a_2^{d_2} = 2^1 \cdot 3^1 = 6\) vertex-level trees, each with degree factor \((1^1 + 1^1)(1^1 \cdot 1^1) = 2\). Total answer: \(6 \times 2 = 12\).
  • Instance 2 (\(N = 3, m = 1, a = (1, 1, 1)\)): There are \(3^{3-2} = 3\) contracted trees on \(\{1, 2, 3\}\), each of which is a path of \(3\) vertices with degrees \((2, 1, 1)\) in some permutation. For each tree, \(\sum_{i=1}^3 d_i^1 = 2 + 1 + 1 = 4\) and \(\prod_{i=1}^3 d_i^1 a_i^{d_i} = 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 2\), contributing \(4 \times 2 = 8\). Across all \(3\) trees, the total weight is \(3 \times 8 = 24\).
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

By Prüfer’s bijection, a degree sequence \((d_1, \dots, d_N)\) with \(d_i = c_i + 1 \ge 1\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^N c_i = N - 2\) occurs \(\frac{(N-2)!}{c_1! \cdots c_N!}\) times. Notice that the weight \(\left(\sum_{j=1}^N d_j^m\right) \prod_{i=1}^N d_i^m a_i^{d_i}\) is a sum over \(j = 1, \dots, N\) where component \(j\) has degree factor \(d_j^{2m} a_j^{d_j}\) and every other component \(i \neq j\) has degree factor \(d_i^m a_i^{d_i}\). How can we express this using two univariate EGFs \(F(x)\) and \(G(x)\) scaled by \(a_i\)?

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Define the Euler-shifted exponential series: \[ F(x) = (\vartheta + 1)^m e^x = \sum_{c=0}^\infty \frac{(c + 1)^m}{c!} x^c, \qquad G(x) = (\vartheta + 1)^{2m} e^x = \sum_{c=0}^\infty \frac{(c + 1)^{2m}}{c!} x^c. \] Then the total weight is: \[ \text{Ans} = (N - 2)! \left(\prod_{i=1}^N a_i\right) [x^{N-2}] \left( \prod_{i=1}^N F(a_i x) \cdot \sum_{j=1}^N \frac{G(a_j x)}{F(a_j x)} \right). \] Take \(\ln\) of the product \(\prod_{i=1}^N F(a_i x) = \exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^N \ln F(a_i x)\right)\) to decouple \(a_i\) from the series coefficients!

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Let \(L(x) = \ln F(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N-2} l_k x^k \bmod x^{N-1}\) and \(H(x) = \frac{G(x)}{F(x)} = \sum_{k=0}^{N-2} h_k x^k \bmod x^{N-1}\). Expanding \(\sum_{i=1}^N L(a_i x)\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^N H(a_i x)\) requires the power sums \(S_k = \sum_{i=1}^N a_i^k\) for \(0 \le k \le N - 2\). By Recipe A (§18.2), the polynomial \(P(x) = \prod_{i=1}^N (1 - a_i x)\) satisfies \(-\vartheta \ln P(x) = -x \frac{P'(x)}{P(x)} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty S_k x^k\), computable in \(O(N \log^2 N)\) via divide-and-conquer NTT!

Complete Derivation & Solution

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

  1. Prüfer Sequence EGF Factorization: For \(N = 1\), a single component has no edges (\(d_1 = 0\)), so the answer is \(0\). Assume \(N \ge 2\). In the Prüfer sequence of length \(N - 2\), component \(i\) appears \(c_i = d_i - 1 \ge 0\) times, with \(\sum_{i=1}^N c_i = N - 2\). Factoring \(\prod_{i=1}^N a_i^{c_i + 1} = \left(\prod_{i=1}^N a_i\right) \prod_{i=1}^N a_i^{c_i}\), we rewrite the target sum by linearity over the distinguished component \(j \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) in \(\sum_{j=1}^N (c_j + 1)^m\): \[ \text{Ans} = (N - 2)! \left(\prod_{i=1}^N a_i\right) \sum_{j=1}^N \sum_{c_1 + \dots + c_N = N - 2} \frac{(c_j + 1)^{2m} a_j^{c_j}}{c_j!} \prod_{i \neq j} \frac{(c_i + 1)^m a_i^{c_i}}{c_i!}. \]

  2. Euler Operator Series \(F(x)\) and \(G(x)\): Using the Euler operator \(\vartheta = x D_x\) from §18.1, since \((\vartheta + 1)^k (x^c) = (c + 1)^k x^c\), we define: \[ F(x) = (\vartheta + 1)^m e^x = \sum_{c=0}^{N-2} \frac{(c + 1)^m}{c!} x^c \pmod{x^{N-1}}, \qquad G(x) = (\vartheta + 1)^{2m} e^x = \sum_{c=0}^{N-2} \frac{(c + 1)^{2m}}{c!} x^c \pmod{x^{N-1}}. \] Then the inner sum over \((c_1, \dots, c_N)\) for a fixed \(j\) is \([x^{N-2}] \left( G(a_j x) \prod_{i \neq j} F(a_i x) \right)\). Summing over \(j = 1, \dots, N\) gives: \[ \text{Ans} = (N - 2)! \left(\prod_{i=1}^N a_i\right) [x^{N-2}] \left( \exp\!\left( \sum_{i=1}^N \ln F(a_i x) \right) \cdot \sum_{j=1}^N \frac{G(a_j x)}{F(a_j x)} \right). \]

  3. Decoupling \(a_i\) via Power Sums \(S_k = \sum_{i=1}^N a_i^k\): Since \([x^0] F(x) = 1 \in \mathbb{F}_{998244353}^\times\), both \(L(x) = \ln F(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N-2} l_k x^k \bmod x^{N-1}\) and \(H(x) = G(x) F(x)^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^{N-2} h_k x^k \bmod x^{N-1}\) are well-defined and computable in \(O(N \log N)\) time. Substituting \(a_i x\) into \(L\) and \(H\) yields: \[ \sum_{i=1}^N L(a_i x) \equiv \sum_{k=1}^{N-2} (l_k S_k) x^k \pmod{x^{N-1}}, \qquad \sum_{j=1}^N H(a_j x) \equiv \sum_{k=0}^{N-2} (h_k S_k) x^k \pmod{x^{N-1}}, \] where \(S_k = \sum_{i=1}^N a_i^k\).

  4. Computing \(S_0, S_1, \dots, S_{N-2}\) via Logarithmic Derivative: Form \(P(x) = \prod_{i=1}^N (1 - a_i x)\) by binary-tree divide-and-conquer NTT in \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time. By Recipe A: \[ -\vartheta \ln P(x) = -\sum_{i=1}^N \vartheta \ln(1 - a_i x) = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{a_i x}{1 - a_i x} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty S_k x^k, \] along with \(S_0 = N\). Finally, one polynomial exp on \(\sum_{k=1}^{N-2} (l_k S_k) x^k\) and one Cauchy product with \(\sum_{k=0}^{N-2} (h_k S_k) x^k\) extract \([x^{N-2}]\) in \(O(N \log N)\) time.

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: \(N, m \le 30000\) and component sizes \(a_1, \dots, a_N \in \mathbb{F}_{998244353}\); requires \(N \ge 2\), \([x^0]F(x) = 1\), and \([x^0]P(x) = 1\) invertible in \(\mathbb{F}_{998244353}\).
  • Postconditions (Output): Total weight of all spanning trees modulo \(998244353\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time for the product tree \(P(x) = \prod_{i=1}^N (1 - a_i x)\) plus \(O(N \log N)\) for ln, inv, exp, and binary exponentiation of \((c+1)^m\); \(O(N)\) space.
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: Each power sum \(S_k = [x^k](-\vartheta \ln P(x))\) aggregates the \(k\)-th powers of all \(N\) component sizes simultaneously, ensuring the scaled series \(\sum_{i=1}^N L(a_i x)\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^N H(a_i x)\) match term-by-term up to \(x^{N-2}\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: If \(N = 1\), the single component is already connected so output \(1\) (or \(0\) if edge weight product requires \(N \ge 2\)); if any \(a_i \equiv 0 \pmod{998244353}\), that component cannot have positive degree in any spanning tree so the answer is \(0\).

Stepping Stone 2 (#064) — Carry-Ordered Digit Recurrence (AtCoder ARC 153 D — Sum of Sum of Digits)

  • Tier: Stepping Stone 2 | Canonical ID: #064
  • Source: AtCoder Regular Contest 153, Problem D — Sum of Sum of Digits
  • Prerequisite Tags: Digit Sum Functional Recurrence, Radix Carry Order, State Space Reduction

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

For a positive integer \(m\), let \(f(m)\) denote the sum of the base-\(10\) digits of \(m\). You are given \(N\) positive integers \(A_1, A_2, \dots, A_N\). Find the minimum possible value of: \[ S(x) = \sum_{i=1}^N f(x + A_i) \] over all non-negative integers \(x \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(1 \le A_i < 10^9\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(D \cdot N \log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space, where \(D = 10\) is the number of decimal digits.

Concrete Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\))

  • Instance (\(N = 3, A = (4, 8, 9)\)):
    • If \(x = 0\): \(f(4) + f(8) + f(9) = 4 + 8 + 9 = 21\).
    • If \(x = 1\): \(f(5) + f(9) + f(10) = 5 + 9 + 1 = 15\).
    • If \(x = 2\): \(f(6) + f(10) + f(11) = 6 + 1 + 2 = 9\).
    • If \(x = 6\): \(f(10) + f(14) + f(15) = 1 + 5 + 6 = 12\).
    • The minimum possible value is \(9\), achieved at \(x = 2\).
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Write the digit sum recurrence connecting the \(k\)-th digit decision to the \((k+1)\)-st digit decision: for any integer \(v \ge 0\), \(f(v) = (v \bmod 10) + f(\lfloor v / 10 \rfloor)\). When we choose the \(k\)-th decimal digit \(d_k \in \{0, 1, \dots, 9\}\) of \(x\), which elements \(A_i\) generate a carry into position \(k + 1\)?

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

At digit position \(k\) (\(0 \le k \le 9\)), an element \(A_i\) has received a carry into position \(k\) if and only if \((A_i \bmod 10^k) + (x \bmod 10^k) \ge 10^k\). Therefore, if we sort \(A_1, \dots, A_N\) in descending order of \((A_i \bmod 10^k)\), the subset of indices \(A_i\) that have a carry at position \(k\) is always a prefix of length \(c \in \{0, 1, \dots, N\}\) of this sorted order!

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Let \(dp[k][c]\) be the minimum total digit sum contributed by positions \(0, 1, \dots, k-1\) such that exactly the first \(c\) elements in the \(\bmod 10^k\) descending order produce a carry into position \(k\). Trying all 10 choices for the \(k\)-th digit \(d \in \{0, \dots, 9\}\) of \(x\) updates both the immediate digit cost and the new carry count \(c' \in \{0, \dots, N\}\) via prefix sums in \(O(1)\) per state!

Complete Derivation & Solution

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

  1. Telescoping the Digit Sum Operator: Every non-negative integer \(v\) satisfies the exact functional relation: \[ f(v) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \left( \left\lfloor \frac{v}{10^k} \right\rfloor - 10 \left\lfloor \frac{v}{10^{k+1}} \right\rfloor \right). \] Since \(A_i < 10^9\), any optimal \(x\) satisfies \(x < 10^9\) (adding \(10^{10}\) only increases the digit sum), and for \(k = 10\), \(\lfloor (x + A_i) / 10^{10} \rfloor = 0\) unless a carry propagates out of \(k = 9\), in which case each remaining carry contributes \(+1\) to the \(10^{10}\) place.

  2. Monotonicity of Carry Sets (State Space Collapse from \(2^N\) to \(N + 1\)): Fix stage \(k \in \{0, 1, \dots, 9\}\). Let \(\pi_k = (i_1, i_2, \dots, i_N)\) be the permutation of \(\{1, \dots, N\}\) sorted in descending order of the remainder \(r_{i, k} = A_i \bmod 10^k\). For any choice of the low \(k\) digits \(x_k = x \bmod 10^k \in [0, 10^k - 1]\), element \(A_i\) carries into stage \(k\) (\(\lfloor (A_i + x_k)/10^k \rfloor = 1\)) if and only if \(r_{i, k} \ge 10^k - x_k\). Because \(\pi_k\) is sorted in descending order of \(r_{i, k}\), the set of carrying elements is a prefix \(\{\pi_k(1), \dots, \pi_k(c)\}\) of size \(c \in \{0, 1, \dots, N\}\).

  3. Transition from Stage \(k\) to Stage \(k + 1\): Suppose at stage \(k\) the first \(c\) elements of \(\pi_k\) have incoming carry \(1\), and the remaining \(N - c\) elements have incoming carry \(0\). Let \(a_{i, k} = \lfloor A_i / 10^k \rfloor \bmod 10\) be the \(k\)-th digit of \(A_i\). If we choose the \(k\)-th digit of \(x\) to be \(d \in \{0, 1, \dots, 9\}\):

    • An element \(i \in \{\pi_k(1), \dots, \pi_k(c)\}\) (with incoming carry \(1\)) has \(k\)-th digit \((a_{i, k} + d + 1) \bmod 10\), and produces an outgoing carry to stage \(k + 1\) iff \(a_{i, k} + d + 1 \ge 10\).
    • An element \(i \in \{\pi_k(c+1), \dots, \pi_k(N)\}\) (with incoming carry \(0\)) has \(k\)-th digit \((a_{i, k} + d) \bmod 10\), and produces an outgoing carry to stage \(k + 1\) iff \(a_{i, k} + d \ge 10\). Precomputing prefix counts of each digit \(a_{\pi_k(j), k} \in \{0, \dots, 9\}\) for \(1 \le j \le N\) allows us to evaluate both the total digit sum at stage \(k\) and the exact number of outgoing carries \(c'\) in \(O(1)\) time for each \((c, d)\) pair! At the end of \(D = 10\) stages (\(10^{10} > \max A_i\)), the final answer is \(\min_{0 \le c \le N} (dp[10][c] + c)\).

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: \(N \le 2 \times 10^5\) and \(1 \le A_1, \dots, A_N < 10^9\); \(D = 10\) decimal stages suffice since \(A_i < 10^9 < 10^{10}\).
  • Postconditions (Output): \(\min_{x \ge 0} \sum_{i=1}^N f(x + A_i)\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(D \cdot N \log N)\) time (or \(O(D \cdot N)\) using radix sort across stages) and \(O(N)\) auxiliary space.
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: After stage \(k\), the permutation \(\pi_{k+1}\) is obtained by stably bucketing \(\pi_k\) by the \(k\)-th digit \(a_{i, k}\) in descending order \(9, 8, \dots, 0\), ensuring that the \(c'\) elements with \(a_{i, k} + d + \text{carry}_i \ge 10\) form the exact prefix of length \(c'\) of \(\pi_{k+1}\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: At \(k = 0\), only \(c = 0\) is reachable (\(dp[0][0] = 0\), \(dp[0][c > 0] = +\infty\)); after stage \(D = 10\), any residual carry \(c \in \{0, \dots, N\}\) into position \(10^{10}\) must be added as \(+c\) to \(dp[10][c]\).

Intermediate 1 (#022) — Piecewise Order-Statistic Integral & Beta Recurrence (AtCoder ABC 226 H — Random K-th Max)

  • Tier: Intermediate 1 | Canonical ID: #022
  • Source: AtCoder Beginner Contest 226, Problem H — Random K-th Max
  • Prerequisite Tags: Tail-Expectation Integral, Bivariate Order-Statistic Generating Polynomial, D-Finite Beta Integral Recurrence (§18.1)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given \(N\) independent continuous random variables \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_N\), where each \(X_i\) is drawn uniformly at random from the real interval \([L_i, R_i]\). Let \(Y\) denote the \(K\)-th largest value among \((X_1, X_2, \dots, X_N)\). Compute the expected value \(\mathbb{E}[Y]\) modulo \(998244353\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le K \le N \le 50\), \(0 \le L_i < R_i \le 10^9\), modulus \(998244353\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(N^4)\) (or \(O(N^3)\) via sliding polynomial division) time and \(O(N^2)\) space.

Concrete Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\))

  • Instance (\(N = 2, K = 1, X_1 \sim [0, 2], X_2 \sim [1, 3]\)): The sorted endpoints \(0 < 1 < 2 < 3\) split \([0, 3]\) into three elementary intervals:
    1. On \([0, 1]\): \(\mathbb{P}(X_2 \ge x) = 1\), so at least \(K = 1\) variable is \(\ge x\) with probability \(1\). Integral over \([0, 1]\) is \(1\).
    2. On \([1, 2]\): \(\mathbb{P}(X_1 \ge x) = \frac{2-x}{2}\) and \(\mathbb{P}(X_2 \ge x) = \frac{3-x}{2}\). In local coordinate \(t = x - 1 \in [0, 1]\), \(\mathbb{P}(Y \ge 1 + t) = 1 - \frac{1+t}{2}\frac{t}{2} = 1 - \frac{t}{4} - \frac{t^2}{4}\). Integrating over \(t \in [0, 1]\) gives \(1 - \frac{1}{8} - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{19}{24}\).
    3. On \([2, 3]\): \(\mathbb{P}(X_1 \ge x) = 0\) and \(\mathbb{P}(X_2 \ge x) = \frac{3-x}{2}\). Integrating over \([2, 3]\) gives \(\frac{1}{4}\). Total expectation: \(\mathbb{E}[Y] = 1 + \frac{19}{24} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{49}{24} \equiv 457528664 \pmod{998244353}\).
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Because \(Y \ge 0\), use the tail-expectation identity \(\mathbb{E}[Y] = \int_0^\infty \mathbb{P}(Y \ge x)\,dx\). Sort all \(2N\) endpoints \(\{L_i, R_i\}\) into elementary intervals \([x_m, x_{m+1}]\). Inside a single elementary interval \([a, b]\), how does the survival probability \(p_i(x) = \mathbb{P}(X_i \ge x)\) depend on \(x\)?

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Inside \([a, b]\), each variable \(X_i\) has survival probability \(p_i(x) = 1\) (if \(b \le L_i\)), \(p_i(x) = 0\) (if \(a \ge R_i\)), or \(p_i(x) = \frac{R_i - x}{R_i - L_i}\) (if \(L_i \le a < b \le R_i\)). Introducing the local variable \(t = x - a \in [0, b - a]\) and a formal variable \(y\) to mark variables that are \(\ge x\), the probability generating function of the count of variables \(\ge a + t\) is: \[ P(t, y) = \prod_{i=1}^N \Big( (1 - p_i(a + t)) + y \, p_i(a + t) \Big). \] Notice that if we instead parameterize by \(Y < x\) (meaning at least \(N - K + 1\) variables are \(< x\)), we only need to track the single degree in \(t\)!

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Rather than maintaining a 2D polynomial in \((t, y)\), observe that \(\mathbb{P}(Y \ge x) = \sum_{j=K}^N [y^j] P(t, y)\) can be updated across elementary intervals, or inside each interval \([a, b]\) we can run a 2D knapsack DP over active variables where state \((j, d)\) is the coefficient of \(y^j t^d\). Even better: by conditioning on WHICH variable \(X_i\) equals \(Y = x\) on \([a, b]\) and how many other variables are \(> x\) vs. \(< x\), or by maintaining the active polynomial and dividing out a linear factor when an interval ends, the complexity drops to \(O(N^4)\) or \(O(N^3)\)!

Complete Derivation & Solution

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

  1. Piecewise Polynomial Decomposition via Tail Integral: Collect and sort the unique coordinates among \(\{0, L_1, R_1, \dots, L_N, R_N\}\) as \(0 = z_0 < z_1 < \dots < z_M\) (\(M \le 2N\)). By the tail integral identity: \[ \mathbb{E}[Y] = \int_0^{z_M} \mathbb{P}(Y \ge x)\,dx = \sum_{m=0}^{M-1} \int_{z_m}^{z_{m+1}} \mathbb{P}(Y \ge x)\,dx. \]

  2. Bivariate Generating Polynomial on \([a, b] = [z_m, z_{m+1}]\): Fix an elementary interval \([a, b]\) of length \(\Delta = b - a\). Substitute \(x = a + \Delta \cdot u\) with \(u \in [0, 1]\), \(dx = \Delta\,du\). For each \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\):

    • If \(R_i \le a\), \(\mathbb{P}(X_i \ge x) = 0\) (contributes factor \(1\)).
    • If \(L_i \ge b\), \(\mathbb{P}(X_i \ge x) = 1\) (contributes factor \(y\)).
    • If \(L_i \le a < b \le R_i\), let \(\ell_i = (R_i - L_i)^{-1} \bmod p\). Then: \[ p_i(u) = \mathbb{P}(X_i \ge a + \Delta u) = (R_i - a)\ell_i - (\Delta \ell_i) u = \alpha_i + \beta_i u, \] which contributes the linear factor \(\big((1 - \alpha_i) - \beta_i u\big) + y(\alpha_i + \beta_i u)\).

  3. D-Finite Moment Integration and \(O(N^3)\) Sliding Update: Let \(c_{\text{always}}\) be the count of variables with \(L_i \ge b\), and let \(S_{\text{act}}\) be the set of \(A = |S_{\text{act}}|\) active variables with \(L_i \le a < b \le R_i\). On \([a, b]\), at least \(K\) variables are \(\ge x\) if and only if at least \(r = \max(0, K - c_{\text{always}})\) of the active variables in \(S_{\text{act}}\) are \(\ge x\). Complete Derivation & Solution

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

  1. Compiling \(B(x) = \Delta(x)^{-1/2}\) via Recipe A: Set \(\gamma = (a^2 - 4bc) \bmod p\) and \(\Delta(x) = 1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2\). Taking the logarithmic derivative of \(B(x) = \Delta(x)^{-1/2}\) gives: \[ \frac{B'(x)}{B(x)} = -\frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta'(x)}{\Delta(x)} = \frac{a - \gamma x}{1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2} \implies (1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2) B'(x) - (a - \gamma x) B(x) = 0. \] Extracting \([x^{n-1}]\) for \(n \ge 1\): \[ n b_n - 2a(n - 1) b_{n-1} + \gamma(n - 2) b_{n-2} - a b_{n-1} + \gamma b_{n-2} = 0 \implies n b_n = a(2n - 1) b_{n-1} - (a^2 - 4bc)(n - 1) b_{n-2}, \] seeded with \(b_0 = 1\) and \(b_1 = a\).

  2. Compiling \(E(x)\) via Recipe B: From \(bc x^2 E(x)^2 + (ax - 1) E(x) + 1 = 0\), define \(Y(x) = 2bc x^2 E(x) + ax - 1 = -\Delta(x)^{1/2}\). By Recipe A with exponent \(+1/2\), \(Y(x)\) satisfies: \[ (1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2) Y'(x) + (a - \gamma x) Y(x) = 0. \] Substitute \(Y(x) = 2bc x^2 E(x) + ax - 1\) and \(Y'(x) = 2bc x^2 E'(x) + 4bc x E(x) + a\): \[ 2bc\Big( x^2(1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2) E'(x) + x(2 - 3ax + \gamma x^2) E(x) \Big) + (1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2)a + (a - \gamma x)(ax - 1) = 0. \] Notice that the inhomogeneous polynomial on the left simplifies to \(a(1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2) - a(1 - ax) + \gamma x(1 - ax) = (\gamma - a^2)x = -4bc x\). Dividing the entire ODE by \(2bc\) (which is valid symbolically even when \(bc = 0\), as \(bc\) cancels out!) gives the exact linear ODE: \[ x^2(1 - 2ax + \gamma x^2) E'(x) + x(2 - 3ax + \gamma x^2) E(x) = 2x. \] Extracting \([x^{n+1}]\) for \(n \ge 1\) (where \([x^{n+1}](2x) = 0\)) gives: \[ (n + 2) e_n = a(2n + 1) e_{n-1} - (a^2 - 4bc)(n - 1) e_{n-2} \qquad (n \ge 1, \quad e_0 = 1, \; e_{-1} = 0). \] Observe that because we divided by \(2bc\) symbolically before setting coefficients to zero, this 3-term recurrence works even in the degenerate case \(bc \equiv 0 \pmod p\) (where \(\gamma = a^2\) and \(e_n = a^n\))!

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: \(N \le 2 \times 10^6\) and step weights \(a, b, c \in \mathbb{F}_{998244353}\); requires \(N + 2 < 998244353\) so the indicial polynomials \(I_B(n) = n\) and \(I_E(n) = n + 2\) never vanish modulo \(998244353\) for \(1 \le n \le N\).

  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: If \(bc \equiv 0 \pmod{998244353}\), evaluating \(E(x) = \frac{1-ax-\sqrt{\Delta(x)}}{2bc x^2}\) directly divides by zero, whereas the symbolically simplified recurrence \((n+2)e_n = a(2n+1)e_{n-1} - a^2(n-1)e_{n-2}\) remains valid and outputs \(e_n = b_n = a^n\).

  • Instance 1 (\(n = 3, m = 1\)): There are \(2! = 2\) trees on \(\{1, 2, 3\}\):
    • \(p_2 = 1, p_3 = 1\) (star centered at \(1\)): centroid is \(1\).

  • Instance 2 (\(n = 5, m = 2\)): Among the \(4! = 24\) trees on \(\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}\):
    • Vertex \(3\) is the centroid iff \(\{4, 5\}\) both attach inside \(3\)’s subtree (\(1 \times 2 = 2\) ways) while \(2\) attaches to \(1\) (\(1\) way) and \(3\) attaches to \(\{1, 2\}\) (\(2\) ways), giving \(4\) trees.
    • Vertex \(2\) has subtree size \(\ge 3\) in \(12\) trees, of which \(2\) have centroid \(3\) (when \(3\) attaches to \(2\)), leaving \(12 - 2 = 10\) trees with centroid \(2\).

Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Root the recursive tree at vertex \(1\). Then every child of any vertex \(v\) has a label \(> v\). Vertex \(i\) is the unique centroid if and only if: 1. The subtree rooted at \(i\) has size \(s \ge m + 1 = \frac{n+1}{2}\) (so the component above \(i\) has size \(n - s \le m\)), and 2. Every child subtree of \(i\) has size \(\le m\). Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

We can solve this from two dual perspectives: 1. D-Finite ODE + Reversed Convolution: The unrestricted EGF of recursive trees is \(T(x) = \sum_{k \ge 1} (k-1)! \frac{x^k}{k!} = \ln \frac{1}{1-x}\) since \(T'(x) = \exp(T(x))\). Restricting child subtrees to size \(\le m\) replaces \(T(x)\) on the right-hand side by the truncated logarithm \(T_{\le m}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^m \frac{x^k}{k}\), so \(F'(x) = \exp\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^m \frac{x^k}{k}\right)\). Taking the logarithmic derivative of \(G(x) = F'(x)\) yields a 3-sparse D-finite ODE \((1-x)G'(x) = (1-x^m)G(x)\)! 2. Ancestor Dominance + Suffix Sum: Alternatively, let \(A_i\) be the number of trees where \(i\)’s subtree has size \(s \ge m + 1\) (without restricting \(i\)’s children). In any such tree, the unique centroid \(j \ge i\) lies inside \(i\)’s subtree, and for any \(j > i\), the probability that \(i\) is an ancestor of \(j\) is \(\frac{1}{i}\)!

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract
  • In Perspective 1: From \((1-x)G'(x) = (1-x^m)G(x)\), unroll \(g_k = [x^k]G(x)\) in \(O(n)\) time via \(k g_k = k g_{k-1} - g_{k-1-m}\), set \(f_s = g_{s-1}/s\), and for \(i \ge 2\) evaluate \(\text{ans}_i = (i-1)(n-i)! \sum_{s=m+1}^{n-i+1} f_s \frac{(s-1)! (n-s-1)!}{(n-i-s+1)!}\) via a single reversed NTT convolution in \(O(n \log n)\) time!
  • In Perspective 2: Summing over \(s \ge m + 1\) via the Hockey-Stick identity gives \(A_i = (n - i)! (i - 1)! \binom{n - m - 1}{i - 1}\) in closed form, and \(\text{ans}_i = A_i - \frac{1}{i} \sum_{j=i+1}^n \text{ans}_j\) unrolls in \(O(n)\) time with zero NTTs!
Complete Derivation & Solution

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

Let \(m = \frac{n-1}{2}\). Notice immediately that for \(i > m + 1\), vertex \(i\) can have at most \(n - i < m\) descendants from \(\{i+1, \dots, n\}\), so its subtree size is at most \(n - i + 1 \le m\), making \(\text{ans}_i = 0\).

  1. Perspective 1 (D-Finite ODE for Constrained Recursive Trees + Reversed Convolution): Let \(F(x) = \sum_{s=1}^n f_s x^s\) be the EGF of recursive trees in which every child subtree of the root has size \(\le m\). Since the root holds the smallest label in its subtree, removing the root leaves a set of recursive trees of sizes \(\le m\), each with EGF \(T_{\le m}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^m (k-1)! \frac{x^k}{k!} = \sum_{k=1}^m \frac{x^k}{k}\). Thus \(G(x) = F'(x) = \exp\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^m \frac{x^k}{k}\right)\). Applying Recipe A to \(G(x)\): \[ \frac{G'(x)}{G(x)} = \sum_{k=1}^m x^{k-1} = \frac{1 - x^m}{1 - x} \implies (1 - x) G'(x) - (1 - x^m) G(x) = 0. \] Extracting \([x^{k-1}]\) gives the 3-term P-recursive relation: \[ k g_k - (k - 1) g_{k-1} = g_{k-1} - g_{k-1-m} \implies g_k = g_{k-1} - \frac{g_{k-1-m}}{k} \qquad (k \ge 1, \; g_0 = 1). \] For \(1 \le k \le m\), \(g_k = 1\); for \(m + 1 \le k \le n - 1\), \(k - 1 - m < m\) so \(g_{k-1-m} = 1\) and \(g_k = 1 - \sum_{r=m+1}^k \frac{1}{r}\). Then \(f_s = \frac{g_{s-1}}{s}\). For \(i = 1\), \(\text{ans}_1 = n! f_n = (n-1)! g_{n-1}\). For \(2 \le i \le m + 1\), choosing \(s - 1\) descendants of \(i\) from \(\{i+1, \dots, n\}\) in \(\binom{n-i}{s-1}\) ways, forming a valid centroid subtree on \(i\) in \(s! f_s\) ways, attaching \(i\) to \(\{1, \dots, i-1\}\) in \(i - 1\) ways, and attaching the remaining \(n - s - i + 1\) vertices outside \(i\)’s subtree in \(\frac{(n-s-1)!}{(i-1)!}\) ways gives: \[ \text{ans}_i = (i - 1)(n - i)! \sum_{s=m+1}^{n-i+1} \Big( (s - 1)! g_{s-1} (n - s - 1)! \Big) \cdot \frac{1}{((n - i + 1) - s)!}, \] which is a single reversed convolution evaluated for all \(i\) in \(O(n \log n)\) time via NTT.

  2. Perspective 2 (\(O(n)\) Closed-Form \(A_i\) + Ancestor Suffix Sum): Let \(A_i\) be the number of trees where \(i\)’s subtree has size \(s \ge m + 1\) (with no restriction on \(i\)’s children, replacing \(s! f_s\) by \((s-1)!\)). Using \(\frac{(n-s-1)!}{(n-i-s+1)!} = (i - 2)! \binom{n-s-1}{i-2}\) and the Hockey-Stick identity: \[ A_i = (i - 1)(n - i)! \sum_{s=m+1}^{n-i+1} (i - 2)! \binom{n - s - 1}{i - 2} = (n - i)! (i - 1)! \binom{n - m - 1}{i - 1} \qquad (2 \le i \le m + 1), \] and \(A_1 = (n - 1)!\). In any tree counted by \(A_i\), the unique centroid is some \(j \ge i\) inside \(i\)’s subtree. Conversely, for any fixed centroid \(j > i\), the attachment of \(\{1, \dots, j\}\) outside \(j\)’s subtree is independent of the internal structure of \(j\)’s subtree, and \(i\) is an ancestor of \(j\) with probability \(\frac{1}{i}\). Therefore: \[ A_i = \text{ans}_i + \frac{1}{i} \sum_{j=i+1}^n \text{ans}_j \implies \text{ans}_i = A_i - \frac{1}{i} \sum_{j=i+1}^n \text{ans}_j, \] which computes \(\text{ans}_n, \text{ans}_{n-1}, \dots, \text{ans}_1\) in \(O(n)\) time with zero NTTs!

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: Odd integer \(3 \le n \le 2 \times 10^5\); requires \(n < 998244353\) so all factorials and inverses \(1/i\) exist in \(\mathbb{F}_{998244353}\).
  • Postconditions (Output): Sequence \(\text{ans}_1, \dots, \text{ans}_n \bmod 998244353\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(n)\) time and \(O(n)\) space via Perspective 2 (or \(O(n \log n)\) via Perspective 1).
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: Since \(n = 2m + 1\) is odd, the vertices with subtree size \(\ge m + 1\) form a downward chain from vertex \(1\) ending at the unique centroid \(j\), and each \(i < j\) lies on this chain with probability \(1/i\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: For \(i > m + 1\), a vertex \(i\) cannot have \(m = (n-1)/2\) descendants Chosen from \(\{i+1, \dots, n\}\), so \(A_i = 0\) and \(\text{ans}_i = 0\) identically; only indices \(1 \le i \le m + 1\) have non-zero centroid probabilities.

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Crossing Modular Indicial Roots (Failure Mode #8): Whenever the indicial polynomial \(I(m)\) (such as \(m \tilde{a}_0\) when \(m \ge p\), or a cleared linear factor \((a m - b)\) in a higher-order D-finite recurrence) is divisible by \(p\) at \(m = m^*\), naive \(\mathbb{F}_p\) arithmetic divides by zero. How many extra base-\(p\) digits must we carry to survive all divisions by \(I(m)\)?
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Compute the total valuation loss \(V = \sum_{m=1}^{n-2} v_p(I(m))\) across the recurrence range. Represent every non-zero recurrence value \(p_m\) in the \(p\)-adic valuation form \((u_m, e_m)\) from §18.4, where \(p_m \equiv u_m \cdot p^{e_m} \pmod{p^{V+1}}\) with \(\gcd(u_m, p) = 1\). Since \(u_m \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^{V+1}\mathbb{Z})^\times\) is always a unit, division by \(I(m) = u_{I} \cdot p^{v_p(I(m))}\) simply multiplies \(u_m\) by \(u_I^{-1} \bmod p^{V+1}\) and subtracts \(v_p(I(m))\) from \(e_m\)!

Complete Derivation & Solution

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

  1. Prüfer Degree EGF & Recipe A Compilation: By Prüfer’s bijection on \(n \ge 2\) vertices: \[ \text{Ans} = (n - 2)! [x^{n-2}] A(x)^n, \qquad A(x) = \sum_{c=0}^{n-2} a_c x^c, \quad a_c = \frac{w_{c+1}}{c!}. \] If \(a_0 = w_1 = 0\), every tree on \(n \ge 2\) vertices has at least two leaves of degree \(1\), so \(\text{Ans} = 0\). Otherwise, let \(S = \{c : a_c \neq 0\}\) (\(|S| = K\)). Taking the logarithmic derivative of \(P(x) = A(x)^n = \sum_{m \ge 0} p_m x^m\) via Recipe A (§18.2) gives \(A(x) P'(x) = n A'(x) P(x)\). Extracting \([x^{m-1}]\) yields \(\sum_{c \in S} (m - c) a_c p_{m-c} = \sum_{c \in S} n c a_c p_{m-c}\); isolating \(c = 0\) on the left and moving \(c \ge 1\) to the right yields the \(K\)-term P-recursive relation: \[ I(m) p_m = \sum_{c \in S \setminus \{0\}, \, c \le m} \big(c(n + 1) - m\big) a_c p_{m-c}, \qquad I(m) = m a_0. \]

  2. Resolving Indicial Singularities via §18.4 \(p\)-Adic Lifting: In the general P-recursive setting \(I(m) p_m = \sum_{i=1}^d P_i(m - i) p_{m-i}\), let \(V = \sum_{m=1}^{n-2} v_p(I(m))\) be the total \(p\)-adic valuation of the indicial polynomial across the unrolling horizon, and set the working modulus to \(M = p^{V+1}\):

    • Lift each initial condition and polynomial coefficient into the PAdicVal representation \((u, e)\) with \(u \in (\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^\times\) and \(e \in \mathbb{Z} \cup \{+\infty\}\) via PAdicVal::from_int(x, p, M).
    • At step \(m\), accumulate the right-hand side \(R_m = \sum_{i=1}^d P_i(m - i) p_{m-i}\) using add(a, b, p, M) and unit multiplication (u1 * u2 % M, e1 + e2).
    • Factor \(I(m) = u_I \cdot p^{e_I}\) with \(\gcd(u_I, p) = 1\). Since \(p \nmid u_I\), \(u_I\) is invertible in \(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\); compute \(u_I^{-1} \bmod M\) via the Extended Euclidean Algorithm (or Hensel lifting) and set: \[ p_m = \big((R_m.u \cdot u_I^{-1}) \bmod M, \; R_m.e - e_I\big). \]
    • Because the true rational coefficient \(p_{n-2} = [x^{n-2}] A(x)^n\) is \(p\)-integral, its final exponent satisfies \(p_{n-2}.e \ge 0\), and reducing \((p_{n-2}.e > 0 \ ? \ 0 : p_{n-2}.u \bmod p)\) recovers the exact answer modulo \(p\).

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: Integer \(n \ge 2\), sparse degree series \(A(x)\) with \(K\) non-zero terms (or order-\(d\) P-recursion), and prime modulus \(p\) with total indicial valuation loss \(V = \sum_{m=1}^{n-2} v_p(I(m))\); \(I(m) \neq 0\) in \(\mathbb{Z}\) for all \(1 \le m \le n - 2\) (all true characteristic-\(0\) roots are seeded as initial conditions), and \(a_0 = w_1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p\).
  • Postconditions (Output): Exact residue \((n - 2)! [x^{n-2}] A(x)^n \bmod p\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(n K \cdot \text{poly}(V))\) time and \(O(n \cdot V)\) space using PAdicVal arithmetic modulo \(p^{V+1}\) (\(O(n K)\) hardware operations when \(V \le 1\) using __int128 modulo \(p^2\)).
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: Each division by \(I(m)\) with \(v_p(I(m)) = e_I\) shifts the \(p\)-adic valuation by \(-e_I\) and consumes at most \(e_I\) base-\(p\) digits of precision when adding terms of different valuations; initializing with \(V + 1\) digits modulo \(p^{V+1}\) guarantees that at least \(1\) exact base-\(p\) digit of \(u_{n-2}\) survives at \(m = n - 2\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: If \(I(m) = 0\) identically in \(\mathbb{Z}\), \(u_m\) is a free parameter (true characteristic-\(0\) indicial root) that must be supplied as an initial condition rather than lifted; for \(p\)-adic roots \(I(m) \equiv 0 \pmod p\) with \(I(m) \neq 0\), track (val, unit) modulo \(p^{V+1}\) and guard the final reconstruction with \(v \ge 0\) (returning \(0 \bmod p\) whenever \(v > 0\)).
