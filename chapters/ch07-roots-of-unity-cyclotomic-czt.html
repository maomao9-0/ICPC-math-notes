NotePrerequisites & Learning Objectives

Prerequisites:

  • Chapter 01 (Ch 01): Formal power series rings \(K[[x]]\), polynomial rings \(K[x]\), units vs. zero divisors in commutative rings, and formal residue arithmetic.
  • Chapter 02 (Ch 02): Truncated quotients \(K[x]/(x^N)\), the algebraic Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) over prime fields \(\mathbb{F}_p\), and the standard radix-2 Number-Theoretic Transform (NTT).

Learning Objectives:

  1. View the Discrete Fourier Transform not just as an \(O(L \log L)\) multiplication black box, but as an explicit Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) ring isomorphism \(K[x]/(x^L - 1) \cong \prod_{r=0}^{L-1} K[x]/(x - \omega_L^r)\) that diagonalizes cyclic shift operators.
  2. Convert multiplicative group convolutions modulo a prime \(P\) (\(i \cdot j \bmod P\)) into cyclic additive convolutions modulo \(P-1\) via discrete logarithms (\(\operatorname{ind}_g\)), while cleanly isolating the absorbing element \(0 \bmod P\).
  3. Execute Arbitrary-Modulus Convolution (MTT) over any integer modulus \(M \le 10^9 + 9\) with zero floating-point precision loss using 3-Prime NTT and Garner’s Mixed-Radix CRT reconstruction.
  4. Derive Bluestein’s Chirp Z-Transform (CZT) to evaluate polynomials on arbitrary geometric progressions \(x_i = a \cdot c^i\) and execute arbitrary-length DFTs (\(L\) not a power of \(2\)) in \(O((n+m)\log(n+m))\) operations—avoiding Failure Mode #12 (\(c^{1/2} \notin \mathbb{F}_p\) and \(c=0\)).

Apply Roots-of-Unity Index Filters (单位根反演) to extract arithmetic progressions \(\sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} f_k\) and floor sums \(\sum_i f_i \lfloor i/m \rfloor\), and resolve Failure Mode #11 (when \(m \nmid (p-1)\) so \(\omega_m \notin \mathbb{F}_p\)) using either the cyclic quotient ring \(K[x]/(x^m - 1)\) or the cyclotomic quotient ring \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_m(\omega))\).

7.1 DFT as Vandermonde Evaluation–Interpolation, Cyclic Wrap-Around & Multiplicative Convolutions

In Chapter 02, we used the Number-Theoretic Transform (NTT) primarily as an algorithmic engine to compute truncated products \(A(x)B(x) \bmod x^N\) by zero-padding both inputs to a power of two \(L \ge 2N - 1\) so that indices never wrap around. In this chapter, we invert our perspective: cyclic wrap-around is not a bug to be padded away, but an exact algebraic tool for counting problems governed by modular arithmetic, cyclic shifts, and group homomorphisms.

7.1.1 The Algebraic Meaning of the DFT: CRT Isomorphism on \(K[x]/(x^L - 1)\)

Suppose we want to track a combinatorial quantity—such as the sum of chosen elements or the position of a random walk—modulo a fixed period \(L \ge 1\). In polynomial language, two monomials \(x^a\) and \(x^b\) represent the same residue class modulo \(L\) whenever \(a \equiv b \pmod L\), which is equivalent to imposing the relation \(x^L = 1\).

The natural algebraic home for sequences indexed by \(\mathbb{Z}_L = \mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}\) is the cyclic quotient ring \[ \mathcal{R}_L = K[x]/(x^L - 1). \] An element of \(\mathcal{R}_L\) is represented uniquely by a polynomial \(A(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} a_i x^i\) of degree \(< L\). Multiplication in \(\mathcal{R}_L\) is cyclic convolution of length \(L\): for \(A(x), B(x) \in \mathcal{R}_L\), their product \(C(x) = A(x)B(x) \bmod (x^L - 1)\) has coefficients \[ c_k = \sum_{\substack{0 \le i, j < L \\ i + j \equiv k \pmod L}} a_i b_j \qquad (0 \le k < L). \]

When can we diagonalize this ring so that cyclic convolution becomes pointwise multiplication? Suppose \(K = \mathbb{F}_p\) is a prime field (or a commutative ring) satisfying two local algebraic hypotheses:

  1. \(L\) is a unit in \(K\) (\(L \in K^\times\), i.e., \(p \nmid L\) when \(K = \mathbb{F}_p\)).
  2. \(K\) contains a primitive (or principal) \(L\)-th root of unity \(\omega_L\): over a field \(K\) (or an integral domain), this means \(\omega_L\) has multiplicative order \(L\), so \(\omega_L^L = 1\) and \(\omega_L^d \ne 1\) for all proper divisors \(d \mid L, d < L\) (over \(\mathbb{F}_p\), this holds if and only if \(L \mid (p-1)\), where \(\omega_L = g^{(p-1)/L} \bmod p\) for a primitive root \(g\) of \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\)); over a general commutative ring \(K\), \(\omega_L\) is a principal \(L\)-th root of unity, meaning \(\omega_L^L = 1\) and \(\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} \omega_L^{jt} = 0\) for all \(1 \le t < L\) (which guarantees \(\omega_L^r - \omega_L^s \in K^\times\) for all \(0 \le r < s < L\)).

Under these two hypotheses, the polynomial \(x^L - 1\) factors completely into \(L\) pairwise coprime linear factors in \(K[x]\): \[ x^L - 1 = \prod_{r=0}^{L-1} (x - \omega_L^r). \] By the Chinese Remainder Theorem for polynomial rings, reducing \(A(x) \bmod (x - \omega_L^r)\) is simply evaluating \(A(\omega_L^r)\). Thus, we obtain an explicit ring isomorphism: \[ \operatorname{DFT}_L : K[x]/(x^L - 1) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \prod_{r=0}^{L-1} K[x]/(x - \omega_L^r) \cong K^L, \qquad A(x) \longmapsto \bigl(A(\omega_L^0),\, A(\omega_L^1),\, \dots,\, A(\omega_L^{L-1})\bigr). \]

In matrix form, \(\operatorname{DFT}_L\) multiplies the coefficient vector \(\mathbf{a} = (a_0, \dots, a_{L-1})^\top\) by the \(L \times L\) Vandermonde matrix \(V(\omega_L)\) with entries \(V_{r, j} = \omega_L^{rj}\): \[ \hat{a}_r = A(\omega_L^r) = \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} a_j \omega_L^{rj} \qquad (0 \le r < L). \] Its inverse \(\operatorname{IDFT}_L\) reconstructs the unique polynomial \(A(x) \in K[x]/(x^L - 1)\) from its \(L\) point values via the inverse Vandermonde matrix \(V(\omega_L)^{-1} = \frac{1}{L} V(\omega_L^{-1})\): \[ a_j = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{r=0}^{L-1} \hat{a}_r \omega_L^{-rj} \qquad (0 \le j < L). \]

TipWhy Exploiting Cyclic Wrap-Around Saves Work

Whenever a problem asks for coefficients of \(A(x)^n \bmod (x^L - 1)\) (for instance, counting length-\(n\) sequences whose sum modulo \(L\) equals \(k\)), we never compute the full degree-\(n(L-1)\) polynomial and reduce afterward. Instead:

  • Case 1 (\(L \mid (p-1)\) and \(L = 2^k\)): Apply a single length-\(L\) forward NTT to obtain \(\hat{a}_r = A(\omega_L^r)\), raise each point value to the \(n\)-th power \(\hat{a}_r^n \bmod p\) in \(O(L \log n)\) field operations, and apply a single length-\(L\) inverse NTT. Total time: \(O(L \log L + L \log n)\) instead of \(O(L \log L \log n)\).
  • Case 2 (\(L\) is not a power of 2 or \(\omega_L \notin \mathbb{F}_p\)): Maintain polynomials of degree \(< L\) during binary exponentiation, reducing modulo \((x^L - 1)\) after each polynomial multiplication by folding the upper half into the lower half: for \(i \ge L\), add the coefficient of \(x^i\) into \(x^{i \bmod L}\) in \(O(L)\) time.

7.1.2 Multiplicative Group Convolution \(\bmod P\) (Ind_g Reduction)

Up to now, every convolution we have studied combines indices additively (\(i + j\) or \((i + j) \bmod L\)). What if a combinatorial process combines states by modular multiplication?

Suppose \(P\) is a prime number, and we are given two arrays \(a, b\) indexed by residues modulo \(P\). We want to compute the multiplicative convolution modulo \(P\): \[ c_k = \sum_{\substack{0 \le i, j < P \\ i \cdot j \equiv k \pmod P}} a_i b_j \qquad (0 \le k < P), \] or, more generally, we are given a subset \(S \subseteq \{0, 1, \dots, P-1\}\) and asked how many length-\(n\) sequences \((s_1, \dots, s_n) \in S^n\) have product \(s_1 s_2 \cdots s_n \equiv x \pmod P\).

The Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • The Diagnostic Signal: The transition or convolution index combines via multiplication modulo a prime \(P\): \(i \cdot j \equiv k \pmod P\), or \(\prod_{t=1}^n s_t \equiv x \pmod P\).
  • The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad:
    1. How do we turn multiplication into addition? Over the positive reals, the logarithm satisfies \(\ln(i \cdot j) = \ln(i) + \ln(j)\). What is the exact finite-field analogue of the logarithm for residues modulo a prime \(P\)?
    2. Be careful: \(0 \bmod P\) is not invertible! In \(\mathbb{Z}_P\), the element \(0\) is an absorbing element (\(0 \cdot j \equiv 0 \pmod P\)). However, the remaining \(P-1\) nonzero residues \(\mathbb{F}_P^\times = \{1, 2, \dots, P-1\}\) form a cyclic group of order \(P-1\) under multiplication modulo \(P\).
    3. Because \(\mathbb{F}_P^\times\) is cyclic of order \(P-1\), there exists a primitive root \(g \in \{1, \dots, P-1\}\) whose powers \(g^0, g^1, \dots, g^{P-2} \bmod P\) visit every nonzero residue in \(\{1, \dots, P-1\}\) exactly once, with \(g^{P-1} \equiv 1 \pmod P\).
    4. Define the discrete logarithm (index) map \(\operatorname{ind}_g : \{1, \dots, P-1\} \to \{0, 1, \dots, P-2\}\) by the unique exponent \(u = \operatorname{ind}_g(x) \in \{0, \dots, P-2\}\) such that \(g^u \equiv x \pmod P\).
    5. For any two nonzero residues \(i = g^u \bmod P\) and \(j = g^v \bmod P\), their product modulo \(P\) is \[ i \cdot j \equiv g^u \cdot g^v \equiv g^{(u + v) \bmod (P-1)} \pmod P. \] Under the bijection \(x \leftrightarrow \operatorname{ind}_g(x)\), multiplication in \(\mathbb{F}_P^\times\) becomes cyclic addition modulo \(P-1\) in \(\mathbb{Z}_{P-1}\)!

Single-Step Verbalized Derivation (Separating \(0\) from \(\mathbb{F}_P^\times\))

Let us write out the complete reduction of \(c_k = \sum_{i \cdot j \equiv k \pmod P} a_i b_j\) into two clean pieces:

  1. Isolate the absorbing element \(k = 0\): Since \(P\) is prime, \(\mathbb{F}_P\) is an integral domain, so a product \(i \cdot j \equiv 0 \pmod P\) if and only if \(i = 0\) or \(j = 0\). Counting all pairs \((i, j) \in \{0, \dots, P-1\}^2\) where at least one index is \(0\) gives \[ c_0 = a_0 \sum_{j=0}^{P-1} b_j + b_0 \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} a_i - a_0 b_0 = a_0 \sum_{j=0}^{P-1} b_j + b_0 \sum_{i=1}^{P-1} a_i. \]
  2. Re-index the nonzero group \(\mathbb{F}_P^\times = \{1, \dots, P-1\}\) via \(\operatorname{ind}_g\): Define new arrays \(\tilde{a}, \tilde{b}\) of length \(L = P-1\) indexed by \(u \in \{0, 1, \dots, P-2\}\): \[ \tilde{a}_u = a_{g^u \bmod P}, \qquad \tilde{b}_v = b_{g^v \bmod P} \qquad (0 \le u, v \le P-2). \]
  3. Perform cyclic additive convolution of length \(P-1\): Form the polynomials \(\tilde{A}(z) = \sum_{u=0}^{P-2} \tilde{a}_u z^u\) and \(\tilde{B}(z) = \sum_{v=0}^{P-2} \tilde{b}_v z^v\), and compute their product in the cyclic quotient ring \(K[z]/(z^{P-1} - 1)\): \[ \tilde{C}(z) = \tilde{A}(z)\tilde{B}(z) \bmod (z^{P-1} - 1) = \sum_{w=0}^{P-2} \tilde{c}_w z^w, \qquad \text{where } \tilde{c}_w = \sum_{u + v \equiv w \pmod{P-1}} \tilde{a}_u \tilde{b}_v. \] In practical NTT implementations where the transform length \(N_{\text{ntt}} = 2^{\lceil \log_2(2(P-1)-1) \rceil}\) exceeds \(P-1\), we simply multiply \(\tilde{A}(z)\) and \(\tilde{B}(z)\) as ordinary polynomials of degree \(\le 2P - 4\) and then fold the upper coefficients back into the lower half: for each \(w \in \{0, \dots, P-2\}\), set \(\tilde{c}_w \leftarrow [z^w](\tilde{A}\tilde{B}) + [z^{w + P - 1}](\tilde{A}\tilde{B})\).
  4. Map exponents back to nonzero residues: For each \(w \in \{0, \dots, P-2\}\), set \[ c_{g^w \bmod P} = \tilde{c}_w. \]

Concrete Hand-Checkable Trace (\(P = 5\))

Let \(P = 5\), and suppose we want to compute the multiplicative self-convolution \(c_k = \sum_{i \cdot j \equiv k \pmod 5} a_i a_j\) for the indicator polynomial of \(S = \{1, 2, 3\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_5\), so \((a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4) = (0, 1, 1, 1, 0)\).

  1. Absorbing element \(0\): Since \(a_0 = 0\), \(c_0 = 0\).
  2. Primitive root and index table for \(\mathbb{F}_5^\times\): Let \(g = 2\) be our primitive root modulo \(5\). The powers of \(2\) modulo \(5\) are:
    • \(u = 0 : 2^0 \equiv 1 \pmod 5 \implies \operatorname{ind}_2(1) = 0\)
    • \(u = 1 : 2^1 \equiv 2 \pmod 5 \implies \operatorname{ind}_2(2) = 1\)
    • \(u = 2 : 2^2 \equiv 4 \pmod 5 \implies \operatorname{ind}_2(4) = 2\)
    • \(u = 3 : 2^3 \equiv 3 \pmod 5 \implies \operatorname{ind}_2(3) = 3\)
  3. Re-indexed polynomial \(\tilde{A}(z)\) of length \(P-1 = 4\): \[ \tilde{a}_0 = a_1 = 1, \quad \tilde{a}_1 = a_2 = 1, \quad \tilde{a}_2 = a_4 = 0, \quad \tilde{a}_3 = a_3 = 1 \implies \tilde{A}(z) = 1 + z + z^3. \]
  4. Ordinary square and cyclic folding \(\bmod (z^4 - 1)\): \[ \tilde{A}(z)^2 = (1 + z + z^3)^2 = 1 + 2z + z^2 + 2z^3 + 2z^4 + z^6. \] Folding \(z^4 \equiv z^0\) and \(z^6 \equiv z^2 \pmod{z^4 - 1}\) gives \[ \tilde{C}(z) = (1 + 2)z^0 + 2z^1 + (1 + 1)z^2 + 2z^3 = 3 + 2z + 2z^2 + 2z^3. \]
  5. Map back via \(w \mapsto 2^w \bmod 5\):
    • \(w = 0 \implies k = 2^0 \bmod 5 = 1 : c_1 = 3\) (pairs \((1,1), (2,3), (3,2)\))
    • \(w = 1 \implies k = 2^1 \bmod 5 = 2 : c_2 = 2\) (pairs \((1,2), (2,1)\))
    • \(w = 2 \implies k = 2^2 \bmod 5 = 4 : c_4 = 2\) (pairs \((2,2), (3,3)\))
    • \(w = 3 \implies k = 2^3 \bmod 5 = 3 : c_3 = 2\) (pairs \((1,3), (3,1)\))
    The total number of pairs is \(3 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 9 = |S|^2\), matching every ordered pair in \(\{1, 2, 3\}^2\).
WarningFailure Mode & Remedy: Non-Cyclic Multiplicative Groups Modulo Composite \(M\) & Prime Powers \(p^k\)

The Trap: What if a problem asks for multiplicative convolution \(i \cdot j \bmod M\) where \(M\) is not prime? A common mistake is assuming that \(\mathbb{Z}_M^\times\) always has a primitive root \(g\), or assuming that non-units simply collapse to \(0\) in a single step.

Why It Fails:

  1. Zero divisors: When \(M\) is composite, \(i \cdot j \equiv 0 \pmod M\) can occur even when \(i \not\equiv 0\) and \(j \not\equiv 0 \pmod M\) (e.g., \(2 \cdot 2 \equiv 0 \pmod 4\), or \(2 \cdot 3 \equiv 0 \pmod 6\)). Non-coprime residues (\(\gcd(i, M) > 1\)) do not belong to the unit group \(\mathbb{Z}_M^\times\).
  2. Non-cyclic unit groups: By Gauss’s theorem, \(\mathbb{Z}_M^\times\) is cyclic (possesses a single primitive root \(g\) of order \(\varphi(M)\)) if and only if \(M \in \{1, 2, 4, p^k, 2p^k\}\) for an odd prime \(p\). For \(M = 2^k\) (\(k \ge 3\)) or any \(M\) divisible by two distinct odd primes (such as \(M = 15\) or \(M = 12\)), no single generator \(g\) exists!

The Exact Algebraic Remedy:

  • Case 1 (Odd prime power \(M = p^k\)): The unit group \((\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})^\times\) is cyclic of order \(\varphi(p^k) = p^{k-1}(p - 1)\) (if \(g\) is a primitive root modulo \(p\) and \(g^{p-1} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}\), then \(g\) is also a primitive root modulo \(p^k\) for all \(k \ge 1\)). For non-units, factor every residue \(x \in \mathbb{Z}_{p^k} \setminus \{0\}\) uniquely as \(x = p^v \cdot u\) with \(0 \le v < k\) and \(u \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^{k-v}\mathbb{Z})^\times\); stratify by the \(p\)-adic valuation \(v = v_p(x)\) (which adds via \(v_p(i \cdot j) = \min(k, v_p(i) + v_p(j))\)) and apply cyclic discrete-log convolution on the unit part \(u\).
  • Case 2 (Power of two \(M = 2^k\) with \(k \ge 3\)): The unit group \((\mathbb{Z}/2^k\mathbb{Z})^\times\) is isomorphic to the 2-dimensional abelian group \(\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_{2^{k-2}}\), generated by \(-1\) (of order \(2\)) and \(5\) (of order \(2^{k-2}\)). Every odd residue \(u \in (\mathbb{Z}/2^k\mathbb{Z})^\times\) has a unique representation \(u \equiv (-1)^a \cdot 5^b \pmod{2^k}\) with \(a \in \{0, 1\}\) and \(b \in \{0, 1, \dots, 2^{k-2}-1\}\). Multiplication of odd residues becomes a 2D cyclic convolution of size \(2 \times 2^{k-2}\)!
  • Case 3 (General composite \(M = \prod_{r=1}^s p_r^{k_r}\)): By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, \(\mathbb{Z}_M \cong \prod_{r=1}^s \mathbb{Z}_{p_r^{k_r}}\), so the unit group \(\mathbb{Z}_M^\times \cong \prod_{r=1}^s (\mathbb{Z}/p_r^{k_r}\mathbb{Z})^\times\) decomposes into a direct product of cyclic groups, transforming multiplicative convolution on units into a multidimensional cyclic DFT across the prime-power factors.

    • dft_cyclic_exp: Coefficient array of \(A(x)^n \bmod (x^L - 1)\) of length \(L\).
    • mult_conv_mod_p: Array \(c \in K^P\) where \(c_k = \sum_{i \cdot j \equiv k \pmod P} a_i b_j\) for \(0 \le k < P\).
  • Preconditions:
    • In mult_conv_mod_p, \(P\) must be prime so that \(\mathbb{F}_P^\times = \{1, \dots, P-1\}\) is cyclic of order \(P-1\).
    • The coefficient ring \(K\) (e.g., \(\mathbb{F}_{998244353}\) or \(\mathbb{F}_{1004535809}\)) must support length-\((2P-3)\) polynomial multiplication (via standard NTT or 3-Prime MTT).
  • Core Invariant:
    • Zero index \(k = 0\) is isolated before taking discrete logarithms: \(c_0 = a_0 \sum_{j=0}^{P-1} b_j + b_0 \sum_{i=1}^{P-1} a_i\).
    • For \(1 \le x < P\), \(\operatorname{ind}_g(x) \in \{0, \dots, P-2\}\) is a group isomorphism \((\mathbb{F}_P^\times, \cdot) \xrightarrow{\sim} (\mathbb{Z}_{P-1}, +)\), and folding index \(w + (P - 1)\) into \(w\) for \(0 \le w \le P - 3\) after degree-\((2P-4)\) Cauchy multiplication preserves exact cyclic wrap-around modulo \(P - 1\).
  • Complexity:
    • dft_cyclic_exp: \(O(L \log L + L \log n)\) time if \(L = 2^k \mid (p-1)\), or \(O(L \log L \log n)\) via binary exponentiation with cyclic folding; \(O(L)\) space.
    • mult_conv_mod_p: \(O(\sqrt{P})\) (or \(O(P)\)) to find primitive root \(g\) and build \(\operatorname{ind}_g\), plus \(O(P \log P)\) for one cyclic convolution of length \(P - 1\); \(O(P)\) space.

7.2 Arbitrary-Modulus Convolution (MTT) via 3-Prime NTT & Garner’s CRT

In Chapter 02, our NTT engine required the modulus \(p\) to be an NTT-friendly prime of the form \(p = c \cdot 2^k + 1\) with \(2^k \ge L\). What happens when a problem asks for polynomial convolution or formal power series inversion modulo \(M = 10^9 + 7\), modulo \(M = 999999999\), or modulo an arbitrary prime \(p\) given at runtime (as we will face in Mas-2: Luogu P5293)?

7.2.1 Why 3-Prime NTT Beats Floating-Point FFT (MTT)

Two competing families of algorithms go by the name MTT (Modulus Transform Technique) in competitive programming:

  1. Split-Radix Complex FFT: Splitting each coefficient into \(15\)-bit halves \(a_i = a_{i,\text{hi}} \cdot 2^{15} + a_{i,\text{lo}}\) and running 4 to 7 complex FFTs in double or long double. While fast in wall-clock time, floating-point MTT is notorious for subtle rounding errors when \(N \ge 2^{18}\) and \(M \approx 10^9\), especially if std::polar or naive trigonometric recurrences drift by \(10^{-12}\) ULPs.
  2. 3-Prime NTT + Garner’s Mixed-Radix CRT: Performing exact integer NTTs modulo three distinct NTT-friendly primes \(p_1, p_2, p_3\) and reconstructing the exact integer convolution coefficients via the Chinese Remainder Theorem before reducing modulo \(M\).

Because 3-Prime NTT uses 100% exact integer arithmetic, it suffers zero floating-point precision loss and reuses your existing NTT library verbatim.

7.2.2 Choosing the Three Canonical NTT Primes

Suppose we convolve two sequences \(A, B\) of length \(N \le 5 \times 10^5\) with coefficients in \(\{0, 1, \dots, M-1\}\), where \(M \le 10^9 + 9\). Over the integers \(\mathbb{Z}\) (before any modular reduction), each exact convolution coefficient \(C_k = \sum_{i+j=k} A_i B_j\) satisfies the strict upper bound \[ 0 \le C_k \le N (M - 1)^2 \approx 5 \times 10^5 \times 10^{18} = 5 \times 10^{23}. \] Therefore, if we compute \(C_k \bmod p_1\), \(C_k \bmod p_2\), and \(C_k \bmod p_3\) for three distinct primes whose product \(P = p_1 p_2 p_3\) exceeds \(N(M-1)^2\), the Chinese Remainder Theorem uniquely determines the exact integer \(C_k \in [0, p_1 p_2 p_3)\)!

The standard triad of NTT primes with primitive root \(g = 3\) for all three is:

Prime \(p_i\) Factorization \(c \cdot 2^k + 1\) Max Power-of-2 Length \(2^k\) Primitive Root \(g\)
\(p_1 = 998244353\) \(119 \cdot 2^{23} + 1\) \(2^{23} = 8{,}388{,}608\) \(3\)
\(p_2 = 1004535809\) \(479 \cdot 2^{21} + 1\) \(2^{21} = 2{,}097{,}152\) \(3\)
\(p_3 = 469762049\) \(7 \cdot 2^{26} + 1\) \(2^{26} = 67{,}108{,}864\) \(3\)

Their product is \[ p_1 p_2 p_3 \approx 4.71 \times 10^{26} > 5 \times 10^{23} \ge N (M - 1)^2, \] which safely covers all convolution lengths up to \(N = 2^{20}\) and moduli up to \(M \le 2 \times 10^9\).

7.2.3 Step-by-Step Derivation of Garner’s Mixed-Radix CRT Reconstruction

How do we reconstruct \(X = C_k \bmod M\) from its three remainders \[ r_1 = X \bmod p_1, \qquad r_2 = X \bmod p_2, \qquad r_3 = X \bmod p_3 \] without needing 128-bit or arbitrary-precision big-integer division by \(p_1 p_2 p_3 \approx 4.71 \times 10^{26}\) (which exceeds \(2^{64} \approx 1.84 \times 10^{19}\))?

The Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • The Diagnostic Signal: We know \(X \bmod p_1, X \bmod p_2, X \bmod p_3\) with \(0 \le X < p_1 p_2 p_3\), and we want \(X \bmod M\), where \(p_1 p_2 p_3 > 2^{64}\) overflows standard 64-bit unsigned integers.
  • The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad:
    1. Instead of writing \(X\) in the standard CRT sum \(\sum_{i=1}^3 r_i M_i (M_i^{-1} \bmod p_i) \bmod (p_1 p_2 p_3)\)—which requires reducing a huge sum modulo the 89-bit integer \(p_1 p_2 p_3\)—let us write \(X\) in mixed-radix representation with base \((1, p_1, p_1 p_2)\): \[ X = x_1 + x_2 p_1 + x_3 p_1 p_2, \] where the “digits” satisfy \(0 \le x_1 < p_1\), \(0 \le x_2 < p_2\), and \(0 \le x_3 < p_3\).
    2. Notice that every integer \(X \in [0, p_1 p_2 p_3)\) has a unique mixed-radix tuple \((x_1, x_2, x_3)\) in those ranges!
    3. How do we find \(x_1, x_2, x_3\) one by one using only arithmetic modulo \(p_1, p_2, p_3\)?
      • Take the equation modulo \(p_1\): since \(p_1 \equiv 0 \pmod{p_1}\), the \(x_2 p_1\) and \(x_3 p_1 p_2\) terms vanish! We immediately get \[ x_1 = r_1 \bmod p_1. \]
      • Now take the equation modulo \(p_2\): the \(x_3 p_1 p_2\) term vanishes! We get \(x_1 + x_2 p_1 \equiv r_2 \pmod{p_2}\). Subtracting \(x_1\) and multiplying by the modular inverse \(inv_{1,2} = p_1^{-1} \bmod p_2\) gives \[ x_2 = (r_2 - x_1) \cdot (p_1^{-1} \bmod p_2) \bmod p_2. \]
      • Finally, take the equation modulo \(p_3\): \(x_1 + x_2 p_1 + x_3 p_1 p_2 \equiv r_3 \pmod{p_3}\). Subtracting \((x_1 + x_2 p_1) \bmod p_3\) and multiplying by \(inv_{12,3} = (p_1 p_2)^{-1} \bmod p_3\) gives \[ x_3 = \bigl((r_3 - x_1 - x_2 p_1) \bmod p_3\bigr) \cdot \bigl((p_1 p_2)^{-1} \bmod p_3\bigr) \bmod p_3. \]
    4. Once the exact bounded digits \(x_1 \in [0, p_1)\), \(x_2 \in [0, p_2)\), and \(x_3 \in [0, p_3)\) are known, the identity \(X = x_1 + x_2 p_1 + x_3 p_1 p_2\) holds as an exact equality of integers in \(\mathbb{Z}\)! Therefore, we can project it directly modulo our target modulus \(M\): \[ X \bmod M = \Bigl(x_1 + x_2 (p_1 \bmod M) + x_3 (p_1 p_2 \bmod M)\Bigr) \bmod M. \] Every multiplication in this final projection is between two numbers \(< 2 \times 10^9\), which fits comfortably inside a standard 64-bit integer (uint64_t / long long)!
WarningFailure Mode & Remedy: Premature Modulo-\(M\) Reduction During Garner’s CRT

The Trap: A frequent bug when implementing Garner’s CRT is computing \(X_{12} = (x_1 + x_2 p_1) \bmod M\) first and then trying to compute \(x_3\) from \(X_{12}\), or forgetting to reduce \(x_3\) into \([0, p_3)\) before multiplying by \((p_1 p_2 \bmod M)\).

Why It Fails:

  1. To compute \(x_3\), we need \((x_1 + x_2 p_1) \bmod p_3\), not \((x_1 + x_2 p_1) \bmod M\). Since \(x_1 + x_2 p_1 < p_1 p_2 \approx 10^{18}\) fits inside a signed 64-bit integer (int64_t), you should store the exact 64-bit integer \(X_{12} = x_1 + x_2 p_1\) first.
  2. In Step 3, \(x_3\) must be reduced modulo \(p_3\) into the canonical range \([0, p_3)\) before we transition to modulo-\(M\) arithmetic. This is because the equality \(X = X_{12} + x_3 p_1 p_2\) in \(\mathbb{Z}\) holds only for the unique representative \(x_3 \in [0, p_3)\). If \(x_3\) is off by a multiple of \(p_3\), then \(X\) changes by a multiple of \(p_1 p_2 p_3\), which is not a multiple of \(M\)!

The Exact 64-Bit Reconstruction Snippet:

// Precomputed constants:
// inv1_2 = qpow(p1, p2 - 2, p2);
// inv12_3 = qpow(1ULL * p1 * p2 % p3, p3 - 2, p3);

uint64_t x3 = (r3 + p3 - X12 % p3) * inv12_3 % p3; // Exact digit in [0, p3)

7.3 The Chirp Z-Transform (Bluestein’s Algorithm) & Failure Mode #12

In Chapter 02, our general multipoint evaluation algorithm evaluated a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(F(x)\) at \(m\) arbitrary points \(x_0, \dots, x_{m-1}\) in \(O((n + m) \log^2(n + m))\) time with a heavy constant factor (requiring a product tree of polynomials and transposed remainders).

However, in a vast range of generating function problems—including arbitrary-length DFTs (\(L\) not a power of \(2\)), \(q\)-analogues, and root-of-unity filters—the evaluation points are not arbitrary: they form a geometric progression \[ x_i = a \cdot c^i \qquad (0 \le i < m), \] where \(a, c \in K\) are fixed scalars. Given \(F(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_j x^j\), how fast can we compute all \(m\) values \[ y_i = F(a \cdot c^i) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_j a^j c^{ij} \qquad (0 \le i < m)? \] Bluestein’s Chirp Z-Transform (CZT) solves this entire multipoint evaluation in a single polynomial convolution—taking just \(O((n + m) \log(n + m))\) field operations!

7.3.1 Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • The Diagnostic Signal: A double sum or evaluation features a bilinear exponent \(c^{i \cdot j}\) (or \(\omega^{i \cdot j}\) where the length \(L\) is not a power of \(2\)), coupling the output index \(i \in [0, m)\) with the summation index \(j \in [0, n)\).
  • The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad:
    1. Why can’t we apply a convolution directly to \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} (f_j a^j) c^{ij}\)? Because in a convolution, the kernel must depend only on the sum \(i + j\) (or difference \(i - j\)) of the two indices, whereas \(c^{ij}\) depends on their product \(i \cdot j\).
    2. How can we rewrite a product \(i \cdot j\) as a linear combination of functions of \(i + j\), \(i\), and \(j\) alone?
    3. Over the complex numbers \(\mathbb{C}\), textbook signal processing uses the identity \(ij = \frac{1}{2}\bigl((i+j)^2 - i^2 - j^2\bigr)\) (or \(ij = -\frac{1}{2}(i-j)^2 + \frac{1}{2}i^2 + \frac{1}{2}j^2\)).
    4. Finite-field stress test over \(\mathbb{F}_p\): If \(i + j\) is odd, then \(\frac{1}{2}(i+j)^2\) is a half-integer! Raising \(c\) to a half-integer power requires choosing a square root \(c^{1/2} \in \mathbb{F}_p\). What if \(c\) is a quadratic non-residue modulo \(p\)? Then \(c^{1/2}\) does not even exist in \(\mathbb{F}_p\)!
    5. Can we write \(i \cdot j\) using a quadratic function of \(k\) that is always an integer for every integer \(k\), with no \(\frac{1}{2}\) outside? Yes! The binomial coefficient \(\binom{k}{2} = \frac{k(k-1)}{2}\) is an integer for every \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\), and expanding \(\binom{i+j}{2}\) gives: \[ \binom{i+j}{2} = \frac{(i+j)(i+j-1)}{2} = \frac{i(i-1)}{2} + \frac{j(j-1)}{2} + ij = \binom{i}{2} + \binom{j}{2} + ij. \] Rearranging this identity gives Bluestein’s Binomial Identity: \[ i \cdot j = \binom{i+j}{2} - \binom{i}{2} - \binom{j}{2}, \] and for negative products \(-i \cdot j\) (needed in inverse DFTs), flipping the sign gives: \[ -i \cdot j = \binom{i}{2} + \binom{j}{2} - \binom{i+j}{2}. \]
    6. Note on Sum-Index \(\binom{i+j}{2}\) vs. Difference-Index \(\binom{i-j}{2}\) Conventions: Some references write \(i \cdot j = \binom{i}{2} + \binom{j}{2} - \binom{i-j}{2}\) (using \(\binom{-k}{2} = \binom{k+1}{2}\) for negative arguments \(i - j < 0\)). Both identities are algebraically valid, but the sum-index identity \(i \cdot j = \binom{i+j}{2} - \binom{i}{2} - \binom{j}{2}\) is cleaner in competitive programming: since \(i \ge 0\) and \(j \ge 0\), the argument \(k = i + j\) is always non-negative (\(0 \le i + j \le n + m - 2\)), avoiding negative index offsets entirely and requiring only a single reversal \(\tilde{u}_{n-1-j} = u_j\) to align \((n - 1 - j) + (i + j) = n - 1 + i\).
WarningFailure Mode #12: Bluestein Square-Root Failure (\(c^{1/2} \notin \mathbb{F}_p\)) & The \(c = 0\) Edge Case

Failure Mode 12A (The \(c^{1/2}\) Quadratic Non-Residue Trap): Never implement CZT over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) using \(ij = \frac{1}{2}\bigl((i+j)^2 - i^2 - j^2\bigr)\). Half the elements of \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\) are quadratic non-residues, so \(\sqrt{c}\) does not exist in \(\mathbb{F}_p\) half the time (for instance, when \(c = g\) is a primitive root of \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\)!). By contrast, Bluestein’s Binomial Identity \[ c^{ij} = c^{\binom{i+j}{2}} \cdot c^{-\binom{i}{2}} \cdot c^{-\binom{j}{2}} \] uses exclusively integer exponents \(\binom{k}{2} = \frac{k(k-1)}{2} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) and works unconditionally for every nonzero element \(c \in K^\times\) in any commutative ring \(K\).

Failure Mode 12B (The \(c = 0\) and \(a = 0\) Non-Unit Trap): Notice that \(c^{-\binom{i}{2}}\) divides by powers of \(c\). If \(c = 0\), \(c\) is not invertible! Before running the binomial substitution, always guard the two degenerate boundary cases explicitly:

  • If \(a = 0\): Every evaluation point is \(x_i = 0 \cdot c^i = 0\), so \(y_i = F(0) = f_0\) for all \(0 \le i < m\).
  • If \(c = 0\) (with \(0^0 := 1\)):
    • For \(i = 0\), \(x_0 = a \cdot 0^0 = a\), so \(y_0 = F(a) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_j a^j\) (evaluated in \(O(n)\) time via Horner’s rule).
    • For all \(i \ge 1\), \(x_i = a \cdot 0^i = 0\), so \(y_i = F(0) = f_0\).

7.3.2 Single-Step Verbalized Derivation of Bluestein’s CZT

Assume \(a \ne 0\) and \(c \in K^\times\) is invertible. Let us transform \(y_i = F(a \cdot c^i)\) into a standard polynomial convolution step by step:

  1. Write out the point evaluation: For each \(0 \le i < m\), \[ y_i = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_j (a \cdot c^i)^j = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_j a^j c^{ij}. \]
  2. Substitute Bluestein’s Binomial Identity \(c^{ij} = c^{\binom{i+j}{2}} c^{-\binom{i}{2}} c^{-\binom{j}{2}}\): \[ y_i = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_j a^j \left(c^{\binom{i+j}{2}} c^{-\binom{i}{2}} c^{-\binom{j}{2}}\right). \]
  3. Factor the \(i\)-only term outside the sum and group the \(j\)-only terms: \[ y_i = c^{-\binom{i}{2}} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \underbrace{\left(f_j a^j c^{-\binom{j}{2}}\right)}_{u_j} \cdot \underbrace{\left(c^{\binom{i+j}{2}}\right)}_{v_{i+j}}. \]
  4. Reverse the array \(u\) to convert the correlation index \(i + j\) into a standard Cauchy convolution: Define the reversed sequence \(\tilde{u}\) of length \(n\) by \(\tilde{u}_{n - 1 - j} = u_j = f_j a^j c^{-\binom{j}{2}}\) for \(0 \le j < n\), and define the chirp sequence \(v_k = c^{\binom{k}{2}}\) for \(0 \le k < n + m - 1\). Then as \(j\) runs from \(0\) to \(n-1\), the sum of the two indices in \(\tilde{u}_{n - 1 - j}\) and \(v_{i + j}\) is \[ (n - 1 - j) + (i + j) = n - 1 + i, \] which depends only on \(i\)! Therefore, the sum is the exact coefficient of \(x^{n - 1 + i}\) in the ordinary polynomial product \(\tilde{U}(x) V(x)\): \[ y_i = c^{-\binom{i}{2}} \cdot [x^{n - 1 + i}] \bigl(\tilde{U}(x) V(x)\bigr) \qquad (0 \le i < m). \]
Tip\(O(n + m)\) Precomputation of \(c^{\binom{k}{2}}\) Without qpow Inside the Loop

Notice that \(\binom{k+1}{2} - \binom{k}{2} = k\). Therefore, the powers \(p_k = c^{\binom{k}{2}}\) and \(s_k = c^k\) satisfy the first-order recurrences \[ p_0 = 1, \quad s_0 = 1, \qquad p_{k+1} = p_k \cdot s_k, \quad s_{k+1} = s_k \cdot c \qquad (k \ge 0). \] Similarly, putting \(c^{-1} = 1/c\) in place of \(c\) generates all negative powers \(q_k = c^{-\binom{k}{2}}\) in \(O(n + m)\) multiplications using only one modular inverse \(c^{-1} \bmod p\) overall!

7.3.3 Concrete Hand-Checkable Trace of Bluestein’s CZT

Let us trace Bluestein’s CZT by hand over \(\mathbb{Q}\) (or \(\mathbb{F}_{998244353}\)) for \[ F(x) = 1 + 2x + 3x^2 \quad (n = 3, \; f = [1, 2, 3]), \qquad a = 1, \quad c = 2, \quad m = 4. \] We want to evaluate \(F(x)\) at \(x_i = 1 \cdot 2^i \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}\) for \(i = 0, 1, 2, 3\). Directly:

  • \(y_0 = F(1) = 1 + 2(1) + 3(1) = 6\),
  • \(y_1 = F(2) = 1 + 2(2) + 3(4) = 17\),
  • \(y_2 = F(4) = 1 + 2(4) + 3(16) = 57\),
  • \(y_3 = F(8) = 1 + 2(8) + 3(64) = 209\).

Now trace the CZT algorithm step by step:

  1. Chirp sequence \(v_k = 2^{\binom{k}{2}}\) for \(0 \le k < n + m - 1 = 6\): Since \(\binom{0}{2} = 0, \binom{1}{2} = 0, \binom{2}{2} = 1, \binom{3}{2} = 3, \binom{4}{2} = 6, \binom{5}{2} = 10\), we have \[ V(x) = \sum_{k=0}^5 2^{\binom{k}{2}} x^k = 1 + 1x + 2x^2 + 8x^3 + 64x^4 + 1024x^5. \]
  2. Weighted reversed coefficients \(\tilde{u}_{2-j} = f_j \cdot 1^j \cdot 2^{-\binom{j}{2}}\) for \(j = 0, 1, 2\):
    • \(j = 0 : u_0 = 1 \cdot 2^{-0} = 1 \implies \tilde{u}_2 = 1\)
    • \(j = 1 : u_1 = 2 \cdot 2^{-0} = 2 \implies \tilde{u}_1 = 2\)
    • \(j = 2 : u_2 = 3 \cdot 2^{-1} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \tilde{u}_0 = \frac{3}{2}\) Thus \(\tilde{U}(x) = \frac{3}{2} + 2x + x^2\).
  3. Multiply \(\tilde{U}(x) V(x)\) and read coefficients at \(x^{n - 1 + i} = x^{2 + i}\) for \(i = 0, 1, 2, 3\):
    • \(i = 0\) (\([x^2](\tilde{U}V)\)): \(\tilde{u}_0 v_2 + \tilde{u}_1 v_1 + \tilde{u}_2 v_0 = \frac{3}{2}(2) + 2(1) + 1(1) = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6\). Multiply by \(2^{-\binom{0}{2}} = 1 \implies y_0 = 6\).
    • \(i = 1\) (\([x^3](\tilde{U}V)\)): \(\tilde{u}_0 v_3 + \tilde{u}_1 v_2 + \tilde{u}_2 v_1 = \frac{3}{2}(8) + 2(2) + 1(1) = 12 + 4 + 1 = 17\). Multiply by \(2^{-\binom{1}{2}} = 1 \implies y_1 = 17\).
    • \(i = 2\) (\([x^4](\tilde{U}V)\)): \(\tilde{u}_0 v_4 + \tilde{u}_1 v_3 + \tilde{u}_2 v_2 = \frac{3}{2}(64) + 2(8) + 1(2) = 96 + 16 + 2 = 114\). Multiply by \(2^{-\binom{2}{2}} = \frac{1}{2} \implies y_2 = \frac{114}{2} = 57\).
    • \(i = 3\) (\([x^5](\tilde{U}V)\)): \(\tilde{u}_0 v_5 + \tilde{u}_1 v_4 + \tilde{u}_2 v_3 = \frac{3}{2}(1024) + 2(64) + 1(8) = 1536 + 128 + 8 = 1672\). Multiply by \(2^{-\binom{3}{2}} = \frac{1}{8} \implies y_3 = \frac{1672}{8} = 209\).

Every single value \([6, 17, 57, 209]\) matches!

    • If \(a = 0\), return \([f_0, f_0, \dots, f_0]\).
    • If \(c = 0\), return \([F(a), f_0, f_0, \dots, f_0]\).
    • For \(a \ne 0\) and \(c \ne 0\), \(c\) must be a unit in \(K\) (\(c \in K^\times\)), and the underlying convolution engine (standard NTT if \(K = \mathbb{F}_p\) is NTT-friendly, or 3-Prime MTT from §7.2 otherwise) must support polynomial multiplication of degree \((n - 1) + (n + m - 2) = 2n + m - 3\).
  • Index Boundaries & Core Invariant:
    • Array \(\tilde{U}\) has length \(n\) with \(\tilde{U}_{n - 1 - j} = f_j a^j c^{-\binom{j}{2}}\) for \(0 \le j < n\).
    • Array \(V\) has length \(n + m - 1\) with \(V_k = c^{\binom{k}{2}}\) for \(0 \le k < n + m - 1\).
    • Core Invariant: For every \(0 \le i < m\) and \(0 \le j < n\), the index \(k = i + j\) satisfies \(0 \le k \le n + m - 2\), so \(V_{i+j}\) is always in bounds, and the unique pair of indices in \(\tilde{U}\) and \(V\) summing to \(n - 1 + i\) with \(0 \le n - 1 - j < n\) is \((n - 1 - j, i + j)\). Thus \([x^{n - 1 + i}](\tilde{U}V)\) sums over all \(j \in [0, n-1]\) without omission or wrap-around contamination.
  • Complexity:
    • \(O(n + m)\) time for the recurrence precomputation of \(c^{\pm\binom{k}{2}}\) plus one polynomial multiplication of length \(L = 2^{\lceil \log_2(2n + m - 2) \rceil}\), giving \(O((n + m) \log(n + m))\) time and \(O(n + m)\) space.

7.4 Root-of-Unity Index Filtering (单位根反演) & Failure Mode #11

Suppose we know a compact closed form (or fast evaluation oracle) for a generating function \(F(x) = \sum_{k \ge 0} f_k x^k\)—such as \(F(x) = (1 + sx)^n\) or \(F(x) = (I + xM)^n\)—and a problem asks us to sum only those coefficients whose indices lie in a specific residue class modulo \(m\): \[ \sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} f_k x^k. \] When \(n \le 10^{18}\) and \(m \le 10^5\), we cannot expand \(F(x)\) term by term. Instead, we need an algebraic filter that annihilates every index \(k \not\equiv r \pmod m\) while preserving \(k \equiv r \pmod m\).

7.4.1 Orthogonality & The Arithmetic Progression Filter

Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • The Diagnostic Signal: We have a closed-form generating function \(F(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n f_k x^k\) with huge degree \(n \le 10^{18}\), and the target sum restricts the summation index by a congruence \(k \equiv r \pmod m\) (or \([m \mid k]\)) for a moderate period \(m \le 10^6\).
  • The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad:
    1. We want an indicator expression for \([m \mid t]\) (which equals \(1\) if \(m\) divides \(t\) and \(0\) otherwise) written as a linear combination of powers \(z^t\), because substituting \(z \leftarrow x \cdot \omega\) inside \(F(x) = \sum_k f_k x^k\) turns \(x^k\) into \(\omega^k x^k\)!
    2. Suppose \(K\) contains a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity \(\omega_m\) and \(m \in K^\times\). Consider the geometric series of the \(m\)-th roots of unity raised to the \(t\)-th power: \[ \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \left(\omega_m^t\right)^j = \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{jt}. \]
    3. Case A (\(m \mid t\)): Since \(\omega_m^m = 1\), we have \(\omega_m^t = 1\). Every term in the sum equals \(1\), so the sum is \(m\).
    4. Case B (\(m \nmid t\)): Since \(\omega_m\) is a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity over a field (or domain) \(K\), \(\omega_m^t \ne 1\). Multiplying the geometric sum \(S = \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} (\omega_m^t)^j\) by \((\omega_m^t - 1) \ne 0\) gives \((\omega_m^t)^m - 1 = (\omega_m^m)^t - 1 = 1^t - 1 = 0\). Canceling the nonzero factor \(\omega_m^t - 1\) yields \(S = 0\)!
    5. Dividing by \(m\) gives the Root-of-Unity Orthogonality Filter: \[ [m \mid t] = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{jt}. \]

Single-Step Verbalized Derivation of the Residue Class Filter

To extract coefficients with \(k \equiv r \pmod m\) (i.e., \(m \mid (k - r)\)), we substitute \(t = k - r\) into the orthogonality filter and swap the order of summation: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} f_k x^k &= \sum_{k \ge 0} f_k x^k [m \mid (k - r)] && \text{(rewrite congruence as divisibility indicator)} \\ &= \sum_{k \ge 0} f_k x^k \left(\frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{j(k - r)}\right) && \text{(apply Root-of-Unity Orthogonality Filter)} \\ &= \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{-jr} \sum_{k \ge 0} f_k (\omega_m^j x)^k && \text{(pull $\omega_m^{-jr}$ out and group $(\omega_m^j x)^k$)} \\ &= \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{-jr} F(\omega_m^j x). && \text{(recognize inner sum as $F(\omega_m^j x)$)} \end{aligned} \] Setting \(x = 1\) gives the scalar Root-of-Unity Index Filter (单位根反演): \[ \sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} f_k = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{-jr} F(\omega_m^j). \]

7.4.2 The Master Floor-Sum Identity (Floor-Sum Trick)

A classic twist on root-of-unity filtering asks for a weighted sum where each coefficient \(f_i\) is multiplied by the floor function \(\lfloor i / m \rfloor\): \[ S = \sum_{i=0}^n f_i \left\lfloor \frac{i}{m} \right\rfloor. \] We will encounter this exact structure in Int-1 (#178 — Luogu P5591).

Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • The Diagnostic Signal: A sum \(\sum_{i=0}^n f_i \lfloor i/m \rfloor\) weights the coefficients of a known generating function \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n f_i x^i\) by \(\lfloor i/m \rfloor\), where \(n \le 10^{18}\) is huge and \(m \le 2^{20}\) divides \(p - 1\).
  • The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad:
    1. Why is \(\lfloor i/m \rfloor\) awkward? Because \(\lfloor i/m \rfloor\) is neither a polynomial in \(i\) nor periodic modulo \(m\).
    2. However, by the division algorithm with remainder, every integer \(i\) decomposes into \[ i = m \left\lfloor \frac{i}{m} \right\rfloor + (i \bmod m) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \left\lfloor \frac{i}{m} \right\rfloor = \frac{i - (i \bmod m)}{m}. \]
    3. Look at the two pieces on the right-hand side:
      • The first piece \(\frac{i}{m}\) is purely linear in \(i\)! Summing \(\sum_{i=0}^n i f_i\) is just evaluating the formal derivative \(F'(1)\)!
      • The second piece \(\frac{i \bmod m}{m}\) is purely periodic modulo \(m\), taking the value \(r/m\) on the residue class \(i \equiv r \pmod m\) for \(r \in \{0, 1, \dots, m-1\}\)!
    4. If we apply the Root-of-Unity Filter to the periodic remainder sum \(\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \sum_{i \equiv r \pmod m} f_i\), we get a double sum over \(r \in \{0, \dots, m-1\}\) and \(j \in \{0, \dots, m-1\}\). If we evaluate that double sum naively, it takes \(O(m^2)\) time. Can we evaluate the inner sum \(\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \omega_m^{-jr}\) in \(O(1)\) closed form for each \(j\)?

Single-Step Verbalized Derivation of the \(O(m)\) Floor-Sum Formula

Let us derive the exact \(O(m)\) reduction step by step:

  1. Split \(\lfloor i/m \rfloor\) via the division identity: \[ S = \sum_{i=0}^n f_i \frac{i - (i \bmod m)}{m} = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=0}^n i f_i - \frac{1}{m} \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \sum_{i \equiv r \pmod m} f_i. \] The first sum is \(\frac{1}{m} F'(1)\).
  2. Apply the Root-of-Unity Filter to the remainder sum and swap summation order: \[ \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \sum_{i \equiv r \pmod m} f_i = \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \left(\frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{-jr} F(\omega_m^j)\right) = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} F(\omega_m^j) \left(\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \omega_m^{-jr}\right). \]
  3. Evaluate the inner arithmetic-geometric sum \(G(z) = \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r z^r\) at \(z = \omega_m^{-j}\):
    • For \(j = 0\): We have \(z = \omega_m^0 = 1\), so \[ \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \cdot 1^r = \frac{m(m-1)}{2}. \]
    • For \(1 \le j \le m-1\): Since \(\omega_m\) is a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity, \(z = \omega_m^{-j} \ne 1\) while \(z^m = \omega_m^{-jm} = 1\). Multiply \(G(z) = \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} r z^r\) by \((z - 1)\): \[ (z - 1) \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} r z^r = \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} r z^{r+1} - \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} r z^r = (m-1)z^m - \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} z^r. \] Since \(z^m = 1\) and \(1 + \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} z^r = \frac{z^m - 1}{z - 1} = 0\), we have \(\sum_{r=1}^{m-1} z^r = -1\). Substituting \(z^m = 1\) and \(\sum_{r=1}^{m-1} z^r = -1\) into the right-hand side gives: \[ (\omega_m^{-j} - 1) \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} r \omega_m^{-jr} = (m - 1)(1) - (-1) = m \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} r \omega_m^{-jr} = \frac{m}{\omega_m^{-j} - 1}. \]
  4. Combine the \(j = 0\) and \(j \ge 1\) contributions: Substituting these closed forms back into Step 2 cancels a factor of \(m\): \[ \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} r \sum_{i \equiv r \pmod m} f_i = \frac{m-1}{2} F(1) + \sum_{j=1}^{m-1} \frac{F(\omega_m^j)}{\omega_m^{-j} - 1}. \] Plugging this into Step 1 yields the Master Floor-Sum Identity: \[ \sum_{i=0}^n f_i \left\lfloor \frac{i}{m} \right\rfloor = \frac{F'(1)}{m} - \frac{m-1}{2m} F(1) - \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1} \frac{F(\omega_m^j)}{\omega_m^{-j} - 1}. \] This evaluates the entire floor sum in just \(O(m)\) point evaluations of \(F\)!

7.4.3 Failure Mode #11: Missing \(m\)-th Root of Unity (\(m \nmid (p-1)\)) & The Cyclotomic Quotient Ring

What happens if a problem asks us to filter indices modulo \(m\), but \(m\) does not divide \(p - 1\) (or the modulus \(M\) is not even prime, as in Adv-1: Luogu P3702 where \(M = 20170408\))? Then no primitive \(m\)-th root of unity \(\omega_m\) exists in \(\mathbb{F}_p\)!

WarningFailure Mode #11: Missing \(m\)-th Root of Unity & Why Naive Adjoining \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\omega^m - 1)\) Fails for Root Evaluation

Suppose \(m \nmid (p-1)\) (while \(\gcd(m, p) = 1\)), so \(\omega_m \notin \mathbb{F}_p\). Two distinct algebraic remedies exist, and confusing them leads to a classic algebraic bug:

Remedy A: Direct Cyclic Quotient Ring \(K[x]/(x^m - 1)\) (Never Needs \(\omega_m\) at All!) If \(F(x) = A(x)^n\) where \(A(x)\) is a scalar polynomial, we don’t need roots of unity to extract \(\sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} f_k\)! By definition, the coefficient of \(x^r\) in \(A(x)^n \bmod (x^m - 1)\) is already \(\sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} [x^k]A(x)^n\). We simply compute \(A(x)^n \bmod (x^m - 1)\) directly by binary exponentiation with length-\(m\) cyclic convolution! This requires zero roots of unity and zero division by \(m\)—working even over non-prime rings like \(\mathbb{Z}_{20170408}\) (Adv-1).

Remedy B: Adjoining a Formal Root \(\omega\) via the Cyclotomic Quotient Ring \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_m(\omega))\) When is Remedy B needed instead of Remedy A? Suppose \(F(x)\) is not a scalar polynomial, or suppose we want to evaluate \(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega^{-jr} F(\omega^j)\) where \(F(\omega^j)\) is computed by a closed-form formula or matrix power \((I + \omega^j M)^n\). Then we want to adjoin a formal symbol \(\omega\) representing a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity.

The Trap: If you represent \(\omega\) as a polynomial in the quotient ring \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\omega^m - 1)\) and evaluate \(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega^{jt} \bmod (\omega^m - 1)\) for \(m \nmid t\), the sum does NOT vanish! For example, take \(m = 3\) and \(t = 1\). In \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\omega^3 - 1)\), the sum \(\sum_{j=0}^2 \omega^j = 1 + \omega + \omega^2\) is a degree-\(2\) polynomial, which is nonzero modulo \(\omega^3 - 1\)! Why did the geometric series proof \((\omega - 1)(1 + \omega + \omega^2) = \omega^3 - 1 = 0 \implies 1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0\) fail? Because in \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\omega^3 - 1)\), \(\omega - 1\) is a zero divisor (\(\omega^3 - 1 = (\omega - 1)(\omega^2 + \omega + 1)\)), so we cannot cancel \(\omega - 1\)!

The Exact Remedy — Quotient by the \(m\)-th Cyclotomic Polynomial \(\Phi_m(\omega)\): To force \(\omega\) to behave as a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity (rather than \(1\) or a root of lower order), we must quotient out all divisors \(d < m\) of \(m\) by working in the cyclotomic quotient ring \[ \mathcal{C}_m = \mathbb{F}_p[\omega] / \bigl(\Phi_m(\omega)\bigr), \qquad \deg \Phi_m = \varphi(m), \] where \(\Phi_m(\omega) = \prod_{d \mid m} (\omega^d - 1)^{\mu(m/d)}\) is the \(m\)-th cyclotomic polynomial (for prime \(m\), simply \(\Phi_m(\omega) = 1 + \omega + \omega^2 + \dots + \omega^{m-1}\)).

Proof that Orthogonality Holds in \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_m(\omega))\) for all \(t\): First, in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) we have the classic factorization \(\omega^m - 1 = \prod_{d \mid m} \Phi_d(\omega)\), so \(\Phi_m(\omega)\) divides \(\omega^m - 1\). Consequently, \(\omega^m \equiv 1 \pmod{\Phi_m(\omega)}\) holds automatically in \(\mathcal{C}_m = \mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_m(\omega))\), and \(\mathcal{C}_m\) is a quotient ring of \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\omega^m - 1)\).

Second, for any integer \(t\) with \(m \nmid t\), let \(g = \gcd(t, m) < m\) and set \(m' = m / g > 1\). Since \(\gcd(t/g, m') = 1\), writing \(j = q m' + r\) with \(0 \le q < g\) and \(0 \le r < m'\) gives \((jt) \bmod m = \bigl((r \cdot (t/g)) \bmod m'\bigr) \cdot g\). As \(r\) runs over \(\{0, 1, \dots, m'-1\}\), multiplication by the unit \(t/g \bmod m'\) permutes \(\{0, 1, \dots, m'-1\}\) bijectively for each of the \(g\) values of \(q\). Therefore, in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]/(\omega^m - 1)\) (and hence in its quotient \(\mathcal{C}_m\)), we have the exact polynomial identity \[ \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega^{jt} \equiv g \sum_{s=0}^{m'-1} \omega^{sg} = g \cdot \frac{\omega^m - 1}{\omega^g - 1} = g \prod_{\substack{d \mid m \\ d \nmid g}} \Phi_d(\omega) \pmod{\omega^m - 1}. \] Because \(g < m\), the divisor \(d = m\) does not divide \(g\), which means \(\Phi_m(\omega)\) appears explicitly as a factor in the product on the right-hand side! Reducing modulo \(\Phi_m(\omega)\) therefore forces \[ \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega^{jt} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } m \mid t, \\ 0 & \text{if } m \nmid t \end{cases} \qquad \text{in } \mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/\bigl(\Phi_m(\omega)\bigr), \] provided only that \(p \nmid m\)! Even better: even when \(\Phi_m(\omega)\) is reducible over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) (so \(\mathcal{C}_m\) is a commutative ring rather than a field), the scalar answer lies in the constant subring \(\mathbb{F}_p \subset \mathcal{C}_m\), so reducing modulo \(\Phi_m(\omega)\) at each multiplication automatically collapses all higher powers \(\omega, \dots, \omega^{\varphi(m)-1}\) to \(0\) at the end!

Concrete Hand-Checkable Trace of Cyclotomic Filtering (\(m = 3\))

Suppose \(m = 3\) and we work over \(\mathbb{F}_5\) (note \(3 \nmid (5 - 1) = 4\), so no cube root of unity exists in \(\mathbb{F}_5\)). We want to compute \(\sum_{i \equiv 0 \pmod 3} \binom{3}{i} = \binom{3}{0} + \binom{3}{3} = 2 \bmod 5\) using the root-of-unity filter on \(F(x) = (1 + x)^3\) in \(\mathcal{C}_3 = \mathbb{F}_5[\omega]/(\Phi_3(\omega))\), where \(\Phi_3(\omega) = \omega^2 + \omega + 1\) (so \(\omega^2 = -\omega - 1 = 4\omega + 4\) and \(\omega^3 = 1\)).

  1. \(j = 0\): \(F(1) = (1 + 1)^3 = 8 \equiv 3 \pmod 5\).
  2. \(j = 1\): \(F(\omega) = (1 + \omega)^3 = 1 + 3\omega + 3\omega^2 + \omega^3 = 2 + 3(\omega + \omega^2)\). Since \(\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0 \implies \omega + \omega^2 = -1\), we get \(F(\omega) = 2 + 3(-1) = -1 \equiv 4 \pmod 5\).
  3. \(j = 2\): \(F(\omega^2) = (1 + \omega^2)^3 = 1 + 3\omega^2 + 3\omega + \omega^6 = 2 + 3(\omega + \omega^2) = -1 \equiv 4 \pmod 5\).
  4. Average over \(j \in \{0, 1, 2\}\): Since \(3^{-1} \equiv 2 \pmod 5\), \[ \frac{1}{3} \bigl(F(1) + F(\omega) + F(\omega^2)\bigr) = 2 \cdot (3 + 4 + 4) = 2 \cdot 11 \equiv 2 \pmod 5. \] All \(\omega\) terms canceled identically modulo \(\Phi_3(\omega) = \omega^2 + \omega + 1\), recovering the exact scalar answer \(2\)!
WarningFailure Mode & Remedy: Characteristic-\(p\) Non-Semisimplicity (\(p \mid m\)) in Root-of-Unity Filtering

The Trap: What if the filter period \(m\) is divisible by the field characteristic \(p\) (\(p \mid m\), e.g., filtering indices modulo \(m = p\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\), or modulo an even \(m\) when the modulus \(M\) is even as in Adv-1)?

Why Both Point-Value DFT and Cyclotomic Filtering Fail When \(p \mid m\):

  1. Division by \(m = 0\): The root-of-unity filter formula \(\frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{-jr} F(\omega_m^j)\) divides by \(m\). When \(p \mid m\), \(m \equiv 0 \pmod p\) is not invertible in \(\mathbb{F}_p\)!
  2. Collapse of distinct roots (Non-semisimplicity): Write \(m = p^v m_0\) with \(p \nmid m_0\) and \(v \ge 1\). By the Freshman’s Dream in characteristic \(p\), \[ x^m - 1 = x^{p^v m_0} - 1 = \left(x^{m_0} - 1\right)^{p^v}. \] Every root of \(x^m - 1\) has multiplicity \(p^v > 1\), so no primitive \(m\)-th root of unity exists in any field extension of \(\mathbb{F}_p\) (and in \(\mathcal{C}_p = \mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_p(\omega)) \cong \mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/((\omega - 1)^{p-1})\), we still cannot divide by \(p = 0\)). Algebraically, by Maschke’s Theorem, the group algebra \(\mathbb{F}_p[\mathbb{Z}_m] \cong \mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^m - 1)\) is not semisimple when \(p \mid m\) and cannot be diagonalized into point evaluations!

The Exact Remedies:

  • Remedy 1 (Direct Cyclic Convolution in \(K[x]/(x^m - 1)\)): As noted in Remedy A above, working in the coefficient basis of \(K[x]/(x^m - 1)\) by folding \(x^{i + m} \equiv x^i\) never divides by \(m\) and never diagonalizes \(x^m - 1\), so it works unconditionally even when \(p \mid m\)!
  • Remedy 2 (Prime-Power Ring Lifting \(\mathbb{Z}/p^{v+1}\mathbb{Z}\)): If a problem requires a closed-form root-of-unity sum where \(m = p^v m_0\), lift the computation from \(\mathbb{F}_p\) to a ring of characteristic \(p^{v+1}\) (or adjoin \(\Phi_m(\omega)\) over \(\mathbb{Z}/p^{v+1}\mathbb{Z}\)), keep all intermediate values unreduced modulo \(p^{v+1}\), perform the exact integer division by \(p^v\) at the very end, and only then project modulo \(p\).
ImportantAlgorithm Contract: root_of_unity_filter, floor_sum_filter & cyclotomic_filter
  • Input:
    • Evaluation oracle for \(F(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n f_k x^k\) (and \(F'(1)\) for floor_sum_filter), period \(m \ge 1\), target residue \(r \in \{0, \dots, m-1\}\), and prime modulus \(p\).
  • Output:
    • root_of_unity_filter: \(\left(\sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} f_k\right) \bmod p\).
    • floor_sum_filter: \(\left(\sum_{i=0}^n f_i \lfloor i/m \rfloor\right) \bmod p\).
    • cyclotomic_filter: \(\left(\sum_{k \equiv r \pmod m} f_k\right) \bmod p\) computed in \(\mathcal{C}_m = \mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_m(\omega))\) when \(m \nmid (p-1)\).
  • Preconditions:
    • For all three filters, \(\gcd(m, p) = 1\) (\(m \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\)). If \(p \mid m\), use direct cyclic convolution in \(\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^m - 1)\) instead.
    • For scalar root_of_unity_filter and floor_sum_filter, \(m \mid (p - 1)\) so \(\omega_m = g^{(p-1)/m} \bmod p \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\) has multiplicative order \(m\).
  • Core Invariant:
    • For every integer \(t\), \(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_m^{jt} = [m \mid t]\) in \(\mathbb{F}_p\) (when \(m \mid (p-1)\)) and in \(\mathcal{C}_m = \mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_m(\omega))\) (for any \(p \nmid m\)).
    • In floor_sum_filter, for every \(1 \le j < m\), \(\omega_m^{-j} \ne 1\) is invertible minus \(1\), and \(\sum_{r=1}^{m-1} r \omega_m^{-jr} = \frac{m}{\omega_m^{-j} - 1}\).
  • Complexity:
    • Scalar filters (m | (p-1)): \(O(m \cdot T_{\text{eval}})\) time and \(O(1)\) (or \(O(m)\)) space, where \(T_{\text{eval}}\) is the cost of one evaluation \(F(\omega_m^j)\).
    • cyclotomic_filter: \(O(m \cdot \varphi(m)^2 \log n)\) (or \(O(m \cdot \varphi(m)\log\varphi(m)\log n)\)) time and \(O(\varphi(m))\) space.

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given \(T\) independent test cases (\(T \le 10^5\)). In each test case, you are given positive integers \(n, s\) and four weights \(a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3\), and you must compute \[ S = \left( \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} s^i a_{i \bmod 4} \right) \bmod 998244353. \]

  • Constraints: \(T \le 10^5\), \(1 \le n \le 10^{18}\), \(1 \le s, a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3 \le 10^9\), modulus \(p = 998244353\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(n = 4, s = 1\) and weights \((a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3) = (1, 2, 3, 4)\), the residue sums of \(\binom{4}{i} 1^i\) are \(r = 0: \binom{4}{0} + \binom{4}{4} = 2\); \(r = 1: \binom{4}{1} = 4\); \(r = 2: \binom{4}{2} = 6\); \(r = 3: \binom{4}{3} = 4\). Thus \(S = 1(2) + 2(4) + 3(6) + 4(4) = 2 + 8 + 18 + 16 = 44\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(T \log p)\) time and \(O(1)\) auxiliary space.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Without the periodic weight \(a_{i \bmod 4}\), the sum \(\sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} s^i = (1 + s)^n\) is just the binomial theorem evaluated at \(x = s\), which represents choosing a subset of \(\{1, \dots, n\}\) where each chosen element contributes a factor of \(s\). Because \(a_{i \bmod 4}\) depends only on the residue class \(r = i \bmod 4 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\), split the sum into four residue classes \(i \equiv r \pmod 4\). Since \(n \le 10^{18}\) is far too large to iterate over \(i\), reach for the Root-of-Unity Index Filter (§7.4.1) with period \(m = 4\).

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad
  1. Define the polynomial \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} (sx)^i = (1 + sx)^n\).
  2. Does \(\mathbb{F}_{998244353}\) contain a primitive \(4\)-th root of unity \(\omega_4\)? Check divisibility: \(p - 1 = 998244352 = 119 \cdot 2^{23}\) is divisible by \(4\)! Specifically, with primitive root \(g = 3\), \(\omega_4 = 3^{(p-1)/4} \bmod p = 86583718\) satisfies \(\omega_4^2 \equiv -1 \pmod p\) and \(\omega_4^4 \equiv 1 \pmod p\).
  3. Apply the residue class filter \(\sum_{i \equiv r \pmod 4} \binom{n}{i} s^i = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=0}^3 \omega_4^{-jr} (1 + s\omega_4^j)^n\) for each \(r \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\), multiply by \(a_r\), and swap the sums over \(r\) and \(j\).
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Swapping the double sum over \(r, j \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\) gives \[ S = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{j=0}^3 (1 + s\omega_4^j)^n \left(\sum_{r=0}^3 a_r \omega_4^{-jr}\right). \] Since \(n \le 10^{18}\), how can we reduce the exponent \(n\) when computing \((1 + s\omega_4^j)^n \bmod p\)? By Fermat’s Little Theorem, \(b^n \equiv b^{n \bmod (p-1)} \pmod p\) whenever \(b \not\equiv 0 \pmod p\). Be sure to check if \((1 + s\omega_4^j) \bmod p = 0\) before reducing \(n \bmod (p-1)\)! This requires at most \(4\) modular exponentiations per test case, running in \(O(T \log p)\) time.

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Mathematical Derivation from First Principles

Let \(p = 998244353\). We want to evaluate \[ S = \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} s^i a_{i \bmod 4} \pmod p. \] First, partition the summation index \(i \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}\) according to its remainder \(r = i \bmod 4 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\): \[ S = \sum_{r=0}^3 a_r \sum_{\substack{0 \le i \le n \\ i \equiv r \pmod 4}} \binom{n}{i} s^i. \] Introduce the generating function \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} s^i x^i = (1 + sx)^n \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]\). Since \(4 \mid (p - 1) = 998244352\), \(\mathbb{F}_p\) contains a primitive \(4\)-th root of unity \[ \omega = 3^{(p-1)/4} \bmod p = 86583718, \] whose powers are \(\omega^0 = 1\), \(\omega^1 = 86583718\), \(\omega^2 = p - 1 \equiv -1\), and \(\omega^3 = p - 86583718 \equiv -\omega\). Also, \(4 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\) has modular inverse \(4^{-1} \equiv \frac{3p + 1}{4} = 748683265 \pmod p\).

Applying the Root-of-Unity Index Filter from §7.4.1 to each residue class \(r \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\) gives: \[ \begin{aligned} S &= \sum_{r=0}^3 a_r \left( \frac{1}{4} \sum_{j=0}^3 \omega^{-jr} F(\omega^j) \right) && \text{(substitute filter for $i \equiv r \pmod 4$)} \\ &= \frac{1}{4} \sum_{j=0}^3 (1 + s\omega^j)^n \left( \sum_{r=0}^3 a_r \omega^{(-jr) \bmod 4} \right). && \text{(swap finite sums over $r$ and $j$, use $\omega^4 = 1$)} \end{aligned} \] Let \(\hat{a}_j = \sum_{r=0}^3 a_r \omega^{(-jr) \bmod 4} \bmod p\) for \(j = 0, 1, 2, 3\):

  • \(\hat{a}_0 = (a_0 + a_1 + a_2 + a_3) \bmod p\),
  • \(\hat{a}_1 = (a_0 + a_1 \omega^3 + a_2 \omega^2 + a_3 \omega^1) \bmod p\),
  • \(\hat{a}_2 = (a_0 + a_1 \omega^2 + a_2 + a_3 \omega^2) \bmod p\),
  • \(\hat{a}_3 = (a_0 + a_1 \omega^1 + a_2 \omega^2 + a_3 \omega^3) \bmod p\).

Then \(S = 4^{-1} \sum_{j=0}^3 \hat{a}_j (1 + s\omega^j)^n \bmod p\).

2. Boundary Cases & Fermat Exponent Reduction

  • Case \(b_j = (1 + s\omega^j) \bmod p = 0\): Since \(n \ge 1\), \(0^n = 0\). (If we blindly computed \(0^{n \bmod (p-1)}\) when \((p-1) \mid n\), we would falsely get \(0^0 = 1\)!)
  • Case \(b_j \ne 0\): By Fermat’s Little Theorem, \(b_j^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p\), so \(b_j^n \equiv b_j^{n \bmod (p-1)} \pmod p\).
  • Remember to reduce the inputs \(s \leftarrow s \bmod p\) and \(a_r \leftarrow a_r \bmod p\) before multiplying, since \(s, a_r \le 10^9\) can slightly exceed \(p = 998244353\).

3. Hand-Checkable Trace & Algorithm Contract

  • Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n = 4, s = 1, a = [1, 2, 3, 4]\)):
    • Using \(\omega^2 = -1\) and \(\omega^3 = -\omega\):
      • \(j = 0\): \((1 + \omega^0)^4 = 2^4 = 16\), and \(\hat{a}_0 = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10 \implies 16 \cdot 10 = 160\).
      • \(j = 1\): \((1 + \omega)^4 = (2\omega)^2 = 4\omega^2 = -4\), and \(\hat{a}_1 = 1 + 2(-\omega) + 3(-1) + 4\omega = -2 + 2\omega \implies (-4)(-2 + 2\omega) = 8 - 8\omega\).
      • \(j = 2\): \((1 + \omega^2)^4 = (1 - 1)^4 = 0 \implies 0\).
      • \(j = 3\): \((1 + \omega^3)^4 = (1 - \omega)^4 = (-2\omega)^2 = 4\omega^2 = -4\), and \(\hat{a}_3 = 1 + 2\omega + 3(-1) + 4(-\omega) = -2 - 2\omega \implies (-4)(-2 - 2\omega) = 8 + 8\omega\).
    • Averaging: \(\frac{1}{4}\bigl(160 + (8 - 8\omega) + 0 + (8 + 8\omega)\bigr) = \frac{176}{4} = 44\).
  • Input & Preconditions: \(n \ge 1\), weights \(s, a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3 \in [0, 10^9]\) reduced modulo \(p = 998244353\), and primitive \(4\)-th root of unity \(\omega = 86583718 \bmod p\).
  • Postconditions (Output): Outputs \(\sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} s^i a_{i \bmod 4} \bmod p\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(1)\) precomputation; \(4\) calls to modular binary exponentiation with exponent \(n \bmod (p-1) < p\), taking \(O(T \log p) \approx 1.2 \times 10^7\) operations and \(O(1)\) space.
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: After evaluating \(j \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\), the running sum accumulates \(\sum_{r=0}^j (1 + s\omega^r)^n \hat{a}_r \bmod p\), where \(\hat{a}_r = \sum_{m=0}^3 a_m \omega^{-rm} \bmod p\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: If \(1 + s\omega^j \equiv 0 \pmod p\), the base of \((1 + s\omega^j)^n\) is \(0 \bmod p\), so the exponent \(n \ge 1\) must not be reduced to \(0 \bmod (p-1)\) via Fermat’s Little Theorem (or \(0^n = 0\) must be guarded explicitly before reducing \(n \bmod (p-1)\)).

Stepping Stone 2 (#248) — BZOJ 3328: PYXFIB

  • Prerequisite Tags: Ch 01 (Matrix Recurrences), Ch 02 (Primitive Roots), §7.4.1 (Root-of-Unity Filter on Matrix GFs)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Let the Fibonacci sequence be indexed such that \(F_0 = 1, F_1 = 1\), and \(F_i = F_{i-1} + F_{i-2}\) for all \(i \ge 2\). You are given \(T\) test cases. In each test case, you are given integers \(n, k\) and a prime \(p\) satisfying \(k \mid (p - 1)\), and you must compute \[ S = \left( \sum_{\substack{0 \le i \le n \\ k \mid i}} \binom{n}{i} F_i \right) \bmod p. \]

  • Constraints: \(T \le 20\), \(1 \le n \le 10^{18}\), \(1 \le k \le 2 \times 10^4\), prime \(p \le 10^9\) with \(k \mid (p - 1)\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(n = 4, k = 2, p = 5\) (where \(2 \mid (5 - 1)\)), the multiples of \(k = 2\) in \(\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}\) are \(i \in \{0, 2, 4\}\). With \(F_0 = 1, F_1 = 1, F_2 = 2, F_3 = 3, F_4 = 5\), the sum is \(\binom{4}{0}F_0 + \binom{4}{2}F_2 + \binom{4}{4}F_4 = 1(1) + 6(2) + 1(5) = 18 \equiv 3 \pmod 5\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O\bigl(T(\sqrt{p} + k \log n)\bigr)\) time and \(O(1)\) auxiliary space.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Two structural features jump out:

  1. The summand contains \(\binom{n}{i} F_i\), which mixes binomial coefficients with a linear recurrence \(F_i = F_{i-1} + F_{i-2}\).
  2. The index \(i\) is restricted to multiples of \(k\) (\([k \mid i]\)), and the problem statement explicitly guarantees \(k \mid (p - 1)\).

How do we represent \(F_i\) so that \(\sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} F_i x^i\) becomes a single binomial power \((1 + xM)^n\)? Use the \(2 \times 2\) Fibonacci transition matrix \(M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\)!

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad
  1. Verify that for \(M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\), the powers \(M^i\) satisfy \[ M^0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad M^1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad M^i = \begin{pmatrix} F_i & F_{i-1} \\ F_{i-1} & F_{i-2} \end{pmatrix} \quad (i \ge 1). \] In particular, the top-left entry \((M^i)_{0,0}\) equals \(F_i\) for every \(i \ge 0\) (including \(i = 0\) since \(I_{0,0} = 1 = F_0\))!
  2. Because the identity matrix \(I\) and \(M\) commute (\(IM = MI\)), the binomial theorem holds in the matrix ring \(\mathbb{F}_p^{2 \times 2}\): \[ \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} x^i M^i = (I + xM)^n. \]
  3. Since \(k \mid (p-1)\), find a primitive root \(g\) of \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\), set \(\omega_k = g^{(p-1)/k} \bmod p\), and apply the Root-of-Unity Orthogonality Filter \([k \mid i] = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega_k^{ji}\).
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Substituting the filter and extracting the \((0, 0)\) entry gives \[ S = \left[ \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \left(I + \omega_k^j M\right)^n \right]_{0,0} \bmod p. \] To find a primitive root \(g \bmod p\): factor \(p - 1\) by trial division in \(O(\sqrt{p})\) time to find its distinct prime factors \(q_1, \dots, q_s\), then test \(g = 2, 3, \dots\) until \(g^{(p-1)/q_r} \not\equiv 1 \pmod p\) for all \(r\). Then for each \(j \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}\), form the \(2 \times 2\) matrix \(A_j = I + \omega_k^j M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + \omega_k^j & \omega_k^j \\ \omega_k^j & 1 \end{pmatrix}\) and compute \((A_j^n)_{0,0}\) by \(2 \times 2\) matrix binary exponentiation in \(8 \log_2 n\) operations.

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Lifting the Scalar Sum to the Matrix Ring \(\mathbb{F}_p^{2 \times 2}[x]\)

Define the \(2 \times 2\) Fibonacci matrix over \(\mathbb{F}_p\): \[ M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}. \] By induction on \(i \ge 0\), multiplying by \(M\) sends \(\begin{pmatrix} F_i \\ F_{i-1} \end{pmatrix} \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} F_i + F_{i-1} \\ F_i \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} F_{i+1} \\ F_i \end{pmatrix}\). Starting from \(M^0 = I = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\), whose \((0,0)\) entry is \(1 = F_0\), and \(M^1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\), whose \((0,0)\) entry is \(1 = F_1\), we have \[ F_i = (M^i)_{0,0} \qquad \text{for all } i \ge 0. \] Because \(I\) and \(xM\) commute in the polynomial matrix ring \(\mathbb{F}_p^{2 \times 2}[x]\), the binomial theorem gives \[ P(x) := \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} x^i M^i = (I + xM)^n. \]

2. Applying the Root-of-Unity Filter

Since \(p\) is prime and \(k \mid (p - 1)\), \(k\) is coprime to \(p\) (so \(k \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\)) and \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\) contains a primitive \(k\)-th root of unity \(\omega_k = g^{(p-1)/k} \bmod p\), where \(g\) is any primitive root modulo \(p\). Using the orthogonality filter \([k \mid i] = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega_k^{ji}\) and the linearity of the \((0,0)\)-entry projection, we obtain: \[ \begin{aligned} S &= \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} (M^i)_{0,0} \left(\frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega_k^{ji}\right) && \text{(insert orthogonality filter and $F_i = (M^i)_{0,0}$)} \\ &= \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \left[ \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} (\omega_k^j M)^i \right]_{0,0} && \text{(swap sums and pull $(0,0)$ projection out)} \\ &= \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \left[ \left(I + \omega_k^j M\right)^n \right]_{0,0} \pmod p. && \text{(apply matrix binomial theorem)} \end{aligned} \] Explicitly, for each \(j \in \{0, 1, \dots, k-1\}\), let \(w = \omega_k^j \bmod p\). Then \[ I + w M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + w & w \\ w & 1 \end{pmatrix} \pmod p. \]

3. Algorithm & Boundary Cases

  1. Primitive root search: Factor \(p - 1\) in \(O(\sqrt{p})\) time into prime factors \(q_1, \dots, q_s\). Find the smallest integer \(g \ge 2\) such that \(g^{(p-1)/q_r} \not\equiv 1 \pmod p\) for all \(1 \le r \le s\) (for \(p = 2\), \(g = 1\)). Set \(\omega_k = g^{(p-1)/k} \bmod p\).
  2. Matrix exponentiation loop: Initialize ans = 0, w = 1. Loop \(j\) from \(0\) to \(k - 1\):
    • Construct \(A = \begin{pmatrix} (1 + w) \bmod p & w \\ w & 1 \end{pmatrix}\).
    • Compute \(B = A^n \bmod p\) via binary exponentiation of \(2 \times 2\) matrices (note: do not reduce \(n \bmod (p-1)\) here, because the multiplicative order of a \(2 \times 2\) matrix over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) divides \(p(p-1)(p+1)\), not \(p-1\)!).
    • Add \(B_{0,0}\) to ans modulo \(p\), and update \(w \leftarrow w \cdot \omega_k \bmod p\).
  3. Final scaling: Multiply ans by \(k^{-1} = k^{p-2} \bmod p\).

4. Hand-Checkable Trace & Algorithm Contract

  • Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n = 4, k = 2, p = 5\)):
    • Primitive root modulo \(5\) is \(g = 2\), so \(\omega_2 = 2^{(5-1)/2} \bmod 5 = 4 \equiv -1\).
    • \(j = 0\) (\(w = 1\)): \(A_0 = I + M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = M^2\). Thus \(A_0^4 = M^8 = \begin{pmatrix} F_8 & F_7 \\ F_7 & F_6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 34 & 21 \\ 21 & 13 \end{pmatrix}\), with \((A_0^4)_{0,0} = 34 \equiv 4 \pmod 5\).
    • \(j = 1\) (\(w = -1 \equiv 4\)): \(A_1 = I - M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\). Squaring gives \(A_1^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(A_1^4 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}\), with \((A_1^4)_{0,0} = 2 \pmod 5\).
    • Averaging: \(\frac{1}{2}(34 + 2) = 18 \equiv 3 \pmod 5\).
  • Input & Preconditions: Prime \(p \le 10^9\) with \(k \mid (p - 1)\) (\(1 \le k \le 2 \times 10^4\)) and \(1 \le n \le 10^{18}\).
  • Postconditions (Output): Outputs \(\sum_{k \mid i} \binom{n}{i} F_i \bmod p\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(\sqrt{p})\) to find a primitive root \(g \bmod p\), plus \(k\) matrix exponentiations each taking \(8 \log_2 n\) modular multiplications, for \(O\bigl(T(\sqrt{p} + k \log n)\bigr)\) total time (\(\approx 2.4 \times 10^7\) operations) and \(O(1)\) space.
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: After processing \(j \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}\) with \(w = \omega_k^j \bmod p\), the accumulator holds \(\sum_{r=0}^j \bigl[(I + \omega_k^r M)^n\bigr]_{0,0} \bmod p\), where \((M^i)_{0,0} = F_i\) for all \(i \ge 0\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: Do not reduce the matrix exponent \(n \bmod (p - 1)\)! The multiplicative order of a \(2 \times 2\) matrix in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\) divides \(p(p-1)(p+1)\) (for instance, eigenvalues in \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2} \setminus \mathbb{F}_p\) have order dividing \(p^2 - 1\), and non-diagonalizable Jordan blocks have order dividing \(p(p-1)\)), so binary exponentiation must use the full 64-bit exponent \(n\).

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given integers \(n, p, k\) where \(k = 2^m\) is a power of two (\(1 \le k \le 2^{20}\)). Compute \[ S = \left( \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} p^i \left\lfloor \frac{i}{k} \right\rfloor \right) \bmod 998244353. \] (Note: Here \(p\) is an input weight parameter, while the prime modulus is \(M_{\text{mod}} = 998244353\).)

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n, p < 998244353\), \(k \in \{2^0, 2^1, \dots, 2^{20}\}\), modulus \(M_{\text{mod}} = 998244353\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(n = 4, p = 1, k = 2\), the terms \(\binom{4}{i} 1^i \lfloor i/2 \rfloor\) for \(i = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\) are \(1(0) + 4(0) + 6(1) + 4(1) + 1(2) = 12\). Using the Master Floor-Sum Identity (§7.4.2) with \(F(x) = (1+x)^4\) and \(\omega_2 = -1\): \(S = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(F'(1) - \frac{1}{2}F(1) - \frac{F(-1)}{-1 - 1}\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(4(2^3) - \frac{1}{2}(16) - \frac{0}{-2}\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}(32 - 8 - 0) = 12\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(k \log n)\) time and \(O(1)\) (or \(O(k)\)) space.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

We have the binomial generating function \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} (px)^i = (1 + px)^n\), and each coefficient \(f_i = \binom{n}{i} p^i\) is weighted by \(\lfloor i / k \rfloor\) where \(k = 2^m \le 2^{20}\) divides \(998244353 - 1 = 119 \cdot 2^{23}\). This is the exact canonical setting for the Master Floor-Sum Identity (§7.4.2)!

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad
  1. Decompose \(\lfloor i / k \rfloor = \frac{i - (i \bmod k)}{k}\).
  2. For \(F(x) = (1 + px)^n\), its formal derivative is \(F'(x) = np(1 + px)^{n-1}\), so the linear sum is \[ \sum_{i=0}^n i \binom{n}{i} p^i = F'(1) = n p (1 + p)^{n-1}. \]
  3. For the remainder sum \(\sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r \sum_{i \equiv r \pmod k} f_i\), apply the Root-of-Unity Filter with \(\omega_k = 3^{(998244353 - 1)/k} \bmod 998244353\), swap the sums over \(r\) and \(j\), and use the closed-form identity \(\sum_{r=1}^{k-1} r \omega_k^{-jr} = \frac{k}{\omega_k^{-j} - 1}\) for \(1 \le j \le k - 1\).
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Substituting \(F(\omega_k^j) = (1 + p\omega_k^j)^n\) into the Master Floor-Sum Identity gives \[ S = \frac{1}{k} \left( np(1 + p)^{n-1} - \frac{k - 1}{2} (1 + p)^n - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \frac{(1 + p\omega_k^j)^n}{\omega_k^{-j} - 1} \right) \bmod 998244353. \] To avoid computing \(k-1\) separate modular inverses \((\omega_k^{-j} - 1)^{-1}\) inside the loop (or simply using qpow since \(k \le 2^{20}\)), note that \(\omega_k^{-j} = \omega_k^{k - j}\) and we can either invert in \(O(\log M_{\text{mod}})\) per term or batch-invert all \(k-1\) denominators in \(O(k)\) time using prefix products!

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Step-by-Step Derivation

Let \(M_{\text{mod}} = 998244353\) and \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n \binom{n}{i} p^i x^i = (1 + px)^n\). Using the division identity \(\lfloor i / k \rfloor = \frac{i - (i \bmod k)}{k}\), we split \(S\) into a linear part and a periodic remainder part: \[ k \cdot S = \sum_{i=0}^n i \binom{n}{i} p^i - \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r \sum_{\substack{0 \le i \le n \\ i \equiv r \pmod k}} \binom{n}{i} p^i. \]

Part 1 (The Linear Sum): Differentiating \(F(x) = (1 + px)^n\) with respect to \(x\) gives \(F'(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n i \binom{n}{i} p^i x^{i-1} = np(1 + px)^{n-1}\). Evaluating at \(x = 1\) yields \[ \sum_{i=0}^n i \binom{n}{i} p^i = F'(1) = n p (1 + p)^{n-1}. \]

Part 2 (The Periodic Remainder Sum): Since \(k = 2^m \le 2^{20}\) and \(2^{23} \mid (M_{\text{mod}} - 1)\), \(k\) divides \(M_{\text{mod}} - 1\), so \(\omega_k = 3^{(M_{\text{mod}} - 1)/k} \bmod M_{\text{mod}}\) is a primitive \(k\)-th root of unity in \(\mathbb{F}_{M_{\text{mod}}}\). Applying the Root-of-Unity Filter to the inner sum gives: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r \sum_{i \equiv r \pmod k} \binom{n}{i} p^i &= \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r \left( \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega_k^{-jr} (1 + p\omega_k^j)^n \right) \\ &= \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} (1 + p\omega_k^j)^n \left( \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r \omega_k^{-jr} \right). \end{aligned} \] We now evaluate the inner sum \(G_j = \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r \omega_k^{-jr}\) for each \(j\):

  • For \(j = 0\): \(\omega_k^0 = 1\), so \(G_0 = \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r = \frac{k(k-1)}{2}\). Multiplying by \(\frac{1}{k}(1 + p)^n\) gives \(\frac{k-1}{2}(1 + p)^n\).
  • For \(1 \le j \le k - 1\): Let \(z = \omega_k^{-j} = \omega_k^{k-j} \ne 1\). Since \(z^k = 1\), we have \(1 + z + \dots + z^{k-1} = 0\), so \[ (z - 1) G_j = \sum_{r=1}^{k-1} r z^{r+1} - \sum_{r=1}^{k-1} r z^r = (k - 1) z^k - \sum_{r=1}^{k-1} z^r = (k - 1)(1) - (-1) = k. \] Thus \(G_j = \frac{k}{\omega_k^{-j} - 1} = \frac{k}{\omega_k^{k-j} - 1}\). Notice how the factor of \(k\) in the numerator cancels the \(\frac{1}{k}\) outside!

Combining Part 1 and Part 2 and dividing by \(k\) gives the final closed-form summation: \[ S = \frac{1}{k} \left( n p (1 + p)^{n-1} - \frac{k - 1}{2} (1 + p)^n - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \frac{(1 + p\omega_k^j)^n}{\omega_k^{k-j} - 1} \right) \bmod 998244353. \]

2. Boundary Cases & Implementation Notes

  • Case \((1 + p) \bmod M_{\text{mod}} = 0\): Since \(1 \le p < M_{\text{mod}}\), \(1 + p \equiv 0 \pmod{M_{\text{mod}}}\) occurs if and only if \(p = M_{\text{mod}} - 1\). Because \(n \ge 1\), computing \((1 + p)^{n-1}\) via qpow(0, n - 1) with \(0^0 = 1\) automatically handles \(n = 1\) (\(1 \cdot p \cdot 0^0 = p\)) and \(n \ge 2\) (\(0\)) without dividing \((1 + p)^n\) by \(1 + p = 0\)!

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Count the number of length-\(n\) sequences \((a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n) \in S^n\) such that \[ \prod_{i=1}^n a_i \equiv x \pmod m, \] and output the answer modulo \(1004535809\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 10^9\), prime \(2 \le m < 8000\), \(1 \le x < m\), \(|S| \le m\), modulus \(P_{\text{mod}} = 1004535809\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(n = 2, m = 5, x = 1\), and \(S = \{0, 1, 2, 3\}\), we discard \(0 \in S\) to get \(S^\times = \{1, 2, 3\}\). Using primitive root \(g = 2 \bmod 5\), \(\operatorname{ind}_2(1) = 0, \operatorname{ind}_2(2) = 1, \operatorname{ind}_2(3) = 3\), so \(F(z) = 1 + z + z^3 \in \mathbb{F}_P[z]/(z^4 - 1)\). Squaring modulo \(z^4 - 1\) gives \(F(z)^2 \equiv 3 + 2z + 2z^2 + 2z^3 \pmod{z^4 - 1}\), so the number of pairs with product \(x = 1\) (\(\operatorname{ind}_2(1) = 0\)) is \([z^0]F(z)^2 = 3\), corresponding to \((1, 1), (2, 3), (3, 2)\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(m \log m \log n)\) (or \(O(m \log m + m \log n)\) via point-value exponentiation when \((m-1) \mid (P_{\text{mod}} - 1)\), and \(O(m \log m \log n)\) in general) time and \(O(m)\) space.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Each step chooses an element \(a_i \in S\) and multiplies the running product by \(a_i \bmod m\). Since \(m\) is prime and the target residue \(x \in \{1, \dots, m-1\}\) is nonzero, can a valid sequence ever pick \(0 \in S\)? No—because \(0\) is an absorbing element under multiplication modulo \(m\), so picking \(0\) even once forces the total product to be \(0 \not\equiv x \pmod m\). Once \(0\) is discarded from \(S\), every remaining element lies in the cyclic multiplicative group \(\mathbb{F}_m^\times = \{1, \dots, m-1\}\)!

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  3. Form the indicator polynomial \(F(z) = \sum_{a \in S \setminus \{0\}} z^{\operatorname{ind}_g(a)} \in \mathbb{F}_{1004535809}[z]\). The answer is the coefficient of \(z^{\operatorname{ind}_g(x)}\) in \(F(z)^n \bmod (z^{m-1} - 1)\)!

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Isomorphism from \(\mathbb{F}_m^\times\) to \(\mathbb{Z}_{m-1}\)

Because \(m\) is prime and \(x \in \{1, \dots, m-1\}\) is nonzero modulo \(m\), any sequence \((a_1, \dots, a_n) \in S^n\) containing \(a_i = 0\) has product \(0 \not\equiv x \pmod m\). Thus we immediately replace \(S\) with \(S^\times = S \setminus \{0\}\).

The multiplicative group \(\mathbb{F}_m^\times = \{1, 2, \dots, m - 1\}\) is cyclic of order \(L = m - 1\). Let \(g\) be a primitive root modulo \(m\), found by factoring \(m - 1\) into prime divisors \(q_1, \dots, q_s\) and testing \(g = 1, 2, \dots\) until \(g^{(m-1)/q_r} \not\equiv 1 \pmod m\) for all \(r\) (note: for \(m = 2\), \(L = 1\) and \(g = 1\)). Precompute the index array \(\operatorname{ind}[v]\) for \(v \in \{1, \dots, m - 1\}\) by iterating \(u = 0, 1, \dots, m - 2\) and setting \(\operatorname{ind}[g^u \bmod m] = u\).

For any \(a_1, \dots, a_n \in S^\times\), write \(a_i \equiv g^{u_i} \pmod m\) where \(u_i = \operatorname{ind}[a_i] \in \{0, \dots, m - 2\}\). Since \(g\) has multiplicative order \(m - 1\) modulo \(m\), \[ \prod_{i=1}^n a_i \equiv g^{\sum_{i=1}^n u_i} \equiv g^{\operatorname{ind}[x]} \pmod m \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \sum_{i=1}^n u_i \equiv \operatorname{ind}[x] \pmod{m - 1}. \]

2. Generating Function in the Cyclic Quotient Ring

Define the generating polynomial \(F(z) \in \mathbb{F}_{P_{\text{mod}}}[z]/(z^{m-1} - 1)\) for a single step: \[ F(z) = \sum_{a \in S^\times} z^{\operatorname{ind}[a]}. \] When we multiply \(n\) copies of \(F(z)\) in the quotient ring \(\mathbb{F}_{P_{\text{mod}}}[z]/(z^{m-1} - 1)\), each \(n\)-tuple \((a_1, \dots, a_n) \in (S^\times)^n\) contributes the monomial \[ z^{(\operatorname{ind}[a_1] + \dots + \operatorname{ind}[a_n]) \bmod (m - 1)}. \] Therefore, the exact number of valid sequences modulo \(P_{\text{mod}} = 1004535809\) is \[ \text{ans} = [z^{\operatorname{ind}[x]}] \Bigl( F(z)^n \bmod (z^{m-1} - 1) \Bigr). \]

3. Cyclic NTT Binary Exponentiation & Boundary Cases

Let \(K = m - 1\). To multiply two polynomials \(A(z), B(z)\) of degree \(< K\) modulo \((z^K - 1)\):

  1. Choose \(N_{\text{ntt}} = 2^{\lceil \log_2(2K - 1) \rceil}\) (since \(m < 8000\), \(2K - 1 < 16000\), so \(N_{\text{ntt}} \le 16384\)).
  2. Compute the ordinary product \(C(z) = A(z)B(z)\) of degree \(\le 2K - 2\) via NTT over \(\mathbb{F}_{1004535809}\) (using primitive root \(G = 3\)).
  3. Fold indices \(i \in [K, 2K - 2]\) via \(C_{i - K} \leftarrow (C_{i - K} + C_i) \bmod P_{\text{mod}}\) and truncate to length \(K\).

Run standard binary exponentiation starting from \(R(z) = 1\) (where \(R_0 = 1\) and \(R_i = 0\) for \(1 \le i < K\)) to compute \(R(z) = F(z)^n \bmod (z^K - 1)\) in \(\lceil \log_2 n \rceil\) cyclic multiplications, and output \(R_{\operatorname{ind}[x]}\).

  • Boundary Case \(m = 2\): Here \(K = m - 1 = 1\), \(x = 1\), and \(\operatorname{ind}[1] = 0\). If \(1 \in S\), \(F(z) = 1 z^0\) and the answer is \(1^n = 1\); if \(1 \notin S\), \(F(z) = 0\) and the answer is \(0\). The algorithm handles \(K = 1\) seamlessly with \(N_{\text{ntt}} = 1\).

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Count the number of sequences \((a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n)\) of length \(n\) such that:

  1. Every element satisfies \(1 \le a_i \le m\).
  2. The sum of the elements is divisible by \(p\): \(p \mid (a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n)\).
  3. At least one element \(a_i\) in the sequence is a prime number.

Output the answer modulo \(M = 20170408\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 10^9\), \(1 \le m \le 2 \times 10^7\), \(1 \le p \le 100\), modulus \(M = 20170408\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(n = 2, m = 4, p = 3\), the allowed integers are \(U = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}\) and the non-primes are \(W = \{1, 4\}\). Modulo \(p = 3\), \(F_{\text{all}}(x) = x^1 + x^2 + x^0 + x^1 = 1 + 2x + x^2\) and \(F_{\text{comp}}(x) = x^1 + x^1 = 2x\). Squaring in \(\mathbb{Z}_M[x]/(x^3 - 1)\) gives \([x^0]F_{\text{all}}(x)^2 = 1^2 + 2(2)(1) = 5\) and \([x^0]F_{\text{comp}}(x)^2 = 0\), so there are \(5 - 0 = 5\) valid sequences: \((2, 4), (4, 2), (2, 1), (1, 2), (3, 3)\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(m + p^2 \log n)\) time and \(O(m)\) space (or \(O(m/\text{word})\) bitset space for the linear sieve).
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Notice three critical features of the constraints:

  1. The condition “at least one prime” is an existential (“OR”) condition across the \(n\) positions. By complementary counting, it is much cleaner to subtract two independent unconstrained problems: \[ \text{Valid} = (\text{All sequences from } \{1, \dots, m\}) - (\text{Sequences using only non-primes in } \{1, \dots, m\}). \]
  2. The modulus \(M = 20170408 = 2^3 \cdot 2521301\) is composite (not prime!), and \(p \le 100\) does not divide \(\varphi(M)\) in general (in fact, \(\gcd(p, M)\) may even be \(> 1\) if \(p\) is even!).
  3. Because \(\omega_p\) does not exist in \(\mathbb{Z}_M\) and \(p\) may not even be invertible modulo \(M\), we are squarely in Failure Mode #11 (§7.4.3).

  1. Look at Remedy A of Failure Mode #11 (§7.4.3): to extract the sum of coefficients with exponent \(\equiv 0 \pmod p\) in \(A(x)^n\), we do not need roots of unity or division by \(p\)! We simply work directly in the cyclic quotient ring \[ \mathbb{Z}_{20170408}[x] / (x^p - 1). \]
  2. Since \(p \le 100\) is tiny, naive cyclic convolution of two degree-\(< p\) polynomials in \(\mathbb{Z}_M[x]/(x^p - 1)\) takes only \(p^2 \le 10^4\) operations—requiring zero NTTs, zero roots of unity, and zero modular inverses!

  • For \(r \in \{0, 1, \dots, p - 1\}\), \([x^r] F_{\text{all}}(x)\) is the number of integers in \(\{1, \dots, m\}\) congruent to \(r \pmod p\), namely \(\lfloor \frac{m - r}{p} \rfloor + [r > 0 \text{ and } r \le m \bmod p]\) (or simply \(\lfloor (m - r + p)/p \rfloor - [r = 0]\)).

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Complementary Counting & Projection to \(\mathbb{Z}_M[x]/(x^p - 1)\)

Let \(U = \{1, 2, \dots, m\}\) be the set of all allowed values, let \(\mathcal{P} = \{q \in U : q \text{ is prime}\}\), and let \(W = U \setminus \mathcal{P}\) be the set of non-prime values in \(U\) (note \(1 \in W\)). A sequence \((a_1, \dots, a_n) \in U^n\) contains at least one prime if and only if \((a_1, \dots, a_n) \notin W^n\). Therefore, \[ \text{ans} = \Bigl| \bigl\{ a \in U^n : \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n a_i \equiv 0 \pmod p \bigr\} \Bigr| - \Bigl| \bigl\{ a \in W^n : \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n a_i \equiv 0 \pmod p \bigr\} \Bigr| \pmod M. \]

Because \(M = 20170408\) is composite (\(20170408 = 8 \times 2521301\)), neither primitive \(p\)-th roots of unity nor the inverse \(p^{-1} \bmod M\) exist in general. Instead, we work directly in the commutative quotient ring \[ \mathcal{R}_p = \mathbb{Z}_M[x] / (x^p - 1). \] Define the single-step generating polynomials \(F_{\text{all}}(x), F_{\text{comp}}(x) \in \mathcal{R}_p\): \[ F_{\text{all}}(x) = \sum_{v \in U} x^{v \bmod p} = \sum_{r=0}^{p-1} c_r x^r, \qquad F_{\text{comp}}(x) = \sum_{v \in W} x^{v \bmod p} = \sum_{r=0}^{p-1} d_r x^r. \] By definition of multiplication in \(\mathcal{R}_p = \mathbb{Z}_M[x]/(x^p - 1)\), for any polynomial \(F(x) = \sum_{r=0}^{p-1} f_r x^r \in \mathcal{R}_p\), the \(n\)-th power \(F(x)^n \bmod (x^p - 1)\) satisfies \[ [x^0] \bigl(F(x)^n \bmod (x^p - 1)\bigr) = \sum_{\substack{0 \le r_1, \dots, r_n < p \\ r_1 + \dots + r_n \equiv 0 \pmod p}} f_{r_1} f_{r_2} \cdots f_{r_n}. \] Hence the desired answer is \[ \text{ans} = \Bigl( [x^0]\bigl(F_{\text{all}}(x)^n \bmod (x^p - 1)\bigr) - [x^0]\bigl(F_{\text{comp}}(x)^n \bmod (x^p - 1)\bigr) \Bigr) \bmod M. \]

  2. Linear Euler Sieve up to \(m\): Use a std::bitset<20000005> is_comp to mark composite numbers up to \(m\) in \(O(m)\) time. Whenever a prime \(q \in [2, m]\) is discovered, decrement \(d_{q \bmod p} \leftarrow (d_{q \bmod p} - 1 + M) \bmod M\).
  3. Cyclic Polynomial Multiplication in \(\mathbb{Z}_M[x]/(x^p - 1)\): For two arrays \(A, B\) of length \(p\), compute \(C = A \ast_p B\) via \[ C_{(i + j) \bmod p} = \left( \sum_{i + j \equiv k \pmod p} A_i B_j \right) \bmod M. \] Since \(M^2 \approx 4.06 \times 10^{14}\) and \(p \le 100\), even \(p \cdot M^2 \approx 4 \times 10^{16} < 2^{63} - 1\), meaning we can accumulate the entire inner sum for \(C_k\) in a single uint64_t and take % M only once per output entry \(k\)!

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Consider a random walk on the path graph with \(2^N + 1\) vertices labeled \(0, 1, \dots, 2^N\).

  • If the walk is currently at an internal vertex \(x \in \{1, 2, \dots, 2^N - 1\}\), in the next step it moves to \(x - 1\) with probability \(1/2\) and to \(x + 1\) with probability \(1/2\).
  • If the walk is currently at an endpoint \(0\) (respectively \(2^N\)), in the next step it moves deterministically with probability \(1\) to \(1\) (respectively \(2^N - 1\)).

Given \(N\), the starting vertex \(X\), the target vertex \(Y\), and the number of steps \(T\), compute the probability that the walk is at vertex \(Y\) after \(T\) steps, modulo \(998244353\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 20\), \(0 \le X, Y \le 2^N\), \(1 \le T \le 10^{18}\), modulus \(p = 998244353\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(N = 1\) (path \(\{0, 1, 2\}\) of length \(2^1 = 2\)), \(X = 0, Y = 2, T = 2\): Step 1 moves deterministically \(0 \to 1\) with probability \(1\); Step 2 moves \(1 \to 2\) with probability \(1/2\) and \(1 \to 0\) with probability \(1/2\). Thus \(\Pr[\text{at } 2] = 1/2 \equiv 499122177 \pmod{998244353}\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(2^N \log T)\) (or \(O(2^N)\) with precomputed powers) time and \(O(2^N)\) space.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

On an infinite line \(\mathbb{Z}\) or a cycle \(\mathbb{Z}_M\), a symmetric random walk is shift-invariant: every step simply multiplies the generating function by \(\frac{1}{2}(z + z^{-1})\). Why isn’t the path graph \(\{0, 1, \dots, 2^N\}\) shift-invariant? Because at the two boundaries \(0\) and \(2^N\), the step to \(-1\) reflects to \(+1\), and the step to \(2^N + 1\) reflects to \(2^N - 1\).

How can we unfold a path of length \(L = 2^N\) with reflecting boundaries into a shift-invariant cycle? Think of a billiard ball bouncing between two mirrors at \(0\) and \(L\): unfolding the reflection creates a periodic cycle of length \(M = 2L = 2^{N+1}\)!

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad
  1. Consider the cycle graph \(\mathbb{Z}_M\) of length \(M = 2^{N+1}\), where at every vertex \(y \in \mathbb{Z}_M\), a step moves to \((y - 1) \bmod M\) with probability \(1/2\) and to \((y + 1) \bmod M\) with probability \(1/2\).
  2. Define the folding projection \(\pi : \mathbb{Z}_M \to \{0, 1, \dots, 2^N\}\) by \[ \pi(y) = \min(y,\, M - y) \qquad (0 \le y < M). \]
  3. Check that \(\pi\) is a Markov chain lumpability homomorphism from the uniform random walk on \(\mathbb{Z}_M\) to the reflecting random walk on \(\{0, \dots, 2^N\}\):
    • If \(\pi(y) = x \in \{1, \dots, 2^N - 1\}\), then \(\pi((y - 1) \bmod M)\) and \(\pi((y + 1) \bmod M)\) are \(\{x - 1, x + 1\}\), each reached with probability \(1/2\).
    • If \(\pi(y) = 0\) (so \(y = 0\)), both \((0 - 1) \bmod M = M - 1\) and \((0 + 1) \bmod M = 1\) project under \(\pi\) to \(\pi(M - 1) = \pi(1) = 1\), with total probability \(1/2 + 1/2 = 1\)!
    • If \(\pi(y) = 2^N\) (so \(y = 2^N\)), both \(2^N - 1\) and \(2^N + 1\) project under \(\pi\) to \(2^N - 1\), with total probability \(1/2 + 1/2 = 1\)!
  4. Therefore, starting at \(X \in \{0, \dots, 2^N\}\) on the cycle \(\mathbb{Z}_M\), the probability of being at \(Y\) on the path after \(T\) steps is the sum of the cycle probabilities of landing in the fiber \(\pi^{-1}(Y)\):
    • If \(Y \in \{0, 2^N\}\), \(\pi^{-1}(Y) = \{Y\}\) (a single point on the cycle).
    • If \(1 \le Y \le 2^N - 1\), \(\pi^{-1}(Y) = \{Y, M - Y\}\) (two symmetric points on the cycle).
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

On the cycle \(\mathbb{Z}_M\) of length \(M = 2^{N+1} \le 2^{21}\), starting at \(X\), the position after \(T\) steps is the coefficient of \(z^r\) in \[ P_{\text{cycle}}(z) = z^X \left( \frac{z + z^{-1}}{2} \right)^T \bmod (z^M - 1). \] Since \(M = 2^{N+1} \le 2^{21}\) divides \(p - 1 = 119 \cdot 2^{23}\), \(\mathbb{F}_p\) contains a primitive \(M\)-th root of unity \(\omega_M = 3^{(p-1)/M} \bmod p\)! Applying the Root-of-Unity Index Filter (§7.4.1) extracts \([z^r] P_{\text{cycle}}(z)\) directly in \(O(M \log T)\) time: \[ [z^r] P_{\text{cycle}}(z) = \frac{1}{M \cdot 2^T} \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} \omega_M^{-jr} \omega_M^{jX} \left(\omega_M^j + \omega_M^{-j}\right)^T. \]

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Unfolding the Reflecting Path onto the Cycle \(\mathbb{Z}_{2^{N+1}}\)

Let \(L = 2^N\) and \(M = 2L = 2^{N+1}\). Define the folding map \(\pi : \mathbb{Z}_M \to \{0, 1, \dots, L\}\) by \[ \pi(y) = \begin{cases} y & \text{if } 0 \le y \le L, \\ M - y & \text{if } L < y < M. \end{cases} \] Let \((Z_t)_{t \ge 0}\) be the standard symmetric random walk on the cycle \(\mathbb{Z}_M\) starting at \(Z_0 = X \in \{0, \dots, L\}\), where at each step \(Z_{t+1} = (Z_t \pm 1) \bmod M\) with probability \(1/2\) each. For every state \(y \in \mathbb{Z}_M\):

  • When \(\pi(y) \in \{1, \dots, L - 1\}\), its two neighbors \(y \pm 1 \pmod M\) satisfy \(\{\pi(y - 1), \pi(y + 1)\} = \{\pi(y) - 1, \pi(y) + 1\}\), so \(\pi(Z_{t+1}) = \pi(Z_t) \pm 1\) with probability \(1/2\) each.
  • When \(\pi(y) = 0\) (i.e., \(y = 0\)), both neighbors \(1\) and \(M - 1\) satisfy \(\pi(1) = \pi(M - 1) = 1\), so \(\pi(Z_{t+1}) = 1\) with probability \(1/2 + 1/2 = 1\).
  • When \(\pi(y) = L\) (i.e., \(y = L\)), both neighbors \(L - 1\) and \(L + 1\) satisfy \(\pi(L - 1) = \pi(L + 1) = L - 1\), so \(\pi(Z_{t+1}) = L - 1\) with probability \(1/2 + 1/2 = 1\).

By induction on \(t\), the projected walk \(\pi(Z_t)\) has the exact same probability distribution as the reflecting random walk on \(\{0, 1, \dots, L\}\) starting at \(X\). Consequently, after \(T\) steps, \[ \Pr[\text{Path at } Y \text{ at time } T] = \sum_{r \in \pi^{-1}(Y)} \Pr[Z_T = r \mid Z_0 = X], \] where the preimage \(\pi^{-1}(Y) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_M\) is \[ \pi^{-1}(Y) = \begin{cases} \{Y\} & \text{if } Y = 0 \text{ or } Y = 2^N, \\ \{Y,\, 2^{N+1} - Y\} & \text{if } 1 \le Y < 2^N. \end{cases} \]

2. Root-of-Unity Filter on the Cycle Generating Function

In the cyclic group ring \(\mathbb{F}_p[z]/(z^M - 1)\), starting at \(z^X\) and taking \(T\) steps with transition operator \(\frac{1}{2}(z + z^{-1}) = \frac{1}{2}(z + z^{M-1})\) produces the state polynomial \[ P_{\text{cycle}}(z) = \frac{1}{2^T} z^X (z + z^{-1})^T \bmod (z^M - 1). \] Since \(N \le 20\), \(M = 2^{N+1} \le 2^{21}\) divides \(p - 1 = 998244352 = 119 \cdot 2^{23}\). Let \(\omega = 3^{(p-1)/M} \bmod p\) be a primitive \(M\)-th root of unity in \(\mathbb{F}_p\). Applying the Root-of-Unity Index Filter from §7.4.1 to extract \([z^r] P_{\text{cycle}}(z)\) gives: \[ \Pr[Z_T = r \mid Z_0 = X] = \frac{1}{M \cdot 2^T} \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} \omega^{j(X - r)} \left(\omega^j + \omega^{-j}\right)^T \pmod p. \] Therefore, summing over \(r \in \pi^{-1}(Y)\) gives the final closed-form formula:

  • If \(Y \in \{0, 2^N\}\): \[ \text{ans} = \frac{1}{M \cdot 2^T} \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} \omega^{j(X - Y)} \left(\omega^j + \omega^{-j}\right)^T \pmod p. \]
  • If \(1 \le Y < 2^N\): Since \(M - Y \equiv -Y \pmod M\), \[ \text{ans} = \frac{1}{M \cdot 2^T} \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} \left(\omega^{j(X - Y)} + \omega^{j(X + Y)}\right) \left(\omega^j + \omega^{-j}\right)^T \pmod p. \]

3. Parity Shortcut, Hand-Checkable Trace & Algorithm Contract

  • Parity Check: On the bipartite path graph, the parity of the current vertex flips at every step (even at the reflecting boundaries \(0 \to 1\) and \(2^N \to 2^N - 1\)!). Thus if \((X + Y + T) \bmod 2 \ne 0\), the probability is identically \(0\). (Algebraically, in the sum above, replacing \(j\) by \(j + M/2\) negates \(\omega^j\), which multiplies the summand by \((-1)^{X \mp Y + T}\).)
  • Symmetry Optimization: Note that \(\omega^{M - j} = \omega^{-j}\), so \(\omega^j + \omega^{-j}\) is invariant under \(j \leftrightarrow M - j\), cutting the number of modular exponentiations \((\omega^j + \omega^{-j})^{T \bmod (p-1)}\) from \(M\) down to \(M/2 + 1 = 2^N + 1\).
  • Hand-Checkable Trace (\(N = 1, X = 0, Y = 2, T = 2\)):
    • Cycle length \(M = 2^{1+1} = 4\), with \(\omega^2 = -1\). Since \(Y = 2 = 2^1\), \(\pi^{-1}(2) = \{2\}\).
    • Summing \(\omega^{-2j}(\omega^j + \omega^{-j})^2 = (-1)^j (\omega^j + \omega^{-j})^2\) over \(j = 0, 1, 2, 3\):
      • \(j = 0\): \((+1)(1 + 1)^2 = 4\).
      • \(j = 1\): \((-1)(\omega - \omega)^2 = 0\).
      • \(j = 2\): \((+1)(-1 - 1)^2 = 4\).
      • \(j = 3\): \((-1)(-\omega + \omega)^2 = 0\).
    • Dividing by \(M \cdot 2^T = 4 \cdot 4 = 16\) gives \(\frac{4 + 0 + 4 + 0}{16} = \frac{1}{2}\).
  • Input & Preconditions: Path exponent \(1 \le N \le 20\) (path length \(L = 2^N\), unfolded cycle length \(M = 2^{N+1} \le 2^{21}\)), endpoints \(0 \le X, Y \le 2^N\), step count \(1 \le T \le 10^{18}\), and prime \(p = 998244353\) with \(M \mid (p - 1)\).
  • Output & Postconditions: Outputs \(\Pr[\text{Path at } Y \text{ at time } T] \bmod 998244353\).
  • Index & Truncation Boundaries: Sum the cycle root-of-unity filter over \(j \in [0, M-1]\) (or \(j \in [0, 2^N]\) by conjugate symmetry \(j \leftrightarrow M - j\)), summing over the preimage \(\pi^{-1}(Y) = \{Y\}\) when \(Y \in \{0, 2^N\}\) and \(\pi^{-1}(Y) = \{Y, M - Y\}\) when \(1 \le Y < 2^N\).
  • 1-Sentence Loop Invariant: After processing frequency index \(j\), the accumulator holds \(\sum_{r=0}^j \left(\sum_{y \in \pi^{-1}(Y)} \omega_M^{r(X - y)}\right) (\omega_M^r + \omega_M^{-r})^T \bmod p\).
  • Complexity & Failure Modes: \(O(2^N \log(\min(T, p)))\) time; \(O(2^N)\) space to store powers \(\omega_M^j\) for \(0 \le j \le M\). Double-counting the boundary fiber \(\pi^{-1}(Y)\) at \(Y = 0\) or \(Y = 2^N\) (where \(Y \equiv M - Y \pmod M\) is a single cycle state, not two) inflates endpoint probabilities by a factor of \(2\), and reducing \(T \bmod p\) instead of \(T \bmod (p - 1)\) in Fermat’s Little Theorem corrupts the exponent when \(T \ge p\) (with base \(\omega_M^j + \omega_M^{-j} \equiv 0 \pmod p\) at \(j = M/4, 3M/4\) yielding \(0^T = 0\) since \(T \ge 1\)).

  • Prerequisite Tags: §7.1.1 (DFT Diagonalization of Cyclic Shifts), §7.3 (Bluestein’s Chirp Z-Transform for Arbitrary Length \(n\))

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

There is a hidden integer sequence \(a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{n-1} \in \mathbb{Z}\). You are given an integer sequence \(b_0, b_1, \dots, b_{n-1}\) and the cyclic squared-distance sequence \(c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{n-1}\) defined for each shift \(0 \le k < n\) by \[ c_k = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \left(a_i - b_{(i + k) \bmod n}\right)^2. \] Find all integer sequences \(a = (a_0, \dots, a_{n-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}^n\) that produce the given sequence \(c\) for the given sequence \(b\), sorted lexicographically. The problem guarantees that all values \(b_i\) are chosen such that the number of valid sequences \(a\) is small (in fact, at most \(2\)).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 10^5\) (\(n\) is an arbitrary integer, NOT necessarily a power of \(2\)!), \(|b_i| \le 10^4\), \(0 \le c_k \le 10^{18}\), and any valid solution satisfies \(|a_i| \le 10^4\) (and it is guaranteed that \(B(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} b_i x^i\) is coprime to \(\frac{x^n - 1}{x - 1}\), i.e., \(\hat{b}_r \ne 0\) for all \(1 \le r < n\)).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(n = 3\), \(b = [1, 2, 4]\), and \(c = [1, 9, 11]\) (generated by hidden \(a = [1, 2, 3]\)): the cyclic half-differences are \(d = [\frac{1-9}{2}, \frac{9-11}{2}, \frac{11-1}{2}] = [-4, -1, 5]\) and \(\Delta b = [1, 2, -3]\). Inverting \(\tilde{a} \ast_3 \Delta b = d\) recovers \(\delta = [a_0 - a_0, a_1 - a_0, a_2 - a_0] = [0, 1, 2]\), and substituting \(a_i = a_0 + \delta_i\) into \(c_0 = \sum_{i=0}^2 (a_0 + \delta_i - b_i)^2 = 1\) gives \((a_0 - 1)^2 + (a_0 - 1)^2 + (a_0 - 2)^2 = 1 \implies 3a_0^2 - 8a_0 + 5 = 0 \implies (a_0 - 1)(3a_0 - 5) = 0\), whose unique integer root \(a_0 = 1\) recovers \(a = [1, 2, 3]\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(n \log n)\) time and \(O(n)\) space.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Expand the square inside \(c_k\): \[ c_k = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i^2 + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} b_i^2 - 2 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i b_{(i + k) \bmod n}. \] Two obstacles immediately appear:

  1. The unknown sum of squares \(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i^2\) is an unknown constant added to every \(c_k\). How do we eliminate an unknown constant independent of \(k\)? Take the cyclic first difference \(c_k - c_{(k+1) \bmod n}\)!
  2. The cross term \(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i b_{(i + k) \bmod n}\) is a cyclic correlation of arbitrary length \(n \le 10^5\), where \(n\) is not a power of \(2\). How do we invert a cyclic convolution of arbitrary length \(n\) in \(O(n \log n)\) time? Diagonalize via a length-\(n\) DFT over a prime \(P \equiv 1 \pmod n\) using Bluestein’s Chirp Z-Transform (§7.3)!
Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  1. Take the cyclic difference between \(c_k\) and \(c_{(k+1) \bmod n}\): the constant terms \(\sum a_i^2 + \sum b_i^2\) cancel completely! Dividing by \(2\) gives \[ d_k := \frac{c_k - c_{(k+1) \bmod n}}{2} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i \left(b_{(i + k + 1) \bmod n} - b_{(i + k) \bmod n}\right). \] (Note: if \(c_k - c_{(k+1)\bmod n}\) is odd for any \(k\), no integer solution exists!)

  2. Define the known cyclic difference sequence \(\Delta b_j = b_{(j + 1) \bmod n} - b_j\) for \(0 \le j < n\), and reverse \(a\) by setting \(\tilde{a}_{(n - i) \bmod n} = a_i\). Then the sum for \(d_k\) is the exact length-\(n\) cyclic convolution of \(\tilde{a}\) and \(\Delta b\): \[ d_k = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \tilde{a}_{(n - i) \bmod n} \Delta b_{(i + k) \bmod n} = (\tilde{a} \ast_n \Delta b)_k. \]

  3. Apply the length-\(n\) DFT (\(\hat{x}_r = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} x_j \omega_n^{rj}\)) to both sides: \[ \hat{d}_r = \hat{\tilde{a}}_r \cdot \widehat{\Delta b}_r \qquad (0 \le r < n). \]

    • For all nonzero frequencies \(1 \le r < n\): Since \(\widehat{\Delta b}_r = (\omega_n^{-r} - 1)\hat{b}_r \ne 0\), we can divide to recover \(\hat{\tilde{a}}_r = \hat{d}_r / \widehat{\Delta b}_r\) uniquely!
    • For the DC frequency \(r = 0\): Because \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \Delta b_j = 0\), we have \(\widehat{\Delta b}_0 = 0\)! Thus the linear difference equations determine every \(a_i\) up to a uniform additive constant (equivalently, \(\hat{\tilde{a}}_0 = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i\) is the single remaining degree of freedom).
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

  4. Then the true integer sequence \(a_i\) must have the form \(a_i = \frac{\alpha_i + S_a}{n}\), where \(\alpha_i = n \cdot a_i^{(0)} \in (-n \cdot 10^4, n \cdot 10^4)\) is recovered uniquely from \(\alpha_i \bmod P\) (since \(P > 2 n \cdot 10^4\)).
  5. Finally, substitute \(a_i = a_0 + \delta_i\) (where \(\delta_i = (\alpha_i - \alpha_0)/n\) must be an exact integer!) into the original equation for \(c_0 = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (a_0 + \delta_i - b_i)^2\). NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Eliminating \(\sum a_i^2\) via Cyclic First Differences

Expanding \(c_k = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (a_i - b_{(i+k)\bmod n})^2\) and subtracting \(c_{(k+1)\bmod n}\) cancels \(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i^2\) and \(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} b_i^2\): \[ c_k - c_{(k+1)\bmod n} = 2 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i \left(b_{(i+k+1)\bmod n} - b_{(i+k)\bmod n}\right). \] First, verify that \(c_k - c_{(k+1)\bmod n}\) is even for every \(0 \le k < n\); if not, output 0 immediately. 2. Exact Arbitrary-Length DFT Inversion via Runtime Prime \(P \equiv 1 \pmod n\) and CZT

Because \(|a_i| \le 10^4\) and \(n \le 10^5\), the scaled quantities \(\alpha_i := n a_i - \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a_j = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} (a_i - a_j)\) satisfy the strict integer bound \[ |\alpha_i - \alpha_0| = n|a_i - a_0| \le 2 n \cdot 10^4 \le 2 \times 10^9. \] Since \(n \le 10^5\) is given at runtime, we select a prime \(P = m \cdot n + 1\) dynamically at runtime by starting at \(m = \lfloor 4 \times 10^9 / n \rfloor + 1\) (or \(m = \lfloor 2.01 \times 10^9 / n \rfloor + 1\), so \(P > 2 n \cdot 10^4\) and every signed difference \(n(a_i - a_0) \in [-2 \times 10^9, 2 \times 10^9]\) lies strictly inside \((-P/2, P/2)\)) and incrementing \(m\) until \(P = m \cdot n + 1\) passes deterministic Miller–Rabin (or \(O(\sqrt{P})\) trial division). By the Prime Number Theorem in arithmetic progressions, this tests only \(O(\log P) \approx 20\) candidates and finishes in \(< 1\text{ ms}\). Let \(g\) be a primitive root modulo \(P\), and set \(\omega = g^{(P-1)/n} \bmod P\), which is a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity in \(\mathbb{F}_P\).

In the frequency domain over \(\mathbb{F}_P\):

  1. Evaluate \(\hat{d}_r = D(\omega^r)\) and \(\widehat{\Delta b}_r = \Delta B(\omega^r)\) for \(0 \le r < n\) by calling our Chirp Z-Transform engine czt(d, 1, omega, n) and czt(db, 1, omega, n) (backed by 3-Prime NTT from §7.2 since \(P\) is not a power-of-two NTT prime).
  2. For \(r = 0\), \(\widehat{\Delta b}_0 = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \Delta b_k = 0\). If \(\hat{d}_0 \ne 0\), no solution exists. Set \(\hat{y}_0 = 0\).
  3. For \(1 \le r < n\), if \(\widehat{\Delta b}_r = 0\) and \(\hat{d}_r \ne 0\), no solution exists. Otherwise (as guaranteed by the problem constraints \(\widehat{\Delta b}_r \ne 0\)), set \[ \hat{y}_r = \hat{d}_r \cdot \left(\widehat{\Delta b}_r\right)^{P - 2} \bmod P. \]
  4. Let \(\omega^{-1} = \omega^{n-1} \bmod P\) (inv_omega) be the multiplicative inverse of \(\omega\). Apply the unnormalized inverse DFT by passing c = inv_omega into czt(y_hat, 1, inv_omega, n) (which evaluates \(\sum_{r=0}^{n-1} \hat{y}_r (\omega^{-1})^{rk} = \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} \hat{y}_r \omega^{-rk}\)) to obtain, for each \(0 \le k < n\): \[ Y_k = \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} \hat{y}_r \omega^{-rk} \equiv \sum_{r=1}^{n-1} \hat{\tilde{a}}_r \omega^{-rk} = \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} \hat{\tilde{a}}_r \omega^{-rk} - \hat{\tilde{a}}_0 = n \tilde{a}_k - \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i \pmod P. \]
  5. Subtracting \(Y_0\) from \(Y_{(n-i)\bmod n}\) eliminates \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a_j\) and gives \[ Y_{(n-i)\bmod n} - Y_0 \equiv n (a_i - a_0) \pmod P. \] Since \(|n(a_i - a_0)| \le 2 \times 10^9 < P / 2\), we recover the exact signed integer \(n(a_i - a_0) \in (-P/2, P/2)\) by mapping residues \(> P/2\) to \(r - P\). Check that \(n \mid n(a_i - a_0)\) for every \(i\); if not, output 0. Otherwise, we now know every difference \[ \delta_i := a_i - a_0 \in \mathbb{Z} \qquad (0 \le i < n). \]

3. Solving the Quadratic Equation for \(a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}\)

Substitute \(a_i = a_0 + \delta_i\) into the \(k = 0\) distance equation \(c_0 = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (a_i - b_i)^2\): \[ \begin{aligned} c_0 &= \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \bigl(a_0 + (\delta_i - b_i)\bigr)^2 \\ &= \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \Bigl(a_0^2 + 2(\delta_i - b_i)a_0 + (\delta_i - b_i)^2\Bigr) \qquad \text{(expand each binomial square)} \\ &= n a_0^2 + 2 \left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (\delta_i - b_i)\right) a_0 + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (\delta_i - b_i)^2 \qquad \text{(distribute summation by linearity)}. \end{aligned} \] Let \(K_1 = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (\delta_i - b_i)\) and \(K_0 = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (\delta_i - b_i)^2 - c_0\). This is the scalar quadratic equation \[ n a_0^2 + 2 K_1 a_0 + K_0 = 0. \] Its discriminant is \(\frac{\Delta}{4} = K_1^2 - n K_0\). If \(K_1^2 - n K_0 < 0\) or is not a perfect square \(s^2\) (\(s \ge 0\)), output 0. Otherwise, test the at most two candidate roots \(a_0 = \frac{-K_1 \pm s}{n}\); keep those where \(n \mid (-K_1 \pm s)\), form \(a_i = a_0 + \delta_i\), sort the valid sequences lexicographically, and print them.

4. Hand-Checkable Trace & Algorithm Contract

  • Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n = 3, b = [1, 2, 4], c = [1, 9, 11]\)):
    • \(d = [-4, -1, 5]\) and \(\Delta b = [1, 2, -3]\).
    • From \(\tilde{a} \ast_3 \Delta b = d\), the difference system \((\tilde{a}_1 - \tilde{a}_0) + 2(\tilde{a}_0 - \tilde{a}_2) = \dots\) yields \(3(\tilde{a}_0, \tilde{a}_1, \tilde{a}_2) - S_a = (-3, 3, 0)\), so \(\delta = (a_0 - a_0, a_1 - a_0, a_2 - a_0) = (0, 1, 2)\).
    • Quadratic for \(a_0\): \(\delta - b = [-1, -1, -2]\), so \(K_1 = -4, K_0 = (1 + 1 + 4) - 1 = 5\). Then \(3a_0^2 - 8a_0 + 5 = 0 \implies \Delta/4 = 16 - 15 = 1 = 1^2 \implies a_0 = \frac{4 \pm 1}{3} \in \{1, 5/3\}\). Only \(a_0 = 1\) is an integer, giving \(a = [1, 2, 3]\).
  • Input & Preconditions: Integer \(n\) (\(1 \le n \le 10^5\)), integer array \(b = (b_0, \dots, b_{n-1})\) (\(|b_i| \le 10^4\)), and squared cyclic distance array \(c = (c_0, \dots, c_{n-1})\) generated from at least one valid array \(a\) (\(|a_i| \le 10^4\)) such that \(\widehat{\Delta b}_r \ne 0\) for all \(1 \le r < n\).
  • Output & Postconditions: Outputs the count of valid integer arrays \(a \in \mathbb{Z}^n\) matching \(c\), followed by all valid arrays in lexicographical order.
  • Invariants: The prime \(P > 2 n^2 \max |a_i - b_i| \approx 4 \times 10^{14}\) satisfies \(P \equiv 1 \pmod n\), guaranteeing that the length-\(n\) DFT over \(\mathbb{F}_P\) separates all non-zero frequencies \(1 \le r < n\) without wrap-around overflow when signed residues are mapped to \((-P/2, P/2]\).
  • Complexity: Finding \(P \equiv 1 \pmod n\) takes \(O(\sqrt{P} \log P)\) time; three calls to length-\(n\) CZT (each executing one 3-Prime NTT of size \(L = 2^{\lceil \log_2(3n) \rceil} \le 2^{19}\)) take \(O(n \log n)\) time and \(O(n)\) space.
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: (1) If \(\frac{\Delta}{4} = K_1^2 - n K_0\) is negative or not a perfect square, or if neither \(-K_1 + s\) nor \(-K_1 - s\) is divisible by \(n\), zero solutions exist; (2) When \(\Delta = 0\) (\(s = 0\)), the two roots \(-K_1 \pm s\) coincide and must be deduplicated so the same sequence \(a\) is not printed twice; (3) Centered modular lifts \(\tilde{x}_i \in (-P/2, P/2]\) must use __int128 during the quadratic coefficients \(K_1, K_0\) and discriminant \(\Delta/4\) computation to avoid 64-bit signed overflow.

Mastery 2 (#168) — Luogu P5293: [HNOI2019] 白兔之舞

  • Source: Luogu P5293 — [HNOI2019] 白兔之舞
  • Prerequisite Tags: §7.2 (3-Prime Arbitrary-Modulus NTT MTT + Garner’s CRT), §7.3 (Bluestein’s CZT), §7.4.1 (Root-of-Unity Filter on Matrix GFs)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given a directed graph on \(n\) vertices (\(1 \le n \le 3\)) with adjacency weight matrix \(W \in \mathbb{Z}^{n \times n}\), a walk bound \(L\), a step-count modulus \(k\), a starting vertex \(x \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), a target vertex \(y \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), and a prime modulus \(p\) satisfying \(k \mid (p - 1)\).

For each remainder \(t \in \{0, 1, \dots, k - 1\}\), compute the total weighted count of walks from \(x\) to \(y\) that take \(m \equiv t \pmod k\) steps chosen from \(L\) time slots: \[ \text{ans}_t = \left( \sum_{\substack{0 \le m \le L \\ m \equiv t \pmod k}} \binom{L}{m} (W^m)_{x, y} \right) \bmod p \qquad (0 \le t < k). \]

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 3\), \(1 \le L \le 10^8\), \(1 \le k \le 65536\) (\(k\) is not necessarily a power of \(2\)!), \(1 \le x, y \le n\), prime \(p \in (10^8, 2^{30})\) with \(k \mid (p - 1)\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N \le 4\)): For \(n = 1\), \(W = (1)\), \(L = 4, k = 3, x = 1, y = 1\), and prime \(p = 7\) (\(3 \mid 6\)), we want \(\text{ans}_t = \sum_{m \equiv t \pmod 3} \binom{4}{m} \bmod 7\) for \(t = 0, 1, 2\). Directly: \(\text{ans}_0 = \binom{4}{0} + \binom{4}{3} = 1 + 4 = 5\); \(\text{ans}_1 = \binom{4}{1} + \binom{4}{4} = 4 + 1 = 5\); \(\text{ans}_2 = \binom{4}{2} = 6\).
  • Required Complexity: \(O(n^3 k \log L + k \log k)\) time and \(O(k)\) space.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Compare this problem with SS-2 (#248 — BZOJ 3328):

  • Just like in SS-2, the summand \(\binom{L}{m} (W^m)_{x, y}\) is the \((x, y)\) entry of \([z^m] (I + zW)^L\), and the problem guarantees \(k \mid (p - 1)\), so a primitive \(k\)-th root of unity \(\omega_k \in \mathbb{F}_p\) exists!
  • However, here we must compute \(\text{ans}_t\) for ALL \(k\) remainders \(t = 0, 1, \dots, k - 1\) simultaneously (\(k \le 65536\)), and the prime \(p \le 2^{30}\) is an arbitrary prime (not an NTT-friendly prime like \(998244353\), and \(k\) is not necessarily a power of \(2\)).
Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad
  1. Apply the Root-of-Unity Filter (§7.4.1) for a general remainder \(t \in \{0, \dots, k - 1\}\): \[ \text{ans}_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega_k^{-jt} \underbrace{\left[ \left(I + \omega_k^j W\right)^L \right]_{x, y}}_{c_j}. \]
  2. First, we can compute all \(k\) point values \(c_j = \bigl[(I + \omega_k^j W)^L\bigr]_{x, y} \bmod p\) for \(0 \le j < k\) by \(n \times n\) matrix binary exponentiation in \(O(n^3 k \log L)\) time (\(\approx 27 \times 65536 \times 27 \approx 4.7 \times 10^7\) operations).
  3. Once the vector \(c = (c_0, \dots, c_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{F}_p^k\) is known, look at the remaining sum for \(\text{ans}_t\): \[ \text{ans}_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} c_j \omega_k^{-jt} \qquad (0 \le t < k). \] This is evaluating the polynomial \(C(z) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} c_j z^j\) at the geometric progression \(z_t = \omega_k^{-t}\) for \(0 \le t < k\)!
  4. How do we evaluate a degree-\(k\) polynomial at \(z_t = 1 \cdot (\omega_k^{-1})^t\) over an arbitrary prime \(p \le 2^{30}\) when \(k \le 65536\) is not a power of \(2\)? Combine Bluestein’s Chirp Z-Transform (§7.3) with 3-Prime Arbitrary-Modulus NTT (MTT, §7.2)!
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Use Bluestein’s negative-product binomial identity: \[ -j t = \binom{j}{2} + \binom{t}{2} - \binom{j + t}{2} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \omega_k^{-jt} = \omega_k^{\binom{j}{2}} \cdot \omega_k^{\binom{t}{2}} \cdot \omega_k^{-\binom{j + t}{2}}. \] Thus \[ \text{ans}_t = \frac{1}{k} \omega_k^{\binom{t}{2}} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \left(c_j \omega_k^{\binom{j}{2}}\right) \omega_k^{-\binom{j + t}{2}}. \] Reversing the first array \(\tilde{u}_{k - 1 - j} = c_j \omega_k^{\binom{j}{2}} \bmod p\) and convolving with \(v_r = \omega_k^{-\binom{r}{2}} \bmod p\) (\(0 \le r < 2k - 1\)) via 3-Prime NTT + Garner’s CRT (§7.2) modulo \(p\) computes all \(\text{ans}_t\) in a single length-\(2^{18}\) MTT convolution!

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

1. Matrix Generating Function & Root-of-Unity Filter

In the matrix polynomial ring \(\mathbb{F}_p^{n \times n}[z]\), the coefficient of \(z^m\) in \((I + zW)^L = \sum_{m=0}^L \binom{L}{m} W^m z^m\) is \(\binom{L}{m} W^m\). Since \(p\) is prime and \(k \mid (p - 1)\), we find a primitive root \(g\) of \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\) in \(O(\sqrt{p})\) time by factoring \(p - 1\) and set \(\omega_k = g^{(p-1)/k} \bmod p\).

By the Root-of-Unity Index Filter (§7.4.1), for each \(t \in \{0, 1, \dots, k - 1\}\): \[ \begin{aligned} \text{ans}_t &= \sum_{\substack{0 \le m \le L \\ m \equiv t \pmod k}} \binom{L}{m} (W^m)_{x, y} \\ &= \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega_k^{-jt} \left[ \left(I + \omega_k^j W\right)^L \right]_{x, y} \pmod p. \end{aligned} \] For each \(0 \le j < k\), define the scalar point value \[ c_j = \left[ \left(I + \omega_k^j W\right)^L \right]_{x, y} \bmod p. \] Because \(n \le 3\), computing \((I + \omega_k^j W)^L \bmod p\) via \(n \times n\) matrix binary exponentiation takes \(O(n^3 \log L)\) operations per \(j\), or \(O(n^3 k \log L)\) overall.

2. Bluestein’s Chirp Z-Transform Reduction

We now have \(c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{k-1} \in \mathbb{F}_p\) and must compute, for all \(0 \le t < k\), \[ \text{ans}_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} c_j \omega_k^{-jt} \pmod p. \] Applying Bluestein’s Binomial Identity \(-jt = \binom{j}{2} + \binom{t}{2} - \binom{j+t}{2}\) (which uses only integer exponents \(\binom{r}{2} = \frac{r(r-1)}{2}\) and therefore avoids Failure Mode #12 even when \(\omega_k\) is a quadratic non-residue modulo \(p\)) gives: \[ \text{ans}_t = \frac{1}{k} \omega_k^{\binom{t}{2} \bmod k} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \underbrace{\left(c_j \omega_k^{\binom{j}{2} \bmod k}\right)}_{u_j} \cdot \underbrace{\left(\omega_k^{-\binom{j+t}{2} \bmod k}\right)}_{v_{j+t}} \pmod p. \] Define two polynomials \(\tilde{U}(z)\) and \(V(z)\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\):

  • \(\tilde{U}(z) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} u_j z^{k - 1 - j}\) of degree \(k - 1\), where \(\tilde{u}_{k - 1 - j} = c_j \omega_k^{\binom{j}{2} \bmod k} \bmod p\).
  • \(V(z) = \sum_{r=0}^{2k-2} v_r z^r\) of degree \(2k - 2\), where \(v_r = \omega_k^{\bigl(k - (\binom{r}{2} \bmod k)\bigr) \bmod k} \bmod p\).

Then for every \(0 \le t < k\), the inner sum over \(j\) is the exact coefficient of \(z^{k - 1 + t}\) in the polynomial product \(\tilde{U}(z) V(z)\): \[ \text{ans}_t = \frac{1}{k} \omega_k^{\binom{t}{2} \bmod k} \cdot [z^{k - 1 + t}] \bigl(\tilde{U}(z) V(z)\bigr) \bmod p. \]

3. 3-Prime Arbitrary-Modulus NTT (MTT) Execution

Because \(p \in (10^8, 2^{30})\) is an arbitrary prime, we multiply \(\tilde{U}(z)\) and \(V(z)\) (total degree \((k - 1) + (2k - 2) = 3k - 3 \le 196605 < 2^{18} = 262144\)) using our 3-Prime NTT + Garner’s Mixed-Radix CRT engine from §7.2:

  1. Check the maximum possible unreduced convolution coefficient: \[ k \cdot (p - 1)^2 \le 65536 \times (2^{30})^2 = 2^{76} \approx 7.55 \times 10^{22} < p_1 p_2 p_3 \approx 4.71 \times 10^{26}, \] where \(p_1 = 998244353\), \(p_2 = 1004535809\), \(p_3 = 469762049\). Thus three NTT primes are strictly sufficient!
  2. Run three length-\(2^{18}\) NTT convolutions of \((\tilde{U} \bmod p_i)\) and \((V \bmod p_i)\) for \(i \in \{1, 2, 3\}\).
  3. Reconstruct \([z^{k - 1 + t}](\tilde{U}V) \bmod p\) via Garner’s Mixed-Radix CRT (§7.2.3), and multiply by \(k^{-1} \omega_k^{\binom{t}{2} \bmod k} \bmod p\).

Using \(\binom{m}{2} \bmod (p - 1)\) directly avoids parity bugs for even \(k\).
