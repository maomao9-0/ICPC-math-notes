15 — 1D Lattice Walks, Reflection Principles, Chebyshev Polynomials & The Kernel Method

Part IV · Probabilistic Generating Functions, Lattice Walks & Multivariate Series

NotePrerequisites & Learning Objectives

Prerequisites:

  • Chapters 01–02 (Ring Convolutions, fps_inv, fps_sqrt, fps_exp): Newton-iteration engines in \(K[[x]] \bmod x^N\) and rational generating function arithmetic.
  • Chapter 05 (Rational GFs, Bostan–Mori & Linear Recurrences): Extracting \([x^N] P(x)/Q(x)\) in \(O(d \log d \log N)\) time.
  • Chapter 11 (Formal Calculus, Residues & Lagrange Inversion): The change-of-variables formula \([x^{-1}] G(x) = [u^{-1}] G(\phi(u))\phi'(u)\) and solving implicit tree equations \(y(x) = x \Phi(y(x))\).

Learning Objectives:

  1. Master Single- and Double-Wall Reflection Principles: Use André’s reflection across a single line \(y = -1\) and Kelvin’s Method of Images across two parallel mirrors \(y = -1\) and \(y = H+1\) (the infinite dihedral group \(D_\infty\)) to count bounded 1D walks in \(O(N/H)\) operations, and diagnose exactly when geometric reflection fails (asymmetric steps such as \(\{+1, -2\}\)).
  2. Ground Bivariate Walk Series in Formal Laurent Rings: Understand why a step Laurent polynomial \(S(y) = \sum_{s=-k}^m w_s y^s \in K[y, y^{-1}]\) yields a well-defined bivariate series \(F(x, y) \in (K[y, y^{-1}])[[x]]\), and why substituting a small branch \(y = y_0(x)\) (\(\operatorname{val}_x(y_0) > 0\)) converges \(x\)-adically while substituting a large branch (\(\operatorname{val}_x(y_{\text{large}}) < 0\)) diverges on the half-line \([0, \infty)\) yet succeeds on a finite strip \([0, H]\).
  3. Execute the 1D Bivariate Kernel Method Across All Three Regimes:
    • Regime A (One Small Root, \(k = 1\)): Cancel the kernel \(K(x, y) = y(1 - x S(y))\) on the half-line \(y \ge 0\) by substituting \(y = y_0(x)\), and handle absorbing boundary operators (\(y^{-1} \mapsto y^0\)) via reversed-walk transposition.
    • Regime B (Bounded Strip \([0, H]\) & Chebyshev Rational Functions): Substitute both roots \(y_0(x), y_1(x)\) into a degree-\(H\) polynomial in \(y\), derive the Chebyshev rational generating function \(Q_H(x)/Q_{H+1}(x)\), and extract \([x^N]\) in \(O(N)\) via the Catalan residue substitution \(x = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\) or in \(O(H \log H \log N)\) via Bostan–Mori.
    • Regime C (\(k\) Negative Steps & Vieta’s Large-Root Trick): Cancel \(k\) boundary unknowns \(F_0(x), \dots, F_{k-1}(x)\) using the \(k\) small branches \(y_1(x), \dots, y_k(x)\), and when \(m = 1\) (steps \(\{-k, \dots, +1\}\)), bypass fractional Puiseux powers \(x^{1/k}\) completely by expressing symmetric functions of the small roots in terms of the single large root \(y_{\text{large}}(x) = 1/u(x)\).
  4. Connect 1D Walks to Łukasiewicz Words & Raney’s Cycle Lemma: Understand both combinatorially and algebraically why Raney’s Cycle Lemma holds if and only if the minimum step is \(-1\) (\(k = 1\)).

In one-dimensional lattice path problems, a particle starts at height \(y_0 = s \ge 0\) and takes \(N\) steps \(s_1, s_2, \dots, s_N\) drawn from a finite step set \(\mathcal{S} \subset \mathbb{Z}\). Without boundary walls, the position after \(N\) steps is simply the sum \(s + s_1 + \dots + s_N\), and the generating function in the spatial variable \(y\) is the \(N\)-th power of the step Laurent polynomial \(S(y) = \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} w_s y^s\):

\[[x^N] \frac{y^s}{1 - x S(y)} = y^s S(y)^N.\]

The entire combinatorial difficulty—and beauty—of 1D lattice walks enters when we impose boundary walls:

  • Half-line restriction (\(y_t \ge 0\) for all \(0 \le t \le N\)): The particle is forbidden from ever stepping onto the negative integers \(\{-1, -2, \dots\}\) (yielding Dyck, Motzkin, Schröder, and Łukasiewicz walks).
  • Bounded strip restriction (\(0 \le y_t \le H\) for all \(0 \le t \le N\)): The particle is trapped between a floor at \(y = 0\) and a ceiling at \(y = H\) (yielding bounded Dyck paths, bounded Cartesian trees, and gambler’s ruin between two barriers).
  • Absorbing / clamping floor (\(y_t = \max(0, y_{t-1} + s_t)\)): Stepping below zero resets the height to \(0\) rather than killing the walk (governing prefix-maximum distributions).

This chapter builds two complementary engines for solving these boundary value problems:

  1. The Geometric Engine (Section 15.1): André’s Reflection Principle and Kelvin’s Method of Images, which unfold walks with simple steps \(\{+1, -1\}\) (or \(\{+1, 0, -1\}\)) across one or two mirrors into unconstrained walks in \(O(1)\) or \(O(N/H)\) binomial evaluations.
  2. The Algebraic Engine (Sections 15.2–15.4): The Bivariate Kernel Method, which translates the step transition and boundary absorption into a single linear functional equation in the formal Laurent ring \((K[y, y^{-1}])[[x]]\) and annihilates the unknown interior series \(F(x, y)\) by substituting the roots of the kernel polynomial \(K(x, y) = y^k(1 - x S(y)) = 0\).

15.1 André’s Reflection Principle & Kelvin’s Method of Images in a Strip \([0, H]\)

Concrete Combinatorial Model: Unconstrained vs. Wall-Bounded Simple Walks

Before touching bivariate generating functions, let us examine the classical geometric setting where steps are unit increments:

  • Time-Height Formulation: At each time step \(t = 1, \dots, N\), the height changes by \(\Delta y_t \in \{+1, -1\}\), starting at \((0, s)\) and ending at \((N, e)\) (requiring \(N \equiv e - s \pmod 2\)). If the walk takes \(u\) up-steps (\(+1\)) and \(d\) down-steps (\(-1\)), then \(u + d = N\) and \(u - d = e - s\), so \(u = \frac{N + e - s}{2}\) and \(d = \frac{N - e + s}{2}\).
  • Monotone Grid Formulation: Rotating the axes by \(45^\circ\) maps each \(+1\) step to an East step \((1, 0)\) and each \(-1\) step to a North step \((0, 1)\). A walk from height \(s\) to height \(e\) of length \(N\) becomes a monotone grid walk from \((0, 0)\) to \((u, d)\) using only steps \((1, 0)\) and \((0, 1)\), where the current height is \(y = s + x_{\text{grid}} - y_{\text{grid}}\).

In the monotone grid coordinates \((x, y)\) starting at \((0, 0)\) and ending at \((X, Y)\):

  1. The unconstrained number of paths from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\) is \(\binom{X+Y}{X}\) (understood to be \(0\) if \(X < 0\) or \(Y < 0\)).
  2. A lower floor \(y_{\text{height}} \ge 0\) (starting at height \(s = a - 1 \ge 0\)) forbids the path from touching the line: \[L_1: y = x + a \quad (a \ge 1).\]
  3. An upper ceiling \(y_{\text{height}} \le H\) additionally forbids the path from touching the parallel line: \[L_2: y = x - b \quad (b = H - s + 1 \ge 1).\]

Single-Boundary Reflection (André’s Reflection Principle)

Suppose we want to count monotone grid paths from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\) that never touch the forbidden line \(L_1: y = x + a\) (\(a \ge 1\)).

  • Diagnostic Signal: Every step changes \(y - x\) by \(\pm 1\), so any path starting at \((0, 0)\) (where \(y - x = 0 < a\)) that reaches or crosses \(y - x \ge a\) must pass through \(L_1: y - x = a\) at some first hitting time \(\tau\).
  • The Reflection Bijection: Given any “bad” path \(P\) from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\) that touches \(L_1\), let \(P_{\le \tau}\) be its prefix up to the first point \((x_\tau, y_\tau) \in L_1\) (where \(y_\tau = x_\tau + a\)), and let \(P_{> \tau}\) be the remaining suffix from \((x_\tau, y_\tau)\) to \((X, Y)\). Reflecting the suffix \(P_{> \tau}\) across the line \(L_1: y = x + a\) swaps every remaining \((1, 0)\) step with a \((0, 1)\) step, mapping any point \((x, y)\) to: \[r_1(x, y) = (y - a,\, x + a).\] Notice that on the mirror \(L_1\) itself (\(y = x + a\)), \(r_1(x, x+a) = (x, x+a)\), so the reflected suffix connects seamlessly to \(P_{\le \tau}\) and terminates at \(r_1(X, Y) = (Y - a, X + a)\). Conversely, because the starting point \((0, 0)\) lies on one side of \(L_1\) (\(0 - 0 = 0 < a\)) and the reflected endpoint \((Y - a, X + a)\) lies strictly on the other side whenever \(X + a > Y - a\) (or any path to \((Y-a, X+a)\) starting at \((0,0)\) must cross \(L_1\) since \(a \ge 1\) and \((X+a) - (Y-a) \ge a\) when \(X \ge Y - a\)), every unconstrained path from \((0, 0)\) to \((Y - a, X + a)\) must intersect \(L_1\) at least once; reflecting its suffix after the first intersection recovers the original bad path \(P\) uniquely.

Therefore, the number of valid paths from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\) avoiding \(L_1: y = x + a\) is:

\[N_{\text{avoid } L_1}(X, Y) = \binom{X + Y}{X} - \binom{X + Y}{X + a} = \binom{X + Y}{X} - \binom{X + Y}{Y - a}.\]

Specializing to \((X, Y) = (n, n)\) and \(a = 1\) recovers the \(n\)-th Catalan number:

\[C_n = \binom{2n}{n} - \binom{2n}{n+1} = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.\]

Double-Boundary Reflection: Kelvin’s Method of Images (#220)

Now suppose the path from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\) must stay strictly between two parallel forbidden lines:

\[L_1: y = x + a \quad\text{and}\quad L_2: y = x - b \qquad (a, b \ge 1,\ -b < Y - X < a).\]

The Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • Diagnostic Signal: You need to count paths of length \(N = X + Y\) confined to a strip of width \(H = a + b - 2\) between two parallel diagonal barriers \(y = x + a\) and \(y = x - b\), where \(N\) can be up to \(10^7\) and \(H\) is moderate or large.
  • Why Naive Subtraction Over-Subtracts: If we simply subtract paths that hit \(L_1\) (ending at \(r_1(X, Y)\)) and paths that hit \(L_2\) (ending at \(r_2(X, Y)\)), a path that hits \(L_1\) and then hits \(L_2\) is subtracted twice!
  • Reverse-Engineering the Image Orbit (\(D_\infty\)):
    1. For any path \(P\), trace the sequence of mirrors it touches (1 for \(L_1\), 2 for \(L_2\)), and compress adjacent identical letters (so 1, 1, 2, 2, 1 becomes the reduced word 121 of alternating mirror hits).
    2. Define the two affine reflections across \(L_1\) and \(L_2\): \[r_1(x, y) = (y - a,\, x + a), \qquad r_2(x, y) = (y + b,\, x - b).\]
    3. By the same suffix-reflection bijection, paths from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\) whose compressed hit sequence starts with 1... are in bijection with all unconstrained paths from \((0, 0)\) to \(r_1(X, Y)\).
    4. What if a path has compressed hit sequence starting with 12... (hitting \(L_1\) first, then \(L_2\))? After reflecting its suffix across \(L_1\), the mirror \(L_2\) is carried to its image across \(L_1\), or equivalently, reflecting the original endpoint first by \(r_2\) and then by \(r_1\) counts paths whose compressed hit sequence ends with ...12 (or by reversing/conjugating, paths hitting at least \(k\) alternating mirrors map bijectively to unconstrained paths from \((0, 0)\) to \((r_1 r_2 \dots)(X, Y)\)).
    5. Let us compute the composite of two reflections \(T = r_2 \circ r_1\): \[T(x, y) = r_2(y - a,\, x + a) = \bigl((x + a) + b,\ (y - a) - b\bigr) = (x + S,\ y - S),\] where \(S = a + b \ge 2\) is the period (strip pitch) between the two mirrors! Similarly, \[T^{-1}(x, y) = (r_1 \circ r_2)(x, y) = r_1(y + b,\, x - b) = (x - S,\ y + S).\]

Let us verify this inclusion-exclusion telescoping directly on the compressed hit word \(w(P) \in \{\epsilon, 1, 2, 12, 21, 121, 212, \dots\}\) of any unconstrained path \(P\) from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\):

  • For any alternating word \(u\) of length \(\ell \ge 1\), the set of paths \(P\) whose compressed hit word \(w(P)\) has \(u\) as a prefix has cardinality equal to the number of unconstrained paths from \((0, 0)\) to the reflected endpoint \(u^{-1}(X, Y)\):
    • Even number of reflections (\(\ell = 2k\), \(k \ge 1\)): \[(r_2 r_1)^k(X, Y) = (X + kS,\ Y - kS), \qquad (r_1 r_2)^k(X, Y) = (X - kS,\ Y + kS).\]
    • Odd number of reflections (\(\ell = 2k + 1\), \(k \ge 0\)): \[r_1(r_2 r_1)^k(X, Y) = r_1(X + kS,\ Y - kS) = (Y - kS - a,\ X + kS + a),\] \[r_2(r_1 r_2)^k(X, Y) = r_2(X - kS,\ Y + kS) = (Y + kS + b,\ X - kS - b).\]

If a path \(P\) has compressed hit word \(w(P)\) of length \(L \ge 1\), then \(w(P)\) has exactly one prefix of each length \(\ell \in \{1, 2, \dots, L\}\) and zero prefixes of length \(\ell > L\). Therefore, in the alternating sum over all words \(u\):

\[1 + \sum_{\ell = 1}^{\infty} (-1)^\ell \#\{\text{prefixes of } w(P) \text{ of length } \ell\} = 1 + \sum_{\ell = 1}^{L} (-1)^\ell = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } L = 0 \text{ (valid path)}, \\ 0 & \text{if } L \ge 1 \text{ (bad path)}. \end{cases}\]

Every bad path cancels out completely! Combining the even shifts \((X \pm kS, Y \mp kS)\) and odd reflections \((Y - kS - a, X + kS + a)\) into a single index \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\) yields Kelvin’s Alternating Image Formula:

\[N_{\text{strip}}(X, Y; a, b) = \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \left[ \binom{X + Y}{X + k(a + b)} - \binom{X + Y}{X + k(a + b) + a} \right].\]

Since \(\binom{X+Y}{m} = 0\) whenever \(m < 0\) or \(m > X + Y\), only indices \(k\) with \(-\frac{X}{a+b} \le k \le \frac{Y}{a+b}\) contribute nonzero terms—at most \(\frac{X+Y}{a+b} + 2\) terms in total!

Concrete Hand-Checkable Trace (\(N = 6\) Dyck Walks in \(0 \le y \le 2\))

Let us count Dyck paths of length \(2n = 6\) (\((X, Y) = (3, 3)\)) starting at \((0, 0)\) and staying inside the strip \(0 \le y_{\text{height}} \le H = 2\).

  • Wall parameters: Starting at height \(0\), the floor \(y_{\text{height}} \ge 0\) forbids \(L_1: y = x + 1\) (\(a = 1\)), and the ceiling \(y_{\text{height}} \le 2\) forbids \(L_2: y = x - 3\) (\(b = 3\)). Thus the period is \(S = a + b = 4\).
  • Direct Enumeration: Of the \(C_3 = 5\) Dyck paths of length \(6\), only the single path +++--- reaches height \(3 > 2\). The remaining \(5 - 1 = 4\) paths (++-+--, ++--+-, +-++--, +-+-+-) stay within \(0 \le y_{\text{height}} \le 2\).
  • Kelvin’s Formula Evaluation at \(X = 3, Y = 3, a = 1, b = 3, S = 4\):
    • \(k = -1\): \(\binom{6}{3 - 4} - \binom{6}{3 - 4 + 1} = \binom{6}{-1} - \binom{6}{0} = 0 - 1 = -1\).
    • \(k = 0\): \(\binom{6}{3} - \binom{6}{3 + 1} = 20 - 15 = 5\).
    • \(k = +1\): \(\binom{6}{3 + 4} - \binom{6}{3 + 4 + 1} = 0 - 0 = 0\).
    • Total sum: \(-1 + 5 + 0 = 4\). Matches our hand count!
WarningFailure Mode & Remedy: Why Geometric Reflection Fails for Asymmetric Steps (\(\{+1, -2\}\))

The Pitfall: Suppose a walk takes steps from \(\mathcal{S} = \{+1, -2\}\) and must stay in \(y_t \ge 0\) (as in Gambler’s Walk, #140). A common mistake is to attempt an André reflection across \(y = -1\) by swapping \(+1\) and \(-2\) steps after the first time the walk drops below \(0\).

Why It Breaks Down in Two Independent Ways:

  1. Boundary Overshoot (Non-Unique Hitting Height): A \(-2\) step from height \(y = 1\) lands at \(y = -1\), whereas a \(-2\) step from height \(y = 0\) jumps completely over \(y = -1\) and lands at \(y = -2\)! Because bad paths can first exit \([0, \infty)\) at either \(y = -1\) or \(y = -2\), there is no single mirror height where all bad suffixes originate.
  2. Step-Length Asymmetry: Even if all bad paths exited at \(y = -1\), swapping remaining \(+1\) steps with \(-2\) steps changes the net displacement per step in an asymmetric way that does not map to a fixed target endpoint \((X', Y')\).

The Remedy: Whenever the step set is asymmetric (\(\{-k, \dots, +m\}\) with \(k \ge 2\) or \(m \ge 2\)), abandon geometric reflection and use the Bivariate Kernel Method (Regime C, Section 15.3) or Raney’s Cycle Lemma (Section 15.4, when \(k = 1\)).

NoteAlgorithm Contract: Kelvin’s Method of Images (strip_lattice_walks)
  • Input & Exact Index Boundaries: Target grid coordinates \((X, Y)\) with \(X, Y \ge 0\), forbidden upper line \(L_1: y = x + a\) (\(a \ge 1\)), forbidden lower line \(L_2: y = x - b\) (\(b \ge 1\)) with \(-b < Y - X < a\), and precomputed factorials \(\operatorname{fact}[0 \dots N], \operatorname{inv\_fact}[0 \dots N]\) for \(N = X + Y\); summation index \(k \in \left[-\left\lfloor \frac{X + a}{a + b} \right\rfloor,\, \left\lfloor \frac{Y}{a + b} \right\rfloor\right]\).
  • Preconditions: Steps must be unit grid steps \((1, 0)\) and \((0, 1)\) (equivalently, 1D steps \(\{+1, -1\}\), or \(\{+1, 0, -1\}\) after conditioning on the number of \(0\)-steps). Prime modulus \(p > X + Y\) so binomial coefficients \(\binom{X+Y}{m}\) are invertible via factorial tables.
  • Postconditions (Output): Returns the number of monotone grid walks from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y)\) staying strictly between \(L_1\) and \(L_2\), modulo \(p\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O\left(\frac{X + Y}{a + b} + 1\right)\) time per query after \(O(X + Y)\) factorial precomputation; \(O(X + Y)\) space.
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: Every path whose compressed sequence of mirror hits has length \(L \ge 1\) possesses a unique prefix of each length \(\ell \in \{1, \dots, L\}\), so its net weight across the \(D_\infty\) orbit is \(\sum_{\ell=0}^L (-1)^\ell = 0\), leaving weight \(+1\) exclusively for valid strip paths (\(L = 0\)).

15.2 Rigorous Algebraic Grounding in the Formal Laurent Ring \((K[y, y^{-1}])[[x]]\)

When steps are weighted, asymmetric (\(\mathcal{S} = \{-k, \dots, +m\}\)), or subject to general boundary rules, we encode the walk in a bivariate generating function

\[F(x, y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n \left( \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} f_{n, j} y^j \right),\]

where \(x\) marks time (number of steps \(n\)) and \(y\) marks spatial height (\(j\)), so \([x^n y^j] F(x, y) = f_{n, j}\) is the total weight of valid \(n\)-step walks ending at height \(j\).

Because the step set \(\mathcal{S} = \{-k, \dots, +m\}\) contains negative steps, the step generator

\[S(y) = \sum_{s = -k}^{m} w_s y^s \in K[y, y^{-1}] \qquad (w_{-k} \neq 0,\ w_m \neq 0)\]

contains negative powers \(y^{-1}, \dots, y^{-k}\). In competitive programming editorials, it is common to see the step equation written down and a root \(y = y_0(x)\) plugged into \(F(x, y)\) without asking: In what ring does \(F(x, y)\) live, and why is plugging a power series \(y_0(x)\) into an infinite series in \(y\) legal for some roots and illegal for others? Getting this distinction wrong is the #1 source of nonsensical equations in the kernel method.

Why \(F(x, y)\) Lives in \((K[y, y^{-1}])[[x]]\) (Not \(K[[x, y, y^{-1}]]\))

Notice that the ring of formal doubly-infinite series \(\sum_{n, j \in \mathbb{Z}} c_{n, j} x^n y^j\) is not a ring: multiplying \(\left(\sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} y^j\right)^2\) would require summing \(\sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} 1 = \infty\) for the coefficient of \(y^0\).

Why does our walk series \(F(x, y)\) avoid this trap?

  • Finite Reach at Fixed Time \(n\): At time \(n = 0\), the walk starts at a single height \(s\) (or a finite initial distribution \(u_0(y) \in K[y]\)). Each step changes the height by at most \(\max(k, m)\). Therefore, at any fixed time \(n \ge 0\), a walk can only reach heights \(j\) in the finite interval \([s - nk,\, s + nm]\).
  • Consequently, the coefficient of \(x^n\) in \(F(x, y)\), \[P_n(y) = [x^n] F(x, y) = \sum_{j = s - nk}^{s + nm} f_{n, j} y^j \in K[y, y^{-1}],\] is a finite Laurent polynomial in \(y\)!
  • When the walk is further restricted to the non-negative half-line \(j \ge 0\), every negative power of \(y\) vanishes from \(F(x, y)\), so \(P_n(y) = \sum_{j=0}^{s + nm} f_{n, j} y^j \in K[y]\) is an ordinary polynomial in \(y\) of degree at most \(s + nm\), and \[F(x, y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n(y) x^n \in K[y][[x]].\]

The Substitution Law: Small Roots vs. Large Roots

After clearing the denominator \(y^k\) from \(S(y)\), we define the polynomial kernel

\[K(x, y) = y^k\bigl(1 - x S(y)\bigr) = y^k - x \sum_{s = -k}^{m} w_s y^{k + s} \in K[x, y].\]

Viewed as a polynomial in \(y\) over the base field \(K((x))\) (or its algebraic closure of Puiseux series \(\bigcup_{d \ge 1} \overline{K}((x^{1/d}))\)), \(K(x, y)\) has degree \(k + m\) in \(y\), so it has \(k + m\) roots \(y_1(x), \dots, y_{k+m}(x)\):

  1. The \(k\) Small Branches (\(y_1(x), \dots, y_k(x)\)): Setting \(x = 0\) in \(K(x, y) = 0\) gives \(y^k = 0\). More precisely, balancing the lowest-degree terms \(y^k \approx w_{-k} x\) shows that exactly \(k\) roots satisfy \[\operatorname{val}_x(y_i(x)) = \frac{1}{k} > 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \lim_{x \to 0} y_i(x) = 0 \qquad (i = 1, \dots, k).\] For \(k = 1\), there is a unique small root \(y_0(x) = w_{-1} x + \dots \in x K[[x]]\) with \(\operatorname{val}_x(y_0) = 1\).
  2. The \(m\) Large Branches (\(y_{k+1}(x), \dots, y_{k+m}(x)\)): Balancing the highest-degree terms \(y^k \approx w_m x y^{k+m}\), i.e., \(y^m \approx \frac{1}{w_m x}\), shows that the remaining \(m\) roots blow up as \(x \to 0\): \[\operatorname{val}_x(y_{\text{large}}(x)) = -\frac{1}{m} < 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad y_{\text{large}}(x) \sim (w_m x)^{-1/m}.\]

Now let us check when substituting a root \(y = \eta(x)\) into \(F(x, y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n \sum_{j=0}^{\deg P_n} f_{n, j} y^j\) is \(x\)-adically convergent (meaning only finitely many pairs \((n, j)\) contribute to any fixed power \([x^r]\)):

  • Case 1: Substituting a Small Root (\(\operatorname{val}_x(\eta) = 1/k > 0\)) on \([0, \infty)\): The \(x\)-adic valuation of the \((n, j)\) summand \(f_{n, j} x^n \eta(x)^j\) is \[\operatorname{val}_x\bigl(x^n \eta(x)^j\bigr) = n + \frac{j}{k} \ge n \qquad (\text{since } j \ge 0).\] To compute \([x^r] F(x, \eta(x))\), only terms with \(n \le r\) (and \(0 \le j \le rk\)) can contribute! The sum is finite at every degree \(r\), so substituting any small root \(\eta(x)\) into \(F(x, y) \in K[y][[x]]\) is always 100% rigorous.
  • Case 2: Substituting a Large Root (\(\operatorname{val}_x(\eta) = -1/m < 0\)) on \([0, \infty)\) — ILLEGAL!: On the half-line \([0, \infty)\), at time \(n\) a walk taking \(n\) steps of \(+m\) reaches height \(j = nm\) with weight \(f_{n, nm} = w_m^n \neq 0\). The \(x\)-adic valuation of the top summand \(f_{n, nm} x^n \eta(x)^{nm}\) is \[\operatorname{val}_x\bigl(x^n \eta(x)^{nm}\bigr) = n + nm\left(-\frac{1}{m}\right) = 0 \quad \text{for every } n \ge 0.\] Infinitely many terms \(n = 0, 1, 2, \dots\) all pile up at degree \(x^0\)! Thus \(F(x, y_{\text{large}}(x))\) diverges in \(K((x))\) on the half-line \([0, \infty)\).
  • Case 3: Substituting a Large Root (\(\operatorname{val}_x(\eta) = -1/m < 0\)) on a Bounded Strip \([0, H]\) — LEGAL!: When the walk is confined to a strip \(0 \le j \le H\), the polynomial \(P_n(y) = \sum_{j=0}^H f_{n, j} y^j\) has degree bounded by \(H\) independently of \(n\). Therefore, the valuation of the \((n, j)\) summand satisfies \[\operatorname{val}_x\bigl(x^n \eta(x)^j\bigr) = n - \frac{j}{m} \ge n - \frac{H}{m} \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} +\infty.\] Equivalently, \(F(x, y) = \sum_{j=0}^H F_j(x) y^j\) is just a finite polynomial of degree \(H\) in \(y\) with coefficients \(F_j(x) \in K[[x]]\), into which we can substitute any element of the Laurent/Puiseux field \(\overline{K}((x^{1/m}))\), small or large!
ImportantThe Fundamental Dichotomy of the Kernel Method
  1. Half-Line \([0, \infty)\) (\(\deg_y P_n = nm \to \infty\)): You may substitute only the \(k\) small roots (\(\operatorname{val}_x(y_i) > 0\)) into \(F(x, y)\). You get \(k\) equations, matching the \(k\) unknown bottom boundary series \(F_0(x), \dots, F_{k-1}(x)\).
  2. Bounded Strip \([0, H]\) (\(\deg_y P_n \le H\) fixed): \(F(x, y) = \sum_{j=0}^H F_j(x) y^j\) is a finite polynomial in \(y\), so you may substitute all \(k + m\) roots (both the \(k\) small roots and the \(m\) large roots) into \(F(x, y)\). You get \(k + m\) equations, matching the \(k\) bottom boundary series and \(m\) top boundary series!

15.3 The 1D Bivariate Kernel Method (Regimes A, B, and C)

Let us now turn the algebraic machinery of Section 15.2 into a step-by-step recipe across all three regimes.

Regime A: Half-Line \([0, \infty)\) with One Negative Step (\(k = 1\))

Suppose our step set has minimum step \(-1\), so the step Laurent polynomial is

\[S(y) = w_{-1} y^{-1} + w_0 + w_1 y + w_2 y^2 + \dots + w_m y^m \qquad (w_{-1} \neq 0).\]

Let \(f_{n, j}\) be the weight of walks of length \(n\) staying in \(y \ge 0\) and ending at height \(j \ge 0\), starting from an initial height distribution \(u_0(y) = \sum_{j \ge 0} f_{0, j} y^j \in K[y]\) (typically \(u_0(y) = y^s\) for a walk starting at height \(s\)).

Single-Step Derivation of the Fundamental Functional Equation

How does a valid walk of length \(n\) (\(n \ge 1\)) arise from a valid walk of length \(n - 1\)?

  1. Taking an unconstrained step multiplies the height polynomial \(P_{n-1}(y) = \sum_{j \ge 0} f_{n-1, j} y^j\) by \(S(y)\).
  2. Which steps from a valid height \(j \ge 0\) violate the floor \(y \ge 0\)? Since the minimum step in \(S(y)\) is \(-1\), a walk at height \(j \ge 1\) stays at height \(\ge j - 1 \ge 0\) regardless of which step it takes. The only forbidden transition from \(j \ge 0\) is taking a \(-1\) step (weight \(w_{-1} y^{-1}\)) from height \(j = 0\) (which has generating coefficient \([y^0] P_{n-1}(y) = f_{n-1, 0}\)), landing at height \(-1\) with term \(w_{-1} f_{n-1, 0} y^{-1}\).
  3. Subtracting this single forbidden transition gives the exact recurrence for \(n \ge 1\): \[P_n(y) = S(y) P_{n-1}(y) - w_{-1} f_{n-1, 0} y^{-1}.\]
  4. Multiply by \(x^n\) and sum over all \(n \ge 1\), adding the initial state \(P_0(y) = u_0(y)\) at \(n = 0\): \[F(x, y) - u_0(y) = x S(y) F(x, y) - w_{-1} x y^{-1} F(x, 0),\] where \(F_0(x) = F(x, 0) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} f_{n, 0} x^n\) is the generating function of walks ending at height \(0\).
  5. Collect \(F(x, y)\) on the left and multiply both sides by \(y\) to clear \(y^{-1}\): \[\underbrace{y\bigl(1 - x S(y)\bigr)}_{K(x, y)} F(x, y) = y u_0(y) - w_{-1} x F(x, 0).\]

The Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

  • Diagnostic Signal: One linear equation in two unknown functions: the bivariate series \(F(x, y) \in K[y][[x]]\) and the univariate boundary series \(F(x, 0) \in K[[x]]\).
  • Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad:
    1. Notice that the right-hand side \(y u_0(y) - w_{-1} x F(x, 0)\) depends on \(F\) only through \(F(x, 0)\) (which does not depend on \(y\)), whereas the left-hand side multiplies \(F(x, y)\) by the kernel \(K(x, y) = y - x \cdot y S(y)\).
    2. If we find a formal power series \(y = y_0(x)\) with \(\operatorname{val}_x(y_0) \ge 1\) such that \(K(x, y_0(x)) = 0\), then substituting \(y = y_0(x)\) is \(x\)-adically legal (by Section 15.2) and annihilates the entire left-hand side \(K(x, y_0(x)) F(x, y_0(x)) = 0\)!
    3. Setting the right-hand side to \(0\) at \(y = y_0(x)\) immediately isolates the boundary series: \[y_0(x) u_0(y_0(x)) - w_{-1} x F(x, 0) = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad F(x, 0) = \frac{y_0(x) u_0(y_0(x))}{w_{-1} x}.\]
    4. Better yet, once \(F(x, 0)\) is known, substituting it back into the numerator shows that \(y u_0(y) - \frac{y_0(x) u_0(y_0(x))}{1}\) vanishes at \(y = y_0(x)\), so \((y - y_0(x))\) divides the numerator cleanly as a polynomial in \(y\), giving the full bivariate generating function \(F(x, y)\)!

Concrete Hand-Checkable Trace: Dyck and Motzkin Walks Starting at \(s = 0\)

Let \(u_0(y) = 1\) (start at height \(s = 0\)) and \(S(y) = w_{-1} y^{-1} + w_0 + w_1 y\) (Motzkin steps with weights \(w_{-1}, w_0, w_1\), or Dyck steps when \(w_0 = 0, w_{-1} = w_1 = 1\)).

  1. The Kernel Equation: \[K(x, y) = y - x\bigl(w_{-1} + w_0 y + w_1 y^2\bigr) = -w_1 x y^2 + (1 - w_0 x)y - w_{-1} x = 0.\]
  2. Selecting the Small Root \(y_0(x)\): The quadratic formula gives two roots in \(K((x))\): \[y_{\pm}(x) = \frac{1 - w_0 x \mp \sqrt{(1 - w_0 x)^2 - 4 w_{-1} w_1 x^2}}{2 w_1 x}.\] Taking the \(+\) sign gives \(y_1(x) \sim \frac{1}{w_1 x}\) (\(\operatorname{val}_x(y_1) = -1\), the large root, illegal on \([0, \infty)\)!). Taking the \(-\) sign gives the unique small root: \[y_0(x) = \frac{1 - w_0 x - \sqrt{(1 - w_0 x)^2 - 4 w_{-1} w_1 x^2}}{2 w_1 x} = w_{-1} x + w_{-1} w_0 x^2 + w_{-1}(w_0^2 + w_{-1} w_1) x^3 + \dots\]
  3. Boundary Series \(F(x, 0)\): Since \(u_0(y) = 1\), \[F(x, 0) = \frac{y_0(x)}{w_{-1} x} = \frac{1 - w_0 x - \sqrt{(1 - w_0 x)^2 - 4 w_{-1} w_1 x^2}}{2 w_{-1} w_1 x^2}.\] For standard Dyck paths (\(w_{-1} = w_1 = 1, w_0 = 0\)), this is \(C(x) = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - 4x^2}}{2x^2} = 1 + x^2 + 2x^4 + 5x^6 + \dots\).
  4. Full Bivariate Series \(F(x, y)\) (Walks Ending at Any Height \(j\)): Since \(K(x, y) = -w_1 x(y - y_0(x))(y - y_1(x))\) and \(w_{-1} x F(x, 0) = y_0(x) = w_{-1} w_1 x^2 F(x, 0) y_1(x)^{-1}\), dividing \(y - y_0(x)\) by \(K(x, y)\) cancels \((y - y_0(x))\) cleanly: \[F(x, y) = \frac{y - y_0(x)}{-w_1 x (y - y_0(x))(y - y_1(x))} = \frac{1}{w_1 x (y_1(x) - y)} = \frac{F(x, 0)}{1 - w_1 x F(x, 0) y},\] where we used Vieta’s product \(y_0(x) y_1(x) = \frac{w_{-1}}{w_1} \implies \frac{1}{w_1 x y_1(x)} = \frac{y_0(x)}{w_{-1} x} = F(x, 0)\). Expanding the geometric series in \(y\) immediately gives the generating function of walks starting at \(0\) and ending at height \(j \ge 0\): \[[y^j] F(x, y) = F(x, 0) \cdot \bigl(w_1 x F(x, 0)\bigr)^j = w_1^j x^j F(x, 0)^{j+1}.\]

Variant: The Absorbing / Clamping Boundary Operator \(y^{-1} \mapsto y^0\) (#128)

What if stepping to \(-1\) from height \(0\) is not killed, but instead clamped back to height \(0\) via the transition \(j \gets \max(0, j + s)\)? As we will see in Problem Adv-1 (#128 CF 1810G), this exact transition governs the maximum prefix sum of a walk when read in reverse!

Under the clamping transition \(y^{-1} \mapsto y^0\) at height \(0\), a \(-1\) step from \(j = 0\) replaces the invalid term \(w_{-1} f_{n-1, 0} y^{-1}\) with the clamped term \(w_{-1} f_{n-1, 0} y^0\). Thus the functional equation becomes:

\[F(x, y) - u_0(y) = x S(y) F(x, y) + w_{-1} x (y^0 - y^{-1}) F(x, 0).\]

Multiplying by \(y\) gives the clamped kernel equation:

\[y\bigl(1 - x S(y)\bigr) F(x, y) = y u_0(y) - w_{-1} x (1 - y) F(x, 0).\]

Notice that the kernel \(K(x, y) = y(1 - x S(y))\) is identical to the killed-walk kernel! Substituting the same small root \(y = y_0(x)\) annihilates the left-hand side and yields:

\[F_{\text{clamped}}(x, 0) = \frac{y_0(x) u_0(y_0(x))}{w_{-1} x (1 - y_0(x))}.\]

Regime B: Bounded Strip \([0, H]\) — Dual Boundaries & Chebyshev Rational Functions

Now suppose a walk with step polynomial \(S(y) = w_{-1} y^{-1} + w_0 + w_1 y\) starts at height \(s \in \{0, 1, \dots, H\}\) (\(u_0(y) = y^s\)) and is confined to the bounded strip \(0 \le j \le H\), so stepping to \(j = -1\) (from \(j = 0\)) or to \(j = H + 1\) (from \(j = H\)) kills the walk.

Single-Step Derivation of the Dual-Boundary Kernel Equation

At step \(n \ge 1\), two transitions from valid states in \([0, H]\) exit the strip:

  • A \(-1\) step from height \(0\) produces the forbidden term \(w_{-1} f_{n-1, 0} y^{-1}\).
  • A \(+1\) step from height \(H\) produces the forbidden term \(w_1 f_{n-1, H} y^{H+1}\).

Subtracting both forbidden terms and summing over \(n \ge 1\) gives:

\[\bigl(1 - x S(y)\bigr) F(x, y) = y^s - w_{-1} x y^{-1} F_0(x) - w_1 x y^{H+1} F_H(x),\]

where \(F_0(x) = [y^0] F(x, y)\) and \(F_H(x) = [y^H] F(x, y)\) are the generating functions of walks ending at the floor \(0\) and the ceiling \(H\). Multiplying by \(y\) gives:

\[\underbrace{y\bigl(1 - x S(y)\bigr)}_{K(x, y)} F(x, y) = y^{s+1} - w_{-1} x F_0(x) - w_1 x y^{H+2} F_H(x).\]

Solving the \(2 \times 2\) System via Both Roots & Chebyshev Polynomials \(Q_k(x)\)

  • Diagnostic Signal: The right-hand side has two unknown boundary series, \(F_0(x)\) and \(F_H(x)\).
  • Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: Because \(F(x, y) = \sum_{j=0}^H F_j(x) y^j \in K_{\le H}[y][[x]]\) has degree \(\le H\) in \(y\), Section 15.2 guarantees that both roots \(y_0(x)\) (small) and \(y_1(x)\) (large) of \(K(x, y) = -w_1 x y^2 + (1 - w_0 x)y - w_{-1} x = 0\) are valid substitutions! Substituting \(y = y_0(x)\) and \(y = y_1(x)\) gives a \(2 \times 2\) linear system for \(F_0(x)\) and \(F_H(x)\): \[\begin{cases} w_{-1} x F_0(x) + w_1 x y_0^{H+2} F_H(x) = y_0^{s+1}, \\ w_{-1} x F_0(x) + w_1 x y_1^{H+2} F_H(x) = y_1^{s+1}. \end{cases}\]

Subtracting the two equations eliminates \(F_0(x)\) immediately:

\[w_1 x \bigl(y_1^{H+2} - y_0^{H+2}\bigr) F_H(x) = y_1^{s+1} - y_0^{s+1} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad F_H(x) = \frac{1}{w_1 x} \cdot \frac{y_1^{s+1} - y_0^{s+1}}{y_1^{H+2} - y_0^{H+2}}.\]

To eliminate \(F_H(x)\), multiply the first equation by \(y_1^{H+2}\) and subtract the second equation multiplied by \(y_0^{H+2}\). Since the \(F_H(x)\) terms cancel identically:

\[w_{-1} x \bigl(y_1^{H+2} - y_0^{H+2}\bigr) F_0(x) = y_0^{s+1} y_1^{H+2} - y_1^{s+1} y_0^{H+2} = (y_0 y_1)^{s+1} \bigl(y_1^{H+1-s} - y_0^{H+1-s}\bigr).\]

Using Vieta’s relation \(y_0 y_1 = \frac{w_{-1}}{w_1}\) and dividing by \(w_{-1} x \bigl(y_1^{H+2} - y_0^{H+2}\bigr)\) yields:

\[F_0(x) = \frac{1}{w_{-1} x} \left(\frac{w_{-1}}{w_1}\right)^{s+1} \frac{y_1^{H+1-s} - y_0^{H+1-s}}{y_1^{H+2} - y_0^{H+2}} = \frac{w_{-1}^s}{w_1^{s+1} x} \cdot \frac{y_1^{H+1-s} - y_0^{H+1-s}}{y_1^{H+2} - y_0^{H+2}}.\]

Do we need square roots to evaluate \(\frac{y_1^k - y_0^k}{y_1 - y_0}\)? No! Because \(y_0, y_1\) are the two roots of \(y^2 - \frac{1 - w_0 x}{w_1 x} y + \frac{w_{-1}}{w_1} = 0\), the rescaled symmetric polynomials

\[Q_k(x) := (w_1 x)^k \frac{y_1^{k+1} - y_0^{k+1}}{y_1 - y_0} \in K[x] \qquad (k \ge -1)\]

satisfy the three-term Chebyshev / Fibonacci polynomial recurrence:

\[Q_{-1}(x) = 0, \qquad Q_0(x) = 1, \qquad Q_k(x) = (1 - w_0 x) Q_{k-1}(x) - w_{-1} w_1 x^2 Q_{k-2}(x) \quad (k \ge 1).\]

Notice that every \(Q_k(x)\) is a polynomial in \(x\) of degree \(\le k\) with constant term \(Q_k(0) = 1\)! Rewriting \(F_0(x)\) and \(F_H(x)\) in terms of \(Q_k(x)\) yields completely rational generating functions in \(K(x)\):

\[F_0(x) = (w_{-1} x)^s \frac{Q_{H - s}(x)}{Q_{H + 1}(x)}, \qquad F_H(x) = (w_1 x)^{H - s} \frac{Q_s(x)}{Q_{H + 1}(x)}.\]

In particular, for walks starting at \(s = 0\) and ending at \(e = 0\) in the strip \([0, H]\):

\[F_0(x) = \frac{Q_H(x)}{Q_{H+1}(x)}.\]

For standard Dyck paths (\(w_{-1} = w_1 = 1, w_0 = 0\), where \(x^2\) marks a pair of steps so replacing \(x^2 \gets z\) counts semilength \(n\)), the polynomials \(Q_k(z)\) satisfy \(Q_k(z) = Q_{k-1}(z) - z Q_{k-2}(z)\) and have the explicit binomial expansion:

\[Q_k(z) = \sum_{j = 0}^{\lfloor k / 2 \rfloor} (-1)^j \binom{k - j}{j} z^j.\]

The Catalan Residue Substitution \(z = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\) (Diagonalizing \(Q_k\))

How can we extract \([z^n] \frac{Q_H(z)}{Q_{H+1}(z)}\) (or any rational function of \(Q_k(z)\)) in \(O(n)\) time without even running polynomial inversion?

  • Diagnostic Signal: Your generating function is a ratio of Chebyshev polynomials \(Q_k(z)\) satisfying \(Q_k(z) = Q_{k-1}(z) - z Q_{k-2}(z)\) (characteristic equation \(\lambda^2 - \lambda + z = 0\)).
  • Reverse-Engineering the Change of Variables:
    1. We want the characteristic roots \(\lambda_{\pm}\) of \(\lambda^2 - \lambda + z = 0\) to be rational functions of a new formal parameter \(u\) with \(\operatorname{val}_z(u) = 1\).
    2. Set \(z = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2} = u - 2u^2 + 3u^3 - \dots\). Then the characteristic quadratic factors automatically: \[\lambda^2 - \lambda + \frac{u}{(1+u)^2} = \left(\lambda - \frac{1}{1+u}\right)\left(\lambda - \frac{u}{1+u}\right) = 0.\]
    3. Substituting \(\lambda_1 = \frac{1}{1+u}\) and \(\lambda_0 = \frac{u}{1+u}\) into \(Q_k(z) = \frac{\lambda_1^{k+1} - \lambda_0^{k+1}}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_0}\) turns \(Q_k\) into a geometric series in \(u\): \[Q_k\left(\frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\right) = \frac{\left(\frac{1}{1+u}\right)^{k+1} - \left(\frac{u}{1+u}\right)^{k+1}}{\frac{1 - u}{1 + u}} = \frac{1 - u^{k+1}}{(1 - u)(1 + u)^k}.\]
    4. Therefore, the ratio \(\frac{Q_H(z)}{Q_{H+1}(z)}\) simplifies to: \[\frac{Q_H\left(\frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\right)}{Q_{H+1}\left(\frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\right)} = (1 + u) \frac{1 - u^{H+1}}{1 - u^{H+2}}.\]
    5. By the Formal Residue Change-of-Variables Formula from Chapter 11 (\([z^n] G(z) = [z^{-1}] z^{-n-1} G(z) = [u^{-1}] z(u)^{-n-1} G(z(u)) z'(u)\)), since \(z(u) = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\) has derivative \[z'(u) = \frac{(1+u)^2 - 2u(1+u)}{(1+u)^4} = \frac{1 - u}{(1 + u)^3},\] we obtain for any series \(G(z)\): \[[z^n] G(z) = [u^{-1}] \frac{(1+u)^{2n+2}}{u^{n+1}} G\left(\frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\right) \frac{1 - u}{(1+u)^3} = [u^n] (1+u)^{2n-1} (1 - u) G\left(\frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\right).\]
    6. Plugging in \(G(z) = \frac{Q_H(z)}{Q_{H+1}(z)}\) cancels a factor of \((1+u)^{-1}\) and gives: \[[z^n] \frac{Q_H(z)}{Q_{H+1}(z)} = [u^n] (1 + u)^{2n} (1 - u) \frac{1 - u^{H+1}}{1 - u^{H+2}} = [u^n] (1+u)^{2n} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \left(u^{k(H+2)} - u^{k(H+2) + 1} - u^{(k+1)(H+2) - 1} + u^{(k+1)(H+2)}\right).\] Notice that this is algebraically identical to Kelvin’s alternating reflection sum from Section 15.1!

Regime C: Half-Line \([0, \infty)\) with \(k\) Negative Steps & Vieta’s Large-Root Trick

Now suppose the step set has minimum step \(-k\) (\(k \ge 2\)) and maximum step \(+m\) (\(m \ge 1\)):

\[S(y) = \sum_{s = -k}^{m} w_s y^s \qquad (w_{-k} \neq 0,\ w_m \neq 0).\]

Why There Are \(k\) Unknown Boundary Series \(F_0(x), \dots, F_{k-1}(x)\)

When a walk is at height \(j \in \{0, 1, \dots, k-1\}\), any step \(s \in \{-k, \dots, -j-1\}\) sends the particle to a negative height \(j + s < 0\), which must be subtracted off. Summing over \(n \ge 1\) and multiplying by \(y^k\) gives the general \(k\)-boundary kernel equation:

\[\underbrace{y^k\bigl(1 - x S(y)\bigr)}_{K(x, y)} F(x, y) = y^k u_0(y) - x \sum_{j = 0}^{k - 1} F_j(x) \left( \sum_{s = -k}^{-j - 1} w_s y^{k + j + s} \right),\]

where \(F_j(x) = [y^j] F(x, y)\) (\(0 \le j \le k - 1\)) are the \(k\) unknown boundary series. Notice that the boundary correction polynomial

\[R(x, y) := x \sum_{j = 0}^{k - 1} F_j(x) \left( \sum_{s = -k}^{-j - 1} w_s y^{k + j + s} \right)\]

is a polynomial in \(y\) of degree at most \(k - 1\) whose coefficients are linear combinations of \(F_0(x), \dots, F_{k-1}(x)\).

Canceling the \(k\) Small Roots & Vieta’s Large-Root Bypass (\(m = 1\))

By Section 15.2, the kernel \(K(x, y) = y^k(1 - x S(y))\) has exactly \(k\) small roots \(y_1(x), \dots, y_k(x)\) with \(\operatorname{val}_x(y_i) = 1/k > 0\). Substituting each \(y = y_i(x)\) into \(K(x, y) F(x, y) = 0\) is \(x\)-adically legal and yields \(k\) equations:

\[R\bigl(x, y_i(x)\bigr) = y_i(x)^k u_0\bigl(y_i(x)\bigr) \qquad (i = 1, \dots, k).\]

When the walk starts at height \(0\) (\(u_0(y) = 1\)), the polynomial \(y^k - R(x, y)\) is a monic polynomial in \(y\) of degree \(k\) that vanishes at all \(k\) small roots \(y_1(x), \dots, y_k(x)\)! Therefore, it factors identically as:

\[y^k - x \sum_{j = 0}^{k - 1} F_j(x) \left( \sum_{s = -k}^{-j - 1} w_s y^{k + j + s} \right) = \prod_{i = 1}^{k} \bigl(y - y_i(x)\bigr).\]

  • Why Fractional Powers \(x^{1/k}\) Disappear (Galois Invariance): Even though each individual small root \(y_i(x) \in \overline{K}[[x^{1/k}]]\) involves fractional powers \(x^{1/k}\) and \(k\)-th roots of unity \(\zeta_k^i\), the coefficients of \(\prod_{i=1}^k (y - y_i(x))\) are the elementary symmetric polynomials \(e_1(y_1, \dots, y_k), \dots, e_k(y_1, \dots, y_k)\), which are invariant under permuting the \(k\) branches and therefore lie in the ordinary formal power series ring \(K[[x]]\)!
TipDiagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: Vieta’s Large-Root Trick (\(m = 1\))
  • Diagnostic Signal: A half-line walk has arbitrary negative steps \(\{-k, \dots, 0\}\) (\(k \ge 2\)) so there are \(k\) small roots \(y_1(x), \dots, y_k(x)\) living in the Puiseux ring \(\overline{K}[[x^{1/k}]]\), but the maximum positive step is \(m = +1\) (e.g., \(\mathcal{S} = \{+1, -k\}\)).
  • Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: Why compute \(k\) messy Puiseux roots \(y_1, \dots, y_k \in \overline{K}[[x^{1/k}]]\) when the kernel \(K(x, y) = y^k(1 - x S(y))\) has total degree \(k + m = k + 1\) in \(y\), meaning there is only \(m = 1\) large root \(y_{k+1}(x)\)? By setting \(y_{k+1}(x) = 1/u(x)\) with \(u(0) = 0\), the equation \(K(x, 1/u) = 0\) becomes a single ordinary formal power series equation \(u(x) = x \sum_{s=-k}^1 w_s u(x)^{1-s} \in K[[x]]\) solvable via Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11) or Newton’s method (Ch 10)! Once the single large root \(1/u(x)\) is known, Vieta’s formulas (or factoring \((1 - u(x)y)\) out of \(K(x, y)\)) determine all elementary symmetric polynomials of the \(k\) small roots—and hence all \(k\) boundary series \(F_0(x), \dots, F_{k-1}(x)\)—without ever leaving \(K[[x]]\)!
  • Vieta’s Large-Root Trick when \(m = 1\) (Steps \(\{-k, \dots, +1\}\), e.g., #140): Suppose the maximum positive step is \(m = 1\) (such as \(\mathcal{S} = \{+1, -2\}\) in Gambler’s Walk). Then \(K(x, y)\) has degree \(k + 1\) in \(y\), with \(k\) small roots \(y_1(x), \dots, y_k(x)\) and only one large root \(y_{k+1}(x)\)! Instead of computing the \(k\) small roots in \(\overline{K}[[x^{1/k}]]\), we factor \(K(x, y)\) completely: \[K(x, y) = -w_1 x \bigl(y - y_{k+1}(x)\bigr) \prod_{i = 1}^{k} \bigl(y - y_i(x)\bigr).\] How do we find the single large root \(y_{k+1}(x) \sim (w_1 x)^{-1}\)? Set \(y_{k+1}(x) = \frac{1}{u(x)}\) where \(\operatorname{val}_x(u) = 1\) (\(u(0) = 0\)). Substituting \(y = 1/u\) into \(1 - x S(1/u) = 0\) and multiplying by \(u\) gives the standard Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11) tree equation: \[u(x) = x \sum_{s = -k}^{1} w_s u(x)^{1 - s} = x\bigl(w_1 + w_0 u(x) + w_{-1} u(x)^2 + \dots + w_{-k} u(x)^{k+1}\bigr).\] Once \(u(x) \in x K[[x]]\) is found via Lagrange Inversion or Newton’s method, polynomial division of \(K(x, y)\) by \((1 - u(x) y)\) (or Vieta’s formulas relating \(y_1, \dots, y_k\) to \(y_{k+1} = 1/u(x)\)) gives every boundary series \(F_0(x), \dots, F_{k-1}(x)\) directly in terms of \(u(x)\)!

Concrete Hand-Checkable Trace: Steps \(\{+1, -2\}\) (\(k = 2, m = 1\), #140)

Let \(S(y) = b y^{-2} + a y\) (step \(+1\) has weight \(a\), step \(-2\) has weight \(b\)) and \(u_0(y) = 1\).

  1. Kernel Equation (\(k = 2\)): \[\bigl(y^2 - x(b + a y^3)\bigr) F(x, y) = y^2 - b x F_0(x) - b x y F_1(x).\]

  2. Factoring via the Single Large Root \(y_3(x) = 1/u(x)\): The kernel is \(-a x y^3 + y^2 - b x = -a x (y - y_1)(y - y_2)(y - y_3)\). Setting \(y_3 = 1/u\) into \(y_3^2 = x(b + a y_3^3)\) and multiplying by \(u^3/y_3^2 = u\) gives: \[u(x) = x\bigl(a + b u(x)^3\bigr).\]

  3. Vieta’s Formulas for \((y - y_1)(y - y_2) = y^2 - b x F_1(x) y - b x F_0(x)\): By Vieta on \(y^3 - \frac{1}{ax} y^2 + \frac{b}{a} = (y - y_1)(y - y_2)(y - 1/u) = 0\):

    • Product of roots: \(y_1 y_2 \cdot \frac{1}{u} = -\frac{b}{a} \implies -b x F_0(x) = y_1 y_2 = -\frac{b}{a} u(x) \implies \mathbf{F_0(x) = \frac{u(x)}{a x}}\).
    • Sum of roots: \(y_1 + y_2 + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{ax}\), and also \(\frac{1}{u^2} = x(b u + \frac{a}{u^3}) \cdot u^2 \implies a x + b x u^3 = u \implies \frac{1}{ax} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{b u^2}{a}\). Thus: \[b x F_1(x) = y_1 + y_2 = \frac{1}{ax} - \frac{1}{u(x)} = \frac{b u(x)^2}{a} \implies \mathbf{F_1(x) = \frac{u(x)^2}{a x}}.\]

  4. Small Coefficient Check (\(a = b = 1\)): Since \(u(x) = x(1 + u^3) = x + x^4 + 3x^7 + 12x^{10} + \dots\):

    • \(F_0(x) = \frac{u(x)}{x} = 1 + x^3 + 3x^6 + 12x^9 + \dots\). At \(m = 1\) (\(n = 3\)), \([x^3] F_0(x) = \frac{1}{4}\binom{4}{1} = 1\) (++-: heights \(0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 0\); note +-+ goes \(0 \to 1 \to -1\), which violates \(y \ge 0\)). At \(m = 2\) (\(n = 6\)), \([x^6] F_0(x) = \frac{1}{7}\binom{7}{2} = 3\) (++++--, +++-+-, ++-++-).
    • \(F_1(x) = \frac{u(x)^2}{x} = x + 2x^4 + 7x^7 + \dots\). At \(n = 1\), \(f_{1, 1} = 1\) (+); at \(n = 4\), \(f_{4, 1} = 2\) (+++-, ++-+). Every single coefficient matches!
WarningFailure Mode & Remedy: Substituting the Large Root on \([0, \infty)\) or Dropping Boundary Terms

Pitfall 1 (Plugging \(y_{\text{large}}(x)\) Directly into \(F(x, y)\) on \([0, \infty)\)): In the \(\{+1, -2\}\) walk above, a tempting shortcut is to plug the rational root \(y_3(x) = 1/u(x)\) directly into \(y^2 - b x F_0(x) - b x y F_1(x) = 0\). That gives only one equation for two unknowns (\(F_0, F_1\)), and worse, that equation is false! Why? Because on \([0, \infty)\), \(F(x, 1/u(x))\) diverges (Case 2 of Section 15.2), so \((y_3^2 - x(b + a y_3^3)) F(x, y_3)\) is \(0 \times \infty\), not \(0\). Indeed, at \(y = 1/u\), \(y^2 - b x F_1 y - b x F_0 = \frac{1}{u^2} - \frac{b u}{a} - \frac{b u}{a} \neq 0\).

The Remedy: Only the \(k\) small roots \(y_1, \dots, y_k\) are roots of the boundary polynomial \(y^k - R(x, y) = 0\). The large root \(y_{k+1} = 1/u(x)\) is used only via Vieta’s formulas (or polynomial division of \(K(x, y)\) by \(y - 1/u(x)\)) to compute the elementary symmetric polynomials of the small roots \(y_1, \dots, y_k\).

Pitfall 2 (Missing Intermediate Boundary Series \(F_1, \dots, F_{k-1}\)): When \(k \ge 2\), a walk at height \(j \in \{1, \dots, k-1\}\) can still jump below \(0\) by taking a step \(s \le -j-1\). Always include all \(k\) boundary series \(F_0(x), \dots, F_{k-1}(x)\).

15.4 Łukasiewicz Words, Raney’s Cycle Lemma & Prefix-Positive Extraction

When a 1D walk on \([0, \infty)\) has minimum step \(-1\) (\(k = 1\)) and arbitrary non-negative steps:

\[S(y) = w_{-1} y^{-1} + w_0 + w_1 y + w_2 y^2 + \dots + w_m y^m \qquad (w_{-1} \neq 0),\]

the step set \(\mathcal{S} \subseteq \{-1, 0, 1, 2, \dots\}\) is called a Łukasiewicz step set. Why are Łukasiewicz walks ubiquitous in combinatorics and tree enumeration?

The Algebraic Bridge: Kernel Small Root \(\Longleftrightarrow\) Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11)

In Regime A (\(k = 1\)), the kernel equation \(K(x, y_0(x)) = y_0(x) - x \cdot y_0(x) S(y_0(x)) = 0\) takes the exact implicit form required by Lagrange Inversion (Chapter 11):

\[y_0(x) = x \Phi\bigl(y_0(x)\bigr), \qquad \text{where } \Phi(y) = y S(y) = \sum_{s = -1}^{m} w_s y^{s + 1} \in K[y] \quad (\Phi(0) = w_{-1} \neq 0).\]

By our Regime A formula, the generating function of walks starting at \(0\), staying in \(y \ge 0\), and ending at height \(0\) is \(F_0(x) = \frac{y_0(x)}{w_{-1} x}\). More generally, what is the generating function of walks starting at \(0\), staying in \(y \ge 0\), and ending at height \(r - 1 \ge 0\) before taking one final \(-1\) step to hit \(-1\) for the very first time at step \(N\)?

A walk hits \(-1\) for the first time at step \(n = 1\) (via a single \(-1\) step) with weight \(w_{-1} x\). If a walk takes a first step of \(+s\) (\(s \ge 0\)), to hit \(-1\) for the first time overall it must independently drop by \(-1\) a total of \(s + 1\) consecutive times! Thus the generating function for hitting \(-1\) for the first time is literally \(y_0(x)\) itself (when \(w_{-1} = 1\)), and the generating function for hitting \(-r\) (\(r \ge 1\)) for the first time is \(y_0(x)^r\).

Applying the Lagrange Inversion Formula (Ch 11) to \(y_0(x)^r\) with \(y_0(x) = x \Phi(y_0(x))\) immediately extracts the \(N\)-th coefficient in closed form:

\[[x^N] y_0(x)^r = \frac{r}{N} [y^{N - r}] \Phi(y)^N = \frac{r}{N} [y^{-r}] S(y)^N.\]

The Combinatorial Bridge: Raney’s Cycle Lemma

Look at the two sides of \([x^N] y_0(x)^r = \frac{r}{N} [y^{-r}] S(y)^N\) when \(w_{-1} = 1\):

  • Right-hand side (\([y^{-r}] S(y)^N\)): The total weight of unconstrained \(N\)-step walks \((s_1, \dots, s_N)\) with \(s_i \in \{-1, 0, 1, \dots\}\) whose total sum is \(s_1 + \dots + s_N = -r\) (or by reversing the walk and negating steps, walks starting at \(0\) and ending at \(+r\) where every step is \(\le +1\), i.e., \(s'_i \in \{+1, 0, -1, -2, \dots\}\)).
  • Left-hand side (\([x^N] y_0(x)^r\)): The total weight of \(N\)-step walks \((s_1, \dots, s_N)\) shifted by \(+r\) (starting at \(r - 1 \ge 0\), staying in \(y \ge 0\), and hitting \(-1\) for the very first time at step \(N\); equivalently, under reversal, walks starting at \(0\) and ending at \(+r\) that stay strictly positive \(y'_t > 0\) for all \(1 \le t \le N\)).

Theorem 15.1 (Raney’s Cycle Lemma): Let \((a_1, a_2, \dots, a_N)\) be a sequence of integers with \(\sum_{i=1}^N a_i = r > 0\) such that every \(a_i \le 1\) (equivalently, in the downward convention, \(\sum_{i=1}^N s_i = -r < 0\) with every \(s_i \ge -1\)). Then among the \(N\) cyclic shifts of \((a_1, \dots, a_N)\), exactly \(r\) cyclic shifts have all prefix sums strictly positive (\(a_1 + \dots + a_t > 0\) for all \(1 \le t \le N\)).

Proof (Both Combinatorial and Algebraic):

  1. Combinatorial Proof (Discrete Intermediate Value Property): Extend the sequence periodically with period \(N\), so \(Y_{t + N} = Y_t + r\). Consider the right-to-left records (times \(t\) such that \(Y_{t'} > Y_t\) for all \(t' > t\)). A cyclic shift starting after index \(t\) has all strictly positive prefix sums if and only if \(t\) is a right-to-left minimum record! Because every step satisfies \(a_i \le 1\), the height \(Y_t\) can increase by at most \(+1\) at each step. Since \(Y_{t+N} = Y_t + r\) increases by \(+r\) over each period of length \(N\) and cannot skip over any integer on its way up (because steps are \(\le +1\)), the sequence of record heights increases by \(+1\) from one record to the next, meaning there are exactly \(r\) records per period of length \(N\)!
  2. Algebraic Explanation of Why Raney’s Lemma Fails for \(k \ge 2\): If the walk can jump by \(+2\) (or drop by \(-2\) in the downward convention, \(k \ge 2\)), the kernel equation \(K(x, y) = 0\) has \(k \ge 2\) small branches \(y_1(x), \dots, y_k(x)\) instead of a single branch \(y_0(x) = x \Phi(y_0(x))\). Combinatorially, a step of \(+2\) can jump straight over a height without creating a record there, so different cyclic classes of length \(N\) summing to \(+r\) can contain different numbers of valid shifts! For \(k \ge 2\), you must use Regime C of the Kernel Method (Section 15.3) rather than dividing \([y^{-r}] S(y)^N\) by \(N/r\).

Stepping Stone 1 (#105) — Natasha, Sasha and the Prefix Sums

  • Source: Codeforces 1204E — Natasha, Sasha and the Prefix Sums
  • Prerequisite Tags: Single-Wall Reflection Principle (15.1), Tail-Sum Identity, Catalan Residue Check (15.3B)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given \(n\) copies of \(+1\) and \(m\) copies of \(-1\) (\(0 \le n, m \le 2000\) in the original problem; your algorithm should run in \(O(n + m)\) time, scaling to \(n, m \le 10^7\)). For each of the \(\binom{n+m}{n}\) distinct sequences \(a = (a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{n+m}) \in \{+1, -1\}^{n+m}\) containing \(n\) ones and \(m\) minus-ones, define its maximum prefix sum as:

\[M(a) = \max\left(0,\, \max_{1 \le i \le n+m} \sum_{j=1}^{i} a_j\right).\]

Compute \(\sum_{a} M(a) \bmod 998244853\) (note that \(998244853\) is prime).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n = 2, m = 2\))

There are \(\binom{4}{2} = 6\) sequences of two \(+1\)’s (+) and two \(-1\)’s (-):

  • ++--: prefix sums \((1, 2, 1, 0) \implies M = 2\).
  • +-+-: prefix sums \((1, 0, 1, 0) \implies M = 1\).
  • +--+: prefix sums \((1, 0, -1, 0) \implies M = 1\).
  • -++-: prefix sums \((-1, 0, 1, 0) \implies M = 1\).
  • -+-+: prefix sums \((-1, 0, -1, 0) \implies M = 0\).
  • --++: prefix sums \((-1, -2, -1, 0) \implies M = 0\).

Total sum: \(2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 = 5\).

Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Instead of grouping sequences by their exact maximum \(M(a) = k\), rewrite the non-negative integer \(M(a)\) using the tail-sum identity \(M(a) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} [M(a) \ge k]\). What does the condition \(M(a) \ge k\) mean geometrically for a lattice walk starting at \((0, 0)\) and ending at height \(n - m\)?

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

A walk from \((0, 0)\) with \(n\) up-steps (\(+1\)) and \(m\) down-steps (\(-1\)) ends at height \(n - m\). It satisfies \(M(a) \ge k\) if and only if it touches or crosses the horizontal line \(y = k\) at least once.

  • If \(k \le n - m\), since the walk starts at \(0\) and ends at \(n - m \ge k\), every walk must cross \(y = k\) by the discrete intermediate value property!
  • If \(k > n - m\), reflect the suffix of the walk after the first time it hits \(y = k\) across the mirror \(y = k\). Where does the reflected walk end?
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Reflecting the suffix after hitting \(y = k\) maps the final endpoint from height \(n - m\) to height \(2k - (n - m) = n - m + 2(k - (n - m))\), which corresponds to \(n - k\) up-steps and \(m + k\) down-steps! Thus \(N_{\ge k} = \binom{n+m}{n - k}\) for \(\max(1, n - m + 1) \le k \le n\), and \(N_{\ge k} = \binom{n+m}{n}\) for \(1 \le k \le n - m\). Summing over \(1 \le k \le n\) takes \(O(n + m)\) time.

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

Step 1: Tail-Sum Decomposition

Since \(0 \le M(a) \le n\) is a non-negative integer for every sequence \(a\), we can write \(M(a) = \sum_{k=1}^n [M(a) \ge k]\). Interchanging the sum over \(a\) and the sum over \(k\) gives:

\[\sum_{a} M(a) = \sum_{k = 1}^{n} N_{\ge k},\]

where \(N_{\ge k}\) is the number of walks from \((0, 0)\) with \(n\) steps of \(+1\) and \(m\) steps of \(-1\) (ending at height \(e = n - m\)) that reach height \(y = k\) at least once.

Step 2: Single-Wall Reflection Across \(y = k\)

Fix \(k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\). There are two cases depending on whether the final height \(n - m\) already lies at or above the threshold \(k\):

  1. Case A (\(1 \le k \le n - m\)): Every walk starts at height \(0 < k\), takes unit steps \(\pm 1\), and ends at height \(n - m \ge k\). Therefore, every unconstrained walk must reach height \(k\) at least once, giving: \[N_{\ge k} = \binom{n + m}{n}.\]
  2. Case B (\(\max(1, n - m + 1) \le k \le n\)): Here the final height \(n - m\) lies strictly below \(k\). By André’s Reflection Principle (Section 15.1), for any walk that reaches height \(k\) at some first hitting time \(\tau\), reflecting the suffix after \(\tau\) across the mirror \(y = k\) negates every step after \(\tau\). Since the suffix originally changed height from \(k\) to \(n - m\) (a net change of \((n - m) - k\)), the reflected suffix changes height from \(k\) to \(k - ((n - m) - k) = 2k - n + m\). A walk of length \(n + m\) starting at \(0\) and ending at height \(2k - n + m > k\) has \(u'\) up-steps and \(d'\) down-steps satisfying \(u' + d' = n + m\) and \(u' - d' = 2k - n + m\), so: \[u' = m + k, \qquad d' = n - k.\] Because every walk from \(0\) to \(2k - n + m \ge k + 1 > k\) must cross \(y = k\), this reflection is a bijection between walks from \(0\) to \(n - m\) that touch \(y = k\) and all unconstrained walks from \(0\) to \(2k - n + m\). Hence: \[N_{\ge k} = \binom{n + m}{n - k}.\]

Step 3: Final Closed-Form Sum & Trace Verification

Combining Cases A and B gives the exact formula:

\[\sum_{a} M(a) = \max(0, n - m) \binom{n + m}{n} + \sum_{k = \max(1, n - m + 1)}^{n} \binom{n + m}{n - k}.\]

  • Verification on \(n = 2, m = 2\): Here \(\max(0, n - m) = 0\), and the sum runs over \(k = 1, 2\): \[N_{\ge 1} + N_{\ge 2} = \binom{4}{2 - 1} + \binom{4}{2 - 2} = \binom{4}{1} + \binom{4}{0} = 4 + 1 = 5.\]
  • Boundary Cases: If \(n = 0\), the sum is empty and returns \(0\). If \(m = 0\), \(\max(0, n - m) = n\), the second sum is empty, and the formula returns \(n \binom{n}{n} = n\).

Stepping Stone 2 (#070) — Ball Eat Chameleons

  • Source: AtCoder Grand Contest 021 E — Ball Eat Chameleons
  • Prerequisite Tags: Prefix-Balance Invariant, Single-Wall Reflection Principle (15.1), Greedy Matching to Lattice Walk Reduction

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

There are \(N\) chameleons, all initially Blue. You will throw \(K\) balls (\(1 \le N \le K \le 5 \times 10^5\)), each colored either Red (R) or Blue (B), one by one; each ball is eaten by one of the \(N\) chameleons of your choice. A chameleon changes color according to a strict majority rule:

  • Whenever a chameleon has eaten strictly more Red balls than Blue balls (\(\#\text{R} > \#\text{B}\)), its color becomes Red.
  • Whenever a chameleon has eaten strictly more Blue balls than Red balls (\(\#\text{B} > \#\text{R}\)), its color becomes Blue.
  • When \(\#\text{R} = \#\text{B}\), its color remains whatever it was before the last ball it ate.

Count how many of the \(2^K\) color sequences of length \(K\) in \(\{\texttt{R}, \texttt{B}\}^K\) can be distributed among the \(N\) chameleons so that all \(N\) chameleons end up Red, modulo \(998244353\).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(N = 2, K = 3\))

Let us check all \(2^3 = 8\) sequences in \(\{\texttt{R}, \texttt{B}\}^3\) for \(N = 2\) chameleons \(C_1, C_2\):

  • RRR: give R to \(C_1\), RR to \(C_2\) \(\implies\) both Red (valid).
  • RRB: give R to \(C_1\) (Red), and RB to \(C_2\) (\(C_2\) turns Red on R and stays Red on B!) \(\implies\) both Red (valid).
  • RBR: give RB to \(C_1\) (Red) and R to \(C_2\) (Red) \(\implies\) both Red (valid).
  • BRR: whoever eats the first B stays Blue, and to turn Red later it must reach \(\#\text{R} > \#\text{B}\), consuming both remaining R’s and leaving zero R’s for the other chameleon \(\implies\) invalid.
  • Sequences with \(\le 1\) R (BBR, BRB, RBB, BBB): cannot turn \(2\) chameleons Red \(\implies\) invalid.

Total valid sequences for \((N, K) = (2, 3)\): \(3\).

Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

First characterize how a single chameleon can end up Red at the end of the process. Either it ends with \(\#\text{R} > \#\text{B}\) (requiring at least \(1\) more R than B, with net excess \(\#\text{R} - \#\text{B} \ge 1\)), OR it ends with \(\#\text{R} = \#\text{B} \ge 1\) and the last ball it ate was B (so right before eating that last B, it had \(\#\text{R} = \#\text{B} + 1 > \#\text{B}\) and was Red!).

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Fix the total number of Red balls \(R\) and Blue balls \(B = K - R\).

  • If \(R < B\) or \(R < N\), it is impossible (\(0\) ways).
  • Suppose first that \(R > B\). To minimize wasted balls, each of the \(N\) chameleons either:
    • Type 1: Eats a single R (or more generally has net \(\#\text{R} - \#\text{B} \ge 1\)), or
    • Type 2: Eats exactly one R followed later by one B (RB pair, net \(\#\text{R} - \#\text{B} = 0\)). Since total net excess is \(R - B\), at most \(\min(N, R - B)\) chameleons can be Type 1, so at least \(m = N - (R - B)\) chameleons must be Type 2 (each needing a matched RB subsequence). When does a sequence of \(R\) R’s and \(B\) B’s contain at least \(m = N - (R - B)\) disjoint RB pairs?
Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

By standard parenthesis/greedy matching, a string with \(R\) R’s and \(B\) B’s contains at least \(m = N - R + B\) disjoint RB pairs if and only if at most \(B - m = R - N\) Blue balls are unmatched—meaning no prefix has \(\#\text{B} - \#\text{R} > R - N\)!

  • For \(R > B\): count grid paths from \((0, 0)\) to \((R, B)\) avoiding \(y = x + (R - N) + 1\), which by André’s Reflection Principle is \(\binom{R+B}{B} - \binom{R+B}{2R - N + 1}\).
  • What if \(R = B\)? Every chameleon with \(\#\text{R} = \#\text{B}\) must have its last ball be B, so the very last ball of the entire sequence must be B! Stripping that final B reduces \((R, R)\) directly to \((R, R - 1)\), where \(R > R - 1\).
NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

Step 1: Classifying Single-Chameleon Final States

A chameleon ends up Red if and only if its final counts \((r, b)\) of Red and Blue balls satisfy one of:

  1. Type 1 (Strict Majority): \(r - b \ge 1\). The most ball-efficient Type 1 chameleon uses \((r, b) = (1, 0)\) (and one Type 1 chameleon can absorb any leftover R and B balls as long as its total \(r - b \ge 1\)).
  2. Type 2 (Tied Majority via RB): \(r = b \ge 1\) and the last ball it eats is B. The most ball-efficient Type 2 chameleon eats exactly one R followed later by one B (RB, so \((r, b) = (1, 1)\)).

Step 2: Analyzing Fixed Counts \((R, B)\) with \(R + B = K\)

Fix the total number of Red balls \(R\) and Blue balls \(B = K - R\). Since every chameleon must end up Red, each of the \(N\) chameleons must eat at least one Red ball (\(R \ge N\)), and the total number of Red balls must be at least the total number of Blue balls (\(R \ge B\), with \(R > B\) strictly required after stripping the final Blue ball in the tied case \(R = B\)).

Case 1 (\(R > B\) and \(R \ge N\)): Suppose we choose \(t\) chameleons of Type 1 and \(N - t\) chameleons of Type 2. Since Type 1 chameleons each contribute \(\ge 1\) to \(\sum (r_i - b_i) = R - B\) and Type 2 chameleons contribute \(0\), we can have at most \(t = \min(N, R - B)\) Type 1 chameleons, leaving \(\max(0, N - (R - B))\) chameleons that must be Type 2.

  • Each of the \(m = \max(0, N - R + B)\) Type 2 chameleons requires a disjoint RB pair (R appearing before B).
  • After extracting \(m\) disjoint RB pairs, the remaining \(R - m\) Red balls and \(B - m\) Blue balls satisfy \((R - m) - (B - m) = R - B \ge t\): we can give one R to each of the \(t - 1\) first Type 1 chameleons, and dump all remaining \(R - m - (t - 1)\) Red balls and \(B - m\) Blue balls onto the last Type 1 chameleon (which gets net excess \((R - B) - (t - 1) \ge 1\), so it turns Red regardless of order!).
  • Therefore, when \(R > B\), a sequence of \(R\) R’s and \(B\) B’s is valid if and only if it contains at least \(m = N - R + B\) disjoint RB pairs!
  • By greedy parenthesis matching (treating R as \(+1\) and B as \(-1\)), the number of unmatched B’s in a sequence is \(\max_{0 \le i \le K} (\#\text{B}_{\le i} - \#\text{R}_{\le i})\). Thus at least \(m\) B’s are matched if and only if at most \(B - m = R - N\) B’s are unmatched: \[\#\text{B}_{\le i} - \#\text{R}_{\le i} \le R - N \quad \text{for all } 0 \le i \le K.\]
  • In monotone grid coordinates \((x, y) = (\#\text{R}, \#\text{B})\) from \((0, 0)\) to \((R, B)\), the forbidden line is \(L_1: y = x + (R - N + 1)\) (\(a = R - N + 1 \ge 1\)). By André’s Reflection Principle (15.1), the number of valid sequences for \((R, B)\) is: \[W(R, B) = \binom{R + B}{B} - \binom{R + B}{R + (R - N + 1)} = \binom{R + B}{B} - \binom{R + B}{2R - N + 1}.\]

Case 2 (\(R = B \ge N\)): Since \(\sum_{i=1}^N (r_i - b_i) = R - B = 0\), zero chameleons can have \(r_i - b_i < 0\), which forces \(r_i = b_i \ge 1\) for all \(N\) chameleons! That means every chameleon is Type 2, so the last ball eaten by every chameleon is B. In particular, the very last ball of the entire \(K\)-ball sequence must be B! Conversely, if the last ball of the sequence is B and the prefix of length \(K - 1\) (which has \(R\) Red balls and \(B - 1 = R - 1\) Blue balls) is valid for \((R, R - 1)\), then in that \((R, R - 1)\) distribution there is \(R - (R - 1) = 1\) Type 1 chameleon (with \((r, b) = (1, 0)\)) and \(N - 1\) Type 2 chameleons; feeding the final B to that single Type 1 chameleon turns it into the \(N\)-th Type 2 chameleon! Thus:

\[W(R, R) = W(R, R - 1) = \binom{2R - 1}{R - 1} - \binom{2R - 1}{2R - N + 1}.\]

Step 3: Verification on \((N, K) = (2, 3)\) & Complexity

For \(N = 2, K = 3\), the valid pairs \((R, B)\) with \(R \ge 2\) and \(R \ge B\) are:

  • \((R, B) = (2, 1)\) (\(R > B\)): \(W(2, 1) = \binom{3}{1} - \binom{3}{4 - 2 + 1} = 3 - \binom{3}{3} = 3 - 1 = 2\) (RRB, RBR).
  • \((R, B) = (3, 0)\) (\(R > B\)): \(W(3, 0) = \binom{3}{0} - \binom{3}{6 - 2 + 1} = 1 - 0 = 1\) (RRR).

Total: \(2 + 1 = 3\). Summing \(W(R, K - R)\) over \(\max(N, \lceil K/2 \rceil) \le R \le K\) takes \(O(K)\) time and \(O(K)\) space.

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: Number of chameleons \(N \ge 1\), total balls \(K \ge N\) (\(1 \le N \le K \le 5 \times 10^5 < p\)), Red ball count \(R \in \{\max(N, \lceil K/2 \rceil), \dots, K\}\) with \(B = K - R\) (replacing \(B\) by \(R - 1\) when \(R = B = K/2\)), and prime modulus \(p = 998244353\).
  • Postconditions (Output): Returns \(\sum_{R = \max(N, \lceil K/2 \rceil)}^{K} W(R, K - R) \bmod 998244353\), the number of valid sequences in \(\{\texttt{R}, \texttt{B}\}^K\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(K)\) time and \(O(K)\) space via factorial precomputation up to \(K\).
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: For each fixed \((R, B')\) with \(R > B'\) (where \(B' = B - [R = B]\)), \(W(R, B') = \binom{R + B'}{B'} - \binom{R + B'}{2R - N + 1}\) counts monotone paths to \((R, B')\) staying strictly below \(L_1: y = x + (R - N + 1)\).

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Given positive integers \(N, M \le 10^6\), count the number of \(N \times M\) integer matrices \(A = (A_{i, j})\) (\(1 \le i \le N\), \(1 \le j \le M\)) with entries \(A_{i, j} \in \{0, 1, \dots, M\}\) satisfying:

  1. Row Strict Increase: \(A_{i, j} < A_{i, j + 1}\) for all \(1 \le i \le N\) and \(1 \le j < M\).
  2. Diagonal Inequality: \(A_{i, j} < A_{i - 1, j + 1}\) for all \(2 \le i \le N\) and \(1 \le j < M\).

Output the number of valid matrices modulo \(10^9 + 7\).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(N = 2, M = 2\))

Each row of length \(M = 2\) is a strictly increasing sequence chosen from \(\{0, 1, 2\}\) (\(3\) elements), so each row is uniquely determined by the single element \(p_i \in \{0, 1, 2\}\) that it omits:

  • \(p_i = 0 \implies \text{row } i = (1, 2)\).
  • \(p_i = 1 \implies \text{row } i = (0, 2)\).
  • \(p_i = 2 \implies \text{row } i = (0, 1)\).

There are \(3 \times 3 = 9\) pairs of rows \((p_1, p_2) \in \{0, 1, 2\}^2\). The diagonal condition for \(N = 2, M = 2\) is simply \(A_{2, 1} < A_{1, 2}\):

  • Since \(A_{2, 1} \in \{0, 1\}\) and \(A_{1, 2} \in \{1, 2\}\), the only way \(A_{2, 1} < A_{1, 2}\) fails is when \(A_{2, 1} = 1\) and \(A_{1, 2} = 1\), which happens exclusively for \((p_1, p_2) = (2, 0)\) (row 1 is \((0, 1)\), row 2 is \((1, 2)\)).

Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

Row \(i\) has \(M\) strictly increasing integers drawn from \(\{0, 1, \dots, M\}\), which has size \(M + 1\). Therefore, row \(i\) omits exactly one value \(p_i \in \{0, 1, \dots, M\}\):

\[A_{i, j} = \begin{cases} j - 1 & \text{if } j \le p_i, \\ j & \text{if } j > p_i. \end{cases}\]

Translate the diagonal condition \(A_{i, j} < A_{i-1, j+1}\) (\(1 \le j < M\)) into an inequality between \(p_i\) and \(p_{i-1}\)!

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Since \(A_{i, j} \in \{j-1, j\}\) and \(A_{i-1, j+1} \in \{j, j+1\}\), the strict inequality \(A_{i, j} < A_{i-1, j+1}\) holds unless \(A_{i, j} = j\) (meaning \(j > p_i\)) and \(A_{i-1, j+1} = j\) (meaning \(j + 1 \le p_{i-1}\)). No such \(j \in \{1, \dots, M-1\}\) exists if and only if \(p_i + 1 \ge p_{i-1}\), i.e.:

\[p_i \ge p_{i-1} - 1 \quad \text{and} \quad 0 \le p_i \le M \qquad (1 \le i \le N).\]

To turn \(p_i \ge p_{i-1} - 1\) into a weakly increasing coordinate \(x_1 \le x_2 \le \dots \le x_N\), set \(x_i = p_i + i\). Then:

\[1 \le x_1 \le x_2 \le \dots \le x_N \le N + M \quad\text{and}\quad i \le x_i \le i + M \quad (1 \le i \le N).\]

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Encode \((x_1, \dots, x_N)\) as a monotone grid path from \((0, 0)\) to \((N + M + 1, N)\) whose \(i\)-th vertical step (from \(y = i - 1\) to \(y = i\)) occurs at abscissa \(x = x_i\).

  • The condition \(i \le x_i \le i + M\) means the point \((x_i, i - 1)\) and \((x_i, i)\) never touch \(L_1: y = x + 1\) (\(a = 1\)) or \(L_2: y = x - (M + 2)\) (\(b = M + 2\)), and choosing the target endpoint \((X, Y) = (N + M + 1, N)\) (so \((N + M + 1, N - 1) \in L_2\)) automatically forces the last step to be horizontal (\((N+M, N) \to (N+M+1, N)\)), ensuring \(x_N \le N + M\)!
  • Apply Kelvin’s Method of Images (15.1) with \(X = N + M + 1, Y = N, a = 1, b = M + 2\) (\(S = M + 3\)) in \(O\left(\frac{N+M}{M}\right)\) time!

Step 1: Omitted-Value Encoding of Rows

Each row \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) is a strictly increasing \(M\)-tuple from \(\{0, 1, \dots, M\}\), so it is uniquely parameterized by the single omitted integer \(p_i \in \{0, 1, \dots, M\}\):

\[A_{i, j} = j - 1 + [j > p_i] \qquad (1 \le j \le M).\]

For \(2 \le i \le N\) and \(1 \le j < M\), we need \(A_{i, j} < A_{i-1, j+1}\), which reads:

\[j - 1 + [j > p_i] < j + [j + 1 > p_{i-1}] \iff [j > p_i] \le [j + 1 > p_{i-1}].\]

This holds for all \(1 \le j < M\) if and only if there is no \(j\) with \(p_i < j \le p_{i-1} - 1\), which is equivalent to:

\[p_i \ge p_{i-1} - 1 \qquad (2 \le i \le N), \qquad 0 \le p_i \le M \qquad (1 \le i \le N).\]

Step 2: Reduction to Monotone Grid Walks in a Strip

Define \(x_i = p_i + i\) for \(1 \le i \le N\). Then the two conditions become:

  1. Monotonicity: \(p_i \ge p_{i-1} - 1 \iff x_i \ge x_{i-1}\), so \(x_1 \le x_2 \le \dots \le x_N\).
  2. Strip Bounds: \(0 \le p_i \le M \iff i \le x_i \le i + M\) for every \(1 \le i \le N\).

Consider monotone grid walks from \((0, 0)\) to \((X, Y) = (N + M + 1, N)\) using East steps \((1, 0)\) and North steps \((0, 1)\). Any such walk takes \(N\) North steps at abscissas \(0 \le x_1 \le x_2 \le \dots \le x_N \le N + M + 1\), where the \(i\)-th North step goes from \((x_i, i - 1)\) to \((x_i, i)\).

  • The lower bound \(x_i \ge i\) holds for all \(1 \le i \le N\) if and only if \((x_i, i)\) never lies on or above the forbidden line \[L_1: y = x + 1 \qquad (a = 1).\]
  • The upper bound \(x_i \le i + M\) holds for all \(1 \le i \le N\) if and only if \((x_i, i - 1)\) never lies on or below the forbidden line \[L_2: y = x - (M + 2) \qquad (b = M + 2).\] Notice the clever choice of final abscissa \(X = N + M + 1\): the point \((N + M + 1, N - 1)\) lies directly on \(L_2\) (since \((N - 1) = (N + M + 1) - (M + 2)\))! Therefore, any path reaching \((N + M + 1, N)\) without touching \(L_2\) cannot arrive from \((N + M + 1, N - 1)\), meaning its \(N\)-th North step must occur at \(x_N \le N + M\), automatically enforcing \(x_N \le N + M\)!

Step 3: Kelvin’s Method of Images & Trace Check

Applying Kelvin’s Alternating Image Formula (15.1) with \(X = N + M + 1\), \(Y = N\), \(a = 1\), \(b = M + 2\), and period \(S = a + b = M + 3\) gives the exact answer:

\[\text{Ans}(N, M) = \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \left[ \binom{2N + M + 1}{N + M + 1 + k(M + 3)} - \binom{2N + M + 1}{N + M + 2 + k(M + 3)} \right] \bmod (10^9 + 7).\]

  • Verification on \(N = 2, M = 2\): Here \(X = 5, Y = 2, X + Y = 7, a = 1, b = 4, S = 5\):
    • \(k = -1\): \(\binom{7}{5 - 5} - \binom{7}{6 - 5} = \binom{7}{0} - \binom{7}{1} = 1 - 7 = -6\).
    • \(k = 0\): \(\binom{7}{5} - \binom{7}{6} = 21 - 7 = 14\).
    • \(k = +1\): \(\binom{7}{10} - \binom{7}{11} = 0\).

Intermediate 2 (#140) — Gambler’s Walk (Steps \(\{+1, -2\}\) on \(y \ge 0\))

  • Source: CF Gym 103415K — Gambler’s Ruin / yukicoder 1341
  • Prerequisite Tags: Bivariate Kernel Method Regime C (\(k = 2, m = 1\), 15.3C), Vieta’s Large-Root Trick, Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

A particle starts at \(y_0 = 0\) on the non-negative integers \(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\). In each step, it moves either by \(+1\) (with weight \(a\)) or by \(-2\) (with weight \(b\)). The particle is never allowed to enter the negative integers (\(y_t \ge 0\) for all \(0 \le t \le N\)). Given \(N \le 10^6\) and weights \(a, b \in \mathbb{F}_{998244353}\), compute for every \(0 \le n \le N\) the total weight of valid \(n\)-step walks ending at height \(r = 0\) (\(f_{n, 0}\)) and ending at height \(r = 1\) (\(f_{n, 1}\)), modulo \(998244353\).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(a = 1, b = 1\))

Let us list all valid walks in \(\{+1, -2\}^n\) staying in \(y_t \ge 0\) for \(0 \le n \le 6\):

  • Ending at \(r = 0\) (\(n = 3m\)):
    • \(n = 0\): \(\emptyset\) (\(1\) walk).
    • \(n = 3\): ++- (heights \(0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 0\); note +-+ hits \(-1 < 0\)) \(\implies \mathbf{1}\) walk.
    • \(n = 6\): ++++-- (\(0,1,2,3,4,2,0\)), +++-+- (\(0,1,2,3,1,2,0\)), ++-++- (\(0,1,2,0,1,2,0\)) \(\implies \mathbf{3}\) walks.
  • Ending at \(r = 1\) (\(n = 3m + 1\)):
    • \(n = 1\): + \(\implies \mathbf{1}\) walk.

Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

The step Laurent polynomial is \(S(y) = b y^{-2} + a y\), which has \(k = 2\) negative steps and \(m = 1\) positive step. Because \(k = 2\), a \(-2\) step can cross the boundary \(y < 0\) from two states: from \(y = 0\) (landing at \(-2\)) and from \(y = 1\) (landing at \(-1\)). Thus the kernel equation involves two boundary series: \(F_0(x) = [y^0] F(x, y)\) and \(F_1(x) = [y^1] F(x, y)\).

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Write down the Regime C kernel equation (15.3C):

\[\bigl(y^2 - x(b + a y^3)\bigr) F(x, y) = y^2 - b x F_0(x) - b x y F_1(x).\]

The kernel \(K(x, y) = -a x y^3 + y^2 - b x\) has \(k = 2\) small roots \(y_1(x), y_2(x)\) (which both annihilate the right-hand side \(y^2 - b x F_1(x) y - b x F_0(x)\)) and \(m = 1\) large root \(y_3(x) = 1/u(x)\), where \(u(x) = x(a + b u(x)^3)\). Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

From Vieta’s formulas in Section 15.3C, \(F_0(x) = \frac{u(x)}{a x}\) and \(F_1(x) = \frac{u(x)^2}{a x}\), where \(u(x) = x \Phi(u(x))\) with \(\Phi(u) = a + b u^3\). NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

Step 1: Deriving the Two-Boundary Kernel Equation

Let \(F(x, y) = \sum_{n, j \ge 0} f_{n, j} x^n y^j \in K[y][[x]]\). At each step, multiplying \(F(x, y)\) by \(x S(y) = x(b y^{-2} + a y)\) produces two invalid negative-height terms:

  • From height \(j = 0\), a \(-2\) step gives \(b x F_0(x) y^{-2}\).
  • From height \(j = 1\), a \(-2\) step gives \(b x F_1(x) y^{-1}\).

Subtracting these two forbidden transitions from \(x S(y) F(x, y)\) and multiplying by \(y^2\) yields:

\[\underbrace{\bigl(y^2 - x(b + a y^3)\bigr)}_{K(x, y)} F(x, y) = y^2 - b x y F_1(x) - b x F_0(x).\]

Step 2: Vieta’s Large-Root Bypass

As a cubic in \(y\), \(K(x, y) = -a x \left(y^3 - \frac{1}{ax} y^2 + \frac{b}{a}\right)\) has \(k = 2\) small roots \(y_1(x), y_2(x) \sim \pm \sqrt{bx}\) (\(\operatorname{val}_x = 1/2 > 0\)) and \(m = 1\) large root \(y_3(x) \sim \frac{1}{ax}\) (\(\operatorname{val}_x = -1 < 0\)).

Since \(y_1(x)\) and \(y_2(x)\) are legal \(x\)-adic substitutions into \(F(x, y) \in K[y][[x]]\), both \(y_1(x)\) and \(y_2(x)\) are roots of the monic quadratic on the right-hand side:

\[y^2 - b x F_1(x) y - b x F_0(x) = \bigl(y - y_1(x)\bigr)\bigl(y - y_2(x)\bigr).\]

Set the single large root to \(y_3(x) = \frac{1}{u(x)}\) with \(\operatorname{val}_x(u) = 1\). Substituting \(y = 1/u\) into \(y^2 - x(b + a y^3) = 0\) and multiplying by \(u^3\) gives the implicit equation:

\[u(x) = x\bigl(a + b u(x)^3\bigr).\]

Now compare the roots of \(y^3 - \frac{1}{ax} y^2 + \frac{b}{a} = (y - y_1)(y - y_2)(y - 1/u) = 0\):

  1. Product of roots: \(y_1 y_2 \cdot \frac{1}{u(x)} = -\frac{b}{a} \implies -b x F_0(x) = y_1 y_2 = -\frac{b}{a} u(x)\), so \[F_0(x) = \frac{u(x)}{a x}.\]
  2. Sum of roots: \(y_1 + y_2 + \frac{1}{u(x)} = \frac{1}{a x}\). Since \(u = a x + b x u^3 \iff \frac{1}{ax} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{b u^2}{a}\), we have \[b x F_1(x) = y_1 + y_2 = \frac{1}{ax} - \frac{1}{u(x)} = \frac{b u(x)^2}{a} \implies F_1(x) = \frac{u(x)^2}{a x}.\]

Step 3: Closed-Form Coefficient Extraction via Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11)

Since \(u(x) = x \Phi(u(x))\) with \(\Phi(u) = a + b u^3\), Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11) gives for any integer \(r \ge 1\):

\[[x^n] u(x)^r = \frac{r}{n} [u^{n - r}] (a + b u^3)^n.\]

Notice that \([u^{n-r}] (a + b u^3)^n\) is nonzero if and only if \(n - r = 3m\) for some integer \(m \ge 0\) (so \(n = 3m + r\)):

  • Walks ending at \(0\) (\(r = 1\), length \(n = 3m\)): \[f_{3m, 0} = [x^{3m}] \frac{u(x)}{a x} = \frac{1}{a} [x^{3m + 1}] u(x) = \frac{1}{a(3m + 1)} [u^{3m}] (a + b u^3)^{3m + 1} = \frac{a^{2m} b^m}{3m + 1} \binom{3m + 1}{m}.\]
  • Walks ending at \(1\) (\(r = 2\), length \(n = 3m + 1\)): \[f_{3m + 1, 1} = [x^{3m + 1}] \frac{u(x)^2}{a x} = \frac{1}{a} [x^{3m + 2}] u(x)^2 = \frac{2}{a(3m + 2)} [u^{3m}] (a + b u^3)^{3m + 2} = \frac{2 a^{2m + 1} b^m}{3m + 2} \binom{3m + 2}{m}.\]

All other lengths have \(f_{n, 0} = 0\) and \(f_{n, 1} = 0\).

  • Trace Check (\(a = b = 1\)):
    • \(f_{0, 0} = 1\), \(f_{3, 0} = \frac{1}{4}\binom{4}{1} = 1\), \(f_{6, 0} = \frac{1}{7}\binom{7}{2} = 3\).
    • \(f_{1, 1} = \frac{2}{2}\binom{2}{0} = 1\), \(f_{4, 1} = \frac{2}{5}\binom{5}{1} = 2\).

  • Input & Preconditions: Maximum step count \(N \le 10^6\), step weights \(a, b \in \mathbb{F}_{998244353}\), down-step count \(m \in \{0, 1, \dots, \lfloor N/3 \rfloor\}\), and prime modulus \(p = 998244353\) with \(N + 2 < p\) so \((3m + 1)^{-1}\) and \((3m + 2)^{-1}\) exist modulo \(p\) for all \(0 \le 3m \le N\).

Advanced 1 (#128) — The Maximum Prefix

  • Source: Codeforces 1810G — The Maximum Prefix
  • Prerequisite Tags: Walk Reversal (Suffix Max \(\leftrightarrow\) Clamped Walk), Absorbing Boundary Operator \(y^{-1} \mapsto y^0\) (15.3A), Backward Conditional Expectation DP (Lindley’s Duality)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given an integer \(n \le 5000\) (with \(\sum n^2 \le 2.5 \times 10^7\) across test cases), probabilities \(p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n \pmod{10^9 + 7}\) where each step \(a_i\) (\(1 \le i \le n\)) is independently \(+1\) with probability \(p_i\) and \(-1\) with probability \(q_i = (1 - p_i) \bmod (10^9 + 7)\), and a score array \(h_0, h_1, \dots, h_n \pmod{10^9 + 7}\).

For each prefix length \(k \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\), let \(M_k = \max_{0 \le j \le k} \sum_{i=1}^j a_i\) be the maximum prefix sum among the first \(k\) elements (with the empty prefix \(j = 0\) giving sum \(0\), so \(0 \le M_k \le k\)). Compute the expected score \(\mathbb{E}[h_{M_k}] \bmod (10^9 + 7)\) for all \(k = 1, 2, \dots, n\).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n = 2, p_1 = p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\))

  • \(k = 1\): \(a_1 = +1\) (prob \(1/2 \implies M_1 = 1\)) or \(a_1 = -1\) (prob \(1/2 \implies M_1 = 0\)). Thus \(\mathbb{E}[h_{M_1}] = \frac{1}{2} h_0 + \frac{1}{2} h_1\).
  • \(k = 2\): The 4 equiprobable paths (+, +), (+, -), (-, +), (-, -) have prefix maximums \(M_2 = 2, 1, 0, 0\). Thus \(\mathbb{E}[h_{M_2}] = \frac{2}{4} h_0 + \frac{1}{4} h_1 + \frac{1}{4} h_2\).
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

If you process \(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_k\) forward from \(i = 1\) to \(k\), you have to track both the current prefix sum \(S_i\) and the running maximum \(M_i = \max(M_{i-1}, S_i)\), which is a 2D state \((S_i, M_i)\) of size \(O(n^2)\) (taking \(O(n^3)\) time). What happens if you process the array \(a_k, a_{k-1}, \dots, a_1\) backward (from right to left)?

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

When you prepend a single element \(a_i \in \{+1, -1\}\) to the front of a sequence whose maximum prefix sum (including the empty prefix \(0\)) is \(m \ge 0\), every non-empty prefix sum shifts by \(+a_i\), while the empty prefix stays at \(0\):

\[m_{\text{new}} = \max(0,\, m + a_i).\]

This is a 1D state \(m \in \{0, \dots, n\}\) governed by the absorbing/clamping boundary operator \(y^{-1} \mapsto y^0\) from Section 15.3A! However, because prepending \(a_k, a_{k-1}, \dots, a_1\) starts from \(a_k\), running this backward distribution DP naively for each \(k = 1, \dots, n\) would restart from \(k\) each time. How can you share the work across all \(k = 1, \dots, n\) simultaneously using conditional expectations (or elementary matrix-vector associativity)?

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Use Backward / Initial-Margin Conditional Expectation DP (Lindley’s Duality)—or equivalently, elementary matrix-vector associativity \(h^\top (T_1 T_2 \cdots T_k e_0) = (((h^\top T_1) T_2) \cdots T_k) e_0\):

  • Define \(dp_i[m] = \mathbb{E}[h_{\mu_0} \mid \mu_i = m]\) as the expected final score after steps \(i, i - 1, \dots, 1\) given that the running suffix maximum entering step \(i\) is \(\mu_i = m\).
  • Base case at \(i = 0\) (no steps left): \(dp_0[m] = h_m\) for \(0 \le m \le n\).
  • For \(i = 1, 2, \dots, n\), update for \(0 \le m \le n - i\): \[dp_i[m] = p_i \cdot dp_{i-1}[m + 1] + q_i \cdot dp_{i-1}[\max(0, m - 1)] \pmod{10^9 + 7}.\]
  • Since prefix \(a_1 \dots a_k\) starts the backward recurrence at \(\mu_k = 0\), after step \(k\) we simply read off \(\mathbb{E}[h_{M_k}] = dp_k[0]\)! This solves all \(k = 1, \dots, n\) in \(O(n^2)\) time and \(O(n)\) space (and in the homogeneous / D&C polynomial setting, the clamped kernel \((y - x(p y^2 + q))F(x, y) = y u_0(y) - q x(1 - y) F(x, 0)\) solves it in \(O(n \log^2 n)\) time).
NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

Step 1: Reversing the Walk to Eliminate the 2D State (Lindley’s Duality)

For any sequence \((a_1, \dots, a_k)\), the maximum prefix sum \(M_k = \max_{0 \le j \le k} \sum_{i=1}^j a_i\) satisfies the backward Horner recurrence for \((\max, +)\):

\[\mu_k = 0, \qquad \mu_{i-1} = \max(0,\, \mu_i + a_i) \quad (i = k, k - 1, \dots, 1), \qquad M_k = \mu_0.\]

In the language of generating functions on \(K[y]\), prepending step \(a_i\) applies the linear transition matrix/operator \(T_i: K[y] \to K[y]\) defined by:

\[T_i(y^m) = p_i y^{m + 1} + q_i y^{\max(0, m - 1)} \qquad (m \ge 0),\]

which is the absorbing boundary operator \(y^{-1} \mapsto y^0\) from Section 15.3A. Since \(\mu_k = 0\) (represented by the unit basis vector \(e_0 = y^0 = 1\)) and we apply \(T_k\), then \(T_{k-1}\), down to \(T_1\), the probability distribution vector of \(M_k = \mu_0\) is:

\[P_k(y) = (T_1 \circ T_2 \circ \dots \circ T_k)(y^0) = \sum_{m=0}^{k} \Pr[M_k = m] y^m.\]

Step 2: Backward Conditional Expectation DP & Elementary Matrix-Vector Associativity

Notice that in \(P_k = T_1 T_2 \cdots T_k e_0\), the operator \(T_k\) is on the right (innermost), whereas \(T_1\) is on the left (outermost). If we computed the column vectors \(v_{i-1} = T_i v_i\) starting from \(v_k = e_0\), changing \(k\) would force us to restart from scratch.

Instead, we want the scalar expectation \(h^\top P_k = h^\top (T_1 T_2 \cdots T_k e_0)\) for every \(k = 1, \dots, n\), where \(h^\top = [h_0, h_1, \dots, h_n]\) is the row vector of scores. By elementary matrix-vector associativity, we can multiply from left to right using row vectors \(u_i^\top = u_{i-1}^\top T_i\) starting from \(u_0^\top = h^\top\):

\[h^\top \bigl(T_1 T_2 \cdots T_k e_0\bigr) = \bigl(\cdots((h^\top T_1) T_2)\cdots T_k\bigr) e_0 = u_k^\top e_0 = u_k[0].\]

Probabilistically, \(dp_i[m] := u_i[m] = \mathbb{E}[h_{\mu_0} \mid \mu_i = m]\) is simply the conditional expectation of the final score \(h_{\mu_0}\) after steps \(i, i - 1, \dots, 1\), given that the backward state entering step \(i\) has initial headroom margin \(\mu_i = m\):

  1. Initialize at \(i = 0\) (after step \(1\) has finished, where state \(\mu_0 = m\) scores \(h_m\)): \[dp_0[m] = h_m \qquad (0 \le m \le n).\]
  2. For \(i = 1, 2, \dots, n\), condition on the outcome of step \(a_i \in \{+1, -1\}\) (from \(\mu_i = m\), step \(a_i\) transitions to \(\mu_{i-1} = m + 1\) with probability \(p_i\) and to \(\mu_{i-1} = \max(0, m - 1)\) with probability \(q_i\)): \[dp_i[m] = p_i \cdot dp_{i-1}[m + 1] + q_i \cdot dp_{i-1}[\max(0, m - 1)] \qquad (0 \le m \le n - i).\]
  3. Read off the answer for prefix length \(k\) at initial margin \(\mu_k = 0\): \[\mathbb{E}[h_{M_k}] = dp_k[0].\]

Step 3: Connection to the Clamped Kernel Method (15.3A) & Trace Verification

When \(p_i = p, q_i = q\) are constant (or inside a divide-and-conquer tree over \(i\)), the bivariate generating function \(F(x, y) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty x^k P_k(y)\) satisfies the clamped kernel equation from Section 15.3A:

\[\bigl(y - x(p y^2 + q)\bigr) F(x, y) = y - q x (1 - y) F(x, 0).\]

Substituting the small root \(y_0(x) = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - 4pq x^2}}{2px}\) immediately gives \(F(x, 0) = \frac{y_0(x)}{q x (1 - y_0(x))}\) and \(F(x, y) = \frac{y - y_0(x) \frac{1-y}{1-y_0(x)}}{y - x(p y^2 + q)}\) in \(O(n \log n)\) time!

  • Verification on \(n = 2, p_1 = p_2 = 1/2\):
    • \(dp_0 = [h_0, h_1, h_2]\).
    • \(i = 1\): \(dp_1[0] = \frac{1}{2} h_1 + \frac{1}{2} h_0\) (\(\implies k = 1\) answer!), \(dp_1[1] = \frac{1}{2} h_2 + \frac{1}{2} h_0\).
    • \(i = 2\): \(dp_2[0] = \frac{1}{2} dp_1[1] + \frac{1}{2} dp_1[0] = \frac{2}{4} h_0 + \frac{1}{4} h_1 + \frac{1}{4} h_2\) (\(\implies k = 2\) answer!).

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: Length \(n \le 5000\), step probabilities \(p_1, \dots, p_n \in \mathbb{F}_{10^9+7}\) (with \(q_i = 1 - p_i \bmod p\)), and score array \(h_0, h_1, \dots, h_n \in \mathbb{F}_{10^9+7}\) already reduced modulo \(p = 10^9 + 7\). Outer loop \(i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\); inner state \(m \in \{0, \dots, n - i\}\).
  • Postconditions (Output): Returns \(\bigl(\mathbb{E}[h_{M_1}], \dots, \mathbb{E}[h_{M_n}]\bigr) = \bigl(dp_1[0], \dots, dp_n[0]\bigr) \bmod (10^9 + 7)\).
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(n^2)\) time (\(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\) transitions) and \(O(n)\) auxiliary space by overwriting the 1D array \(dp[0 \dots n - i]\) in-place.
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: After outer iteration \(i \in \{0, \dots, n\}\), \(dp_i[m] = (h^\top T_1 T_2 \cdots T_i)_m = \mathbb{E}[h_{\mu_0} \mid \mu_i = m]\) for all \(0 \le m \le n - i\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: Propagating the distribution column vector \(T_1 \cdots T_k e_0\) from right to left (which forces an \(O(n^2)\) restart for each \(k\), totaling \(O(n^3)\) time), or updating \(dp[0]\) in-place before reading the old \(dp[0]\) needed for \(\max(0, m - 1)\) at \(m = 0, 1\).

Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

To count arrays \(a\) for which there exists a valid walk \(b\), run the subset construction (powerset DFA): at step \(i\), let \(S_i \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) be the set of all reachable positions \(b_i\) given \(a_1, \dots, a_i\). Notice two remarkable properties of \(S_i\):

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Let \(B_i = \max S_i\). By induction, \(S_i\) always contains \(B_i\) (and in fact, once \(B_i\) reaches \(m\), at the next step \(B_i + 1 = m + 1 \notin \{1, \dots, m\}\) can never be blocked by any \(a_{i+1} \in \{1, \dots, m\}\), so the walk is permanently safe for all remaining steps!).

While \(0 \le B_{i-1} \le m - 1\):

  • If \(a_i = B_{i-1} + 1\) (\(1\) choice of \(a_i \in \{1, \dots, m\}\)), the top step \(B_{i-1} + 1\) is blocked, so the new maximum drops to \(B_i = B_{i-1} - 1\) (and if \(B_i = -1\), then \(S_i = \emptyset\) and the array \(a\) dies!).
  • If \(a_i \neq B_{i-1} + 1\) (\(m - 1\) choices of \(a_i \in \{1, \dots, m\}\)), the top step \(B_{i-1} + 1\) is unblocked, so the new maximum increases to \(B_i = B_{i-1} + 1\)!

Thus \(B_i\) is a 1D lattice walk starting at \(b_0 \in [0, m - 1]\): it dies if it hits \(-1\), it becomes immortal (with all \(m\) choices valid per remaining step) the first time it hits \(m\), and inside \([0, m - 1]\) it steps \(+1\) with weight \(m - 1\) and \(-1\) with weight \(1\)!

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Because at each step inside \([0, m - 1]\) the \(+1\) step has weight \(m - 1\) and the \(-1\) step has weight \(1\), their sum is \((m - 1) + 1 = m\)—which is identical to the \(m\) unconstrained choices per step after hitting the safe barrier \(m\)! Therefore, an \(n\)-step walk with step weights \((m - 1)\) for \(+1\) and \(1\) for \(-1\) is invalid if and only if it hits \(-1\) before hitting \(m\). Use Kelvin’s Method of Images (15.1) with a single linear-time pass \(G(r) = \binom{n}{r} + G(r - (m + 1))\) to sum all bad paths in \(O(n)\) time!

Step 1: Equivalence Between the Subset DFA and the 1D Walk \(B_i = \max S_i\)

Let \(S_i \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) be the set of reachable positions \(b_i\) after \(i\) steps. We claim that for any \(b_0 \in \{0, \dots, m - 1\}\), the maximum reachable position \(B_i = \max S_i\) governs survival prior to hitting \(m\):

  • At each step \(i\), every \(x \in S_{i-1}\) proposes \(x - 1\) (if \(x \ge 1\)) and \(x + 1\).
  • Since \(a_i \in \{1, \dots, m\}\) blocks at most one integer:
    • If \(a_i \neq B_{i-1} + 1\) (\(m - 1\) choices of \(a_i\)), the top transition \(B_{i-1} \to B_{i-1} + 1\) is unblocked, so \(B_i = B_{i-1} + 1\).
    • If \(a_i = B_{i-1} + 1\) (\(1\) choice of \(a_i\)), no element in \(S_{i-1}\) can reach \(B_{i-1} + 1\), while \(B_{i-1} \to B_{i-1} - 1\) is unblocked (as \(B_{i-1} - 1 \neq B_{i-1} + 1\)), so \(B_i = B_{i-1} - 1\) (and if \(B_i = -1\), then \(S_i = \emptyset\) and the walk dies).
  • Once \(B_i\) reaches \(m\), the upward transition \(m \to m + 1\) can never be blocked by any \(a \in \{1, \dots, m\}\), so the walk survives forever with all \(m\) choices of \(a_t\) valid at every subsequent step.

Step 2: Chebyshev Strip Generating Function & The Unconstrained Continuation Identity

By Regime B of the Kernel Method (15.3B), in the strip \([0, m - 1]\) with step polynomial \(S(y) = y^{-1} + (m - 1)y\), the generating function of walks starting at \(b_0\) and hitting \(-1\) for the first time (before hitting \(m\)) is:

\[w_{-1} x F_0(x) = x^{b_0 + 1} \frac{Q_{m - 1 - b_0}\bigl((m - 1) x^2\bigr)}{Q_m\bigl((m - 1) x^2\bigr)},\]

where \(Q_k(z) = Q_{k-1}(z) - z Q_{k-2}(z)\). Under the Catalan residue substitution \(z = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\) (15.3B), \([z^r] \frac{Q_{m - 1 - b_0}(z)}{Q_m(z)} = [u^r] (1 + u)^{2r + b_0} (1 - u) \frac{1 - u^{m - b_0}}{1 - u^{m + 1}}\).

Better still, notice the fundamental weight identity at the upper barrier \(m\):

\[\bigl((m - 1) + 1\bigr)^{n - k} = m^{n - k}.\]

Because the sum of the two step weights \((m - 1)\) and \(1\) is already \(m\), extending a walk that hits \(m\) (or \(-1\)) at step \(k\) in \(m^{n - k}\) ways is identical to letting the walk continue taking unconstrained steps of \(+1\) (weight \(m - 1\)) and \(-1\) (weight \(1\)) up to step \(n\)! Consequently, an unconstrained \(n\)-step walk from \(b_0\) is invalid if and only if it hits \(-1\) before hitting \(m\).

Step 3: \(O(n)\) Extraction via Kelvin’s Method of Images & Period-\(S\) Prefix Sums

In monotone grid coordinates \((u, d)\) where \(d \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}\) is the number of \(-1\) steps and \(u = n - d\) is the number of \(+1\) steps, every path to \((u, d)\) has weight \((m - 1)^u = (m - 1)^{n - d}\). The lower barrier \(y = -1\) is the mirror \(L_1: y = x + a\) with \(a = b_0 + 1\), and the upper barrier \(y = m\) is the mirror \(L_2: y = x - b\) with \(b = m - b_0\), giving period \(S = a + b = m + 1\).

Define the period-\(S\) binomial sum \(G(r) = \sum_{k \ge 0} \binom{n}{r - kS}\) for \(0 \le r \le n\), which we precompute in \(O(n)\) time via the single recurrence:

\[G(r) = \binom{n}{r} + \begin{cases} G(r - S) & \text{if } r \ge S, \\ 0 & \text{if } 0 \le r < S, \end{cases}\]

with \(G(r) = 0\) for \(r < 0\). Then for each \(d \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}\) (with \(u = n - d\) and final height \(h = b_0 + u - d = b_0 + n - 2d\)), the number of grid paths \(B(d)\) from \((0, 0)\) to \((u, d)\) whose compressed mirror-hit word starts with 1 (hitting \(L_1\) before \(L_2\)) is evaluated in \(O(1)\) time:

  • Case 1 (\(h \ge 0\), i.e., \(d < u + a\)): By Kelvin’s alternating reflection (15.1), odd reflections \(r_1(r_2 r_1)^k(u, d)\) have \(x\)-coordinate \(d - a - kS\) (contributing \(+G(d - a)\)) and even reflections \((r_1 r_2)^{k+1}(u, d)\) have \(x\)-coordinate \(u - (k + 1)S\) (contributing \(-G(u - S)\)): \[B(d) = G(d - a) - G(u - S).\]
  • Case 2 (\(h \le -1\), i.e., \(u \le d - a\)): Since \((u, d)\) lies on or beyond \(L_1\), every path to \((u, d)\) ends with a hit on \(L_1\). Even shifts \((r_2 r_1)^k(u, d)\) have \(y\)-coordinate \(d - kS\) (contributing \(+G(d)\)), while odd reflections \(r_2(r_1 r_2)^k(u, d)\) have \(y\)-coordinate \(u - b - kS\) (contributing \(-G(u - b)\)): \[B(d) = G(d) - G(u - b).\]

Subtracting all bad walks from \(m^n\) gives the answer in \(O(n)\) time:

\[\text{Ans} = m^n - \sum_{d = 0}^{n} B(d) (m - 1)^{n - d} \bmod 998244353.\]

  • Verification on \(n = 2, m = 2, b_0 = 0\): Here \(a = 1, b = 2, S = 3\), and \(G(0) = 1, G(1) = 2, G(2) = 1\):

    • \(d = 0\) (\(u = 2, h = 2 \ge 0\)): \(B(0) = G(-1) - G(-1) = 0\).
    • \(d = 1\) (\(u = 1, h = 0 \ge 0\)): \(B(1) = G(0) - G(-2) = 1\), weight \((2-1)^1 = 1\).
    • \(d = 2\) (\(u = 0, h = -2 \le -1\)): \(B(2) = G(2) - G(-2) = 1\), weight \((2-1)^0 = 1\).
    • Total valid arrays: \(2^2 - (0 + 1 + 1) = \mathbf{2}\). Matches our hand trace!

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given integers \(N \le 2 \times 10^7\) and \(1 \le B \le A\) with \(A + B \le N\). Let \(X = N - A - B\). Count the number of strings \(S \in \{\texttt{A}, \texttt{B}, \texttt{X}\}^N\) containing \(A\) copies of \(\texttt{A}\), \(B\) copies of \(\texttt{B}\), and \(X\) copies of \(\texttt{X}\) such that:

  1. No Adjacent Identical Steps: Neither \(\texttt{AA}\) nor \(\texttt{BB}\) appears as a contiguous substring in \(S\) (note that \(\texttt{XX}\) is allowed!).
  2. Ballot Condition: In every prefix of \(S\), the number of \(\texttt{A}\)’s is at least the number of \(\texttt{B}\)’s (\(\#\texttt{A}_{\le i} \ge \#\texttt{B}_{\le i}\) for all \(1 \le i \le N\)).

Output the count modulo \(10^9 + 7\).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(N = 5, A = 2, B = 2, X = 1\))

Among strings of \(2\) A’s, \(2\) B’s, and \(1\) X with no AA or BB where every prefix has \(\#\texttt{A} \ge \#\texttt{B}\), the first non-X letter must be A. With only one X, if the two A’s are separated by X (AXA), avoiding BB while satisfying \(\#\texttt{A} \ge \#\texttt{B}\) on all prefixes is impossible (since one B would have to precede the first A or follow the last A, violating \(\#\texttt{A} \ge \#\texttt{B}\) at index \(1\) or forcing BB). Thus the A’s and B’s must alternate as ABAB, with the single X placed in any of the \(5\) slots:

  • XABAB, AXBAB, ABXAB, ABAXB, ABABX \(\implies \mathbf{5}\) valid strings.
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

First solve the unconstrained problem \(f(a, b, x)\): how many strings of \(a\) A’s, \(b\) B’s, and \(x\) X’s have no AA and no BB?

  • Suppose the \(a\) copies of A are partitioned into \(a - k\) non-empty blocks separated by B’s or X’s, where exactly \(k\) of the \(a - 1\) gaps between consecutive A’s are filled by only a single B (ABA), and the remaining \(a - k\) blocks of A are placed into the \(x + 1\) slots between the \(x\) copies of X!
Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Let us check the unconstrained formula \(f(a, b, x)\):

  1. Start with \(a\) A’s and \(x\) X’s. To avoid AA, between any two adjacent A’s with no X between them, we must insert at least one B (and at most one B, since BB is forbidden!).
  2. If \(a - k\) is the number of X-separated slots (out of \(x + 1\) slots) that receive at least one A (\(\binom{x+1}{a-k}\) ways to choose the slots, and \(\binom{a-1}{a-1-k} = \binom{a-1}{k}\) ways to distribute the \(a\) A’s into those \(a - k\) slots), then the \(k\) internal adjacencies AA inside those slots must each receive one B.
  3. The remaining \(b - k\) copies of B can be placed (at most one per gap) into any of the \((a + x + 1) - k\) remaining gaps (X-adjacent gaps or outer ends of A-blocks), in \(\binom{a + x + 1 - k}{b - k}\) ways: \[f(a, b, x) = \sum_{k = 0}^{a - 1} \binom{a - 1}{k} \binom{x + 1}{a - k} \binom{a + x + 1 - k}{b - k}.\]

Now, how do we subtract the “bad” strings that violate \(\#\texttt{A} \ge \#\texttt{B}\) when reflecting \(\texttt{A} \leftrightarrow \texttt{B}\) after the first hitting time \(\tau\) where \(\#\texttt{B} = \#\texttt{A} + 1\)?

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

Let \(\tau\) be the first index where \(\#\texttt{B} = \#\texttt{A} + 1\). Necessarily \(S[\tau] = \texttt{B}\). If we reflect \(\texttt{A} \leftrightarrow \texttt{B}\) in the suffix \(S[\tau + 1 \dots N]\), the reflected string \(S'\) has \(a' = B - 1\) copies of A, \(b' = A + 1\) copies of B, and \(x' = X\) copies of X, and has no AA or BB strictly before \(\tau\) or strictly after \(\tau\). What can happen at the boundary \(\tau\) in \(S'\)?

Because \(S[\tau] = \texttt{B}\), in the original string \(S\) the character \(S[\tau + 1]\) could not be B, so in the reflected string \(S'\), \(S'[\tau + 1]\) cannot be A! Classify \(S'\) by what follows \(S'[\tau] = \texttt{B}\):

  1. \(S'[\tau + 1] = \texttt{B}\) (creates a single forbidden BB at \((\tau, \tau + 1)\)): contracting BB \(\to\) B gives an unconstrained valid string counted by \(f(B - 1, A, X)\)!
  2. \(S'[\tau + 1 \dots \tau + 2] = \texttt{BXB}\): contracting BXB \(\to\) B gives \(f(B - 1, A, X - 1)\)!
  3. \(S'[\tau + 1 \dots \tau + 2] = \texttt{BXA}\) or \(\texttt{BXX}\) (or \(\tau + 1 = N\) with \(S'[N] = \texttt{X}\)): contracting BX \(\to\) B gives \(f(B - 1, A + 1, X - 1)\)!

Thus \(\text{Ans} = f(A, B, X) - f(B - 1, A, X) - f(B - 1, A, X - 1) - f(B - 1, A + 1, X - 1)\) in \(O(N)\) time!

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

Step 1: Unconstrained Count \(f(a, b, x)\) of AA- and BB-Free Strings

Let \(f(a, b, x)\) be the number of strings with \(a\) A’s, \(b\) B’s, and \(x\) X’s containing neither AA nor BB:

  • If \(b < 0\) or \(x < 0\), \(f(a, b, x) = 0\).
  • If \(a = 0\), there are no A’s, so the \(b\) B’s must go into the \(x + 1\) gaps between the \(x\) X’s with at most one B per gap: \(f(0, b, x) = \binom{x + 1}{b}\).
  • For \(a \ge 1\): Delete all \(b\) B’s first. The \(a\) A’s fall into the \(x + 1\) slots defined by the \(x\) X’s. Suppose \(a - k\) slots (\(0 \le k \le a - 1\)) are non-empty:
    1. There are \(\binom{x + 1}{a - k}\) ways to choose which \(a - k\) slots contain A’s, and \(\binom{a - 1}{a - k - 1} = \binom{a - 1}{k}\) ways to choose the sizes of those \(a - k\) blocks of A’s.
    2. Within those blocks of A’s, there are exactly \(k\) adjacent AA pairs. To break every AA without using BB, we must place exactly one B into each of those \(k\) internal gaps (ABA).
    3. The remaining \(b - k\) copies of B can now be inserted into any of the \((a + x + 1) - k\) remaining gaps (since none of the remaining gaps has B’s on either side, and placing at most one B per gap never creates BB), in \(\binom{a + x + 1 - k}{b - k}\) ways.

Summing over \(k\) gives the \(O(a)\) formula:

\[f(a, b, x) = \sum_{k = 0}^{a - 1} \binom{a - 1}{k} \binom{x + 1}{a - k} \binom{a + x + 1 - k}{b - k}.\]

Step 2: Reflection Across #B = #A + 1 & Local Boundary Contraction

A string \(S\) with no AA or BB violates the ballot condition \(\#\texttt{A}_{\le i} \ge \#\texttt{B}_{\le i}\) if and only if there is a first index \(\tau \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) where \(\#\texttt{B}_{\le \tau} = \#\texttt{A}_{\le \tau} + 1\). Necessarily:

  • \(S[\tau] = \texttt{B}\).
  • Because \(S\) has no BB, the next character \(S[\tau + 1]\) (if \(\tau < N\)) cannot be B, so \(S[\tau + 1] \in \{\texttt{A}, \texttt{X}\}\).

Reflect the suffix \(S[\tau + 1 \dots N]\) by swapping \(\texttt{A} \leftrightarrow \texttt{B}\) and leaving \(\texttt{X}\) unchanged, producing a string \(S'\) with counts:

\[(a', b', x') = (B - 1,\ A + 1,\ X).\]

In \(S'\), the prefix \(S'[1 \dots \tau]\) and the suffix \(S'[\tau + 1 \dots N]\) each have no AA or BB, and at index \(\tau\) we have \(S'[\tau] = \texttt{B}\), while \(S'[\tau + 1] \in \{\texttt{B}, \texttt{X}\}\) (since \(S[\tau + 1] \in \{\texttt{A}, \texttt{X}\}\)). Notice that \(\tau\) is still the first index in \(S'\) where \(\#\texttt{B}_{\le \tau} = \#\texttt{A}_{\le \tau} + 1\). Furthermore, since \(b' = A + 1 > B - 1 = a'\) (as \(A \ge B\)), every string with counts \((B - 1, A + 1, X)\) must reach \(\#\texttt{B} = \#\texttt{A} + 1\) at some first index \(\tau\) with \(S'[\tau] = \texttt{B}\)!

Partition all bad strings \(S\) into 3 disjoint classes according to the local pattern in \(S'\) right after \(S'[\tau] = \texttt{B}\):

  1. Class 1 (\(S'[\tau + 1] = \texttt{B}\), so \(S'[\tau \dots \tau + 1] = \texttt{BB}\)): Here \(S'\) has a unique BB at \((\tau, \tau + 1)\) and no other AA or BB. Contracting the pair \(S'[\tau \dots \tau + 1] = \texttt{BB}\) into a single \(\texttt{B}\) produces a valid AA/BB-free string of counts \((B - 1, A, X)\) whose first hitting time of \(\#\texttt{B} = \#\texttt{A} + 1\) is \(\tau\)! Conversely, in any valid string of \((B - 1, A, X)\) (since \(A \ge B > B - 1\), it always hits \(\#\texttt{B} = \#\texttt{A} + 1\) at some first index \(\tau\) with character \(\texttt{B}\)), duplicating that \(\tau\)-th character \(\texttt{B} \to \texttt{BB}\) uniquely reconstructs \(S'\). Thus Class 1 has size: \[|C_1| = f(B - 1,\ A,\ X).\]

  2. Class 2 (\(S'[\tau + 1 \dots \tau + 2] = \texttt{BXB}\), so \(S'[\tau \dots \tau + 2] = \texttt{BXB}\)): Contracting the 3-character block \(S'[\tau \dots \tau + 2] = \texttt{BXB}\) into a single \(\texttt{B}\) removes one B and one X without creating any AA or BB, producing an arbitrary valid string of counts \((B - 1, A, X - 1)\) (whose first hitting time \(\tau\) of \(\#\texttt{B} = \#\texttt{A} + 1\) is the contracted B). Expanding that \(\tau\)-th B back to BXB is a bijection, so Class 2 has size: \[|C_2| = f(B - 1,\ A,\ X - 1).\]

  3. Class 3 (\(S'[\tau + 1] = \texttt{X}\) and \(S'[\tau + 2] \neq \texttt{B}\), i.e., \(S'[\tau \dots \tau + 1] = \texttt{BX}\) followed by \(\texttt{A}\), \(\texttt{X}\), or end-of-string): Because the character after BX is not B (it is A, X, or end-of-string), contracting \(S'[\tau \dots \tau + 1] = \texttt{BX}\) into a single \(\texttt{B}\) (by deleting the X at \(\tau + 1\)) places \(S'[\tau] = \texttt{B}\) directly in front of A, X, or end-of-string, which never creates BB! Moreover, it preserves \(\tau\) as the exact first hitting time of \(\#\texttt{B} = \#\texttt{A} + 1\), while reducing the X-count by \(1\) and keeping the B-count at \(A + 1\). Thus deleting \(S'[\tau + 1] = \texttt{X}\) maps Class 3 bijectively onto all valid strings of counts \((B - 1,\ A + 1,\ X - 1)\): \[|C_3| = f(B - 1,\ A + 1,\ X - 1).\]

Step 3: Final Formula & Trace Verification

Subtracting the three disjoint bad classes from \(f(A, B, X)\) gives:

\[\text{Ans}(A, B, X) = f(A, B, X) - f(B - 1, A, X) - f(B - 1, A, X - 1) - f(B - 1, A + 1, X - 1) \bmod (10^9 + 7).\]

  • Verification on \(N = 5, A = 2, B = 2, X = 1\):
    • \(f(2, 2, 1) = \binom{1}{0}\binom{2}{2}\binom{4}{2} + \binom{1}{1}\binom{2}{1}\binom{3}{1} = 6 + 6 = 12\).
    • \(f(1, 2, 1) = \binom{0}{0}\binom{2}{1}\binom{3}{2} = 6\).
    • \(f(1, 2, 0) = \binom{0}{0}\binom{1}{1}\binom{2}{2} = 1\).
    • \(f(1, 3, 0) = \binom{0}{0}\binom{1}{1}\binom{2}{3} = 0\).
    • \(\text{Ans}(2, 2, 1) = 12 - 6 - 1 - 0 = \mathbf{5}\). Matches our hand trace!

  • Postconditions (Output): Returns \(\bigl(f(A, B, X) - f(B - 1, A, X) - f(B - 1, A, X - 1) - f(B - 1, A + 1, X - 1)\bigr) \bmod (10^9 + 7)\).

Mastery 2 (#210) — [集训队作业2018] count (Bounded-Alphabet Cartesian Trees)

  • Source: UOJ 424 — 【集训队作业2018】count
  • Prerequisite Tags: Left-Spine Depth of Cartesian Trees, Catalan Rotation to Strip Dyck Paths, Chebyshev Rational OGF & Catalan Residue (15.3B)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Two integer arrays \(A, B \in \{1, 2, \dots, m\}^n\) (\(1 \le n, m \le 2 \times 10^5\)) are called equivalent if for every subsegment \([l, r]\) (\(1 \le l \le r \le n\)), the index \(p \in [l, r]\) that achieves the maximum value in \(A[l \dots r]\) (breaking ties by choosing the leftmost occurrence of the maximum) is equal to the index that achieves the leftmost maximum in \(B[l \dots r]\).

Count the number of equivalence classes of arrays in \(\{1, 2, \dots, m\}^n\), modulo \(998244353\).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n = 3, m = 2\))

By the fundamental property of Cartesian trees, two arrays are equivalent if and only if they have the same binary Cartesian tree (where the root of \([l, r]\) is the leftmost maximum \(p\)). There are \(C_3 = 5\) binary trees on \(n = 3\) nodes.

Because ties are broken to the left, for any node \(u\) in the Cartesian tree:

  • Every node \(v\) in the left subtree of \(u\) must satisfy \(A[v] < A[u]\) (strict decrease on left edges!).
  • Every node \(w\) in the right subtree of \(u\) must satisfy \(A[w] \le A[u]\) (weak decrease on right edges!).

Therefore, the minimum alphabet size \(m\) needed to realize a binary tree \(T\) is \(1\) plus the maximum number of left-child edges on any root-to-node path in \(T\)! For \(n = 3\), only the pure left-chain \(3 \xleftarrow{L} 2 \xleftarrow{L} 1\) has \(2\) left edges (requiring \(A[1] < A[2] < A[3]\), i.e., \(m \ge 3\)). The remaining \(5 - 1 = \mathbf{4}\) trees require at most \(1\) left edge, so for \(n = 3, m = 2\) the answer is \(4\).

Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model

By the trace above, a binary tree \(T\) on \(n\) nodes is realizable over \(\{1, \dots, m\}\) if and only if every root-to-node path in \(T\) contains at most \(m - 1\) left-child edges (if \(n < m\), all \(C_n\) trees are valid).

Under the standard Catalan bijection between binary trees on \(n\) nodes and Dyck paths of semilength \(n\) (traverse the tree by pre-order/left-spine DFS: stepping to a left child is \(+1\), stepping to a right child after popping the left subtree is \(-1\)), what does “at most \(m - 1\) left-child edges on any root-to-node path” correspond to?

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Let \(F_k(z)\) be the generating function of binary trees where every node has left-depth \(\le k\). A tree with root at left-depth \(0\) (capable of taking up to \(k = m - 1\) left steps) decomposes into a left subtree (capable of taking up to \(k - 1\) left steps) and a right subtree (capable of taking up to \(k\) left steps!):

\[F_k(z) = 1 + z F_{k-1}(z) F_k(z) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad F_k(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z F_{k-1}(z)} \qquad (k \ge 0,\ F_{-1}(z) = 1).\]

This is the exact continued fraction of Dyck paths of semilength \(n\) in the strip \(0 \le y \le m - 1\) (or height \(\le m\) including the initial push)! By Regime B of the Kernel Method (15.3B), \(F_{m-1}(z) = \frac{Q_m(z)}{Q_{m+1}(z)}\) where \(Q_k(z) = Q_{k-1}(z) - z Q_{k-2}(z) = \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor} (-1)^j \binom{k-j}{j} z^j\).

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

We want \([z^n] F_{m-1}(z) = [z^n] \frac{Q_m(z)}{Q_{m+1}(z)}\):

  • Method 1 (fps_inv, \(O(n \log n)\)): Explicitly form the degree-\(\lfloor (m+1)/2 \rfloor\) polynomials \(Q_m(z)\) and \(Q_{m+1}(z)\) and compute \(Q_m(z) Q_{m+1}(z)^{-1} \bmod z^{n+1}\).
  • Method 2 (Catalan Residue \(z = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\) / Kelvin Reflection, \(O(n)\)): \[[z^n] \frac{Q_m(z)}{Q_{m+1}(z)} = [u^n] (1 + u)^{2n} (1 - u) \frac{1 - u^{m+1}}{1 - u^{m+2}} = \sum_{k \ge 0} \left[ \binom{2n}{n - L_k} - \binom{2n}{n - L_k - 1} - \binom{2n}{n - R_k} + \binom{2n}{n - R_k - 1} \right]\] where \(L_k = k(m + 2)\) and \(R_k = k(m + 2) + m + 1\), running in \(O(n/m + 1)\) query time after \(O(n)\) factorial precomputation!
NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

Step 1: Greedy Realizability & Left-Depth Characterization

Given a binary tree \(T\) on \(\{1, \dots, n\}\), define the left-depth \(d_L(v)\) of each node \(v\) by \(d_L(\text{root}) = 0\), \(d_L(\text{left}(u)) = d_L(u) + 1\), and \(d_L(\text{right}(u)) = d_L(u)\).

  • Necessity: Since the leftmost maximum is chosen as the root of every subsegment, moving to a left child \(v = \text{left}(u)\) requires \(A[v] < A[u]\), whereas moving to a right child \(w = \text{right}(u)\) only requires \(A[w] \le A[u]\). By induction down the tree, any node \(v\) must satisfy \(A[v] \le A[\text{root}] - d_L(v) \le m - d_L(v)\). Since \(A[v] \ge 1\), we must have \(d_L(v) \le m - 1\) for every node \(v \in T\).
  • Sufficiency: Conversely, if \(\max_{v \in T} d_L(v) \le m - 1\), assigning \(A[v] := m - d_L(v) \in \{1, \dots, m\}\) strictly decreases on every left edge and remains constant on every right edge, so the Cartesian tree of \(A\) is \(T\).

Step 2: Continued Fraction & Chebyshev Rational Function \(Q_m(z)/Q_{m+1}(z)\)

Let \(F_k(z)\) (\(k \ge -1\)) be the OGF of binary trees where every node has left-depth \(\le k\), with \(F_{-1}(z) = 1\) (only the empty tree of size \(0\) is allowed when even the root at \(d_L = 0\) is forbidden). For \(k \ge 0\), a non-empty tree has a root (\(z\)), a left subtree of max left-depth \(\le k - 1\) (\(F_{k-1}(z)\)), and a right subtree of max left-depth \(\le k\) (\(F_k(z)\)):

\[F_k(z) = 1 + z F_{k-1}(z) F_k(z) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad F_k(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z F_{k-1}(z)}.\]

Writing \(F_{k-1}(z) = \frac{Q_k(z)}{Q_{k+1}(z)}\) with \(Q_0(z) = 1, Q_1(z) = 1\) yields the linear recurrence:

\[F_k(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z \frac{Q_k(z)}{Q_{k+1}(z)}} = \frac{Q_{k+1}(z)}{Q_{k+1}(z) - z Q_k(z)} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad Q_{k+2}(z) = Q_{k+1}(z) - z Q_k(z).\]

Thus the OGF of valid equivalence classes over \(\{1, \dots, m\}\) (\(k = m - 1\)) is:

\[F_{m-1}(z) = \frac{Q_m(z)}{Q_{m+1}(z)}, \qquad \text{where } Q_r(z) = \sum_{j = 0}^{\lfloor r / 2 \rfloor} (-1)^j \binom{r - j}{j} z^j.\]

Step 3: Extraction via Both fps_inv (\(O(n \log n)\)) and Catalan Residue (\(O(n)\))

By the Catalan Residue Substitution \(z = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\) (15.3B):

\[[z^n] \frac{Q_m(z)}{Q_{m+1}(z)} = [u^n] (1 + u)^{2n} (1 - u) \frac{1 - u^{m+1}}{1 - u^{m+2}} = [u^n] (1 + u)^{2n} \sum_{k = 0}^{\infty} (1 - u)\bigl(u^{k(m+2)} - u^{k(m+2) + m + 1}\bigr).\]

Extracting \([u^n]\) term-by-term gives the closed-form \(O(n/m + 1)\) summation:

\[\text{Ans}(n, m) = \sum_{k = 0}^{\lfloor n / (m + 2) \rfloor} \left[ \binom{2n}{n - k(m+2)} - \binom{2n}{n - k(m+2) - 1} - \binom{2n}{n - k(m+2) - m - 1} + \binom{2n}{n - k(m+2) - m - 2} \right].\]

  • Verification on \(n = 3, m = 2\): Here \(m + 2 = 4 > n = 3\), so only \(k = 0\) contributes: \[\binom{6}{3} - \binom{6}{2} - \binom{6}{3 - 3} + \binom{6}{3 - 4} = 20 - 15 - 1 + 0 = \mathbf{4}.\] Matches our hand trace!
