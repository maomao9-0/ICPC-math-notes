09 · Practice — Formal Power Series

The three solved exercises each attack one contract from the chapter: normalization before a logarithm, coefficients that are invisible at a given precision, and reading a convolution as a product you can divide by. Do them before the contest problem — they are small enough to finish on paper.

Solved exercises

A. Normalize before taking logarithms

Over the rationals, find the normalized logarithm of \(A=2+2x\) through degree three. What is its exponential?

TipHint

The logarithmic derivative ignores a constant multiplicative factor.

NoteSolution

\(A'/A=1/(1+x)=1-x+x^2+\cdots\), so normalized \(\log A=x-x^2/2+x^3/3\). Its exponential is \(1+x\pmod{x^4}\), not \(2+2x\). Recovering \(A\) requires remembering its constant factor separately.

B. Invisible coefficients

How many roots does \(B^2=0\pmod{x^5}\) have over \(\mathbb F_5\)?

TipHint

Use the first nonzero coefficient rather than solving five equations.

NoteSolution

A nonzero square has valuation twice the valuation of its root, so \(v(B)\ge3\). Precisely the polynomials \(b_3x^3+b_4x^4\) work, giving \(5^2=25\) roots including zero. This count is in the truncated ring, not the infinite domain \(\mathbb F_5[[x]]\).

C. Undo a first-occurrence convolution

Suppose events observed at time \(n\) split uniquely by their first occurrence: \(g_n=\sum_{i=0}^nf_i h_{n-i}\) with \(h_0=1\). Given \(G=1/(1-2x)\) and \(H=1/(1-x)\), find all \(f_n\).

TipHint

Interpret the convolution as a product before selecting an FPS operation.

NoteSolution

\(FH=G\) and \(H\) is a unit, so \(F=(1-x)/(1-2x)\). Thus \(f_0=1\), and for \(n\ge1\), \(f_n=2^n-2^{n-1}=2^{n-1}\). The decomposition must be unique; allowing several candidate first occurrences would overcount and invalidate the product equation. For arbitrary prefixes, one inverse and one convolution compute the answer in \(O(M(n))\).
