09 — Transforms on Subsets and Divisors

This chapter is about changing coordinates. A convolution that appears quadratic often becomes pointwise multiplication after the correct transform. The central habit is to identify the underlying partially ordered set (poset): subset inclusion, divisibility, or the Boolean group under XOR.

1. Subset zeta and Möbius transforms

For a function f[S] on subsets of an n-element universe, the subset zeta transform is

\[F[S]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}f[T].\]

Process one bit at a time, adding the state without the current bit into the state with it; invert by subtracting in the same schedule. After processing bits 0..i, the current value has summed over all ways of deleting those bits. This induction invariant explains the transform—not merely its loop pattern.

Both take \(O(n2^n)\) time and \(O(1)\) extra memory. The inverse works because the Möbius function of the Boolean lattice is \(\mu(T,S)=(-1)^{|S\setminus T|}\). Reverse neither outer loop ordering nor the update direction: each butterfly is simply the invertible map (a,b) -> (a,a+b).

The superset variants switch the condition and update f[s] += f[s | 1<<b]. Recognition signals include “sum over all submasks/supermasks”, compatibility masks, and inclusion–exclusion over missing features.

OR and AND convolution

For \(h[S]=\sum_{A\cup B=S}f[A]g[B]\), subset-zeta both arrays, multiply pointwise, then subset-Möbius. For \(A\cap B=S\), use superset-zeta instead. Complexity is \(O(n2^n)\).

2. XOR convolution and FWT

XOR masks form the group \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\). Its characters are \(\chi_k(x)=(-1)^{\langle k,x\rangle}\). Therefore

\[\widehat f[k]=\sum_x f[x]\chi_k(x),\qquad \widehat{f *_\oplus g}[k]=\widehat f[k]\widehat g[k].\]

The Walsh–Hadamard butterfly evaluates these characters recursively.

Over a field the characteristic must not be two, and n must be invertible. Under a prime modulus use inv(n). With integers, transform, multiply using a wide type, then divide exactly on inversion. OR/AND FWT uses triangular butterflies and has no normalization.

Worked example. With f[0]=1,f[1]=2 and g[0]=3,g[1]=4, the XOR convolution is [1·3+2·4, 1·4+2·3]=[11,10]. Their transforms are [3,-1] and [7,-1]; pointwise product [21,1], inverse butterfly gives [11,10].

3. Subset convolution

The disjoint-union convolution is

\[h[S]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}f[T]g[S\setminus T].\]

Plain enumeration costs \(O(3^n)\). Split each array by rank: \(f_k[S]=f[S]\) if \(|S|=k\), else zero. Zeta-transform every rank, form

\[H_k[S]=\sum_{i=0}^k F_i[S]G_{k-i}[S],\]

Möbius-transform each rank, and take \(h[S]=H_{|S|}[S]\). Terms with overlapping operands cancel under Möbius inversion. Time \(O(n^2 2^n)\) and memory \(O(n2^n)\).

Use it when the transition partitions a set into two labeled, disjoint parts and n is around 20. If one side can be enumerated sparsely, \(O(3^n)\) may be smaller and easier.

4. Divisor-poset transforms

On positive integers ordered by divisibility:

\[F[x]=\sum_{d\mid x}f[d],\qquad G[x]=\sum_{x\mid m}f[m].\]

For arrays up to N, the first transform adds from i to its multiples; its inverse subtracts in the same prime-by-prime schedule. A linear-sieve list of primes makes the structural analogy with subset bits explicit.

These divisor transforms diagonalize LCM convolution: zeta over divisors, multiply, invert. For GCD convolution, use sums over multiples; process x downward for the forward transform and upward for its inverse. Complexity is \(O(N\log\log N)\) with prime-stage transforms (or \(O(N\log N)\) with direct divisor loops).

Preconditions: indices start at 1; define deliberately whether zero exists. gcd(0,x) and lcm(0,x) make zero a special element, so most contest implementations handle it separately.

5. Pitfalls and decision guide

  • A | B, A & B, and A ^ B require three different transforms; visual similarity is not algebraic equivalence.
  • Möbius inversion must be performed in the same ring as the sums. Under a modulus, normalize subtraction.
  • \(O(n^2 2^n)\) subset convolution becomes memory-bound first. Use contiguous rank-major storage.
  • SOS DP may need only one transformed array, not a convolution. Do not pay for an inverse you do not need.
  • For counting reachable XORs, a linear basis can beat FWT; FWT is appropriate when multiplicities/distributions matter for every XOR value.

6. Practice, in order

Labels: Foundation isolates one idea; Advanced combines it; Mastery requires recognition; Capstone is suitable for team practice. All statements below are available in English.

  1. Foundation — Compatible Numbers, CF 165E (English, 2200). Superset/subset SOS DP. Official tutorial.
    • Prerequisite: bitmasks.
    • Why: the canonical “find a disjoint present mask” transform.
      Hints
    1. A number compatible with x is a submask of the complement of x.
    2. Store any present witness, then propagate witnesses from submasks.
    3. Run subset zeta with “choose a nonempty source” instead of addition.
  2. Advanced — Jzzhu and Numbers, CF 449D (English, 2400). Superset zeta plus inclusion–exclusion. Official tutorial.
    • Prerequisite: counting subsequences, Möbius inversion.
    • Why: converts AND-zero into counts of elements containing each mask.
      Hints
    1. Count subsequences whose AND contains S.
    2. If c[S] input values contain S, there are 2^c[S]-1 nonempty choices.
    3. Möbius-invert, or take the alternating sum by popcount.
  3. Advanced — Nim Counting, AtCoder ABC212 H (English, 600). XOR FWT. Official editorial.
    • Prerequisite: modular arithmetic, generating counts by length.
    • Why: forces character-space reasoning rather than treating FWT as a black box.
      Hints
    1. Winning/losing is determined by the XOR of chosen pile sizes.
    2. Transform the one-choice frequency distribution.
    3. Sum transformed powers for allowed sequence lengths, invert, and inspect XOR zero.
  4. Mastery — Sum the Fibonacci, CF 914G (English, 2600). AND/OR/XOR convolutions together. Official tutorial.
    • Prerequisite: all three FWT variants, careful modular implementation.
    • Why: a rare full transform toolbox stress test.
      Hints
    1. Express each stage as a convolution under the operation in its subscript.
    2. Compute frequency arrays; transform–multiply–invert one operation at a time.
    3. Track ordered versus unordered triples and the final Fibonacci weighting.
