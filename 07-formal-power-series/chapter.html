07 — Formal Power Series

An FPS \(A(x)=\sum_{i\ge0}a_ix^{i}\) is an infinite coefficient sequence used as algebra in \(R[[x]]\), not an analytic function. We never substitute a real \(x\). Saying two series are equal modulo \(x^n\) means only that their first \(n\) coefficients agree. Therefore every calculation requested to precision \(n\) is finite, and an approximation with \(m\) correct coefficients can be improved systematically. This viewpoint makes Newton iteration natural.

Why this topic matters, prerequisites, and objectives

Recognition signals are equations between whole coefficient sequences: an inverse to undo a convolution, an exponential to assemble labelled components, or an implicit equation whose low coefficients determine higher ones. Read polynomial operations and units in rings. Counting interpretations are developed further in generating functions. You should learn to prove precision doubling, specify existence and uniqueness, track leading zeros, and choose sparse recurrences versus dense operations.

The coefficient ring \(R\) is commutative with identity. The notation \(R[[x]]\) means all sequences \((a_0,a_1,\ldots)\), with coefficientwise addition and convolution multiplication. Each product coefficient is a finite sum. The valuation \(v(A)\) is the first nonzero coefficient index, with \(v(0)=\infty\). Over a field, \(v(AB)=v(A)+v(B)\) for nonzero series; over a ring with zero divisors only the inequality \(v(AB)\ge v(A)+v(B)\) is automatic. This inequality suffices for error squaring.

Writing \(A\equiv B\pmod{x^n}\) means \(A-B=x^nC\) for a series \(C\). Multiplication by any series preserves this agreement. Differentiation loses one coefficient of precision; integration with constant zero gains one when its divisions are legal. This is an information contract for every operation. A series is a unit precisely when \(a_0\) is: in \(AB=1\), first solve \(b_0=a_0^{-1}\) and then determine \(b_j\) recursively from earlier coefficients.

Let \(M(n)\) be polynomial multiplication time, \(O(n\log n)\) with NTT. All operations below truncate immediately to the requested precision.

1. Differential toolkit and contracts

\[ A'=\sum_{i\ge1}ia_ix^{i-1},\qquad \int A=\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{a_i}{i+1}x^{i+1}. \]

For example, if \(A=1+2x+3x^2\), then \(A'=2+6x\). Integration divides coefficient \(x^i\) by \(i+1\). To produce coefficients of degrees below \(n\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), it therefore needs \(1,\ldots,n-1\) to be nonzero, equivalently \(n\le p\). The table records the usual contracts over a field; in a more general ring, replace every “nonzero” condition used for division by “invertible.”

operation required constant term defining identity
\(\operatorname{inv}(A)\) \(a_0\) is a unit \(AB=1\)
normalized \(\log(A)\) \(a_0\) and integers \(1,\ldots,n-1\) are units at precision \(n\) \((\log A)'=A'/A\), constant term \(0\)
\(\exp(A)\) \(a_0=0\); required integration denominators are units \(\log(\exp A)=A\)
\(\operatorname{pow}(A,k)\) handle leading zeros \(A^{k}\)
\(\sqrt{A}\) even valuation; leading coefficient a square (odd characteristic) \(B^{2}=A\)
\(\operatorname{compose}(A,B)\) \(b_0=0\) for finite-prefix dependence \(A(B(x))\)
\(\operatorname{revert}(A)\) \(a_0=0,a_1\) is a unit \(A(B(x))=x\)

2. Newton inversion

Suppose \(B\) is an inverse approximation: \(AB\equiv1\pmod{x^{m}}\). The error \(E=1-AB\) has no terms below degree \(m\). Rather than solve missing coefficients individually, correct all of them in one multiplication: \[ B_{new}=B(2-AB)\pmod{x^{2m}}. \] Indeed \(AB_{new}=AB(2-AB)=(1-E)(1+E)=1-E^2\). Thus the old error is squared; if \(E\) starts at degree \(m\), the new error starts at degree \(2m\). Precision doubles, and the total cost over lengths \(1,2,4,\ldots,n\) is \(O(M(n))\).

For \(A=1-x-x^2\), start \(B=1\). The first update modulo \(x^2\) gives \(1+x\). Using that prefix, \(AB=1-2x^2-x^3\), so the next correction is \((1+x)(1+2x^2+x^3)=1+x+2x^2+3x^3\pmod{x^4}\). The invariant is \(AB\equiv1\) at the current precision; no coefficient is guessed from a numerical approximation. At the final step use \(\min(2m,n)\), so a non-power-of-two request has exact boundaries too.

Precision doubling should operate on a truncated copy so that a caller’s longer series is not accidentally destroyed.

3. Logarithm and exponential

For a unit series with constant coefficient \(1\), define the logarithm to be the unique zero-constant series whose derivative is \(A'/A\):

\[ \log A=\int A'A^{-1}\,dx, \]

in \(O(M(n))\). More generally \(A'/A\) makes sense whenever \(A[0]\) is a unit, but the integral above deliberately discards the scalar factor \(A[0]\): it is a normalized logarithm with constant term zero. Thus \(\exp(\log A)=A\) only under the clean contract \(A[0]=1\); otherwise it equals \(A/A[0]\). The divisions from integration also require \(1,\ldots,n-1\) to be units at precision \(n\).

For exponential, the desired identity is \(\log B=A\). If \(B\) is already correct in degrees below \(m\), then \(A-\log B\) begins at degree \(m\), so multiplying by \(1+A-\log B\) corrects only unknown coefficients: \[ B_{new}=B(1+A-\log B)\pmod{x^{2m}}. \]

The bracket is \(1 \bmod x^{m}\), so known coefficients remain unchanged. Standard implementations are \(O(M(n))\), though careless repeated inversions may add a constant or logarithmic factor.

To prove the improvement, set \(E=A-\log B\), so \(v(E)\ge m\). At precision \(2m\), all powers \(E^j\) for \(j\ge2\) vanish, hence \(\log(1+E)\equiv E\). Therefore \(\log(B(1+E))\equiv\log B+E=A\pmod{x^{2m}}\). The logarithm product identity follows by differentiating both sides and matching zero constants; the required integers through the working degree must be units. This proves both preservation and correction.

For example, over the rationals, \(\exp(x+x^2)\) begins \(1+x+\tfrac32x^2+\tfrac76x^3\). From \(B'= (1+2x)B\) we get \(b_1=1\), \(2b_2=b_1+2b_0=3\), and \(3b_3=b_2+2b_1=7/2\). Over \(\mathbb F_5\) the same prefix is \((1,1,4,2)\). At degree five the usual division recurrence cannot continue: \(5b_5\) loses information.

4. Powers and square roots

When the logarithm and exponential contracts above hold, \(A(0)=1\) gives \(A^{k}=\exp(k\log A)\) because logarithm turns multiplication into addition. Here \(k=0\) is the separate identity case \(A^0=1\). For nonnegative \(k\) and a nonzero series whose leading coefficient is a unit, find the first nonzero term \(a_tx^{t}\). A nonzero field coefficient is sufficient; a nonzero coefficient in a general ring need not be. Under this additional condition:

\[ A^k=x^{tk}a_t^k \exp\!\left(k\log\frac{A}{a_tx^t}\right). \]

If \(tk \ge n\), the truncated answer is zero. This handles huge nonnegative \(k\) without reducing \(k\) when computing the shift; only the scalar \(k\) multiplying \(\log A\) is reduced in the coefficient field. Negative powers require a unit series and are a different contract.

Over a field of odd characteristic, solve \(B^2-A=0\). For a unit series, the ordinary Newton correction \(B-(B^2-A)/(2B)\) simplifies to \[ B_{new}=\frac12(B+A/B). \] Start with a square root of the constant coefficient. For a nonunit \(A\), first strip an even valuation \(2t\) and solve for the remaining unit series; an odd finite valuation makes a root impossible. Tonelli–Shanks supplies the initial scalar root over an odd prime field. A unit series over an odd prime field has roots exactly when its constant coefficient is a quadratic residue; then it has exactly two, differing by sign. For a positive even valuation, shifting either unit root gives two canonical roots when the unused high coefficients are set to zero. Coefficients of \(B\) in degrees at least \(n-t\) are invisible in \(B^2\bmod x^n\) and may be arbitrary. Thus a nonunit truncated series can have more than two roots. The zero series modulo \(x^n\) is another non-unique case; choose the all-zero root unless the problem states a different normalization. Problems must specify a canonical root or accept any valid one.

Worked failure: counting roots of a truncated nonunit

Over \(\mathbb F_5\), let \(A=x^2\pmod{x^5}\). Write \(B=b_1x+b_2x^2+b_3x^3+b_4x^4\). Comparing degrees two, three, and four gives \(b_1^2=1\), \(2b_1b_2=0\), and \(b_2^2+2b_1b_3=0\). Thus \(b_1=\pm1\), \(b_2=b_3=0\), while \(b_4\) is arbitrary: there are ten roots \(B=\pm x+cx^4\). In the infinite series ring the exact equation \(B^2=x^2\) has only \(B=\pm x\). Confusing exact equality with a truncated congruence causes the erroneous “exactly two roots” conclusion.

5. Composition

Naive composition computes powers \(1,B,B^{2},\ldots\) in \(O(nM(n))\). Brent–Kung baby-step/giant-step chooses \(k\approx\sqrt n\):

\[ A(B)=\sum_q B^{qk}\left(\sum_{r<k}a_{qk+r}B^r\right). \]

Precompute baby powers \(B^{r}\), form each block polynomial, then combine by Horner in \(B^{k}\). Merely forming every dense block as a separate linear combination can still cost \(O(n^2)\). The full Brent–Kung algorithm batches those combinations with rectangular matrix multiplication; its classical FFT bound is \(O((n\log n)^{3/2})\) field operations. Exact practical complexity and memory depend on the polynomial- and matrix-multiplication routines. The block decomposition is the conceptual step; it is not by itself a proof of an \(O(M(n)\sqrt n)\) implementation.

The condition \(B[0]=0\) is about information flow: \(B(x)^r\) then starts at degree \(r\), so a coefficient below \(n\) can only use \(a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}\). With a nonzero constant term, arbitrarily high powers of \(B\) can influence the constant coefficient, so a finite prefix does not generally determine the composition.

6. Compositional inverse (reversion)

For \(A(0)=0\) and \(A'(0)\) a unit, there is one series \(B\) beginning with \(x/A'(0)\) that satisfies \(A(B)=x\). Newton solves the equation \(A(B)-x=0\) by the familiar tangent correction:

\[ B_{new}=B-\frac{A(B)-x}{A'(B)}\pmod{x^{2m}}. \]

This needs composition plus multiplicative inversion and has the same asymptotic bottleneck as the chosen composition routine. If only one or a structured set of coefficients is needed, Lagrange inversion is usually better:

For integers \(1\le k\le n\),

\[ [x^n]B(x)^k=\frac{k}{n}[t^{n-k}]\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^n. \]

This divided form requires \(n\) to be invertible in the coefficient field; in small characteristic, use a division-free identity or a method designed for that characteristic.

For \(A=x+x^2\), write \(B=x+b_2x^2+b_3x^3+\cdots\). The condition \(B+B^2=x\) first gives \(b_2=-1\), then \(b_3+2b_2=0\), so \(b_3=2\). Thus \(B=x-x^2+2x^3+\cdots\). The derivative in the Newton denominator has constant term \(A'(0)\), so it remains a unit throughout. Composition is the expensive step; reversion should not be billed at ordinary multiplication cost.

7. Sparse series

Dense NTT is wasteful when only \(K\) coefficients of the defining series are nonzero. Coefficient recurrences from derivatives often give \(O(nK)\): for \(B=\exp(A)\), \(B'=A'B\), hence

\[ nb_n=\sum_{i=1}^n i a_i b_{n-i}. \]

Similarly, after \(b_0=a_0^{-1}\), \(B=A^{-1}\) uses \(b_n=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_{n-i}\) for \(n\ge1\). The exponential recurrence additionally divides by \(n\), so it is valid only while each \(1,\ldots,n\) is a unit (for example \(n<MOD\) over \(\mathbb F_{MOD}\)). Choose sparse versus dense by estimated work, not merely by degree.

8. Worked example and debugging invariants

Let \(A=1-x\). Then \(\operatorname{inv}(A) = 1+x+x^{2}+...\), \(\log(A)=-x-x^{2}/2-x^{3}/3-...\), and \(\operatorname{pow}(A,k)\) reproduces \(\sum_i(-1)^{i}{k\choose i}x^{i}\). This tiny example checks signs, integration inverses and truncation.

The point of the example is that every defining equation can be checked without knowing the implementation. When integrating these operations with your library, test algebraic identities on random small series:

  • \(A*\operatorname{inv}(A) = 1 \bmod x^{n}\);
  • \(\log(\exp(A)) = A\) when \(A[0]=0\);
  • \(\exp(\log(A)) = A\) when \(A[0]=1\);
  • \(\sqrt{A}^{2} = A\);
  • \(\operatorname{compose}(A,\operatorname{revert}(A)) = x\).

9. Contest checklist and reference

Before selecting an operation, specify output indices \(0,\ldots,n-1\), coefficient ring, constant normalization, valuation, and required integer divisions. For powers, test \(k=0\) and the zero input before finding a leading coefficient. For logarithms distinguish \(A\) from \(A/A(0)\). For composition, distinguish a finite outer polynomial from an unknown infinite outer series: only the latter generally requires inner constant zero for finite-prefix data.

Operation to precision \(n\) Typical cost Invariant or restriction
Inverse \(O(M(n))\) \(AB=1\); \(a_0\) a unit
Log / exp \(O(M(n))\) Integer divisors 1,…,n−1 units
Nonnegative power via log \(O(M(n))\) plus scalar exponentiation Preserve integer shift \(kv(A)\)
Unit square root \(O(M(n))\) plus scalar root Choose root of \(a_0\); \(2b_0\) a unit
Sparse inverse / exp \(O(nK)\) \(K\) nonzero defining coefficients
General composition / reversion Depends on composition routine Dense block assembly is a real cost

The classical Brent–Kung result gives \(O((n\log n)^{3/2})\) operations for general composition and reversion; the baby-step decomposition alone does not establish that bound. Work the solved exercises to practice mathematical contracts.
