Part V Blind Diagnostic Assessment (Chapters 18–21 · Decoy Elimination & Untagged Contest Triage)

When solving an exercise at the end of Chapter 18 or Chapter 20, the chapter header immediately tells you whether to set up a polynomial cross-correlation over \(\mathbb{F}_{998244353}\) or a window-clipped KMP failure table. In a live contest, problems arrive completely untagged. You are handed a specification about corrupted transmissions, relative trend matching, or exponentially long string macros, together with numerical constraints (\(N \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(k \le 2500\), or \(|T| \le 10^{18}\)), and you must diagnose which algebraic, Kangaroo, structural-isomorphism, or grammar-compression invariant governs the task.

This checkpoint tests blind diagnostic recognition across all four chapters of Part V (Ch 18–Ch 21). None of the visible problem titles or statements in Section 2 reveal which chapter or technique applies.

1. Blind Diagnostic Triage Guide & Decoy Elimination

The most expensive tactical mistake in Part V contest problems is confusing non-transitive algebraic compatibility with transitive structural isomorphism, or confusing approximate matching with grammar-compressed indexing. Before tackling the seven blind problems below, study the two primary Part V Diagnostic Decoy Comparisons and the routing flowchart.

1.1 Decoy Triad A: Polynomial/Involution Matching (Ch 18) vs. Window-Clipped Structural KMP (Ch 20)

Both paradigms generalize exact character equality \(P[j] = T[i+j]\) to a broader compatibility rule across all alignments \(i \in [0, N-M]\). Why does KMP solve some of these in deterministic \(O(N+M)\) time while failing catastrophically on others?

Diagnostic Dimension Ch 18 Non-Transitive Polynomial FFT (T-3B-01, T-3B-02) Ch 18 Involution Exception (\(\pi^2 = \mathrm{id}\) in T-3B-02) Ch 20 Window-Clipped Structural KMP (T-3B-04, T-5B-06)
Governing Compatibility Relation Non-transitive point-wise relation \(R(P_j, T_{i+j})\): wildcards '?', distance \(|a-b| \le k\), general permutation \(T_{i+j} \in \{P_j, \pi(P_j)\}\) (\(\pi^2 \ne \mathrm{id}\)), or affine \(T = \alpha P + \beta\). Symmetric 2-cycle compatibility: \(T_{i+j} \in \{P_j, \pi(P_j)\}\) where \(\pi(\pi(c)) = c\) for every \(c \in \Sigma\). Context-relative substring equivalence \(U \cong V\): bijective renaming (p-KMP), relative rank (Order-KMP), or min-heap topology (Cartesian-KMP).
Is Substring Equivalence Transitive? No: \(\texttt{a} \approx \texttt{?}\) and \(\texttt{?} \approx \texttt{b}\), yet \(\texttt{a} \not\approx \texttt{b}\). Border transitivity collapses completely. Yes: \(\Sigma\) partitions into disjoint 2-cycles \(\{c, \pi(c)\}\) and fixed points; canonicalizing each 2-cycle to \(\min(c, \pi(c))\) restores exact equality! Yes (after window clipping!): \(U \cong V\) and \(V \cong W \implies U \cong W\), and restricting to a suffix preserves equivalence once backward offsets \(> q\) are clipped to \(0\).
Optimal Engine & Time Complexity Index-Reflection FFT / NTT (\(P^R[M-1-j]\)) in \(O(N \log N)\) (1-NTT Indicator, 2-NTT Kalai, or \(\sqrt{M\log M}\) value buckets). Canonicalized Exact KMP in deterministic \(O(N + M)\) time with zero FFTs! Window-Clipped KMP (clip(v, q) = v <= q ? v : 0 or LMax/LMin) in \(O(N + M)\) (or \(O(N + M\log M)\)).
Fatal Misdiagnosis Running KMP on wildcards or general \(\pi\), OR forgetting harmonic multiple sieving bad[d] \|= conflict[k*d] when a single wildcard string must have period \(d\). Paying 3 NTTs or complex FFT precision risk when the given cipher satisfies \(\pi(\pi(c)) = c\). Comparing unclipped prev_T[i] against prev_P[q+1], or caching clip(prev_T[i], q) outside the KMP while (q > 0) loop!

Both paradigms rely on LCE (Longest Common Extension) queries and Run-Length / Periodic Compression to bypass character-by-character scanning. How do you distinguish their operating regimes?

Problem CP-V-01 — Minimum Alphabet-Coalescing Distance

You are given a text string \(T[0..N-1]\) and a pattern string \(P[0..M-1]\) (\(1 \le M \le N \le 1.25 \times 10^5\)), both consisting only of the first six lowercase English letters \(\Sigma = \{\texttt{a}, \texttt{b}, \texttt{c}, \texttt{d}, \texttt{e}, \texttt{f}\}\).

In a single coalescing operation applied simultaneously to a substring \(U = T[i..i+M-1]\) and \(P[0..M-1]\), you may choose any two letters \(x, y \in \Sigma\) and replace every occurrence of \(x\) in both \(U\) and \(P\) with \(y\). For each starting alignment \(i \in \{0, 1, \dots, N - M\}\), compute the minimum number of coalescing operations required to make \(T[i..i+M-1]\) identical to \(P[0..M-1]\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le M \le N \le 1.25 \times 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| = 6\), Time Limit \(4.0\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(256\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination (Ch 18 vs. Ch 20 Decoy Triad): At first sight, renaming characters to make \(T[i..i+M-1]\) match \(P\) sounds like Ch 20 Parameterized Matching (p-KMP). Why does p-KMP fail here? Because p-KMP tests for a bijection (zero coalescing operations, where distinct characters stay distinct), whereas here we are allowed to merge distinct characters into a single equivalence class! Merging characters is a many-to-one quotient map governed by connected components on \(\Sigma\).
  • Diagnostic Signal: At alignment \(i\), position \(j \in [0, M-1]\) demands that character \(P[j]\) and character \(T[i+j]\) be merged into the same equivalence class. Viewing \(\Sigma\) as a \(6\)-vertex undirected graph \(G_i = (\Sigma, E_i)\) where edge \(\{a, b\} \in E_i\) exists iff there is at least one \(j \in [0, M-1]\) with \(\{P[j], T[i+j]\} = \{a, b\}\), the minimum number of coalescing operations at shift \(i\) is simply: \[\sum_{\text{component } C \text{ of } G_i} (|C| - 1) = |\Sigma| - \operatorname{cc}(G_i) = 6 - \operatorname{cc}(G_i).\]
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

How do we determine for all \(i \in [0, N-M]\) and all ordered pairs \((a, b) \in \Sigma \times \Sigma\) (\(a \ne b\)) whether there exists \(j \in [0, M-1]\) with \(P[j] = a\) and \(T[i+j] = b\)? Define binary indicator polynomials using Index-Reflection Duality (Ch 18 Lemma 18.1): \[A_a(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} \mathbf{1}[P[M-1-k] = a]\, x^k, \qquad B_b(x) = \sum_{t=0}^{N-1} \mathbf{1}[T[t] = b]\, x^t.\] Then the coefficient \([x^{i + M - 1}]\bigl(A_a(x) B_b(x)\bigr) = \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} \mathbf{1}[P[j] = a]\, \mathbf{1}[T[i+j] = b]\) counts the exact number of positions \(j\) aligning \(a\) in \(P\) with \(b\) in \(T\). Thus undirected edge \(\{a, b\}\) is present in \(G_i\) if and only if: \[[x^{i + M - 1}]\Bigl(A_a(x) B_b(x) + A_b(x) B_a(x)\Bigr) > 0.\]

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly
  1. Choose FFT length \(L = 2^{\lceil \log_2(N + M - 1) \rceil} \le 2^{18} = 262144\).
  2. Pre-transform Caching: Instead of running an FFT inside the pair loop \((a, b)\), compute the 6 forward DFTs \(\widehat{A}_a = \operatorname{DFT}_L(A_a)\) and 6 forward DFTs \(\widehat{B}_b = \operatorname{DFT}_L(B_b)\) once (only \(12\) forward transforms total, or \(6\) via 2-in-1 complex packing!).
  3. For each of the \(\binom{6}{2} = 15\) unordered pairs \(\{a, b\}\) (\(0 \le a < b < 6\)):
    • Pointwise compute \(\widehat{C}_{a,b}[k] = \widehat{A}_a[k]\widehat{B}_b[k] + \widehat{A}_b[k]\widehat{B}_a[k]\) for \(k \in [0, L-1]\).
    • Run 1 inverse transform \(C_{a,b} = \operatorname{IDFT}_L(\widehat{C}_{a,b})\) (or pack pairs of \(C_{a,b}\) into 8 complex IFFTs).
    • For each shift \(i \in [0, N-M]\), if \(\lfloor C_{a,b}[i + M - 1] + 0.5 \rfloor > 0\), union \(a\) and \(b\) in a 6-element Disjoint Set Union (DSU) for shift \(i\).
  4. Output \(6 - \operatorname{cc}(G_i)\) for each \(i \in [0, N-M]\).
TipComplete Derivation, Complexity Proof & Reference Solution — CP-V-01

  • Target Chapter & Technique ID: Chapter 18 (T-3B-01: Index-Reflection Cross-Correlation, Frequency-Domain Reuse & Dual-Real Complex FFT Packing; CF 954I — Yet Another String Matching Problem).

  • Why Naive Pairwise Convolution TLEs & How Frequency Reuse Cuts Transforms by \(3.4\times\): There are \(6 \times 5 = 30\) ordered pairs \((a, b)\) with \(a \ne b\). A naive loop calling multiply(A_a, B_b) 30 times executes \(30 \times 3 = 90\) FFTs of length \(2^{18}\). By computing \(\widehat{A}_0, \dots, \widehat{A}_5\) and \(\widehat{B}_0, \dots, \widehat{B}_5\) up front (\(12\) transforms) and combining \(\widehat{A}_a \odot \widehat{B}_b + \widehat{A}_b \odot \widehat{B}_a\) in the frequency domain before inverting for each of the \(\binom{6}{2} = 15\) undirected edges (\(15\) inverse transforms), the total transform count drops from \(90\) to \(27\) (and to \(6 + 8 = 14\) complex FFTs with conjugate packing!).

  • Boundary Pitfall Audit (BP-5-01):

    • Re-running Forward DFTs Inside the \((a, b)\) Loop: Causes immediate Time Limit Exceeded. Always cache the \(|\Sigma|\) forward DFTs whenever pairwise alphabet cross-correlations are needed.

  • Complexity: \(O\bigl((|\Sigma| + \binom{|\Sigma|}{2}) N \log N + |\Sigma|^2 N \alpha(|\Sigma|)\bigr)\) time and \(O(|\Sigma| N)\) space.

Problem CP-V-02 — Dual-Mode Telemetry Frame Repair

A satellite telemetry frame \(S[0..N-1]\) (\(2 \le N \le 5 \times 10^5\)) is transmitted over a 26-letter alphabet \(\Sigma = \{\texttt{a}, \dots, \texttt{z}\}\) together with erasure markers '?'. You are also given a hardware cipher permutation \(\tau: \Sigma \to \Sigma\) that is self-inverse (\(\tau(\tau(c)) = c\) for every \(c \in \Sigma\)). Each '?' in \(S\) represents a lost character that can be replaced by any letter in \(\Sigma\).

You must analyze the self-overlap structure of \(S\) under two distinct engineering specifications:

  1. Mode A (Two-Copy Paired-Cipher Border Check): Suppose two independent receivers receive copies of \(S\) and fill in their '?' erasures independently to produce concrete strings \(S_1, S_2 \in \Sigma^N\). Find all proper border lengths \(b \in \{1, 2, \dots, N-1\}\) for which there exist repairs \(S_1, S_2\) satisfying \(S_1[k] = \tau(S_2[N - b + k])\) for all \(0 \le k < b\).
  2. Mode B (Single-Copy Consistent Period & Border Check, \(\tau = \mathrm{id}\)): Now suppose \(\tau\) is the identity (\(\tau(c) = c\)) and a single repaired string \(S^* \in \Sigma^N\) must be formed by filling each '?' in \(S\) once. Find all proper border lengths \(b \in \{1, 2, \dots, N-1\}\) for which there exists a single consistent repair \(S^*\) satisfying \(S^*[0..b-1] = S^*[N-b..N-1]\).
  • Constraints: \(2 \le N \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(\tau(\tau(c)) = c\), Time Limit \(2.0\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(256\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination: Why do Mode A and Mode B produce different answers whenever \(b > N / 2\)?
    • In Mode A, the prefix \(S_1[0..b-1]\) and suffix \(S_2[N-b..N-1]\) come from two independent repairs of \(S\). Even when \(b > N/2\) (so index \(k \in [N-b, b-1]\) lies in both the prefix and the suffix), \(S_1[k]\) and \(S_2[k]\) are chosen independently! Thus \(b\) is valid in Mode A iff shift \(d = N - b\) has no direct conflict between \(S[k]\) and \(S[k + d]\).
    • In Mode B, there is only one string \(S^*\). If \(b > N/2\) and \(S[d] = \texttt{?}\), then \(S^*[d]\) must simultaneously equal \(S^*[0]\) (from \(k = 0\)) and \(S^*[2d]\) (from \(k = d\)). By Period–Border Duality (Ch 01), \(S^*\) has border \(b\) iff \(S^*\) has period \(d = N - b\), which holds iff every residue class modulo \(d\) contains at most one distinct concrete letter—i.e., no multiple \(m = c \cdot d < N\) has a direct conflict \(S[k] \ne S[k + m]\)!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

  1. Handling the Involution \(\tau\) Without Increasing Polynomial Degree: In Mode A, we require \(S_1[k] = \tau(S_2[k + d])\) whenever both \(S[k]\) and \(S[k + d]\) are non-'?'. Define the \(\tau\)-transformed copy \(T[j] = \tau(S[j])\) (with \(\tau(\texttt{?}) = \texttt{?}\)). Then the condition \(S_1[k] = \tau(S_2[k+d])\) between \(S\) and \(S\) is 100% equivalent to standard wildcard compatibility \(S[k] \approx T[k + d]\) between \(S\) and \(T\)! (Even better: if there were no wildcards and the rule were \(T[i+j] \in \{P[j], \tau(P[j])\}\) as in CF 1334G with \(\tau^2 = \mathrm{id}\), collapsing each 2-cycle \(\{c, \tau(c)\}\) to \(\min(c, \tau(c))\) solves it via \(O(N)\) KMP with zero FFTs!)

  2. Single-Prime Collision-Free Degree-Reduced Indicator NTT (Ch 18 T-3B-01): Map '?' to \((u_x, v_x) = (0, 0)\) and each letter \(c \in \Sigma\) to \((1, \operatorname{ord}(c) \in \{1, \dots, 26\})\). For shift \(d \in [1, N-1]\), the discrepancy between \(S[0..N-1-d]\) and \(T[d..N-1]\) is: \[D_{\mathrm{ind}}(d) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1-d} u_{S[k]}\, u_{T[k+d]}\, \bigl(v_{S[k]} - v_{T[k+d]}\bigr)^2.\] Because \(0 \le D_{\mathrm{ind}}(d) \le N (|\Sigma| - 1)^2 = 5 \times 10^5 \times 625 = 3.125 \times 10^8 < 998244353\), a single NTT prime \(Q = 998244353\) has zero modular wrap-around and zero false positives!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly

  1. Mode A: Build \(T[j] = (S[j] == \texttt{?} \text{ ? } \texttt{?} : \tau(S[j]))\). Reverse \(S\) to \(S^R[k] = S[N-1-k]\) and evaluate: \[D_{\mathrm{ind}}(d) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1-d} (u_{S[k]} v_{S[k]}^2)\, u_{T[k+d]} - 2 \sum_{k=0}^{N-1-d} (u_{S[k]} v_{S[k]})\, (u_{T[k+d]} v_{T[k+d]}) + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1-d} u_{S[k]}\, (u_{T[k+d]} v_{T[k+d]}^2)\] at convolution index \((N - 1 - k) + (k + d) = N - 1 + d\) using 3 NTT forward pairs + 1 accumulated INTT modulo \(998244353\). Border \(b = N - d\) is valid in Mode A iff \(D_{\mathrm{ind}}(d) = 0\).

  2. Mode B (\(\tau = \mathrm{id}\), so \(T = S\)): Using the conflict array conflict[m] = (D_ind(m) > 0) for \(m \in [1, N-1]\), run a harmonic sieve over all multiples of \(d = N - b\):

    for (int d = 1; d < N; ++d)
    for (int m = d; m < N; m += d)
        if (conflict[m]) { bad_period[d] = true; break; }

    Border \(b\) is valid in Mode B iff !bad_period[N - b].

TipComplete Derivation, Complexity Proof & Reference Solution — CP-V-02

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination

For any prefix length \(m\), the relative order of \(A[i..i+m-1]\) is uniquely determined by the sequence of local rank pairs \((\operatorname{less}_i(k), \operatorname{eq}_i(k))\) for \(k = 0, \dots, m - 1\), where: \[\operatorname{less}_i(k) = \#\bigl\{r \in [i, i + k - 1] : A[r] < A[i + k]\bigr\}, \qquad \operatorname{eq}_i(k) = \#\bigl\{r \in [i, i + k - 1] : A[r] = A[i + k]\bigr\}.\] Equivalently, by Kubica et al.’s Immediate-Neighbor Sufficiency (Ch 20 §2.1 / Theorem 20.3), if \(A[i..i+k-1] \cong_{\mathrm{ord}} A[j..j+k-1]\), then \(A[i..i+k] \cong_{\mathrm{ord}} A[j..j+k]\) iff \(A[j+k]\) satisfies the exact same < or == relation against the immediate predecessor witness \(A[j + \operatorname{LMax}_i(k)]\) and successor witness \(A[j + \operatorname{LMin}_i(k)]\) that \(A[i+k]\) satisfies in \(A[i..i+k-1]\)!

    • Even simpler to implement without dynamic tree splits: in prefix-doubling step \(h = 0, 1, \dots, \lceil \log_2 N \rceil\), two substrings \(A[i..i+2^h-1]\) and \(A[j..j+2^h-1]\) are order-isomorphic iff they have the same order-isomorphism class at length \(2^{h-1}\) and the merged sorted order of their left and right halves matches—or by maintaining a Persistent Fenwick/Segment Tree to query \((\operatorname{less}_i(k), \operatorname{eq}_i(k))\) in \(O(\log N)\) time at the first mismatching offset!
  3. Once all \(N\) suffixes are sorted into \(\operatorname{SA}[1..N]\) with adjacent common prefix lengths \(\operatorname{ord\text{-}LCP}[r] = \operatorname{ord\text{-}LCE}(\operatorname{SA}[r-1], \operatorname{SA}[r])\), output: \[\sum_{r=1}^{N} \bigl(N - \operatorname{SA}[r] + 1 - \operatorname{ord\text{-}LCP}[r]\bigr).\]

Problem CP-V-05 — Compressed Binary Composition Oracle

A high-frequency binary sensor stream \(S \in \{\texttt{0}, \texttt{1}\}^N\) of huge total length \(N \le 10^9\) is stored in compressed form as \(R\) alternating maximal contiguous blocks (\(1 \le R \le 5000\)): \[S = c_1^{\ell_1} c_2^{\ell_2} \dots c_R^{\ell_R}, \qquad c_k \in \{\texttt{0}, \texttt{1}\}, \quad c_k \ne c_{k+1}, \quad \sum_{k=1}^{R} \ell_k = N.\] You are given \(Q\) online queries (\(1 \le Q \le 10^5\)). Each query provides two non-negative integers \((z, o)\) with \(1 \le z + o \le N\) and asks whether there exists at least one contiguous substring of \(S\) of length \(L = z + o\) that contains exactly \(z\) zeros and \(o\) ones. Output YES or NO for each query.

  • Constraints: \(1 \le R \le 5000\), \(1 \le N = \sum \ell_k \le 10^9\), \(1 \le Q \le 10^5\), Time Limit \(2.0\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(256\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination (Ch 20 RLE Parikh Envelope vs. Ch 21 SLP Seam Matching):
    • Because \(S\) is compressed to length \(N \le 10^9\), it is tempting to treat \(S\) as a grammar-compressed string (Ch 21). However, the query does not specify the order of 0s and 1s—it only asks for the multiset (Parikh vector) \((z, o)\)!
    • Conversely, in standard uncompressed binary jumbled matching (Ch 20 ladder problem W3B-34), we precompute the minimum and maximum 1-counts \([m_L, M_L]\) for every window length \(L \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) in \(O(N)\) space. Here \(N \le 10^9\) makes an \(O(N)\)-element array impossible, while the number of runs \(R \le 5000\) is small!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

By the Binary Parikh Interval Continuity Theorem (Ch 20 §3.1 / Theorem 20.5 — Burcsi et al. 2010), for any fixed window length \(L \in [1, N]\), sliding a window of length \(L\) by one character changes its 1-count by \(S[i+L] - S[i] \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\). By the discrete Intermediate Value Theorem, the set of achievable 1-counts of length \(L\) is the entire contiguous integer interval \([m_L, M_L]\): \[\exists\, \text{substring of } S \text{ with } (z, o) \iff m_{z+o} \le o \le M_{z+o}.\] Now exploit the Run-Length Encoding \(c_1^{\ell_1} \dots c_R^{\ell_R}\):

The RLE Extremal Boundary Alignment Lemma: For any window length \(L \in [1, N]\), the maximum 1-count \(M_L\) is always achieved by a window \([p, p + L - 1]\) where at least one endpoint (\(p\) or \(p + L - 1\)) coincides with a boundary between a 1-run and a 0-run (or lies completely inside a single 1-run)! Why? If neither \(p\) nor \(p + L - 1\) touches a run boundary, we can slide the window left or right without crossing any run boundary:

  • If \(S[p]\) and \(S[p + L]\) are equal, sliding preserves the 1-count until an endpoint hits a run boundary;
  • If \(S[p] = \texttt{0}\) and \(S[p + L] = \texttt{1}\), sliding right strictly increases the 1-count!
NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly
  1. Let \(P_k = \sum_{r=1}^k \ell_r\) be the prefix lengths and \(O_k = \sum_{r=1}^k \mathbf{1}[c_r = \texttt{1}] \ell_r\) be the prefix 1-counts (\(0 \le k \le R\)).
  2. Consider any pair of run indices \(1 \le i \le j \le R\):
    • Maximum Envelope \(M_L\): When the left endpoint of the window starts at the beginning of a 1-run \(i\) (\(P_{i-1}\)) and the right endpoint moves across run \(j\) (\(L \in [P_{j-1} - P_{i-1}, P_j - P_{i-1}]\)):
      • If \(c_j = \texttt{1}\), the 1-count increases linearly with slope \(+1\): \(o(L) = (O_{j-1} - O_{i-1}) + \bigl(L - (P_{j-1} - P_{i-1})\bigr)\), i.e., the number of 0s \(z = L - o(L) = (P_{j-1} - P_{i-1}) - (O_{j-1} - O_{i-1})\) is constant across the entire segment!
      • If \(c_j = \texttt{0}\), the 1-count stays constant at \(O_{j-1} - O_{i-1}\) while \(L\) increases.
  3. Even simpler in \((z, o)\) coordinates:
    • For a fixed number of zeros \(z\), what is the maximum number of ones \(M_{\mathrm{ones}}(z)\) that can accompany \(z\) zeros in a substring? Any such substring contains some contiguous range of 0-runs cleanly inside it plus partial 0-runs at its two ends, and grabs 100% of the adjacent outer 1-runs on both sides!

Then for any \(z\) obtained by taking \(Z(i, j)\) plus at most \(\ell_{i-1} + \ell_{j+1}\) boundary zeros, we can achieve all \(O(i, j)\) ones!

  • Inside \(T\), RComp compresses the surrounding run of as into \(\langle \texttt{a}, 5 \rangle\), whereas at the left end of \(P\), RComp sees only \(\langle \texttt{a}, 2 \rangle\)!
  • Similarly, during PComp (\(\Sigma_L \times \Sigma_R\)), if \(P\) starts with a symbol \(P[0] \in \Sigma_R\), then inside \(T\) that symbol \(P[0]\) might pair with the preceding character \(T[i-1] \in \Sigma_L\), whereas at the very front of \(P\) there is no preceding character!

Jeż’s Boundary-Popping Synchronization Invariant (2015): Before executing RComp or PComp at level \(h\) on both \(P\) and \(T\):

  1. If \(|P^{(h)}|\) is a single monochromatic run \(a^m\): Stop immediately! Every occurrence of \(a^m\) in \(T^{(h)}\) either lies inside a single terminal/run or crosses a seam \(a^{\ell_1} \mid a^{\ell_2}\) in \(T^{(h)}\) (contributing \(\max(0, \min(\ell_1, m-1) + \min(\ell_2, m-1) - m + 1)\) occurrences).
  2. Before RComp: Pop the maximal prefix run \(a^{\ell_{\mathrm{pref}}}\) and maximal suffix run \(b^{\ell_{\mathrm{suff}}}\) from \(P^{(h)}\) (leaving \(P'^{(h)}\)), and replace every maximal run \(a^{\ell}\) in \(T^{(h)}\) (\(\ell \ge \ell_{\mathrm{pref}}\) at right seam, etc.) by compound meta-symbols that expose the required prefix/suffix sub-runs, or simply note that an occurrence of \(a^{\ell_{\mathrm{pref}}} P'^{(h)} b^{\ell_{\mathrm{suff}}}\) in \(T^{(h)}\) matches \(P'^{(h)}\) on interior blocks and requires the preceding RLE block in \(T^{(h)}\) to be \(\langle a, \ge \ell_{\mathrm{pref}} \rangle\) and the following RLE block to be \(\langle b, \ge \ell_{\mathrm{suff}} \rangle\)!
  3. Before PComp (\(\Sigma_L \times \Sigma_R\)): If \(P^{(h)}\) starts with \(r \in \Sigma_R\), pop \(r\) from the front of \(P^{(h)}\) and prepend \(r\) to the right-hand side of wherever it is needed (or split \(r\) out of its \(\Sigma_L \times \Sigma_R\) pair in \(P\)); symmetrically, if \(P^{(h)}\) ends with \(\ell \in \Sigma_L\), pop \(\ell\) from the back of \(P^{(h)}\). Now every remaining symbol of \(P^{(h)}\) starts in \(\Sigma \setminus \Sigma_R\) and ends in \(\Sigma \setminus \Sigma_L\), so \(P^{(h)}\) cannot straddle any \(\Sigma_L \times \Sigma_R\) pair boundary in \(T^{(h)}\)!
NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly

  2. Alternate Phase A (RComp) and Phase B (PComp):

  3. Because \(|P|\) shrinks by a factor of \(\le 3/4\) per phase, after \(O(\log |P|) \le 130\) phases \(P\) reduces to a single character or run, and we count its occurrences bottom-up on \(T\) in \(O((V_P + V_T) \log |P|)\) time!

Problem CP-V-07 — Dynamic Manuscript Editor & Pattern Census

You are maintaining a text manuscript \(T\) of initial length \(N \le 10^5\) under \(Q \le 10^5\) online operations of two types:

  1. CutPaste(l, r, pos): Excise the contiguous substring \(U = T[l..r]\) from \(T\) (shrinking \(T\) to \(T[1..l-1] + T[r+1..|T|]\)) and immediately re-insert \(U\) right after index pos of the remaining string.
  2. Count(P): Given an explicit query pattern \(P[1..M]\) (\(\sum M \le 2 \times 10^5\)), return the exact number of occurrences of \(P\) as a contiguous substring of the current manuscript \(T\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le N, Q \le 10^5\), \(\sum M \le 2 \times 10^5\), Time Limit \(3.0\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(512\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination (Ch 21 Locally Consistent Parsing vs. Static LZ77/Wavelet Indexing vs. Treap Hashing):
    • A static Suffix Automaton, Suffix Array, or LZ77 2D Wavelet Index (Ch 21 §3.3 / Theorem 21.5) cannot survive arbitrary \(O(\log N)\) substring CutPaste edits without expensive rebuilds.
    • Conversely, maintaining \(T\) in a standard FHQ-Treap only supports \(O(\log N)\) substring hashing, which would require \(O(|P| \log N)\) per candidate alignment—still \(\Theta(N)\) per Count(P) query unless the internal tree nodes of \(T\) and \(P\) align deterministically regardless of global shift!
  • Diagnostic Signal: Maintain \(T\) in a Locally Consistent Parse Tree (Ch 21 §1–§2) of height \(H = O(\log N)\) where block boundaries depend only on a local neighborhood of radius \(\Delta = O(1)\). Then:
    • Each CutPaste(l, r, pos) is two Splits and two Concats, modifying at most \(O(1)\) blocks per level (\(O(\log N)\) total time)!
    • Each Count(P) parses \(P[1..M]\) using the exact same local block compressor, stripping \(O(\Delta) = O(1)\) margin symbols from the left and right ends of \(P^{(h)}\) at each level \(h = 0, \dots, O(\log M)\)!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Let \(T^{(0)}, T^{(1)}, \dots, T^{(H)}\) be the hierarchy of compressed sequences of \(T\) produced by alternating RComp (maximal runs \(c^\ell \to \langle c, \ell \rangle\)) and PComp (splitmix64 local-extrema cutting or Cole–Vishkin 6-coloring, lookahead radius \(\Delta = O(1)\)).

Now apply the exact same level-\(h\) parser to the query pattern \(P^{(h)}\):

The Margin-Stripping Interior Alignment Invariant (Alstrup–Brodal–Rauhe / Mehlhorn et al.): Because whether a block boundary is placed between \(S^{(h)}[i-1]\) and \(S^{(h)}[i]\) depends only on \(S^{(h)}[i - \Delta .. i + \Delta]\), any occurrence of \(P^{(h)}\) inside \(T^{(h)}\) induces the 100% identical level-\((h+1)\) block sequence on the interior of \(P^{(h)}\) once we strip at most \(\Delta + 1 = O(1)\) blocks from the left end and \(\Delta + 1 = O(1)\) blocks from the right end of \(P^{(h)}\)!

Explicitly, at each level \(h\):

  1. Any occurrence of \(P^{(h)}\) in \(T^{(h)}\) that is short enough to be spanned by a single parent block (or a pair of adjacent blocks at a seam when \(|P^{(h)}| \le 2\Delta + 2\)) is counted directly at level \(h\) and recorded!
  2. For longer occurrences (\(|P^{(h)}| > 2\Delta + 2\)), we strip the exact left margin \(L^{(h)}\) and right margin \(R^{(h)}\) at the first/last interior cut points of \(P^{(h)}\), compress the interior into \(P^{(h+1)}\), and promote the search to level \(h + 1\)!
NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly
  1. Dynamic Parse DAG Maintenance (Split & Concat):
    • Store the parse tree of \(T\), where each level-\(h\) node \(u\) stores its symbol ID \(\operatorname{id}(u)\), expanded length \(\operatorname{len}(u)\), child list (size \(\in [2, 4]\) for PComp or (char, count) for RComp), and the active occurrence count \(\operatorname{cnt}[u]\) of symbol \(\operatorname{id}(u)\) in the current tree \(T\)!
    • During Split and Concat, only \(O(1)\) nodes per level are unpacked and re-compressed; decrement \(\operatorname{cnt}[\text{old\_id}]\) when a block is unpacked and increment \(\operatorname{cnt}[\text{new\_id}]\) when a new block is formed (\(O(\log N)\) updates per CutPaste).
  2. Answering Count(P) in \(O(|P| + \log^2 N)\) Time:
    • Even simpler than tracking margin constraints across levels: since every occurrence of \(P\) in \(T\) crosses a unique highest seam \(X_u \to X_{c_1} \dots X_{c_d}\) in the Locally Consistent Parse Tree (its Primary Seam, Ch 21 §2.2 / Theorem 21.2!), how many distinct symbol types \(X_u\) in the parse tree can have \(P\) cross their primary seam and be the minimal node containing \(P\)?
    • By the Margin-Stripping Invariant, as we compress \(P = P^{(0)} \to P^{(1)} \to \dots \to P^{(k)}\) until \(|P^{(k)}| = O(1)\), at each level \(h\) there are only \(O(1)\) canonical ways that \(P^{(h)}\) can straddle a seam of \(T^{(h)}\) without being promoted to \(P^{(h+1)}\)!
    • Thus we compress \(P\) bottom-up in \(O(|P| + \log M)\) time; at each level \(h\), we query the hash table of active level-\((h+1)\) block types \(X_u\) whose child seam matches the stripped/unstripped core of \(P^{(h)}\), summing \(\operatorname{cnt}[X_u]\) in \(O(1)\) time per level!
