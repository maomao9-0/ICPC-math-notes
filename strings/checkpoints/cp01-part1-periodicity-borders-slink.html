Blind Checkpoint I — Structural Periodicity, Border APs & slink DP

Part I Blind Diagnostic Assessment (Chapters 01–04 · Decoy Elimination & Untagged Contest Triage)

When solving an exercise at the end of Chapter 1 or Chapter 4, the chapter title itself hands you the primary mathematical invariant before you read a single line of the problem statement. In a live contest, problems arrive completely untagged. You are given a narrative about overlapping transmissions, symmetric partitions, or substring decompositions, along with numerical constraints (\(N \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(W \le 10^{18}\), or \(Q \le 10^5\)), and you must determine on your own which structural periodicity or border-compression mechanism governs the problem.

This checkpoint tests blind diagnostic recognition across all four chapters of Part I (Ch 01–Ch 04). None of the visible problem statements in Section 2 reveal which chapter or technique applies.

ImportantZero-Spoiler Protocol & Progressive Hints
  1. Strict Zero-Spoiler Rule in Visible Statements: Every problem statement (CP-I-01 through CP-I-07) is written in pure contest form without naming the target chapter (Ch 01–Ch 04) or technique (Fine–Wilf, Border AP, Modulo Shortest Path, slink DP).
  2. Diagnose Before Peeking: Before opening any dropdown on a problem, write down on scratch paper:
    • Which Part I paradigm (Ch 01–Ch 04) is triggered by the constraints and objective;
    • Which lookalike decoy paradigm must be ruled out, and why;
    • The combinatorial word invariant that collapses the naive \(\Theta(N^2)\) or \(\Theta(W)\) bottleneck.
  3. Four-Stage Progressive Unmasking:
    • Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination: Explains how to identify the governing paradigm and rule out lookalike decoys purely from constraints and query semantics.
    • Hint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant: States the exact algebraic or periodicity identity needed for the reduction.
    • Hint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly: Outlines the state representation, transition order, and complexity target.
    • Complete Derivation, Complexity Proof & Reference Solution: Full proof, boundary pitfall audit (BP-1A-01 through BP-1A-08), and non-standard reference kernel where clarifying.

1. Blind Diagnostic Triage Guide & Decoy Elimination

The most common strategic failure in Part I contest problems is confusing two surface-similar periodicity or border patterns that require completely different algorithmic machinery. Before attempting the seven blind problems below, study the two primary Part I Decoy Pairs and the decision flowchart.

1.1 Decoy Pair A: Simultaneous Period Intersection (Ch 01) vs. Sequential Overlap Semigroup (Ch 03)

Both paradigms involve a base length \(N \le 5 \times 10^5\), periods \(p = N - b\) derived from borders \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S)\), and often a huge length parameter \(L, W \le 10^{18}\). How do you tell them apart in 30 seconds?

Diagnostic Dimension Ch 01 Paradigm (T-1A-01: Fine–Wilf & Orbit Counting) Ch 03 Paradigm (T-1A-03: Border-Group Modulo Shortest Path)
How Periods Act on the Target Word Simultaneous global or interval constraints: the same word (or overlapping interval \(I\)) simultaneously satisfies \(W[i] = W[i+p]\) and \(W[i] = W[i+q]\) for all valid \(i\). Sequential additive concatenation: we place copies of \(S\) one after another; the \(t\)-th transition chooses one border \(b_t \in \operatorname{Border}(S)\) and advances total length by \(+ (N - b_t)\).
Governing Algebraic Object Equivalence relation / Undirected orbit graph on indices \(\{1, \dots, L\}\) generated by edges \(i \leftrightarrow i + p_k\). Additive semigroup / Frobenius knapsack of subset sums \(\sum_{t=1}^m (N - b_t)\) inside \(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) modulo a dynamically chosen base period \(x_k\).
Behavior at Huge Length (\(L, W \gg N\)) Above threshold \(L \ge p + q - \gcd(p, q)\), periods collapse to a single period \(\gcd(p, q)\). Below threshold, orbit count is \(p + q - L\). Above the Frobenius number, achievable lengths become periodic modulo \(x_{\min}\), governed by shortest paths \(D[r]\) on residue cycles.
Fatal Misdiagnosis Running shortest-path knapsack when the problem imposes simultaneous periods on a single string (which collapses to \(\gcd\)). Replacing the set of available shift weights \(\{N - b\}\) by their \(\gcd\) immediately without paying the minimum reachability cost \(D[r] \le W\)!

1.2 Decoy Pair B: Online Active-Border Maintenance (Ch 02) vs. Fixed-Left-Endpoint slink DP (Ch 04)

Both paradigms process a string \(S[1..N]\) left-to-right (\(r = 1, 2, \dots, N\)), cannot afford walking the \(\Theta(N)\)-deep KMP or PAM fail chain at step \(r\), and rely on \(O(\log r)\) shortcut links (slink[u] or anc[u]). Yet using Ch 04’s rolling g[u] array on a Ch 02 problem—or vice versa—produces an immediate wrong answer.

Diagnostic Dimension Ch 02 Paradigm (T-1A-02: Active Border Deletion & AP Queries) Ch 04 Paradigm (T-1A-04 / T-1A-05: Series-Link g[u] Shift-Overlap DP)
Nature of the Transition Weight Right-endpoint-coupled or path-dependent: the weight of a border \(b\) at step \(r\) depends on a dynamic range property of \([r-b+1..r]\) (such as \(\min_{k \in [r-b+1, r]} W[k]\)) or on which borders survive character \(S[r+1]\). Fixed-left-endpoint (or polynomial in \(L = r - i\)): the contribution from cutting a palindromic/border suffix of length \(L\) at \(r\) depends only on the prefix state \(dp[i]\) at the invariant left endpoint \(i = r - L\) (or \((r-i)^m dp[i]\)).
Why \(\Theta(1)\) Work per AP Holds Either each border dies at most once when \(S[b+1] \ne S[r]\) (skipped in \(O(1)\) via anc[u]), or an AP \((x_k, d_k, c_k)\) is queried in closed form / dyadic IPM. Shift-Overlap Identity: \(\mathcal{I}(u, r) = \mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d) \sqcup \{r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\}\), so g[fail[u]] computed at \(r - d\) already stores the exact sum over the first \(k - 1\) left endpoints!
Fatal Misdiagnosis Trying to maintain \(\sum_{b} \min_{k \in [r-b+1, r]} W[k]\) inside g[u] = g[fail[u]] + ... (invalid because appending \(W[r]\) mutates the suffix minimum of all active left endpoints simultaneously). Maintaining an explicit std::map of all \(\Theta(N)\) individual borders when g[u] can aggregate each AP block in \(O(1)\) time via left-endpoint invariance.

2. Unspoiled Blind Checkpoint Problems (CP-I-01 – CP-I-07)

Problem CP-I-01 — Minimum Stamp Cover

You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) over the lowercase English alphabet, where \(1 \le N \le 5 \times 10^5\). You wish to manufacture a single rubber stamp carrying a string \(T[1..L]\) of minimum possible length \(L\). Using this stamp, you must be able to print \(S[1..N]\) on an initially blank strip of length \(N\) by pressing \(T\) onto one or more (possibly overlapping) intervals \([i, i + L - 1] \subseteq [1, N]\) such that:

  1. Every position \(k \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) is covered by at least one stamp impression;
  2. Whenever two stamp impressions overlap at position \(k\), they print the exact same character \(S[k]\) (so the final printed string is \(S[1..N]\)).

Find the minimum possible length \(L\) of such a stamp \(T\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 5 \times 10^5\), Time Limit \(1.0\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(256\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination: This is not a partition DP into disjoint blocks (Ch 04), nor is it building a huge string of length \(W > N\) by arbitrary overlaps (Ch 03). We are covering a fixed string \(S[1..N]\) using overlapping occurrences of a single pattern \(T\).
  • Diagnostic Signal: Because \(T\) must cover index \(1\) (the left end of \(S\)) and index \(N\) (the right end of \(S\)) without hanging outside \([1, N]\), \(T\) must be both a prefix and a suffix of \(S[1..N]\)—that is, \(T = S[1..L]\) where \(L \in \operatorname{Border}(S) \cup \{N\}\). By induction, for every prefix \(S[1..i]\), its shortest valid stamp \(T_i = S[1..dp[i]]\) must lie on the KMP fail chain of \(i\), restricting \(dp[i]\) to at most two candidates: either \(dp[\pi[i]]\) or \(i\) itself!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Let \(dp[i]\) be the minimum length of a stamp that covers prefix \(S[1..i]\). Since any stamp covering \(S[1..i]\) must be a border of \(S[1..i]\), and any nontrivial border of \(S[1..i]\) is also a border of \(S[1..\pi[i]]\) (and therefore covers \(S[1..\pi[i]]\)), any proper stamp for \(S[1..i]\) must have length \(\ge dp[\pi[i]]\). Moreover, since \(S[1..dp[\pi[i]]]\) already covers \(S[1..\pi[i]]\), if any proper border of \(S[1..i]\) covers \(S[1..i]\), then \(S[1..dp[\pi[i]]]\) itself covers \(S[1..i]\)!

When does \(x = dp[\pi[i]]\) cover \(S[1..i]\)? Since the suffix \(S[i - \pi[i] + 1 .. i] = S[1..\pi[i]]\) is already covered by stamps of length \(x\), \(x\) covers \(S[1..i]\) without leaving an uncovered gap before \(i - \pi[i] + 1\) if and only if there exists an earlier prefix \(j < i\) such that: \[dp[j] = dp[\pi[i]] \quad \text{and} \quad j \ge i - \pi[i].\]

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly
  1. Compute the KMP prefix array \(\pi[1..N]\) of \(S\) in \(O(N)\) time.
  2. Maintain an array last_end[1..N], where last_end[x] stores the largest prefix index \(j < i\) seen so far whose minimum stamp length is \(dp[j] = x\).
  3. Sweep \(i = 1, 2, \dots, N\):
    • If \(\pi[i] > 0\) and \(\text{last\_end}[dp[\pi[i]]] \ge i - \pi[i]\), set \(dp[i] = dp[\pi[i]]\).
    • Otherwise, set \(dp[i] = i\).
    • Update \(\text{last\_end}[dp[i]] = i\).
  4. Output \(dp[N]\) in \(O(N)\) total time and \(O(N)\) space.
TipComplete Derivation, Complexity Proof & Reference Solution — CP-I-01

  • Target Chapter & Technique ID: Chapter 1 (T-1A-01: Period–Border Duality & KMP Fail-Tree Cover Invariant · [POI XII] Szablon / Luogu P3426).

  • Proof of the Two-Candidate Collapse (\(dp[i] \in \{dp[\pi[i]], i\}\)): Suppose a stamp \(T = S[1..b]\) with \(b < i\) covers \(S[1..i]\). Because the rightmost impression of \(T\) ends at \(i\) and the leftmost starts at \(1\), \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..i])\), so \(b \le \pi[i]\) and \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..\pi[i]]) \cup \{\pi[i]\}\). Furthermore, every impression of \(T\) that lies completely inside \([1, \pi[i]]\) (including the one ending at \(\pi[i]\), which exists because the suffix \([i-\pi[i]+1..i]\) is identical to \([1..\pi[i]]\)) covers \(S[1..\pi[i]]\). Hence \(T\) is a valid stamp for \(S[1..\pi[i]]\), and by definition \(x = dp[\pi[i]]\) is the shortest stamp covering \(S[1..\pi[i]]\)—so \(x\) is itself a stamp of \(T\), meaning every impression of \(T\) can be replaced by impressions of \(S[1..x]\)! Conversely, \(S[1..x]\) covers the suffix \(S[i-\pi[i]+1..i]\) (since that suffix equals \(S[1..\pi[i]]\)). To cover all of \(S[1..i]\) without a gap between the prefix impressions and the suffix \([i-\pi[i]+1..i]\), we need an impression of \(S[1..x]\) ending at some \(j \in [i - \pi[i], i - 1]\). Such an impression exists (and connects back to index \(1\) without gaps) iff \(S[1..x]\) covers \(S[1..j]\), which by minimality of \(x\) is equivalent to \(dp[j] = x\) for some \(j \ge i - \pi[i]\).

  • Boundary Pitfall Audit:

    • Testing last_end[x] >= i - x instead of last_end[x] >= i - pi[i]: This is the classic implementation bug. The entire suffix \(S[i - \pi[i] + 1 .. i]\) of length \(\pi[i] \ge x\) is already covered by \(S[1..x]\) because \(x = dp[\pi[i]]\) covers \(S[1..\pi[i]]\). Thus the previous covered prefix \(j\) only needs to reach \(i - \pi[i]\), not \(i - x\)!

  • Complexity: \(O(N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

Problem CP-I-02 — Tandem-Repeat Necklace Prefixes

Given a string \(S[1..N]\) of lowercase letters and a positive integer \(K\) (\(1 \le N, K \le 10^6\)), we say that a prefix \(S[1..i]\) has a \((K, A, B)\)-necklace decomposition if there exist two (possibly empty) strings \(A\) and \(B\) such that: \[S[1..i] = \underbrace{A B \, A B \, \dots \, A B}_{K \text{ times}} A = (AB)^K A.\] For every prefix length \(i \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}\), output 1 if \(S[1..i]\) admits at least one valid \((K, A, B)\)-necklace decomposition, and 0 otherwise.

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N, K \le 10^6\), Time Limit \(1.0\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(256\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination: We must output an answer for every prefix \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) in \(O(1)\) amortized time per prefix. Enumerating divisors (Ch 01 SPF sieve) or walking slink chains (Ch 02 \(O(\log N)\)) is neither necessary nor optimal when a single quotient inequality on the minimum weak period \(p_{\min}(i) = i - \pi[i]\) suffices.
  • Diagnostic Signal: Notice what \((AB)^K A\) means in terms of periodicity: let \(p = |AB|\) and \(r = |A| = i - K p\). Then \(0 \le r \le p\) (since \(|B| = p - r \ge 0\)), \(i = K p + r\), and the prefix \(S[1..i]\) has weak period \(p\) because shifting \((AB)^K A\) by \(p = |AB|\) matches the remaining \((AB)^{K-1} A\)!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

A prefix \(S[1..i]\) can be written as \((AB)^K A\) with \(|AB| = p \ge 1\) if and only if there exists a weak period \(p \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..i])\) such that: \[K \cdot p \le i \le (K + 1) \cdot p.\] If \(K = 1\), this holds for every \(i \ge 1\) (take \(A = \varepsilon, B = S[1..i]\)). For \(K \ge 2\), any \(p\) satisfying \(K p \le i\) automatically obeys \(p \le i / 2\). By the Half-Length Single-AP Theorem (Ch 02 Theorem 2.1 / Weak Periodicity Lemma), every period \(p \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..i])\) with \(p \le i / 2\) is an exact multiple of the minimum period \(p_{\min} = i - \pi[i]\): \[p = c \cdot p_{\min} \quad \text{for some integer } c \ge 1.\]

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly

  1. Substitute \(p = c \cdot p_{\min}\) into \(K p \le i \le (K + 1) p\): \[c K \cdot p_{\min} \le i \le c (K + 1) \cdot p_{\min}.\]

  2. Let \(q = \lfloor i / p_{\min} \rfloor\) be the number of full minimum-period blocks inside \(S[1..i]\). Since we want \(c K \cdot p_{\min} \le i\), the largest integer \(c\) satisfying \(c K \le q\) is: \[c = \left\lfloor \frac{\lfloor i / p_{\min} \rfloor}{K} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{i}{K \cdot p_{\min}} \right\rfloor.\]

  3. Check in \(O(1)\) time whether this largest \(c \ge 1\) satisfies the upper bound \(i \le c(K + 1) p_{\min}\), i.e., \(i - c K p_{\min} \le c p_{\min}\).

TipComplete Derivation, Complexity Proof & Reference Solution — CP-I-02

  • Target Chapter & Technique ID: Chapter 1 & Chapter 2 (T-1A-01 / T-1A-02: Period–Border Duality & Half-Length Period Divisibility · CF 526D — Om Nom and Necklace).

  • Why Only the Single Largest Multiple \(c = \lfloor i / (K p_{\min}) \rfloor\) Needs to Be Checked: Suppose some \(c' \ge 1\) satisfies \(c' K p_{\min} \le i \le c' (K + 1) p_{\min}\). Since \(c' K \le \lfloor i / p_{\min} \rfloor\), we have \(c' \le \lfloor i / (K p_{\min}) \rfloor = c\). Because \(c \ge c'\) still satisfies \(c K p_{\min} \le i\) while making the right-hand bound \(c(K + 1) p_{\min}\) as large as possible, a valid \(c'\) exists if and only if \(c = \lfloor i / (K p_{\min}) \rfloor \ge 1\) satisfies \(i \le c(K + 1) p_{\min}\)!

  • Boundary Pitfall Audit (BP-1A-02):

    • Splitting into \(p_{\min} \mid i\) vs. \(p_{\min} \nmid i\) with Buggy Remainder Checks: Many contestants try to test \(q \bmod K \le q / K\) on \(q = i / p_{\min}\) without accounting for the fractional tail \(i \bmod p_{\min}\): when \(i \bmod p_{\min} > 0\), the remaining tail \(A\) must have length \(i - c K p_{\min} \le c p_{\min}\) (and when \(i - c K p_{\min} = c p_{\min}\), \(B\) is empty, which is valid!). Using the single exact inequality c >= 1 && i - 1LL * c * K * p_min <= 1LL * c * p_min handles both exact and weak periods uniformly.

  • Complexity: \(O(N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

Problem CP-I-03 — Dual-Lock Periodic Word Synthesis

A communication protocol specifies a message \(W[1..N]\) of length \(N\) over an alphabet \(\Sigma\) of size \(\sigma\) (\(2 \le \sigma \le 10^9\)). You are given a reference framing template \(T[1..M]\) (\(2 \le M \le 5 \times 10^5\)) and \(Q\) independent synthesis queries (\(1 \le Q \le 2 \times 10^5\)).

In each query, you are given two distinct non-empty border lengths \(b_1, b_2 \in \operatorname{Border}(T)\) of the reference template \(T\) and a target message length \(N\) (\(1 \le N \le 10^{18}\)). A message \(W[1..N] \in \Sigma^N\) is dual-lock compliant if it simultaneously satisfies both overlap shift equations transferred from \(T\): \[W[i] = W[i + (M - b_1)] \quad (\forall\, 1 \le i \le N - (M - b_1)) \qquad \text{and} \qquad W[i] = W[i + (M - b_2)] \quad (\forall\, 1 \le i \le N - (M - b_2)).\] (Notice that \(W\) does not need to contain \(T\); it only inherits the two period lengths \(p_1 = M - b_1\) and \(p_2 = M - b_2\)!)

For each query \((b_1, b_2, N)\), verify first that \(b_1, b_2 \in \operatorname{Border}(T)\) (output 0 if either is not a valid border of \(T\)), and otherwise compute the number of dual-lock compliant messages \(W[1..N] \in \Sigma^N\) modulo \(10^9 + 7\).

  • Constraints: \(2 \le M \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(1 \le Q \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(1 \le b_1, b_2 < M\), \(1 \le N \le 10^{18}\), \(2 \le \sigma \le 10^9\), Time Limit \(1.5\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination (Ch 01 vs. Ch 03 Decoy Pair): At first glance, seeing borders of \(T\), period weights \(p_k = M - b_k\), and a huge length \(N \le 10^{18}\) looks identical to a Ch 03 Border-Group Modulo Shortest Path problem ([WC2016]). Do not fall for the decoy! Here, \(W[1..N]\) is a single string of length \(N\) on which \(p_1 = M - b_1\) and \(p_2 = M - b_2\) act simultaneously across the entire domain \([1, N]\). This is a Ch 01 Fine–Wilf Periodicity & Sub-Threshold Orbit Counting problem!
  • Diagnostic Signal: Each character equality \(W[i] = W[i + p_1]\) and \(W[i] = W[i + p_2]\) links indices \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) into connected components (character orbits). If there are \(C(N; p_1, p_2)\) connected components on \(\{1, \dots, N\}\), the number of valid strings is \(\sigma^{C(N; p_1, p_2)} \bmod (10^9 + 7)\).
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Without loss of generality, order the two periods as \(1 \le p < q < M\), where \(p = \min(M - b_1, M - b_2)\) and \(q = \max(M - b_1, M - b_2)\), and let \(g = \gcd(p, q)\). Problem CP-I-07 — Range-Coupled Palindromic Factorization

You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) of lowercase letters (\(1 \le N \le 2 \times 10^5\)), an array of threshold positions \(L[1..N]\) (\(0 \le L[r] < r\)), and a penalty weight \(C[1..N]\) (\(-10^9 \le C[r] \le 10^9\)). We wish to partition prefixes of \(S\) into palindromic blocks:

  • Start with \(dp[0] = 0\).
  • For each right endpoint \(r = 1, 2, \dots, N\) in order:
    1. First, before computing \(dp[r]\), every earlier partition state \(dp[i]\) residing in the prefix range \(0 \le i \le L[r]\) receives an additive bonus \(+C[r]\) (i.e., \(dp[i] \leftarrow dp[i] + C[r]\) for all \(0 \le i \le L[r]\)).
    2. Next, \(dp[r]\) is set to the maximum of the current (updated) values \(dp[r - \ell]\) over all lengths \(\ell\) such that \(S[r - \ell + 1 .. r]\) is a palindrome: \[dp[r] = \max_{\substack{1 \le \ell \le r \\ S[r-\ell+1..r] \text{ is a palindrome}}} dp[r - \ell].\]

Compute the final array \(dp[1..N]\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(0 \le L[r] < r\), \(-10^9 \le C[r] \le 10^9\), Time Limit \(2.0\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(256\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination (Ch 04 vs. Ch 02 Decoy Pair): Compare this carefully against CP-I-06:
    • Here, we are transitioning over all palindromic suffixes \(\ell \in \operatorname{PalSuffix}(S[1..r])\), not prefixes/borders of \(S[1..r]\) where mismatched suffixes permanently die. A palindromic suffix that disappears at \(r\) can reappear at \(r + d\)! Thus Ch 02’s permanent-death argument (anc[u]) does not apply.
    • Instead, this is a Ch 04 Series-Link (slink) Left-Endpoint Shift-Overlap DP (T-1A-04 / W1A-42) coupled with range updates on the left-endpoint array \(dp[0..N]\)!
  • Diagnostic Signal: By Ch 04 Theorem 4.2 (Shift-Overlap Invariant), for each slink chain head \(u\) at position \(r\) with step \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\), the set of left endpoints \(\mathcal{I}(u, r) = \{r - \ell_1, \dots, r - \ell_k\}\) queried by \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) equals \(\mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d) \sqcup \{r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\}\). Why can’t a plain scalar g[u] handle step 1’s range update \(dp[0..L[r]] \mathrel{+}= C[r]\)? Because between \(r - d\) and \(r\), range updates have modified a subset of the left endpoints inside \(\mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d)\)!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Look at the structure of the left-endpoint set \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) for any PAM node \(u\) with step \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\): \[\mathcal{I}(u, r) = \bigl\{\,r - \operatorname{len}[u],\; r - \operatorname{len}[u] + d,\; \dots,\; r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\,\bigr\}.\] Every element \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\) has the exact same residue modulo \(d\), and as \(r\) advances by \(+d\), the set \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) simply appends one new element \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\) at the right end while keeping its leftmost element \(i_{\text{old}} = r - \operatorname{len}[u]\) fixed!

Even simpler: to maintain \(g[u] = \max_{i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)} dp[i]\) under range additions \(dp[0..L[r]] \mathrel{+}= C[r]\) when arbitrary \(d\)-APs cannot be grouped in a single standard segment tree, observe how \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) evolves on the PAM fail tree: Whenever \(u\) is a slink chain head at step \(r\), \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) is a contiguous range of indices within the sequence of left endpoints pushed onto the unique period-\(d\) chain containing \(u\)! Specifically, in the PAM fail tree, group nodes into maximal chains of constant \(\operatorname{diff}[v] = d\). Along any maximal period-\(d\) chain \(\mathcal{C}\), each time the chain is visited at a right endpoint \(r\), at most one new left endpoint \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\) is appended to \(\mathcal{C}\)’s list of left endpoints in strictly increasing order of \(i\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly

Because every maximal constant-diff chain \(\mathcal{C}\) in the PAM fail tree receives new left endpoints \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\) in strictly increasing order (and the active subset \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) for a chain head \(u \in \mathcal{C}\) is simply the contiguous suffix of \(\mathcal{C}\)’s list with \(i \ge r - \operatorname{len}[u]\)!):

  1. Verify whether any element of \(\mathcal{C}\)’s list is ever \(< r - \operatorname{len}[u]\): by Ch 04 Theorem 4.2, \(\mathcal{I}(u, r) = \mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d) \sqcup \{i_{\text{new}}\}\), which starts at \(r - \operatorname{len}[u]\)—the very first left endpoint ever inserted into \(\mathcal{C}\)!
  2. Furthermore, to support range add on \(i \in [0, L[r]]\) across all chains simultaneously in \(O(\log N)\) time:
    • Each step \(r\) visits \(O(\log r)\) slink chains and inserts \(O(\log r)\) pairs \((\text{chain\_id}, i_{\text{new}})\).
    • Three Structural Regimes for Range Updates:
      • Case 1 (Additive weight on the palindrome / suffix itself, i.e., updates on PAM fail-tree subtrees or right-endpoint windows): A lazy segment tree over the DFS order of the PAM fail tree updates all active palindromic suffixes in \(O(\log N)\).
      • Case 2 (Monotone Frontier / Sliding Window \(L[r-1] \le L[r]\)): When \(L[r]\) is non-decreasing (\(L[1] \le L[2] \le \dots \le L[N]\)), each left endpoint \(i\) crosses the frontier \(i \le L[r]\) at most once! When \(i\) crosses \(L[r]\), at most \(\lceil \log_2 N \rceil\) slink chains contain \(i\) (the chains visited at step \(i + \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + d\)). Updating those \(O(\log N)\) chains in a segment tree over PAM nodes takes \(O(N \log^2 N)\) total time!
      • Case 3 (General \(L[r]\) via Period Thresholding): For any chain head \(u\), \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) has size \(k = (\operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]]) / d\). For \(d > B\), \(k \le N / B\) elements are queried directly in a global Segment Tree over \(i \in [0, N]\); for \(d \le B\), ordering a Segment Tree by \((d, i \bmod d, \lfloor i / d \rfloor)\) turns \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) into a single contiguous interval!
TipComplete Derivation, Complexity Proof & Reference Solution — CP-I-07

  • Target Chapter & Technique ID: Chapter 4 (T-1A-04 / T-1A-05: PAM slink Shift-Overlap Invariant Coupled with Segment Tree / Frontier Activation, W1A-42).

  • Complete Structural Decomposition: By Theorem 4.2 (Ch 04), at right endpoint \(r\), the half-open slink chain \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) queries the exact left-endpoint set: \[\mathcal{I}(u, r) = \mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d) \sqcup \bigl\{\,i_{\text{new}}(u, r)\,\bigr\}, \qquad i_{\text{new}}(u, r) = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u].\] Notice the crucial cardinality bound: at each right endpoint \(x \in \{1, \dots, N\}\), the slink loop visits at most \(\lceil \log_2 x \rceil\) chain heads \(u\), and each visit creates exactly one new left-endpoint membership \(i = x - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u]\) in the constant-diff chain containing \(u\).

    • Therefore, across the entire algorithm, each left endpoint \(i \in \{0, \dots, N - 1\}\) belongs to at most \(\lceil \log_2 N \rceil\) constant-diff chains (or, if we maintain a lazy segment tree / monotonic deque over the elements of each constant-diff chain indexed by their insertion rank, the total number of leaves across all chains is \(\sum_{r=1}^N |\text{chains}(r)| = O(N \log N)\)!).
    • When range updates follow a monotone frontier (or sliding window \([L_1[r], L_2[r]]\)), each of the \(O(N \log N)\) chain leaves enters and leaves the active window at most once, updating its chain’s segment-tree/deque accumulator in \(O(\log N)\) (or \(O(1)\)) time, for a total complexity of \(O(N \log^2 N)\) (or \(O(N \log N)\))!
    • Even for completely arbitrary \(L[r]\) without monotonicity, splitting by period threshold \(d \le B\) (where \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) is a contiguous range in the \((d, i \bmod d)\)-ordered segment tree) versus \(d > B\) (where \(|\mathcal{I}(u, r)| \le N / B\) is queried directly) or using slink tree paths solves the problem strictly sub-quadratically.

  • Boundary Pitfall Audit (BP-1A-05, BP-1A-06):

    • The \(r - \operatorname{len}[u]\) vs. \(r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u]\) Trap (BP-1A-05): The newly entering left endpoint at step \(r\) is always \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u]\) (the rightmost / shortest palindrome in the half-open chain \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\)). The leftmost endpoint \(r - \operatorname{len}[u]\) was the very first element inserted into the chain when its bottom node was first visited!

  • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) to \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time and \(O(N \log N)\) space.
