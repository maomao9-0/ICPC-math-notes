Blind Checkpoint IV — Internal Pattern Matching, 6-Method Borders, Deque PAM & Range Palindromes

Part IV Blind Diagnostic Assessment (Chapters 15–17 · Substring Dictionary vs. Palindromic Unclipping & Deque vs. Telescoping Triage)

When solving an exercise inside Chapter 15, Chapter 16, or Chapter 17, the surrounding section headings immediately tell you whether to deploy Kociumaka’s Basic Substring Dictionary (IPM), an unamortized double-ended Palindromic Automaton (Deque PAM), or periodic suffix telescoping (slink cancellation). In a live contest, substring and palindrome problems arrive with zero structural labels. You are simply handed a string \(S[1..N]\) and a stream of range queries \([l, r]\) or double-ended mutations, and you must diagnose which algebraic or combinatorial invariant governs the query.

This checkpoint tests blind diagnostic recognition across all three chapters of Part IV (Ch 15–Ch 17). None of the visible problem statements in Section 2 reveal which chapter or technique applies.

1. Blind Diagnostic Triage Guide & Decoy Elimination

The most dangerous traps in Part IV arise from confusing two range-query or dynamic-window problems whose surface statements differ by a single word (e.g., “longest border of \(S[l..r]\)” vs. “longest palindrome of \(S[l..r]\)”, or “distinct palindromes in a sliding/deque window” vs. “distinct palindromes in an arbitrary range \(S[l..r]\)”). Before attempting the seven blind problems below, study the two primary Part IV Decoy Pairs and the decision flowchart.

1.1 Decoy Pair A: Non-Hereditary Substring Borders (Ch 15) vs. Center-Hereditary Substring Palindromes (Ch 17)

Both paradigms take a static string \(S[1..N]\) and answer \(Q\) online or offline queries on a substring \(W = S[l..r]\) (\(N, Q \le 2 \times 10^5\)). Why does Range Longest Palindromic Substring (Range LPS) admit a true \(O(1)\) query via Sparse Table RMQ, whereas Range Longest Proper Border ([BJWC2018]) requires \(O(\log N)\) dyadic Internal Pattern Matching (IPM) or \(O(\log^2 N)\) Link-Tree HLD?

Diagnostic Dimension Ch 15 Paradigm (T-2B-04: Dyadic IPM & 6-Method Substring Borders) Ch 17 Paradigm (T-3A-03: Two-Boundary Unclipping & Midpoint Decoupling)
Hereditary vs. Boundary-Coupled Geometry Two-boundary coupled (non-hereditary): A border \(S[l..l+b-1] = S[r-b+1..r]\) compares the left boundary \(l\) against the right boundary \(r-b+1\). Shifting \([l, r]\) changes both compared substrings simultaneously! Center-hereditary: A palindrome centered at half-integer \(c \in [l, r]\) remains a palindrome when clipped to \([l, r]\); its valid radius is simply \(\min\bigl(R_{\text{man}}[c],\, c - (2l-1) + 1,\, (2r-1) - c + 1\bigr)\).
Governing Combinatorial Invariant Bounded-Ratio IPM AP Theorem: For \(b \in [2^m, 2^{m+1}-1]\), \(b\) is a valid border of \(S[l..r]\) iff \(i = r - b + 1\) lies in the intersection of two 1D APs: \(\operatorname{Occ}(P_m, S[r-2^{m+1}+2..r])\) and \(\operatorname{Occ}(Q_m, S[l..l+2^{m+1}-2])\). Two-Boundary Unclipping Theorem: Let \(c_L\) be the center of the longest palindromic prefix of \(S[l..r]\) and \(c_R\) the center of its longest palindromic suffix. Every center \(c \in (c_L, c_R)\) lies 100% unclipped inside \([l, r]\)!
Suffix Comparison & Tie-Breaking Truncating \(\operatorname{suf}(i)\) and \(\operatorname{suf}(j)\) at \(r\) flips their global SA order \(\operatorname{rk}[i] < \operatorname{rk}[j]\) iff \(S[j..r]\) is a proper prefix-border of \(S[i..r]\). Palindromic prefixes/suffixes of \(S[l..r]\) are ancestor states of \(\operatorname{first}[l]\) in \(\operatorname{PAM}(S^R)\) and \(\operatorname{last}[r]\) in \(\operatorname{PAM}(S)\), found via slink ceiling division.
Fatal Misdiagnosis Testing only whether \(P_m = S[l..l+2^m-1]\) occurs in the tail window without intersecting the symmetric suffix AP of \(Q_m\) (fails on \(W = \texttt{ababacababab}\), BP-4-01). Using a single midpoint \(m = \lfloor(l+r)/2\rfloor\) for both odd and even Manacher centers in range multiplicity queries (misclassifies even center \(m + 0.5\), BP-4-05).

1.2 Decoy Pair B: Online 4-Way Deque / Tree PAM (Ch 16) vs. Static Range slink Telescoping & Rich Words (Ch 17)

Both paradigms track distinct palindromes and palindromic lengths across changing intervals \([L, R]\) of a string or tree. How do you decide between an online Deque PAM (Ch 16), an offline/persistent slink Telescoping Fenwick/Segment Tree (Ch 17), and a \(O(N)\) Queue PAM two-pointer threshold (Ch 17 Rich Words)?

Diagnostic Dimension Ch 16 Paradigm (T-3A-01 / T-3A-02: Unamortized Tree & 4-Way Deque PAM) Ch 17 Paradigm (T-3A-04 / T-3A-05: slink Telescoping & Rich Word Closure)
Mutation vs. Random-Access Query Regime Incremental endpoint mutations or tree branching: The active string \(S[L..R]\) changes by \(\pm 1\) character at either end (push_front, push_back, pop_front, pop_back) or branches on a version tree. Arbitrary random-access range queries \([l, r]\): Both endpoints \(l\) and \(r\) jump arbitrarily across \(S[1..N]\) in each query.
Why Amortized / Naive Updates Collapse On a tree or under pop, amortized while fail walks take \(\Theta(N)\) per step, and popping \(S[R]\) removes the boundary suffix \(u = \operatorname{max\_suff}[R]\). At right endpoint \(r\), up to \(\Theta(N)\) ancestors of \(\operatorname{pos}[r]\) in the PAM fail tree acquire a new latest occurrence ending at \(r\).
Governing Combinatorial Invariant Uniform Preceding-Character Lemma (quick_link[u][c] in \(O(1)\) or slink-skip in \(O(\log N)\)) + Unique Vanishing Palindrome & Lazy Surface Push-Down (max_pref[l_u] <- fail[u] in \(O(1)\) without cascading!). Periodic Suffix Telescoping: Along any slink chain \(u_1 \to \dots \to u_k\), \(\operatorname{start}_{r-1}(u_j) = \operatorname{start}_r(u_{j-1})\) (\(2 \le j \le k\)), so intermediate \(+1/-1\) updates cancel! And for Rich Words, Factor Closure reduces range richness to \(l \ge L_{\text{rich}}[r]\) in \(O(1)\)!
Fatal Misdiagnosis Physically deleting PAM Trie nodes on pop_back() or cascading fail updates below \(f = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) (BP-4-03, BP-4-04). Building a heavy \(O(N \log^2 N)\) slink telescoping structure to test whether \(S[l..r]\) is a Rich Word when Factor Closure solves it in \(O(N)\) preprocess + \(O(1)\) query (BP-4-07).

2. Unspoiled Blind Checkpoint Problems (CP-IV-01 – CP-IV-07)

Problem CP-IV-01 — Truncated Substring Minimum Suffix & Length-Capped Self-Overlap

You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) of lowercase English letters (\(1 \le N \le 2 \times 10^5\)) and \(Q\) online queries (\(1 \le Q \le 2 \times 10^5\)). Each query specifies a substring \(W = S[l..r]\) and a length cap \(K\) (\(1 \le K \le r - l\)), and asks for two structural quantities of \(W = S[l..r]\):

  1. Minimum Truncated Suffix: Find the starting index \(i^* \in [l, r]\) that minimizes the truncated suffix \(S[i..r]\) in standard lexicographical order (if two indices produce identical strings, which is impossible since their lengths \(r - i + 1\) differ, no tie occurs).
  2. Length-Capped Self-Overlap: Find the largest integer \(b \in [0, K]\) such that the length-\(b\) prefix \(S[l .. l+b-1]\) equals the length-\(b\) suffix \(S[r-b+1 .. r]\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le N, Q \le 2 \times 10^5\), Online Queries, Time Limit \(2.5\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(512\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination (Ch 15 vs. Ch 17 Decoy Pair): Neither part involves mirror reversal (\(S = S^R\)). Moreover, in Part 1, why can’t you simply return \(i_0 = \arg\min_{i \in [l, r]} \operatorname{rk}[i]\) from the global Suffix Array of \(S[1..N]\)?
    • Counterexample: On \(S = \texttt{abad}\) and \([l, r] = [1, 3]\) (\(W = \texttt{aba}\)), globally \(\operatorname{suf}(1) = \texttt{abad} < \operatorname{suf}(3) = \texttt{ad}\) (\(\operatorname{rk}[1] < \operatorname{rk}[3]\)), yet inside \(W = \texttt{aba}\), truncating at \(r = 3\) gives \(S[3..3] = \texttt{a} < S[1..3] = \texttt{aba}\)!
  • Diagnostic Signal: When does truncating \(\operatorname{suf}(i)\) and \(\operatorname{suf}(j)\) at \(r\) flip their global Suffix Array order (\(\operatorname{rk}[i] < \operatorname{rk}[j]\) globally, yet \(S[j..r] <_{\text{lex}} S[i..r]\) locally)? If and only if \(\operatorname{LCE}(i, j) \ge r - j + 1\)—which means \(j > i\) and \(S[j..r]\) is a proper prefix (and therefore a proper border!) of \(S[i..r]\)! Thus both Part 1 and Part 2 are governed by Ch 15’s Dyadic Bounded-Ratio Internal Pattern Matching (T-2B-04)!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

  1. For Part 1 (Truncated Minimum Suffix via Dyadic Prefix-Border Tie-Breaking): Let \(i \in [l, r]\) be our current best candidate and \(j \in (i, r]\) be a shorter candidate in scale \(m\) where \(r - j + 1 \in [2^m, 2^{m+1}-1]\). For \(S[j..r]\) to beat \(S[i..r]\), \(S[j..r]\) must be a proper prefix of \(S[i..r]\)—so in particular \(P_m = S[i .. i + 2^m - 1]\) must occur at starting position \(j \in [r - 2^{m+1} + 2, \; r - 2^m + 1]\). By the Bounded-Ratio IPM AP Theorem (Ch 15 Theorem 15.2), all occurrences of \(P_m\) inside the tail window \(T_m = S[r - 2^{m+1} + 2 .. r]\) (of length \(2^{m+1}-1 < 2|P_m|\)) form a single arithmetic progression of step \(d = \operatorname{per}(P_m)\)! And among any arithmetic progression of occurrences of \(P_m\) that are prefixes of \(S[i..r]\), which one gives the lexicographically smallest truncated suffix \(S[j..r]\)? By periodicity on the overlapping core, only the first (leftmost) occurrence \(j_{\min}\) of \(P_m\) in \([r - 2^{m+1} + 2, \; r - 2^m + 1]\) (or the shortest border in each AP) ever needs to be compared against \(i\)!

  2. For Part 2 (Length-Capped Border \(b \in [1, K]\) via Two-Sided AP Intersection): In each dyadic scale \(m \le \lfloor \log_2 K \rfloor\) where \(b \in [2^m, \min(K, 2^{m+1}-1)]\):

    • Let \(P_m = S[l .. l + 2^m - 1]\) and find its occurrence AP \(\mathcal{A}_P\) inside \(S[r - \min(K, 2^{m+1}-1) + 1 .. r]\);
    • Let \(Q_m = S[r - 2^m + 1 .. r]\) and find its occurrence AP \(\mathcal{A}_Q\) inside \(S[l .. l + \min(K, 2^{m+1}-1) - 1]\). Since \(2^m + 2^m > b\), \(S[l..l+b-1] = S[r-b+1..r]\) holds if and only if \(b\) belongs to both the shifted AP of \(\mathcal{A}_P\) and the shifted AP of \(\mathcal{A}_Q\)!
NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly
  1. Build the Suffix Array \(\text{SA}[1..N]\), inverse rank \(\text{rk}[1..N]\), \(O(1)\) LCE RMQ, and a Persistent Segment Tree (Chairman Tree) over \(\text{SA}[1..N]\) (storing minimum and maximum string index in each SA range) in \(O(N \log N)\) time.
  2. Answering Part 1 in \(O(\log^2 N)\):
    • Start with \(i^* = \text{SA}[\min_{k \in [l, r]} \text{rk}[k]]\) (the globally smallest suffix starting in \([l, r]\)).
    • For each scale \(m = \lfloor \log_2(r - l) \rfloor\) down to \(0\): find the SA interval \([L_m, R_m]\) of \(P_m = S[i^* .. i^* + 2^m - 1]\) via RMQ binary search around \(\text{rk}[i^*]\), query the Chairman Tree on \([L_m, R_m]\) for the largest (or smallest valid border) index \(j \in [\max(i^* + 1, r - 2^{m+1} + 2), \; r - 2^m + 1]\), and update \(i^* \leftarrow \arg\min_{\text{trunc}}(i^*, j)\) via an \(O(1)\) truncated LCE comparison!
  3. Answering Part 2 in \(O(\log^2 N)\):
    • Iterate \(m = \lfloor \log_2 K \rfloor\) down to \(0\) (stopping as soon as a valid \(b\) is found). In scale \(m\), extract at most 3 points (min, 2nd min, max) of \(\mathcal{A}_P\) in \([r - \min(K, 2^{m+1}-1) + 1, \; r - 2^m + 1]\) via the Chairman Tree over \([L(P_m), R(P_m)]\). Either verify the \(\le 2\) points directly via \(\operatorname{LCE}(l, r - b + 1) \ge b\), or intersect \(\mathcal{A}_P\) with \(\mathcal{A}_Q\) in \(O(1)\) time!

  • Proof of the One-Sided \(P_m\) Failure & Two-Sided \(P_m \cap Q_m\) Exact Coverage: Why is checking \(P_m = S[l..l+2^m-1]\) alone insufficient in Part 2 unless you also verify \(\operatorname{LCE}(l, r-b+1) \ge b\) or intersect with \(Q_m = S[r-2^m+1..r]\)?     • Yet \(b = 6\) is not a border of \(W\) because \(W[1..6] = \texttt{ababac} \ne \texttt{ababab} = W[7..12]\) (mismatch at \(W[6] = \texttt{c} \ne \texttt{b} = W[12]\))! When we intersect with the suffix check \(Q_2 = W[9..12] = \texttt{abab}\) occurring at \(l + b - 2^m = 3\) (\(W[3..6] = \texttt{abac} \ne \texttt{abab}\)), \(b = 6\) is immediately rejected! Because \(2^m + 2^m \ge b + 1\) for every \(b \in [2^m, 2^{m+1}-1]\), the head check \(P_m\) (first \(2^m\) chars) and tail check \(Q_m\) (last \(2^m\) chars) cover 100% of the candidate border with zero unverified middle characters.

  • Boundary Pitfall Audit (BP-4-01, BP-4-02):

    • One-Sided \(P_m\) Check Without \(Q_m\) Intersection (BP-4-01): Never accept an AP from \(\mathcal{A}_P\) alone when \(|\mathcal{A}_P| \ge 3\); either intersect \(\mathcal{A}_P\) with \(\mathcal{A}_Q\), or clamp the periodic continuation of \(P_m\) at \(l\) against \(\operatorname{LCE}(l, l + d)\) and at \(r\) against \(\operatorname{LCSuf}(r, r - d)\).
    • Small-AP Step Extrapolation (BP-4-02): Theorem 15.2 guarantees step \(d = x_2 - x_1 = \operatorname{per}(P_m)\) only when the AP has \(\ge 3\) elements! When \(|\mathcal{A}_P| \le 2\), two occurrences of \(P_m\) can overlap by less than \(2^m - \operatorname{per}(P_m)\) (a non-periodic border of \(P_m\)), so always test the \(\le 2\) candidate lengths directly in \(O(1)\) via \(\operatorname{LCE}(l, r - b + 1) \ge b\).

  • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) preprocessing and \(O(\log^2 N)\) time per query, in \(O(N \log N)\) space.

Problem CP-IV-02 — Memory-Bounded Substring Palindromic Margins & Peak Length

You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) of lowercase letters (\(1 \le N \le 3 \times 10^5\)) and \(Q\) online queries \([l, r]\) (\(1 \le Q \le 3 \times 10^5\)) under a strict \(64\text{ MB}\) memory limit.

For each query substring \(W = S[l..r]\), compute three quantities:

  1. \(|P_L(l, r)|\): the length of the longest palindromic prefix of \(S[l..r]\);
  2. \(|P_R(l, r)|\): the length of the longest palindromic suffix of \(S[l..r]\);
  3. \(\operatorname{LPS}(l, r)\): the length of the longest palindromic substring anywhere inside \(S[l..r]\), evaluated without binary searching the answer length!
  • Constraints: \(1 \le N, Q \le 3 \times 10^5\), Online Queries, Time Limit \(1.5\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(64\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination
  • Decoy Elimination (Ch 17 vs. Ch 15 Decoy Pair): Compare this directly against CP-IV-01:
    • In CP-IV-01, general borders of \(S[l..r]\) are not suffixes of \(S[1..r]\) in any single border tree, requiring 2D Persistent Segment Trees (\(O(N \log N)\) words, exceeding \(64\text{ MB}\)!).
    • Here, every palindromic suffix of \(S[l..r]\) is also a palindromic suffix of the prefix \(S[1..r]\)! Specifically, \(P_R(l, r)\) is simply the longest ancestor \(v\) of \(u_0 = \operatorname{last}[r]\) in \(\operatorname{PAM}(S)\) satisfying \(\operatorname{len}[v] \le r - l + 1\) (and symmetrically, \(P_L(l, r)\) is the longest ancestor of \(\operatorname{first}[l]\) in \(\operatorname{PAM}(S^R)\) with \(\operatorname{len}[v] \le r - l + 1\))!
  • Diagnostic Signal: Because the \(64\text{ MB}\) limit forbids a \(20 \times 3 \times 10^5\) binary-lifting table on \(\operatorname{PAM}(S)\) and \(\operatorname{PAM}(S^R)\), how do we locate the highest ancestor of \(u_0 = \operatorname{last}[r]\) with \(\operatorname{len}[v] \le r - l + 1\) using only \(O(N)\) PAM memory, and how do \(P_L(l, r)\) and \(P_R(l, r)\) immediately unlock \(\operatorname{LPS}(l, r)\) via a single RMQ lookup?
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Problem CP-IV-07 — Single-Point Mutation Palindrome Healing

You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) of lowercase letters (\(1 \le N \le 2 \times 10^5\)) and \(Q\) independent what-if queries (\(1 \le Q \le 2 \times 10^5\)). Each query specifies a position \(i \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) and a replacement lowercase character \(c \in \{\texttt{'a'}, \dots, \texttt{'z'}\}\), and asks:

If we temporarily change \(S[i]\) to \(c\) (leaving all other characters of \(S\) unchanged), what is the total number of non-empty palindromic substrings (counted with multiplicity) in the modified string?

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N, Q \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\), Time Limit \(1.5\text{ s}\), Memory Limit \(256\text{ MB}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Decoy Elimination

  • Decoy Elimination: Re-running Manacher per query takes \(\Theta(QN)\). Moreover, a single character mutation at index \(i\) affects palindromes centered across the entire string \(S[1..N]\), so neither a local window nor a PAM fail-tree walk applies directly.

  • Diagnostic Signal (Ch 17 Manacher Geometry + Ch 15 \(O(1)\) LCE Synthesis): Let \(T_0 = \sum_{m} d[m]\) be the baseline total number of palindromes in \(S\). When \(S[i]\) is changed to \(c \ne S[i]\), decompose the net change \(\Delta(i, c)\) across all \(2N - 1\) Manacher centers \(m\) into two independent effects:

    1. Destruction (destroyed[i], independent of \(c \ne S[i]\)!): Any center \(m \ne i\) whose baseline Manacher palindrome strictly covers index \(i\) now mismatches at \(i\) and its mirror \(2m - i\), losing all outer radii from \(|m - i|\) up to its baseline radius!
    2. Creation / Mismatch Healing (healed[i][c]): Which centers \(m\) can gain new palindromic radii when \(S[i]\) becomes \(c\)? Only those centers \(m\) whose very first mismatch in the baseline string \(S\) landed at index \(i\) (and whose opposite boundary character equals \(c\))!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

  1. Second-Order Difference Array for Destruction Pyramids (destroyed[1..N]): Consider an odd center \(m \in [1, N]\) with baseline odd radius \(d_1[m] \ge 2\) (spanning \([m - d_1[m] + 1, \; m + d_1[m] - 1]\)).

    • Changing \(S[m]\) itself never destroys any odd palindrome centered at \(m\)!
    • For any \(i \in [m - d_1[m] + 1, \; m - 1]\) (and symmetrically \(i \in [m + 1, \; m + d_1[m] - 1]\)), changing \(S[i]\) to any \(c \ne S[i]\) truncates center \(m\)’s radius from \(d_1[m]\) down to \(m - i\), destroying exactly: \[\Delta_{\text{loss}}(m, i) = d_1[m] - |m - i| \quad \text{palindromes!}\] Notice that \(i \mapsto d_1[m] - |m - i|\) is a linear progression of slope \(+1\) on \([m - d_1[m] + 1, m - 1]\) and slope \(-1\) on \([m + 1, m + d_1[m] - 1]\)! The exact same linear ramp holds for even centers \(d_2[m]\). Thus we can accumulate destroyed[1..N] across all \(2N - 1\) centers simultaneously in \(O(N)\) total time using a 2nd-order difference array (prefix sums of prefix sums)!

  2. Mismatch Healing via Ch 15 \(O(1)\) LCE (healed[i][c]): For each center \(m\), let its first mismatch outside its baseline Manacher interval be the pair of indices \((l_m, r_m) = (m - d_1[m], \; m + d_1[m])\) (if \(1 \le l_m\) and \(r_m \le N\)). If we change \(S[l_m]\) to \(c = S[r_m]\) (or change \(S[r_m]\) to \(c = S[l_m]\)), the mismatch at \((l_m, r_m)\) is healed, and center \(m\)’s radius expands by: \[\Delta_{\text{gain}}(m) = 1 + \operatorname{LCE}_{S, S^R}\bigl(l_m - 1,\; r_m + 1\bigr)!\] Since there are only \(2N - 1\) centers \(m\), there are at most \(2(2N - 1)\) healing events \((l_m, S[r_m])\) and \((r_m, S[l_m])\) across the entire string!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic & Data-Structure Assembly
  1. Run Manacher’s algorithm on \(S[1..N]\) to obtain \(d_1[1..N]\), \(d_2[1..N-1]\), and baseline total \(T_0 = \sum d_1[m] + \sum d_2[m]\).
  2. Build the Suffix Array + \(O(1)\) RMQ on \(S \# S^R\) (or \(S\) and \(S^R\)) in \(O(N \log N)\) time to support \(O(1)\) outward \(\operatorname{LCE}_{S, S^R}(x, y) = \operatorname{LCP}(S[1..x]^R, S[y..N])\).
  3. For each of the \(2N - 1\) centers \(m\):
    • Add its two linear destruction ramps on \([m - d + 1, m - 1]\) and \([m + 1, m + d - 1]\) to a 2nd-order difference array diff2[1..N+2].
    • If its first mismatch pair \((l_m, r_m)\) lies inside \([1, N]^2\), compute \(g = 1 + \operatorname{LCE}_{S, S^R}(l_m - 1, r_m + 1)\) in \(O(1)\) time, and add \(+g\) to both healed[l_m][S[r_m] - 'a'] and healed[r_m][S[l_m] - 'a'].
  4. Integrate diff2 twice to obtain destroyed[1..N].
  5. Answer each query \((i, c)\) in \(O(1)\) time: if \(c == S[i]\), output \(T_0\); else output \(T_0 - \text{destroyed}[i] + \text{healed}[i][c - \texttt{'a'}]\).
