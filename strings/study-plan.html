Study Plan, Multi-Track Roadmaps & Blind Checkpoint Protocol

Navigating the 7-Part, 30-Chapter Zero-Forward-Reference Prerequisite DAG, 4-Tier Difficulty Ladders, and Unspoiled Diagnostic Checkpoints

Studying advanced string algorithms requires a fundamentally different training protocol from learning introductory string templates. Copying a Suffix Automaton or Palindromic Tree from your library takes two minutes; recognizing why a contest problem collapses via the Weak Periodicity Lemma, Significant Suffixes, Bannai’s Lyndon-Root Runs, \([l+L-1, r]\)-Clipped \(\text{endpos}\) Merging, or Tiskin’s Unit-Monge Seaweed Permutation is what separates grandmasters from template users.

This page provides four targeted Multi-Track Study Roadmaps (Tracks A–D), the complete 30-Chapter Zero-Forward-Reference Prerequisite DAG, the 4-Tier Smooth Difficulty Ladder & Blind Checkpoint Protocol (CP-I–CP-VII), and the Complete 30-Chapter + 7-Checkpoint Mastery Gate Table. Keep Notation, Operators & Failure Modes open alongside your work.

1. Multi-Track Study Roadmaps (Tracks A–D)

Choose the track below that matches your upcoming contest circuit and current fluency:

3. How to Use the 4-Tier Difficulty Ladders & Blind Checkpoints (CP-I–CP-VII)

3.1 The Per-Chapter 4-Tier Smooth Difficulty Ladder (8–10 Problems per Chapter)

Every chapter concludes with a curated problem ladder structured into four deliberate tiers:

  1. Tier 1 · Stepping Stone (2 Problems): Isolates the core combinatorial word invariant on a clean geometry (e.g., direct slink partition count or single-query \([l+L-1, r]\)-clipped \(\text{endpos}\) lookup). Goal: Verify your index boundaries and invariant invariants before adding auxiliary data structures.
  2. Tier 2 · Intermediate (2 Problems): Couples the chapter’s word invariant with a non-trivial bijection or secondary reduction (e.g., double-end interleaving \(T = S[1]S[N]S[2]S[N-1]\dots\) in CF 932G, or single-witness chain testing in CF 700E).
  3. Tier 3 · Advanced (2–3 Problems): Full NOI / Codeforces Div. 1 D–E / AtCoder ARC/AGC scale problems requiring careful complexity balancing, two-threshold sweeps, or predecessor length jumps ([NOI2018] 你的名字, [ZJOI2017] 字符串, [BJWC2018] Border 的四种求法).
  4. Tier 4 · Mastery (2 Problems): Grandmaster frontier tasks requiring binomial moment splitting, persistent tree-branching, Unit-Monge \(\boxdot\) divide-and-conquer, or \(N \le 10^{18}\) compressed/morphic queries (Universal Cup, QOJ, 国家集训队论文).

3.2 The Unspoiled Blind Checkpoint Protocol & Diagnostic Decoy Pairs

In a real contest, no problem comes labeled “Use Significant Suffixes (Ch 06)” or “Use PAM slink Telescoping (Ch 17)”. Even worse, many advanced string problems exhibit nearly identical surface symptoms while requiring completely different mathematical invariants.

Therefore, each Part concludes with a Blind Topic-Group Checkpoint (CP-I–CP-VII) built around Diagnostic Decoy Pairs:

WarningStrict 3-Stage No-Spoiler Protocol for Blind Checkpoints (CP-I–CP-VII)

When attempting any problem in CP-I through CP-VII:

  1. Stage 1 · Cold Diagnostic Classification (10–15 Minutes, Zero Hints Open):
    • Read the raw mathematical problem statement and constraints (\(N, Q, |\Sigma|\), online vs. offline).
    • Without opening any dropdown, write down: (a) why the naive automaton/array approach takes \(\Omega(N^2)\), (b) which Diagnostic Decoy Pair the problem belongs to, and (c) your candidate combinatorial word invariant.
  2. Stage 2 · Progressive Hint Escalation (Hint 1 \(\to\) Hint 2 \(\to\) Hint 3):
    • Open Hint 1 (Diagnostic Signal) only to verify whether you diagnosed the right structural bottleneck—Hint 1 still withholds the exact formula.
    • Open Hint 2 (Combinatorial Word Invariant) if you need the double-overlap / periodicity / Monge identity.
    • Open Hint 3 (Algorithmic Assembly) if you need the data-structure coupling or boundary clipping rule.
  3. Stage 3 · Post-Solve Counterexample Audit:
    • After coding or deriving your solution, open the Complete Derivation & Solution and check the Boundary Pitfall box to ensure your solution survives Sturmian/Fibonacci and prefix-tie counterexamples. Do not advance to the next Part until you solve at least 6 of 7 (or 6 of 8) blind problems.

3.3 The 7-Part Diagnostic Decoy Atlas (Disambiguating Look-Alike Contest Symptoms)

Each blind checkpoint tests whether you can separate competing techniques whose problem statements look nearly identical at first glance:

Blind Checkpoint Competing Look-Alike Symptoms (The Decoy Pair / Triad) Decisive Structural Discriminator
CP-I (Part I) Online Border Weight Maintenance (CF 1286E) vs. Palindrome/Border Partition DP (CF 932G) If weights change dynamically across all active borders of \(S[1..r]\), use slink active-set deletion (Ch 02); if each left endpoint \(i = r - L\) has a fixed prefix DP value \(dp[i]\), use shift-overlap g[u] accumulation (Ch 04).
CP-II (Part II) All-Prefix Minimum Suffix (Luogu P6127) vs. Substring Minimum Suffix under Right-Extension ([ZJOI2017]) When right-extension \(T = \varepsilon\) on prefixes \(S[1..r]\), Duval state sharing \(r \to r-dp\) (Ch 05) works in \(O(1)\); when \(T \ne \varepsilon\) or \([l, r]\) is a substring, left-truncation revives killed suffixes (aaba), requiring Significant Suffixes \(|\text{Cand}| \le \lfloor \log_2 |U| \rfloor + 1\) (Ch 06).
CP-III (Part III) External Pattern in \(S[l..r]\) ([NOI2018]) vs. Internal Pairwise LCP in \(S[l..r]\) (CF 1043G) vs. Online push_front ([BZOJ 3682]) External pattern containment \(\implies\) \([l+L-1, r]\)-clipped \(\text{endpos}\) SegTree Merge (Ch 10); internal \(\binom{r-l+1}{2}\) suffix pairs \(\implies\) \(2N\log_2 N\) Dominant Pairs (Ch 13); online encrypted prepend \(\implies\) 64-bit Scapegoat Suffix Balanced Tree (Ch 14).
CP-IV (Part IV) Range Longest Border ([BJWC2018]) vs. Range Longest Palindrome (Range LPS) Borders are non-hereditary (must intersect dyadic prefix \(P_m\) and suffix \(Q_m\) APs in \(O(\log N)\), Ch 15); palindromes are center-hereditary (all centers \(c \in (c_L, c_R)\) are 100% unclipped, giving true \(O(1)\) Range LPS, Ch 17).
CP-V (Part V) Involution Matching \(\pi^2=\text{id}\) (CF 1334G) vs. General \(\pi\) vs. Parameterized/Cartesian KMP If \(\pi^2 = \text{id}\), canonical 2-cycle representatives solve it in \(O(N)\) KMP (Ch 18); general non-involution \(\pi\) requires FFT (Ch 18); dynamic bijective renaming or heap shape requires \(O(1)\) window-clipped backward offsets (Ch 20).
CP-VI (Part VI) Range Distinct Subsequences vs. Gap-2 / Tropical \((\min,+)\) Subsequences vs. Interval LCS ([THUPC 2021]) Ring subsequence DP has \(\det M_c = 1\) (\(O(|\Sigma|)\) matrix-free dot product \(\mathbf{u}_r^T\cdot \mathbf{v}_{l-1}\), Ch 22); gap-2 (\(\det=0\)) and tropical \((\min,+)\) lack inverses (require Segment Trees); 2-string interval alignment uses Tiskin’s Seaweed \(W[k] < l\) (Ch 23).
CP-VII (Part VII) Bulk Palindrome Equations ([SCOI2016]) vs. Adjacency Stack Cancellation (CF 1223F) Static substring/palindrome equalities \(S[l_1..r_1] = S[l_2..r_2]^R\) use Signed Binary-Lifting DSU with is_pal[k][r] (Ch 26); dynamic adjacent cancellation cc \(\to \varepsilon\) uses Stack-Trie Path \(\text{LCA}\) LCE or \(GL_2/SL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\) (Ch 29).
