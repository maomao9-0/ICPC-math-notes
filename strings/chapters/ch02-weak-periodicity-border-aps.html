1. Diagnostic Signal: Why Walking Raw \(\pi\)-Chains Takes \(\Theta(N)\) Per Step

1.1 Three Contest Scenarios Where Standard KMP Degrades to \(\Theta(N^2)\)

In basic string matching, the KMP algorithm processes an entire string \(S[1..N]\) in \(O(N)\) amortized time because the failure pointer \(u \leftarrow \pi[u]\) decreases \(u\) by at least \(1\) while each character append increases \(u\) by at most \(1\). That amortization breaks completely in three common competitive programming settings:

  1. Enumerating All Borders of Every Prefix: Suppose a DP or counting problem requires inspecting all proper borders \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..u]) = \{\pi[u], \pi[\pi[u]], \dots, 0\}\) at every right endpoint \(u = 1 \dots N\). On \(S = \texttt{a}^N\), \(\pi[u] = u - 1\), so \(|\operatorname{Border}(S[1..u])| = u\) and walking the \(\pi\)-chain takes \(\sum_{u=1}^N u = \Theta(N^2)\) time.
  2. Online Short-Append Queries (CF 1721E — Prefix Function Queries): Suppose a base string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 10^6\)) is fixed, and \(Q \le 10^5\) independent queries each temporarily append a short string \(T_q\) (\(|T_q| \le 10\)), asking for \(\pi[N + 1 .. N + |T_q|]\). Because each query resets \(S\) back to length \(N\), a single query on \(S = \texttt{a}^N\) with \(T_q = \texttt{b}\) walks \(\pi[N] = N - 1 \to N - 2 \to \dots \to 0\) in \(\Theta(N)\) time, giving \(\Theta(Q N)\) total time! (And building a full \(N \times |\Sigma|\) KMP automaton table uses \(26 \times 4\text{ MB} = 104\text{ MB}\) or fails entirely over large alphabets.)
  3. Online Weighted Border Maintenance (CF 1286E — Fedya the Potter Strikes Back): Suppose characters \(S[i]\) and weights \(W[i]\) arrive online, and we maintain a weight map over all active borders \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..i])\). At step \(i\), any border \(u\) of \(S[1..i-1]\) with \(S[u + 1] = S[i]\) survives (extending to \(u + 1\)), while any border with \(S[u + 1] \ne S[i]\) dies permanently. If we walk the raw \(\pi\)-chain from \(\pi[i - 1]\) down to \(0\), we can visit \(\Theta(i)\) surviving borders at every step (e.g., on \(S = \texttt{a}^N\)), again degrading to \(\Theta(N^2)\)!

Every one of these \(\Theta(N)\) failure chains is long only because its borders form long arithmetic progressions. Once we compress each arithmetic progression into a single \(O(1)\) jump via \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\) or \(\operatorname{anc}[u]\), the worst-case chain depth collapses from \(\Theta(N)\) to \(\le \lceil \log_2 N \rceil\).

2. Core Combinatorial Word Invariants: Half-Length APs & The \(\frac{2}{3} / \frac{1}{2}\) Length Drop (T-1A-02)

2.1 The Weak Periodicity Lemma & The Half-Length Single-AP Theorem

Recall from Chapter 01 (Theorem 1.2) that if \(p, q \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u])\) and \(u \ge p + q - \gcd(p, q)\), then \(\gcd(p, q) \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u])\). Even the slightly weaker bound \(u \ge p + q\) (known as the Weak Periodicity Lemma) is sufficient whenever both periods are at most \(u / 2\): \[p \le \frac{u}{2} \;\land\; q \le \frac{u}{2} \implies p + q \le u \implies \gcd(p, q) \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u]).\]

What does this say about all borders of \(S[1..u]\) whose lengths lie in the upper half \([\lceil u/2 \rceil, u - 1]\)? By Period–Border Duality (Theorem 1.1), a border \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..u])\) satisfies \(b \ge \lceil u/2 \rceil\) if and only if its complementary period \(p = u - b \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u])\) satisfies \(p \le \lfloor u/2 \rfloor\).

ImportantTheorem 2.1 (Half-Length Single-AP Theorem)

Let \(S[1..u]\) be a word of length \(u \ge 1\) with shortest period \(p_{\min} = \operatorname{per}(S[1..u]) = u - \pi[u]\).

  1. Divisibility of Short Periods: If \(p_{\min} \le \lfloor u/2 \rfloor\), then every period \(p \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u])\) with \(p \le \lfloor u/2 \rfloor\) is an exact integer multiple of \(p_{\min}\) (\(p_{\min} \mid p\)).
  2. Single Arithmetic Progression of Long Borders: Equivalently, all proper borders of \(S[1..u]\) of length \(\ge \lceil u/2 \rceil\) form a single arithmetic progression with common difference \(d = p_{\min} = u - \pi[u]\): \[\boxed{\operatorname{Border}(S[1..u]) \cap \bigl[\lceil u/2 \rceil,\, u - 1\bigr] = \bigl\{\,u - k \cdot p_{\min} \;\big|\; 1 \le k \le \lfloor u / (2 p_{\min}) \rfloor\,\bigr\}.}\]

Proof of Theorem 2.1

Suppose \(p \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u])\) satisfies \(p \le \lfloor u/2 \rfloor\). Since \(p_{\min} = \min \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u]) \le p \le \lfloor u/2 \rfloor\), the sum of the two periods satisfies: \[p_{\min} + p \le \lfloor u/2 \rfloor + \lfloor u/2 \rfloor \le u.\] By the Weak Periodicity Lemma (Theorem 1.2), \(g = \gcd(p_{\min}, p)\) is also a valid period in \(\operatorname{Per}(S[1..u])\). Because \(p_{\min}\) is the minimum positive period of \(S[1..u]\) and \(1 \le g \le p_{\min}\), we must have \(g = p_{\min}\), which means \(p_{\min} \mid p\). Conversely, any integer multiple \(k \cdot p_{\min} < u\) (\(k \ge 1\)) is automatically a period of \(S[1..u]\) because \(S[i] = S[i + p_{\min}] = \dots = S[i + k \cdot p_{\min}]\) for all \(1 \le i \le u - k \cdot p_{\min}\). Translating via \(b = u - p\) yields the arithmetic progression \(b_k = u - k \cdot p_{\min}\). \(\blacksquare\)

2.2 Defining diff[u] and slink[u] on KMP and PAM Fail Trees

Theorem 2.1 motivates compressing every chain of consecutive borders (or palindromic suffixes) that share the same period step into a single pointer jump. Let \(\operatorname{fail}[u]\) denote the parent of node \(u\) in the failure tree:

  • In KMP: Node \(u \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) represents prefix \(S[1..u]\) of length \(\operatorname{len}[u] = u\), and \(\operatorname{fail}[u] = \pi[u]\).
  • In PAM (Eertree): Node \(u\) represents a distinct palindromic substring of length \(\operatorname{len}[u]\), and \(\operatorname{fail}[u]\) is its longest proper palindromic suffix.

Why does PAM obey the exact same border algebra as KMP? Because for any palindrome \(Q = Q[1..m]\), its reversal is \(Q^R = Q\), so any proper suffix \(Q[m - b + 1 .. m]\) of length \(b < m\) is a palindrome if and only if it equals the prefix \(Q[1..b]\)!

NoteLemma 2.2 (Palindromic Suffix ↔︎ Border Equivalence)

For any palindrome \(Q\) of length \(m\), a proper suffix of \(Q\) of length \(b \in [0, m - 1]\) is a palindrome if and only if \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(Q)\). Consequently, the PAM failure chain \(u \to \operatorname{fail}[u] \to \operatorname{fail}[\operatorname{fail}[u]] \to \dots \to 0\) enumerates the exact border lengths of the palindrome \(u\).

For every node \(u\) with \(\operatorname{len}[u] \ge 1\) in either KMP or PAM, define its local period step \(\operatorname{diff}[u]\) and series link (等差数列跳链) \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\) by: \[\boxed{\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[u]], \qquad \operatorname{slink}[u] = \begin{cases} \operatorname{slink}[\operatorname{fail}[u]] & \text{if } \operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{fail}[u]], \\ \operatorname{fail}[u] & \text{if } \operatorname{diff}[u] \ne \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]. \end{cases}}\]

2.3 Two Proofs of the \(O(\log N)\) Depth Bound: Dyadic Scales & The Exact \(\frac{2}{3} / \frac{1}{2}\) Drop

How many slink jumps can occur along the path from \(u\) down to \(0\)? We give two complementary proofs: a macro-scale dyadic argument and a sharp micro-scale inequality at every diff change.

ImportantTheorem 2.3 (Exact \(\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\frac{1}{2}\) Length-Drop Inequalities & Half-Open AP Partition)

Let \(u\) be a state in the KMP or PAM fail tree with \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) and \(w = \operatorname{fail}[v]\), and let \(d_1 = \operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[v]\) and \(d_2 = \operatorname{diff}[v] = \operatorname{len}[v] - \operatorname{len}[w]\).

  1. Monotonicity of Period Steps (\(d_2 \le d_1\)): Because \(v\) is a prefix of \(u\), the minimum period \(d_1 = \operatorname{per}(u)\) of \(u\) is also a valid period of \(v\). Therefore \(d_2 = \operatorname{per}(v) \le d_1\).

  2. Sharp \(\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\frac{1}{2}\) Length Drop When \(d_2 < d_1\): Whenever a series-link boundary occurs between \(u\) and \(v\) (i.e., \(d_2 < d_1\), so \(\operatorname{slink}[u] = v\)), the overlap between the prefix copy of \(v\) and suffix copy of \(v\) inside \(u\) has length \(2\operatorname{len}[v] - \operatorname{len}[u] = \operatorname{len}[v] - d_1\), which must satisfy \(\operatorname{len}[v] - d_1 < d_2\). Consequently: \[\boxed{\operatorname{len}[v] < d_1 + d_2 < 2 d_1 \implies \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[u]] < \frac{2}{3}\operatorname{len}[u], \qquad \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]] = \operatorname{len}[v] - d_2 < d_1 < \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{len}[u]!}\] In particular, every jump \(u \leftarrow \operatorname{slink}[u]\) strictly shrinks \(\operatorname{len}[u]\) by at least \(\frac{1}{2}\): if the chain \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) has \(\ge 2\) edges (\(u \to v \to \dots \to \operatorname{slink}[u]\)), then \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] \le \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]] < \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{len}[u]\); and even on consecutive single-edge jumps, two jumps reduce the length to \(< \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{len}[u]\).

  3. Exact Disjoint Half-Open Partition \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\): Let \(v_c\) be the lowest ancestor of \(u\) with \(\operatorname{diff}[v_c] = \operatorname{diff}[u] = d\), so \(\operatorname{slink}[u] = \operatorname{fail}[v_c]\). Because \(\operatorname{len}[v_c] - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] = \operatorname{diff}[v_c] = d\), the half-open interval of ancestors \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) has lengths forming the exact arithmetic progression: \[\boxed{\mathcal{L}(u) = \bigl\{\,\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + d,\; \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + 2d,\; \dots,\; \operatorname{len}[u]\,\bigr\}.}\] Iterating \(u \leftarrow \operatorname{slink}[u]\) while \(\operatorname{len}[u] > 0\) partitions all positive borders/palindromic suffixes into \(\le \lceil \log_2 \operatorname{len}[u] \rceil\) disjoint half-open arithmetic progressions.

Proof of Theorem 2.3

  1. Why \(\operatorname{len}[v] - d_1 < d_2\) when \(d_2 < d_1\): Inside \(u\), the prefix \(u[1..\operatorname{len}[v]]\) equals \(v\) and the suffix \(u[d_1 + 1 .. \operatorname{len}[u]]\) also equals \(v\). Both copies of \(v\) have minimum period \(d_2 = \operatorname{per}(v) < d_1\), and they overlap on the middle substring \(M = u[d_1 + 1 .. \operatorname{len}[v]]\) of length \(|M| = \operatorname{len}[v] - d_1\). Suppose for contradiction that \(|M| = \operatorname{len}[v] - d_1 \ge d_2\). Then for every index \(i \in [1, \operatorname{len}[u] - d_2]\):

    • If \(i + d_2 \le \operatorname{len}[v]\), both \(i\) and \(i + d_2\) lie in the prefix copy of \(v\), so \(u[i] = u[i + d_2]\) by period \(d_2\) of \(v\).
    • If \(i + d_2 > \operatorname{len}[v]\), then since \(|M| = \operatorname{len}[v] - d_1 \ge d_2\), we have \(i > \operatorname{len}[v] - d_2 \ge d_1\), meaning \(i \ge d_1 + 1\). Thus both \(i\) and \(i + d_2\) lie inside the suffix copy \(u[d_1 + 1 .. \operatorname{len}[u]]\) of \(v\), so again \(u[i] = u[i + d_2]\) by period \(d_2\) of \(v\)! This implies that \(d_2\) is a valid period of the entire word \(u\), contradicting that \(d_1 = \operatorname{per}(u) > d_2\) is the minimum period of \(u\)!

  2. Deriving the \(\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\frac{1}{2}\) bounds: Since \(\operatorname{len}[v] - d_1 < d_2\), we have: \[\operatorname{len}[v] < d_1 + d_2.\]

    • Using \(d_2 \le d_1 - 1 < d_1\) and \(d_1 = \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[v]\): \[\operatorname{len}[v] < 2 d_1 = 2(\operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[v]) \implies 3\operatorname{len}[v] < 2\operatorname{len}[u] \implies \operatorname{len}[v] < \frac{2}{3}\operatorname{len}[u].\]

    • For the grandchild \(w = \operatorname{fail}[v]\), its length is \(\operatorname{len}[w] = \operatorname{len}[v] - d_2\). Rearranging \(\operatorname{len}[v] - d_1 < d_2\) gives: \[\operatorname{len}[w] = \operatorname{len}[v] - d_2 < d_1 = \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[v] < \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[w] \implies 2\operatorname{len}[w] < \operatorname{len}[u] \implies \operatorname{len}[w] < \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{len}[u]!\]

  3. Why \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]]\) always drops by \(> 2\times\) when \(k \ge 2\) (and in every dyadic interval \([2^{j-1}, 2^j)\)): By Theorem 2.1, all borders of \(u\) in \([\lceil \operatorname{len}[u]/2 \rceil, \operatorname{len}[u] - 1]\) share step \(d_1 = \operatorname{diff}[u]\), so \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\) must have \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] < \lceil \operatorname{len}[u]/2 \rceil\), i.e., \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] \le \lfloor \operatorname{len}[u]/2 \rfloor\) on every single jump! \(\blacksquare\)

3. Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: slink[u] vs. anc[u] & Online Border Deletion

3.1 Unifying KMP Shortcut Links: slink[u] vs. anc[u]

Across grandmaster KMP problems (CF 1721E, CF 1286E, CF 1764G), two seemingly different shortcut pointers on the KMP failure tree appear in the literature:

  1. The Period-Difference Link (\(\operatorname{slink}[u]\)): Depends only on prefix \(S[1..u]\): \[\operatorname{slink}[u] = \begin{cases} \operatorname{slink}[\pi[u]] & \text{if } u - \pi[u] = \pi[u] - \pi[\pi[u]], \\ \pi[u] & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}\]

  2. The Next-Character Mismatch Link (\(\operatorname{anc}[u]\)): Depends on \(S[1..u + 1]\) (including the character \(S[u + 1]\) immediately following prefix \(u\)): \[\operatorname{anc}[u] = \begin{cases} \operatorname{anc}[\pi[u]] & \text{if } S[\pi[u] + 1] = S[u + 1], \\ \pi[u] & \text{if } S[\pi[u] + 1] \ne S[u + 1]. \end{cases}\]

How are \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\) and \(\operatorname{anc}[u]\) related? Why can \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\) skip an entire arithmetic progression during a KMP character transition without even looking at \(S[u + 1]\)? The answer is a fundamental structural theorem:

ImportantTheorem 2.4 (Next-Character Equality Inside a slink Chain & Coarsening Duality)

Suppose \(u \ge 1\) satisfies \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\pi[u]] = d\). Let \(v = \pi[u] = u - d\) and \(w = \pi[v] = u - 2d\). Then the characters immediately following prefixes \(v\) and \(w\) in \(S[1..u]\) are guaranteed to be identical: \[\boxed{\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\pi[u]] = d \implies S\bigl[\pi[u] + 1\bigr] = S\bigl[\pi[\pi[u]] + 1\bigr]!}\] Consequently:

  1. Uniform Next Character Across Strict Ancestors in \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u)\): For every node \(x\) in the chain \(\pi[u], \pi[\pi[u]], \dots, v_c\) above \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\), the next character \(S[x + 1]\) equals \(S[\pi[u] + 1]\). Thus testing \(S[\pi[u] + 1] == c\) once tests all borders in \([\operatorname{slink}[u], \pi[u]]\) simultaneously!
  2. Coarsening Duality (\(\operatorname{anc}[u]\) Coarsens \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\)): Whenever \(\operatorname{diff}[v] = \operatorname{diff}[\pi[v]]\), Theorem 2.4 forces \(S[\pi[v] + 1] = S[v + 1]\), so \(\operatorname{anc}[v] = \operatorname{anc}[\pi[v]]\) automatically skips across every internal edge of a slink chain (plus any accidental matches \(S[\pi[v]+1] = S[v+1]\) across different APs).

Proof of Theorem 2.4

Because \(d = u - \pi[u]\) is a period of \(S[1..u]\), every index \(i \in [1, u - d]\) satisfies \(S[i] = S[i + d]\). Now evaluate this period equality at the specific index \(i = w + 1 = \pi[\pi[u]] + 1 = u - 2d + 1\):

  • Lower bound (\(i \ge 1\)): Since \(w = \pi[\pi[u]] \ge 0\), we have \(i = w + 1 \ge 1\).

  • Upper bound (\(i + d \le u\)): Since \(\operatorname{diff}[v] = v - w = d \ge 1\), we have: \[i + d = (u - 2d + 1) + d = u - d + 1 = \pi[u] + 1 = v + 1 \le v + d = u!\]

  • Because both indices \(w + 1 = \pi[\pi[u]] + 1\) and \(v + 1 = \pi[u] + 1\) lie strictly inside the already-known prefix \(S[1..u]\), period \(d \in \operatorname{Per}(S[1..u])\) forces: \[S\bigl[\pi[\pi[u]] + 1\bigr] = S[w + 1] = S[w + 1 + d] = S[v + 1] = S\bigl[\pi[u] + 1\bigr]. \quad \blacksquare\]

3.2 Algorithmic Compilation: Three Production Contracts

Contract 1: \(O(N)\) Precomputation of diff[u] and slink[u]

// Works identically on KMP (where len[u] = u, fail[u] = pi[u]) and PAM:
diff[u] = len[u] - len[fail[u]];
slink[u] = (diff[u] == diff[fail[u]]) ? slink[fail[u]] : fail[u];

Contract 2: \(O(\log N)\) KMP Next-Character Transition via slink (CF 1721E, CF 1764G)

Suppose we are at KMP state \(u\) and want the longest border \(v \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..u]) \cup \{0\}\) that extends by character \(c\) (i.e., \(S[v + 1] == c\)).

  • First check \(v = u\): if \(S[u + 1] == c\), return \(u + 1\).
  • Otherwise, while \(u > 0\):
    • Check \(v = \pi[u]\): if \(S[\pi[u] + 1] == c\), then \(\pi[u]\) is the longest matching border in \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\), so return \(\pi[u] + 1\) immediately!
    • If \(S[\pi[u] + 1] \ne c\): by Theorem 2.4, every node in \([\operatorname{slink}[u], \pi[u]]\) has the exact same next character \(S[\pi[u] + 1] \ne c\), so none of them can match \(c\)! We therefore jump directly to \(u \leftarrow \operatorname{slink}[u]\) in \(O(1)\) time!
  • Because \(u \leftarrow \operatorname{slink}[u]\) halves \(u\) at every step (Theorem 2.3), this loop executes at most \(\lceil \log_2 u \rceil\) iterations in the worst case—requiring zero \(|\Sigma|\)-sized automaton tables!

Contract 3: Amortized \(O(N)\) Online Active-Border Deletion via anc[u] (CF 1286E)

When appending \(S[i]\) online at step \(i\):

  1. Before advancing \(\pi[i]\), we know \(S[i]\), so we can finalize \(\operatorname{anc}[i - 1]\): \[\operatorname{anc}[i - 1] = \bigl(S[\pi[i - 1] + 1] == S[i]\bigr) \;\text{?}\; \operatorname{anc}[\pi[i - 1]] : \pi[i - 1].\]

  2. To find all borders \(u \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..i - 1])\) that fail to extend by \(S[i]\) (\(S[u + 1] \ne S[i]\)) without touching surviving borders:

    int u = pi[i - 1];
while (u > 0) {
    if (S[u + 1] != S[i]) {
        delete_border_from_active_set(u); // u dies permanently at step i!
        u = pi[u];
    } else {
        u = anc[u]; // Skip ALL consecutive ancestors v with S[v+1] == S[u+1] == S[i]!
    }
}

    Why is this \(O(N)\) total work across all \(i = 1 \dots N\)? Every time the if branch runs, border \(u\) is permanently deleted (at most \(1\) border is inserted per step \(i\), so the if branch runs \(\le N\) times in total). And whenever the else branch runs (\(u \leftarrow \operatorname{anc}[u]\)), it lands directly on a node \(u'\) with \(S[u' + 1] \ne S[u + 1] = S[i]\) (or \(0\)), so the very next iteration is guaranteed to take the if branch (or terminate)! Thus the else branch also runs \(\le N\) times in total!

Problem 2.1 (W1A-05) — [POI XIII / 2006] OKR-Periods of Words (Okresy słów / Luogu P3435)

  • Mathematical Statement: For a word \(W[1..i]\), an integer \(p \in [1, i - 1]\) is called a proper period of \(W\) if \(p \in \operatorname{Per}(W)\) and \(2 p \ge i\) (equivalently, \(W[1..p]\) has length \(< i\) and \(W\) is a prefix of \((W[1..p])^2\)).   • Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 10^6\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\), time limit \(1.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Walking the KMP failure chain \(\pi[i] \to \pi[\pi[i]] \to \dots\) from each \(i\) down to its smallest positive border takes \(\Theta(N^2)\) total time on \(S = \texttt{a}^N\).

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Thus the minimum positive border \(b_{\min}(i)\) (where \(\pi[b_{\min}] = 0\)) is always \(\le \lfloor i / 2 \rfloor\). Precompute min_pos_border[u] = (pi[u] == 0 ? u : min_pos_border[pi[u]]) in \(O(N)\) time via path compression!

Problem 2.3 (W1A-12) — Luogu P5829 — Border Tree LCA & Period-Constrained Common Borders

  • Mathematical Statement: Given a string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 10^6\)) and \(Q \le 10^5\) queries \((p_q, q_q)\) (\(1 \le p_q, q_q \le N\)), find for each query the longest string \(W\) that is simultaneously a proper border of \(S[1..p_q]\) and a proper border of \(S[1..q_q]\), using \(O(N)\) memory and \(O(\log N)\) time per query via slink chain jumping (without \(O(N \log N)\)-space binary lifting tables!).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 10^6\), \(1 \le Q \le 10^5\), memory limit \(64\text{ MB}\)–\(125\text{ MB}\), time limit \(1.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

A \(20 \times 10^6\) binary lifting table up[20][N] uses \(80\text{ MB}\) of memory and \(O(N \log N)\) preprocessing. How can we compute the Lowest Common Ancestor (LCA) on the KMP failure tree in \(O(\log N)\) time per query using only the three \(O(N)\) arrays \(\pi[u]\), \(\operatorname{diff}[u]\), and \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\)?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

By Theorem 2.3, the path from any node \(u\) to the root \(0\) in the KMP failure tree is partitioned into at most \(\lceil \log_2 u \rceil\) half-open arithmetic progressions \((\operatorname{slink}[x], x]\), acting exactly like a zero-overhead Heavy-Light Decomposition (HLD) where each “heavy chain” is a constant-diff arithmetic progression!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

First replace \((u, v) \leftarrow (\pi[p_q], \pi[q_q])\) because the common border must be proper for both \(p_q\) and \(q_q\). While \(\operatorname{slink}[u] \ne \operatorname{slink}[v]\), jump the node with the deeper chain top (\(\max(u, v)\) or larger \(\operatorname{slink}\)) via \(x \leftarrow \operatorname{slink}[x]\). Once two nodes lie in the same AP chain (or when one is an ancestor of the other within the same AP chain of step \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\) and \(u \equiv v \pmod d\)), their LCA is simply \(\min(u, v)\)!

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W1A-12)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Standard LCA binary lifting uses \(O(N \log N)\) space; raw \(\pi\)-chain walking uses \(O(N)\) time per query.
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • A string \(S[1..b]\) is a proper border of both \(S[1..p_q]\) and \(S[1..q_q]\) iff \(b\) is a common ancestor of \(\pi[p_q]\) and \(\pi[q_q]\) in the KMP fail tree.
    • Suppose \(u > v > 0\) are in the KMP fail tree. When is \(v\) an ancestor of \(u\) inside \(u\)’s current half-open AP chain \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) of step \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\)? Precisely when: \[v > \operatorname{slink}[u] \quad \land \quad (u - v) \bmod d == 0 \quad \land \quad \operatorname{slink}[v] == \operatorname{slink}[u] \;\text{(or } v == \operatorname{slink}[u]\text{)}!\] Why? Because inside \(S[1..u]\) of period \(d\), every shift by a multiple of \(d\) above \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\) is a valid border of \(u\)!
  3. Algorithmic Compilation (\(O(N)\)-Space slink-HLD LCA):

    • Precompute \(\pi[1..N]\), \(\operatorname{diff}[1..N]\), and \(\operatorname{slink}[1..N]\) in \(O(N)\) time and \(3 \times 4\text{ MB} = 12\text{ MB}\) space.

    • To query \(\operatorname{LCA}(u, v)\) (initialized to \(u = \pi[p_q], v = \pi[q_q]\)):

      int border_lca(int u, int v) {
    while (u != v) {
        if (u < v) swap(u, v);
        // Check if v lies directly inside u's AP chain [slink[u], u]:
        int d = diff[u], s = slink[u];
        if (v >= s && (u - v) % d == 0) {
            // Verify v is the actual chain ancestor of u:
            // Since u - d, u - 2d, ..., s are the exact ancestors of u down to s,
            // (u - v) % d == 0 with v >= s is BOTH necessary and sufficient!
            return v;
        }
        u = s; // Jump to the bottom of u's AP chain in O(1)!
    }
    return u;
}

    • Verification of Arithmetic Membership: Why is \(v \ge \operatorname{slink}[u]\) and \((u - v) \bmod \operatorname{diff}[u] == 0\) (when \(v\) is already reached from \(q_q\)) both necessary and sufficient? Because the ancestors of \(u\) down to \(s = \operatorname{slink}[u]\) are literally the integers \(\{u, u - d, u - 2d, \dots, s\}\) inside \(\{0, 1, \dots, N\}\)!     • Let \(0 = u_0 < u_1 < u_2 < \dots < u_k < u_{k+1} = N\) be the border chain of \(S[1..N]\), with \(d_r = u_r - u_{r-1}\).

    • By Theorem 2.3, whenever \(d_r > d_{r-1}\), the overlap \(u_{r-1} - d_r\) between the prefix and suffix copies of \(S[1..u_{r-1}]\) inside \(S[1..u_r]\) must be strictly smaller than the minimum period \(\operatorname{per}(S[1..u_{r-1}])\) of \(S[1..u_{r-1}]\).

    • On the Fibonacci words \(F_1 = \texttt{b}, F_2 = \texttt{a}, F_m = F_{m-1} F_{m-2}\) (\(|F_m| = 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, \dots\)), the central Sturmian prefix of length \(L_m = |F_m| - 2\) (\(1, 3, 6, 11, 19, 32, \dots\)) has minimum period \(|F_{m-2}|\) and border chain: \[L_m \xrightarrow{-|F_{m-2}|} L_{m-1} \xrightarrow{-|F_{m-3}|} L_{m-2} \xrightarrow{-|F_{m-4}|} \dots \xrightarrow{-2} 3 \xrightarrow{-2} 1 \xrightarrow{-1} 0.\]

    • Because the Fibonacci differences \((|F_{m-2}|, |F_{m-3}|, \dots, 3, 2, 1)\) are strictly decreasing at every step, every border is its own slink jump, achieving slink depth \(k\) at the minimal binary length \(L_{\min}(k) = F_{k+4} - 2\) (e.g., \(u_1 = 1 (\texttt{a}), u_2 = 3 (\texttt{aba}), u_3 = 6 (\texttt{abaaba}), u_4 = 11 (\texttt{abaababaaba})\))!

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Generate the infinite Fibonacci word \(F_\infty = \texttt{abaababaabaababaababa}\dots\) in \(O(N)\) time via \(F_m = F_{m-1} F_{m-2}\) until \(|F_m| \ge N\).
    • To achieve the maximum slink depth from \(\pi[N]\) at length \(N\): let \(L_m = |F_m| - 2\) be the largest central Sturmian length \(< N\). Place the central Sturmian word of length \(L_m\) as a border of \(S[1..N]\) (setting \(S[1..L_m]\) and \(S[N - L_m + 1 .. N]\) to that Sturmian word) in \(O(N)\) time.

Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Using the Zimin word \(Z_k\) (a, aba, abacaba, length \(2^k - 1\)) requires \(N = 2^{k+1} - 1\) for depth \(k\) and uses \(k + 1\) distinct letters, whereas the binary Central Sturmian word achieves depth \(k\) at length \(F_{k+4} - 2 \approx \phi^{k+4}/\sqrt{5} \ll 2^{k+1} - 1\) using only 2 letters!
