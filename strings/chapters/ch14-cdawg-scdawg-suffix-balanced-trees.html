1. Compact DAWG (CDAWG) & Maximal-Repeat Symmetry (T-2A-02)

1.1 The Diagnostic Signal: Why \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) Still Has Redundant Unary Chains

In Chapter 09, we proved that the suffix-link tree \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{SAM}(S))\) is the compacted Suffix Tree \(\operatorname{ST}(S^R)\): it merges substrings \(w\) and \(cw\) whenever extending on the left preserves the right-endpoint occurrence set (\(\operatorname{endpos}_S(cw) = \operatorname{endpos}_S(w)\)).

However, look at the transition DAG of \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) (which extends strings on the right, \(w \to wd\)):

  • Suppose every occurrence of \(w\) in \(S\) is immediately followed by the exact same character \(d\) (and \(w\) does not touch the right boundary \(S[N]\)). Then \(\operatorname{startpos}_S(wd) = \operatorname{startpos}_S(w)\), so in \(\operatorname{ST}(S)\), \(w \to wd\) is an unbranched unary edge that gets compressed into a single multi-character edge!
  • Yet in \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\), because \(w\) and \(wd\) have ending positions shifted by \(+1\) (\(\operatorname{endpos}_S(wd) = \operatorname{endpos}_S(w) + 1 \ne \operatorname{endpos}_S(w)\)), \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) allocates separate states for \(w\) and \(wd\), connected by a unary DAG edge \(u \xrightarrow{d} v\) of out-degree \(1\)!
  • Consequently, on highly repetitive strings such as \(S = \texttt{a}^N\) or standard Fibonacci words \(F_k\), \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) still requires \(\Theta(N)\) states even though \(F_k\) has only \(O(\log N)\) branching repeats!

1.2 The Combinatorial Word Invariant: Left Closure, Right Closure, and the Maximal-Repeat Bijection

For any non-empty substring \(w\) of \(S[1..N]\), let us define its two canonical context closures:

  1. Left-Extension Closure \(\overleftarrow{w}\): The longest substring in \(w\)’s \(\operatorname{endpos}_S\) equivalence class (i.e., \(\operatorname{longest}(u)\) for the state \(u \in \operatorname{SAM}(S)\) containing \(w\)). Extending \(w\) to \(\overleftarrow{w}\) prepends all characters that precede every occurrence of \(w\) in \(S\).
  2. Right-Extension Closure \(\overrightarrow{w}\): The longest substring in \(w\)’s \(\operatorname{startpos}_S\) equivalence class (i.e., the path label to the nearest branching or leaf descendant of \(w\)’s locus in \(\operatorname{ST}(S)\), or \(\bigl(\overleftarrow{w^R}\bigr)^R\) in \(\operatorname{SAM}(S^R)\)). Extending \(w\) to \(\overrightarrow{w}\) appends all characters that follow every occurrence of \(w\) in \(S\).

A substring \(w\) occurring at least twice in \(S\) is called a maximal repeat (极长重复子串) if and only if it is simultaneously left-maximal (\(w = \overleftarrow{w}\)) and right-maximal (\(w = \overrightarrow{w}\))—meaning extending \(w\) by even a single character to the left (\(cw\)) or to the right (\(wd\)) strictly decreases its occurrence count in \(S\).

NoteTheorem 14.1 (Maximal-Repeat Vertex Bijection of the Compact DAWG)

Let \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S)\) be the directed acyclic multi-graph obtained from \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) by:

  1. Splitting off occurrences that touch the right boundary \(S[N]\) (or appending a unique terminal sentinel \(\$\) so the whole string \(S\$\) is the unique sink \(t_{\text{sink}}\)), and
  2. Compacting every non-sink state \(u \in \operatorname{SAM}(S)\) of DAG out-degree \(1\) into a single multi-character edge labeled by the substring interval \(S[l_e .. r_e]\) (merging \(u\) into its unique DAG successor \(v\)).

Then:

  1. Maximal-Repeat Bijection: The internal vertices (excluding source \(\varepsilon\) and sink \(S\$\)) of \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S)\) correspond bijectively to the maximal repeats of \(S\): \[\boxed{V_{\text{int}}\bigl(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S)\bigr) \;\cong\; \bigl\{\,w \in \operatorname{Sub}(S) \;\big|\; \operatorname{occ}_S(w) \ge 2 \;\land\; w = \overleftarrow{w} = \overrightarrow{w}\,\bigr\}.}\]

  2. Reversal Vertex Symmetry: Because \(w\) is a maximal repeat of \(S\) if and only if \(w^R\) is a maximal repeat of \(S^R\), \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S)\) and \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S^R)\) have the exact same number of vertices! On Sturmian / Fibonacci words of length \(N\), \(|V(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S))| = \Theta(\log N)\) while \(|E(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S))| = \Theta(\log N)\).

Fibonacci prefix S = "abaababa" (N = 8):
  SAM(S) has 11 states, but only 3 substrings are Maximal Repeats (w = <-w = w->):
    1. "a"     (occ = 5 at {1,3,4,6,8}: left chars {$,b,a}, right chars {b,a,$})
    2. "aba"   (occ = 3 at {1..3, 4..6, 6..8}: left chars {$,a,b}, right chars {a,b,$})
    3. "abaab" (occ = 2 at {1..5, 4..8}: left chars {$,a}, right chars {a,$})
    --- By contrast, "ab" (occ=3) is NOT right-maximal because every "ab" is followed by 'a' ("aba")!
    --- And "ba" (occ=2) is NOT left-maximal because every "ba" is preceded by 'a' ("aba")!

Proof of Theorem 14.1

Every state \(u \in \operatorname{SAM}(S)\) has \(\operatorname{longest}(u) = \overleftarrow{w}\) for all \(w \in E(u)\), so \(\operatorname{longest}(u)\) is automatically left-maximal. In \(\operatorname{SAM}(S\$\)), state \(u \notin \{\text{root}, \text{sink}\}\) has DAG out-degree \(\ge 2\) if and only if there exist at least two distinct characters \(d_1 \ne d_2\) in \(\Sigma \cup \{\$\}\) such that \(\operatorname{longest}(u) d_1\) and \(\operatorname{longest}(u) d_2\) both occur in \(S\$\), which is the exact definition of \(\operatorname{longest}(u)\) being right-maximal (\(\operatorname{longest}(u) = \overrightarrow{\operatorname{longest}(u)}\)) with \(\operatorname{occ}_S(\operatorname{longest}(u)) \ge 2\). Conversely, if \(u\) has out-degree \(1\) along edge \(u \xrightarrow{d} v\), then \(\operatorname{longest}(u)\) is always followed by \(d\), so \(\overrightarrow{\operatorname{longest}(u)} = \overrightarrow{\operatorname{longest}(v)}\); compacting \(u\) into \(v\) replaces the unary chain with its unique right-maximal endpoint. \(\blacksquare\)

2. Unamortized Deterministic SAM on Version Trees (T-2A-02)

2.1 The Diagnostic Signal: The Star-Branching \(\Theta(N^2)\) Trap

In Chapter 09, we saw how to build a Generalized SAM on a known Trie in \(O(V|\Sigma|)\) time via Offline Trie BFS. What if a problem builds a version tree of strings online (append(version_v, c) creates a new version \(v_{\text{new}}\) representing \(S_{v} + c\)) and immediately queries substring properties of the single root-to-\(v_{\text{new}}\) string \(S_{v_{\text{new}}}\) (independently of other branches)?

Standard single-string extend(c) fails catastrophically in two ways:

  1. Amortized Link-Walk Blowup (\(\Theta(N^2)\) TLE): The standard loop while (p && !ch[p][c]) p = link[p]; relies on the potential \(\Phi = \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(\text{last}))\) decreasing along a single path. If an adversary creates version \(v\) for \(\texttt{a}^m\) (where \(\operatorname{link}\) forms a chain of length \(m\)) and then issues \(m\) independent queries appending \(\texttt{b}\) to \(v\), each query walks all \(m\) suffix links from scratch, taking \(\Theta(m^2)\) time!
  2. Cross-Branch Transition Corruption: Mutating ch[p][c] = cur on ancestors \(p\) of \(u\) overwrites transitions belonging to sibling versions on the version tree.

2.2 Persistent Ancestor-Transition Tables (anc_trans[u][c])

To achieve unamortized worst-case \(O(\log |\Sigma|)\) time and space per version extension without mutating ancestor states, we replace the amortized link walk with a direct ancestor-transition table anc_trans[u][c]:

NoteTheorem 14.2 (Unamortized Persistent Tree SAM via anc_trans[u][c])

For each state \(u \in \operatorname{SAM}(S_{\text{root} \to v})\), define:

  1. \(\delta(u, c) = \operatorname{ch}[u][c]\): the outgoing DAG transition from \(u\) on character \(c\), stored as a persistent (functional) balanced bitwise trie of depth \(\lceil \log_2 |\Sigma| \rceil\).
  2. \(\operatorname{anc\_trans}[u][c]\): the deepest ancestor \(p\) of \(u\) on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\) (including \(u\) itself) such that \(\delta(p, c) \ne 0\) (or \(0\) if no ancestor of \(u\) has transition \(c\)), also stored as a persistent bitwise trie of depth \(\lceil \log_2 |\Sigma| \rceil\).

When extending version \(v\) (at state \(\text{last}\)) by character \(c\) to create state \(\text{cur}\) with \(\operatorname{len}(\text{cur}) = \operatorname{len}(\text{last}) + 1\):

  • Direct \(O(1)\) Jump: Instead of walking p = link[p], look up \(p = \operatorname{anc\_trans}[\text{last}][c]\) in a single query!
  • Deferred Lazy Transition Assignment: Instead of mutating \(\delta(p', c) \leftarrow \text{cur}\) on every state \(p'\) along the link path from \(\text{last}\) to \(p\), observe that in a single-string SAM extension, the only newly created states are \(\text{cur}\) and at most one split state \(\text{clone}\). How do future extensions down this branch resolve transitions without mutating ancestor states?
    • Whenever last is extended by \(c\) to cur, every ancestor of last that previously lacked transition \(c\) now transitions to cur on \(c\), so for cur (and its future link-descendants), the deepest ancestor with transition \(c\) is updated when cur’s own link is wired.
    • Furthermore (Lazy Edge-Target Resolution): every DAG transition edge \((x \xrightarrow{c} y)\) originally added when \(x\) was extended by \(c\) always points to the unique state covering length \(\operatorname{len}(x) + 1\) in \(y\)’s suffix-link subtree. Specifically, if \(p = \operatorname{anc\_trans}[\text{last}][c] \ne 0\), the true target \(q = \delta_{\text{true}}(p, c)\) at step \(v\) is the unique ancestor of \(\delta_{\text{init}}(p, c)\) on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\) having \(\operatorname{len}(q) \ge \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) (found via binary lifting on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\), or maintained directly if quick_link on a shared Online Tree GSAM is used).

Let us state both regimes clearly so you can deploy the exact right weapon in contests:

Version-Tree Regime State / Transition Mechanism Worst-Case Step Cost
Regime 1: Online Tree GSAM (all branches coexist in one shared automaton) Maintain quick_link[u][c] = deepest proper ancestor \(p\) of \(u\) with \(\delta(p, c) = \delta(\operatorname{link}(u), c)\) (skipping unary redirection chains) \(O(1)\) amortized / \(O(\log N)\) worst-case
Regime 2: Fully Persistent Single-Path SAM (independent root-to-\(v\) strings) Store link_up[k][u] (binary lifting table on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\)) and persistent first_cur[u][c] (the state cur created when an ancestor \(p\) first saw \(c\)); resolve \(q\) by lifting first_cur[p][c] to \(\operatorname{len}(q) \ge \operatorname{len}(p)+1\) \(O(\log N + \log |\Sigma|)\) strict worst-case

3. Bi-Directional SCDAWG & Ukkonen–Weiner Frontier Duality (T-2B-05)

3.1 The Diagnostic Signal: Online Two-Ended push_front and push_back

Suppose a string \(S\) starts empty and grows online at both ends via push_front(c) (\(S \leftarrow cS\)) and push_back(c) (\(S \leftarrow Sc\)), and operations are encrypted so we cannot pre-simulate the deque offline. How do we maintain the exact count of distinct substrings and maximal repeats in real time?

3.2 Two-Sided Closure Commutativity & Active-Frontier Duality

First, why do \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S)\) and \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S^R)\) glue together into a single symmetric structure?

NoteTheorem 14.3 (Two-Sided Closure Commutativity & Ukkonen–Weiner Frontier Duality)

  1. Closure Commutativity (Blumer et al. 1987 / Inenaga et al. 2005): For every substring \(w\) of \(S\), taking its left-extension closure followed by its right-extension closure equals taking its right-extension closure followed by its left-extension closure: \[\boxed{\overrightarrow{\bigl(\overleftarrow{w}\bigr)} \;=\; \overleftarrow{\bigl(\overrightarrow{w}\bigr)} \;=\; \overleftrightarrow{w}.}\] Thus every substring \(w\) maps to a unique minimal enclosing maximal repeat (or sink) \(\overleftrightarrow{w}\) in the Symmetric Compact DAWG (SCDAWG), which simultaneously embeds \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S)\) (via right-extension edges) and \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S^R)\) (via left-extension edges) on the exact same vertex set of size \(\le N + 1\)!

  2. Ukkonen–Weiner Active-Frontier Duality: Let \(L_{\max}(S)\) be the longest prefix of \(S\) occurring at least twice in \(S\), and \(R_{\max}(S)\) be the longest suffix of \(S\) occurring at least twice in \(S\). Then:

    • push_back(c) (\(S \leftarrow Sc\)): Increases distinct substrings by \(\Delta_{\text{distinct}} = |Sc| - |R_{\max}(Sc)|\). On \(\operatorname{ST}(S)\), Ukkonen’s active suffix point \((u_{\text{suf}}, d_{\text{suf}})\) steps along suffix links until transition \(c\) exists; on \(\operatorname{ST}(S^R)\), Weiner’s algorithm inserts a single new leaf \(c S^R\) at the locus of \(R_{\max}(Sc)^R\).
    • push_front(c) (\(S \leftarrow cS\)): Increases distinct substrings by \(\Delta_{\text{distinct}} = |cS| - |L_{\max}(cS)|\). On \(\operatorname{ST}(S^R)\), Ukkonen’s active prefix point \((u_{\text{pref}}, d_{\text{pref}})\) steps along suffix links; on \(\operatorname{ST}(S)\), Weiner’s algorithm inserts a single new leaf \(c S\) at the locus of \(L_{\max}(cS)\).

Proof of Theorem 14.3

  1. Commutativity: Write \(\overleftarrow{w} = \alpha w\) where \(\alpha \in \Sigma^*\) is the longest string such that every occurrence of \(w\) in \(S\) is preceded by \(\alpha\). Write \(\overrightarrow{(\alpha w)} = \alpha w \beta\) where \(\beta \in \Sigma^*\) is the longest string such that every occurrence of \(\alpha w\) is followed by \(\beta\). Since \(\operatorname{occ}_S(\alpha w) = \operatorname{occ}_S(w)\), every occurrence of \(w\) is also followed by \(\beta\), so \(\overrightarrow{w} = w \beta\) for the exact same right extension \(\beta\)! Symmetrically, every occurrence of \(w \beta\) is preceded by \(\alpha\), so \(\overleftarrow{(\overrightarrow{w})} = \overleftarrow{(w \beta)} = \alpha w \beta = \overrightarrow{(\overleftarrow{w})}\).
  2. Active-Frontier Coupling: The only potential coupling between the left active frontier \(L_{\max}(S)\) and the right active frontier \(R_{\max}(S)\) occurs when push_back(c) causes \(L_{\max}(S)\) to match the newly created suffix at the right end of \(Sc\) (i.e., \(L_{\max}(S)c\) is a suffix of \(Sc\), so \(L_{\max}(S)c\) is a border of \(Sc\)), in which case \(\operatorname{occ}_{Sc}(L_{\max}(S)c) = 2\) and the active prefix point advances by \(+1\) character (\(L_{\max}(Sc) = L_{\max}(S)c\)). Symmetrically, push_front(c) advances \(R_{\max}(cS) = c R_{\max}(S)\) iff \(c R_{\max}(S)\) is a prefix (border) of \(cS\). \(\blacksquare\)

4. Hash-Free Suffix Balanced Trees (后缀平衡树, T-2B-06)

4.1 The Diagnostic Signal: Online Encrypted push_front / pop_front

Suppose a problem maintains a string \(S\) online under encrypted front-prepends push_front(c) (and optionally pop_front()) and requires:

  • \(O(1)\) lexicographical comparison of any two existing suffixes ([BZOJ 3682] Phorni),
  • Pattern occurrence counting \(\operatorname{occ}_S(P)\) for query strings \(P\) ([BZOJ 4768] wxh loves substring), or
  • Real-time maintenance of \(\operatorname{SA}\) rank, \(K\)-th suffix, and adjacent height (LCP) values.

Why is push_front(c) fundamentally friendly to a Balanced Binary Search Tree of suffixes, whereas push_back(c) is not?

  • Index the suffixes from right to left as \(S\) grows at the front: let \(\operatorname{suf}(1) = S[1..1]\), \(\operatorname{suf}(2) = c_2 \cdot \operatorname{suf}(1)\), \(\dots\), \(\operatorname{suf}(n) = c_n \cdot \operatorname{suf}(n-1)\) be the suffix of length \(n\) created at step \(n\).
  • When push_front(c) prepends \(c\) to form \(\operatorname{suf}(n) = c \cdot \operatorname{suf}(n-1)\), the existing \(n - 1\) suffixes \(\operatorname{suf}(1), \dots, \operatorname{suf}(n-1)\) do not change at all! Only one new suffix \(\operatorname{suf}(n)\) is inserted into the sorted suffix order!

4.2 Dietz–Sleator 64-Bit Scapegoat Tags & Hash-Free BST RMQ LCP

If we store \(\operatorname{suf}(1), \dots, \operatorname{suf}(n-1)\) in a Balanced BST, comparing a newly inserted suffix \(\operatorname{suf}(n) = c \cdot \operatorname{suf}(n-1)\) against a BST node \(u\) (\(\operatorname{suf}(u) = S[u] \cdot \operatorname{suf}(u-1)\)) takes \(O(\log n)\) time per node if we use binary search + hashing, resulting in \(O(\log^2 n)\) per insertion. Can we reduce node comparison to \(O(1)\) deterministic time and LCP maintenance to \(O(\log n)\) hash-free time?

NoteTheorem 14.4 (Dietz–Sleator 64-Bit Scapegoat Tags & Hash-Free BST LCP Invariant)

Maintain the sorted suffixes \(\operatorname{suf}(1), \dots, \operatorname{suf}(n-1)\) in an \(\alpha\)-Weight-Balanced BST (Scapegoat Tree with \(\alpha = 0.75\)). Assign the root the integer tag interval \([0, 2^{62}]\), and recursively assign each node \(x\) with interval \([L_x, R_x]\) the midpoint tag: \[\boxed{\operatorname{tag}[x] \;=\; \frac{L_x + R_x}{2} \;\in\; \bigl[0,\, 2^{62}\bigr],}\] passing \([L_x, \operatorname{tag}[x]]\) to its left child and \([\operatorname{tag}[x], R_x]\) to its right child (with \(\operatorname{tag}[0] = -1\) for the empty suffix \(\varepsilon\)).

  1. \(O(1)\) Existing-Suffix Comparison: For any \(i, j \in \{1, \dots, n-1\}\), \(\operatorname{suf}(i) < \operatorname{suf}(j) \iff \operatorname{tag}[i] < \operatorname{tag}[j]\) in \(O(1)\) time.

  2. \(O(1)\) New-Suffix Comparison During Insertion: When inserting \(\operatorname{suf}(n) = c \cdot \operatorname{suf}(n-1)\) and comparing against BST node \(u\): \[\boxed{\operatorname{suf}(n) \lessgtr_{\text{lex}} \operatorname{suf}(u) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \bigl(c,\; \operatorname{tag}[n-1]\bigr) \;\lessgtr\; \bigl(S[u],\; \operatorname{tag}[u-1]\bigr) \quad \text{in } O(1) \text{ time!}}\]

  3. Hash-Free \(O(\log n)\) Combinatorial LCP Bootstrapping: Once \(\operatorname{suf}(n)\) is inserted between inorder predecessor \(u^-\) and successor \(u^+\), its adjacent LCP with \(u^-\) (and symmetrically \(u^+\)) satisfies the exact recurrence: \[\boxed{\operatorname{LCP}\bigl(\operatorname{suf}(n),\, \operatorname{suf}(u^-)\bigr) \;=\; \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } S[n] \ne S[u^-], \\ 1 + \operatorname{LCP}\bigl(\operatorname{suf}(n-1),\, \operatorname{suf}(u^- - 1)\bigr) & \text{if } S[n] = S[u^-], \end{cases}}\] where \(\operatorname{LCP}(\operatorname{suf}(n-1), \operatorname{suf}(u^- - 1))\) is answered in \(O(\log n)\) deterministic time as a standard BST range-minimum query over the adjacent height values stored inside the Suffix Balanced Tree itself—requiring zero rolling hashes!

Inserting suf(n) = c + suf(n-1) into the Scapegoat Suffix Balanced Tree:
  At each BST node u with interval [L_u, R_u] and tag[u] = (L_u + R_u) / 2:
    Compare (S[n], tag[n-1]) vs. (S[u], tag[u-1]) in O(1) integer operations!
        /                                      \
  Go Left: [L_u, tag[u]]                 Go Right: [tag[u], R_u]

Proof of Theorem 14.4

  1. Depth & Precision Bound: In a Scapegoat Tree with balance factor \(\alpha = 0.75\), every child \(y\) of \(x\) satisfies \(\operatorname{sz}[y] \le \frac{3}{4} \operatorname{sz}[x]\). Thus for \(n \le 10^6\), the maximum tree depth is \(\le \lfloor \log_{4/3}(10^6) \rfloor + 1 = 49 < 62\), so integer halving of \([0, 2^{62}]\) never collapses \(L_x = R_x\). When the highest unbalanced ancestor of size \(s\) is flattened and rebuilt into a perfectly balanced binary tree in \(O(s)\) time (\(O(\log n)\) amortized per insertion), all \(\operatorname{tag}[\cdot]\) values in that subtree are reassigned within \([L_{\text{sub}}, R_{\text{sub}}]\), preserving global tag order.
  2. Hash-Free LCP: Peeling off the first character \(S[n] = S[u^-] = c\) reduces \(\operatorname{LCP}(\operatorname{suf}(n), \operatorname{suf}(u^-))\) to \(1 + \operatorname{LCP}(\operatorname{suf}(n-1), \operatorname{suf}(u^- - 1))\). Because \(\operatorname{suf}(n-1)\) and \(\operatorname{suf}(u^- - 1)\) are already in the BST at known positions (identifiable via their tags \(\operatorname{tag}[n-1]\) and \(\operatorname{tag}[u^- - 1]\)), their LCP is the minimum of height[v] (where height[v] = LCP(v, pred(v))) over all nodes \(v\) strictly after the smaller tag and at or before the larger tag—a standard \(O(\log n)\) BST range-minimum query! \(\blacksquare\)

4.3 Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: Contract 14.1 — Scapegoat Suffix Balanced Tree (后缀平衡树)

struct SuffixBalancedTree {
    static constexpr double ALPHA = 0.75;
    static constexpr long long INF_TAG = 1ULL << 62;

    int rt = 0, tot = 0;
    std::vector<int> ls, rs, sz, s_char;
    std::vector<long long> tag;
    std::vector<int> flat;

    SuffixBalancedTree(int max_n)
        : ls(max_n + 2, 0), rs(max_n + 2, 0), sz(max_n + 2, 0),
          s_char(max_n + 2, 0), tag(max_n + 2, -1) {}

    bool cmp_suffix(int u, int v) const { // Returns suf(u) < suf(v) in O(1)
        if (s_char[u] != s_char[v]) return s_char[u] < s_char[v];
        return tag[u - 1] < tag[v - 1];
    }

    void pushup(int x) { sz[x] = sz[ls[x]] + sz[rs[x]] + 1; }

    bool bad(int x) const {
        return std::max(sz[ls[x]], sz[rs[x]]) > ALPHA * sz[x];
    }

    void flatten(int x) {
        if (!x) return;
        flatten(ls[x]);
        flat.push_back(x);
        flatten(rs[x]);
    }

    int build(int l, int r, long long L_tag, long long R_tag) {
        if (l > r) return 0;
        int mid = (l + r) >> 1;
        int x = flat[mid];
        long long mid_tag = (L_tag + R_tag) >> 1;
        tag[x] = mid_tag;
        ls[x] = build(l, mid - 1, L_tag, mid_tag);
        rs[x] = build(mid + 1, r, mid_tag, R_tag);
        pushup(x);
        return x;
    }

    int* insert(int& x, int id, long long L_tag, long long R_tag) {
        if (!x) {
            x = id;
            sz[x] = 1;
            tag[x] = (L_tag + R_tag) >> 1;
            return nullptr;
        }
        long long mid_tag = (L_tag + R_tag) >> 1;
        int* scapegoat = cmp_suffix(id, x)
            ? insert(ls[x], id, L_tag, mid_tag)
            : insert(rs[x], id, mid_tag, R_tag);
        pushup(x);
        if (bad(x)) scapegoat = &x; // Track highest unbalanced ancestor
        return scapegoat;
    }

    // Prepends character c to create suffix `id` (1-indexed: id = 1, 2, ..., N)
    void push_front(int id, int c) {
        s_char[id] = c;
        long long L_tag = 0, R_tag = INF_TAG;
        int* sg = insert(rt, id, L_tag, R_tag);
        if (sg) {
            // Find the exact [L_tag, R_tag] bounds of *sg by walking from rt
            int cur = rt;
            while (cur != *sg) {
                long long mid = (L_tag + R_tag) >> 1;
                if (tag[*sg] < tag[cur]) { R_tag = mid; cur = ls[cur]; }
                else                     { L_tag = mid; cur = rs[cur]; }
            }
            flat.clear();
            flatten(*sg);
            *sg = build(0, (int)flat.size() - 1, L_tag, R_tag);
        }
    }
};

4.4 Boundary Pitfalls & Broken-Invariant Counterexamples (BP-14 / BP-14-01–BP-14-03 / BP-2B-06–BP-2B-08)

  1. Counterexample 14.5 (BP-14-01 / BP-2B-06) — Why push_back(c) Flips Existing Suffix Order ("aa" -> "aab"): Suppose \(S = \texttt{"aa"}\). Its two suffixes are \(\operatorname{suf}(1) = \texttt{"aa"}\) and \(\operatorname{suf}(2) = \texttt{"a"}\), ordered as: \[\operatorname{suf}(2) = \texttt{"a"} \;<\; \operatorname{suf}(1) = \texttt{"aa"}.\] Now append 'b' to the right (push_back('b')), producing \(S' = \texttt{"aab"}\). The two existing suffixes become \(\operatorname{suf}(1) = \texttt{"aab"}\) and \(\operatorname{suf}(2) = \texttt{"ab"}\), whose order flips: \[\operatorname{suf}(1) = \texttt{"aab"} \;<\; \operatorname{suf}(2) = \texttt{"ab"}!\] In general, appending \(c\) to the right simultaneously modifies all existing suffixes and flips the relative order of any pair where one suffix was a proper prefix (border) of the other. Rule: A Suffix Balanced Tree can only insert at the front (push_front(c)). If a problem appends to the back and queries prefix/substring properties, reverse the string and patterns so push_back becomes push_front!

  2. Counterexample 14.6 (BP-14 / BP-14-02 / BP-2B-07) — 53-Bit IEEE-754 double Tag Underflow in Randomized Treaps: Many older tutorials implement Suffix Balanced Trees using a randomized Treap with double tags in \([0.0, 1.0]\). Because IEEE-754 double has only a 53-bit mantissa, any root-to-leaf path of length \(\ge 53\) collapses mid = (L + R) / 2 == L, causing distinct suffixes to receive identical tags and corrupting both the BST and external Segment Trees ([BZOJ 3682]). Always use an \(\alpha = 0.75\) Scapegoat Tree with uint64_t / long long tags in \([0, 2^{62}]\) (maximum depth \(\le 49\)).

  3. Counterexample 14.7 (BP-14-03 / BP-2B-08) — Rebuilding the Highest vs. Lowest Unbalanced Scapegoat Node: During insert, multiple ancestors on the insertion path may satisfy bad(x). Rebuilding the lowest unbalanced node can leave higher ancestors at depth \(> \log_{4/3} n\), causing tag interval exhaustion \([L_x, R_x]\). Always overwrite scapegoat = &x on the way up the recursion so only the highest (shallowest) unbalanced ancestor is rebuilt!

  1. Problem 14.5 (W2A-41) — Online Persistent Version-Tree SAM Substring Queries (Universal Cup / QOJ Paradigm)

  • Mathematical Statement: A version tree starts with version \(0\) representing the empty string \(S_0 = \varepsilon\). Process \(Q\) online encrypted queries of two types:
    1. append(v, c): Create a new version \(i\) by appending character \(c\) to version \(v\) (\(S_i = S_v + c\)), and output the number of distinct substrings in \(S_i\).
    2. max_suffix_match(v, P): Given a version \(v\) and a query string \(P[1..m]\), find the maximum length of a suffix of \(P\) that occurs as a substring of \(S_v\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le Q \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(\sum |P| \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Queries are online encrypted on a version tree, and max_suffix_match(v, P) must only match substrings of the single path string \(S_v\), NOT substrings from other branches of the version tree!

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Why does a shared Online Tree GSAM fail max_suffix_match(v, P)? Because in a shared GSAM, \(\delta(u, c)\) includes transitions created by other branches! However, combine a shared Online Tree GSAM (using quick_link[u][c] from Theorem 14.2) with a Persistent Segment Tree Merge over Euler-tour intervals of the version tree—or even simpler, use Theorem 14.2’s Fully Persistent Single-Path SAM (anc_trans[u][c])!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

With Theorem 14.2 (Persistent Single-Path SAM):

  • At each SAM state \(u\), maintain its binary-lifting link table up[k][u] and persistent array anc_trans[u][c] (recording the deepest ancestor \(p\) of \(u\) that has outgoing transition \(c\), and the initial target state init_to[p][c] created when \(p\) first saw \(c\)).
  • To evaluate \(\delta_v(u, c)\) on version \(v\): if anc_trans[u][c] has \(\operatorname{len}(p) < \operatorname{len}(u)\), then \(\delta_v(u, c) = 0\); otherwise lift init_to[u][c] on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\) to the highest ancestor \(q\) with \(\operatorname{len}(q) \ge \operatorname{len}(u) + 1\)!
TipFull Derivation & Solution (W2A-41)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Online version-tree branching with branch-isolated transition queries defeats both amortized single-string SAM (\(\Theta(Q^2)\) star-branch blowup) and offline Trie-BFS GSAM.
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (Theorem 14.2 — Unamortized Persistent Tree SAM):

    • Along any single root-to-\(v\) string \(S_v\), suppose state \(p\) first gained an outgoing transition on character \(c\) when prefix \(S_v[1..r_0]\) was extended to state \(y_0 = \operatorname{pos}[r_0]\). At all later versions \(v\) down that subtree, what is the current target state \(q = \delta_v(p, c)\)?

    • Whenever a later extension splits \(q\), the new clone has length \(\operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) and becomes the parent of \(q\) on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\)! Therefore, at any version \(v\), \(\delta_v(p, c)\) is always the unique ancestor \(q\) of \(y_0\) on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\) satisfying: \[\operatorname{len}\bigl(\operatorname{link}(q)\bigr) \;<\; \operatorname{len}(p) + 1 \;\le\; \operatorname{len}(q)!\]

    • Furthermore, maintaining anc_trans[u][c] = deepest ancestor \((p, y_0)\) of \(u\) on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\) that has transition \(c\) (copied from link[u] in \(O(|\Sigma|)\) when \(u\)’s link is wired) finds both \(p\) and \(y_0\) in \(O(1)\) time without walking a single suffix link!

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • For each state \(u\), store len[u], up[0..18][u] (binary lifting on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\)), and anc_p[u][26], anc_y0[u][26].
    • Helper get_trans(u, c): if anc_p[u][c] != u, return 0; else lift y = anc_y0[u][c] via up[k][y] while len[up[k][y]] >= len[u] + 1 and return y.
    • Helper wire_node(u, par, self_c, self_y0): set up[0][u] = par, fill up[1..18][u], copy anc_p[u] and anc_y0[u] from par, and if self_c != -1, set anc_p[u][self_c] = u and anc_y0[u][self_c] = self_y0.
    • On append(v, c) from last = ver_state[v]:
      • Allocate cur = ++tot, len[cur] = len[last] + 1.
      • Look up \(p = \text{anc\_p}[\text{last}][c]\) in \(O(1)\) time!
      • If \(p == 0\): wire_node(cur, 1, -1, 0).
      • Else find \(q = \text{get\_trans}(p, c)\). If \(\operatorname{len}(q) == \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\), wire_node(cur, q, -1, 0). Otherwise allocate clone = ++tot, len[clone] = len[p] + 1, wire_node(clone, up[0][q], -1, 0) (inheriting \(q\)’s own transitions at length \(\operatorname{len}(p)+1\)), and wire_node(cur, clone, -1, 0)! (Note that since cur’s link path only goes through states of length \(\le \operatorname{len}(p)+1\), states between last and \(p\) are strictly longer than clone and are never ancestors of cur!).

    • max_suffix_match(v, P) walks \(P^R\) or matches \(P\) along ver_state[v]. Total time: \(O((Q + \sum |P|) (\log Q + |\Sigma|))\).
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Never mutate up[0][q] = clone in-place on an existing state \(q\) shared with other version branches! Instead, because \(q\) is only reached via binary lifting from \(y_0\) down the current branch, either insert clone on the new path cur -> clone -> up[0][q] (so future lifts from cur hit clone!) or, if \(y_0\) is lifted, check whether \(\operatorname{len}(q) > \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) and return a virtual split state \((q, \operatorname{len}(p)+1)\) on the fly! Returning a virtual split pair (state_q, cur_len) eliminates clone allocation entirely!

Problem 14.6 (W2B-45) — Tree-Branched Suffix Balanced Tree with Ancestor LCP RMQ

  • Mathematical Statement: Given a rooted tree being built online via \(Q\) encrypted operations add_leaf(parent_u, c) (creating node \(v\) whose upward string to the root is \(\operatorname{str}(v) = c \cdot \operatorname{str}(\text{parent}_u)\)), support:
    1. cmp_nodes(u, v): Compare \(\operatorname{str}(u)\) and \(\operatorname{str}(v)\) lexicographically in \(O(1)\) time.
    2. lcp_nodes(u, v): Compute \(\operatorname{LCP}(\operatorname{str}(u), \operatorname{str}(v))\) in \(O(\log Q)\) deterministic time (without rolling hash).
    3. kth_tree_string(k): Return the node ID of the \(k\)-th lexicographically smallest upward tree string.
  • Constraints: \(1 \le Q \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| \le 10^9\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Each tree node \(v\) satisfies \(\operatorname{str}(v) = c \cdot \operatorname{str}(\operatorname{par}(v))\)—the exact recurrence of a Suffix Balanced Tree where \(n - 1\) is replaced by \(\operatorname{par}(v)\)!

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

By Theorem 14.4, replacing tag[u - 1] with tag[par[u]] in Contract 14.1 compares any two upward tree paths in \(O(1)\) time: \[\operatorname{str}(u) \lessgtr \operatorname{str}(v) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \bigl(S[u],\; \operatorname{tag}[\operatorname{par}(u)]\bigr) \;\lessgtr\; \bigl(S[v],\; \operatorname{tag}[\operatorname{par}(v)]\bigr),\] breaking exact string ties (\(\operatorname{str}(u) = \operatorname{str}(v)\)) by node ID \(u < v\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

What if \(\operatorname{str}(u) = \operatorname{str}(v)\) (two distinct tree nodes spell the exact same string to the root)? Assign a secondary representative rep[u] (the first node inserted with that exact string) so equal strings share tag[rep[u]] for comparisons, while the Hash-Free LCP recurrence becomes: \[\operatorname{LCP}(u, u^-) = \bigl[S[u] == S[u^-]\bigr] \cdot \Bigl(1 + \operatorname{LCP}\bigl(\operatorname{par}(u),\, \operatorname{par}(u^-)\bigr)\Bigr)!\]

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W2B-45)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: On a tree, multiple nodes can have identical upward strings (\(\operatorname{str}(u) = \operatorname{str}(v)\)), and \(|\Sigma| \le 10^9\) forbids Trie child arrays.
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • Maintain a representative rep[u] for each tree node \(u\). Two nodes \(u, v\) have \(\operatorname{str}(u) = \operatorname{str}(v)\) iff \(S[u] == S[v]\) and \(\operatorname{rep}[\operatorname{par}(u)] == \operatorname{rep}[\operatorname{par}(v)]\).
    • When inserting \(v\) into the Scapegoat Suffix Balanced Tree: if we encounter an existing BST node \(w\) with \(S[v] == S[w]\) and \(\operatorname{rep}[\operatorname{par}(v)] == \operatorname{rep}[\operatorname{par}(w)]\), we set \(\operatorname{rep}[v] = \operatorname{rep}[w]\) (so \(\operatorname{tag}[\operatorname{rep}[v]] = \operatorname{tag}[\operatorname{rep}[w]]\)), and break the BST insertion tie by \(v > w\), setting h_pred[v] = depth[v].
    • Otherwise, \(\operatorname{str}(v) \ne \operatorname{str}(w)\), and \(\operatorname{str}(v) < \operatorname{str}(w) \iff (S[v], \operatorname{tag}[\operatorname{rep}[\operatorname{par}(v)]]) < (S[w], \operatorname{tag}[\operatorname{rep}[\operatorname{par}(w)]])\) in \(O(1)\) time!
    • By Theorem 14.4, \(\operatorname{LCP}(v, u^-) = [S[v] == S[u^-]] \cdot \bigl(1 + \operatorname{LCP}(\operatorname{par}(v), \operatorname{par}(u^-))\bigr)\), queried via BST range-minimum over h_pred in \(O(\log Q)\) time.
  3. Algorithmic Compilation: Implement Contract 14.1 with par[u], rep[u], h_pred[u], and min_h[u]. Each add_leaf, lcp_nodes, and kth_tree_string runs in \(O(\log Q)\) time, and cmp_nodes runs in \(O(1)\) time.
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Failing to unify rep[u] = rep[w] when \(\operatorname{str}(u) = \operatorname{str}(w)\) assigns slightly different tags \(\operatorname{tag}[u] \ne \operatorname{tag}[w]\) to identical strings, which then causes children \((c, u)\) and \((c, w)\) with the same character \(c\) to misorder against third nodes!

Problem 14.7 (W2B-40) — Bi-Directional SCDAWG Online push_front / push_back Distinct Substrings ([THUPC 2019 / QOJ] Paradigm)

  • Mathematical Statement: Starting with an empty string \(S = \varepsilon\), process \(Q\) online encrypted deque character additions:
    1. push_front(c): Prepend character \(c\) to the left of \(S\) (\(S \leftarrow cS\)).
    2. push_back(c): Append character \(c\) to the right of \(S\) (\(S \leftarrow Sc\)). After each operation, output the current number of distinct non-empty substrings of \(S\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le Q \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Because operations are encrypted, we cannot pre-simulate the deque offline and run static SAM + LCT (Filter 1 vs. Filter 3 in Section 4). We must maintain distinct substrings online at both ends!

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

By Theorem 14.3 (Ukkonen–Weiner Frontier Duality):

  • push_front(c) adds \(|cS| - |L_{\max}(cS)|\) new distinct substrings, where \(L_{\max}(cS)\) is the longest prefix of \(cS\) occurring at least twice in \(cS\).
  • push_back(c) adds \(|Sc| - |R_{\max}(Sc)|\) new distinct substrings, where \(R_{\max}(Sc)\) is the longest suffix of \(Sc\) occurring at least twice in \(Sc\). How can we find \(|L_{\max}(cS)|\) and \(|R_{\max}(Sc)|\) online?
NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

We have two clean online architectures:

  • Architecture A (Coupled Forward & Backward Frontiers via Online SCDAWG): Maintain the active frontier \((L_{\max}, R_{\max})\) on the Online SCDAWG (or maintain the Suffix Balanced Tree of \(S\) where push_back only affects suffixes shorter than the active frontier \(R_{\max}\)); SCDAWG updates both frontiers in \(O(\log |\Sigma|)\) amortized time!
TipFull Derivation & Solution (W2B-40)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: True online encrypted bi-directional distinct substring maintenance.
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (Theorem 14.3 — Online SCDAWG & Frontier Duality):
    • Maintain the Symmetric Compact DAWG (SCDAWG) of \(S\) with two sets of directed edges on the same vertex set \(V\): right-extension edges \(u \xrightarrow{\text{right}: S[l..r]} v\) (forming \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S)\)) and left-extension edges \(u \xrightarrow{\text{left}: S[l..r]} v\) (forming \(\operatorname{CDAWG}(S^R)\)), together with the active suffix locus \(R_{\max} = (u_R, d_R)\) and active prefix locus \(L_{\max} = (u_L, d_L)\).
    • When push_back(c) is called (\(S \leftarrow Sc\)):
      1. Walk the active suffix locus \((u_R, d_R)\) up the left-extension parent links (slink_R, which are the explicit tree parents in \(\operatorname{ST}(S)\)!) while \((u_R, d_R)\) cannot extend right by \(c\), adding a right-extension leaf edge to sink at each visited state.
      2. If \((u_R, d_R)\) stops in the middle of a right-extension edge (or at a non-left-maximal locus), split the edge/vertex to preserve \(w = \overleftarrow{w} = \overrightarrow{w}\).
      3. Advance \((u_R, d_R)\) by \(c\); its new string depth is \(|R_{\max}(Sc)|\), so ans += |Sc| - |R_{\max}(Sc)|.
      4. Frontier Coupling Check: If the active prefix locus \((u_L, d_L)\) had length \(|L_{\max}(S)| = |S| - \text{offset}\) and the newly created suffix of length \(|L_{\max}(S)| + 1\) matches \(L_{\max}(S)c\), advance \((u_L, d_L)\) by \(+1\) along its right-extension edge!
    • By exact symmetry, push_front(c) executes the same loop swapping left and right extensions!
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Each vertex \(u \in V(\operatorname{SCDAWG})\) stores its length \(\operatorname{len}(u)\), right-transitions \(\delta_R(u, c) = (v, l, r)\), and left-transitions \(\delta_L(u, c) = (v, l, r)\).
    • By Inenaga et al.’s amortization theorem, the potentials \(\Phi_R = |R_{\max}(S)|\) and \(\Phi_L = |L_{\max}(S)|\) increase by at most \(+1\) per push_front/push_back (even across the frontier coupling check!) and decrease by \(\ge 1\) on every link step, bounding total time to \(O(Q \log |\Sigma|)\)!
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Forgetting Step 4 (the Frontier Coupling Check when \(L_{\max}(S)c\) is a suffix of \(Sc\)) leaves \(|L_{\max}|\) stale by \(1\) whenever the entire active prefix matches the newly completed border at the opposite end of \(S\)!

Problem 14.8 (W2B-46) — Two-Ended Affix Automaton / Deque Substring Occurrence Queries

  • Mathematical Statement: A string \(S\) is modified over \(Q\) steps by push_front(c) and push_back(c). In Subtask A (Unencrypted / Offline Deque), you are also given \(M\) queries asking for \(\operatorname{occ}_S(P_q)\) inside a sub-window \(S[l_q .. r_q]\) of the current deque. In Subtask B (Online Encrypted Deque), you are given query patterns \(P_q\) (\(\sum |P_q| \le 5 \times 10^5\)) asking whether \(P_q\) occurs in the current string \(S\) and how many times \(P_q\) occurs.
  • Constraints: \(1 \le Q, M \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(\sum |P_q| \le 5 \times 10^5\), time limit \(3.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Apply the Contest Decision Filter:

  • In Subtask A (Unencrypted): pre-simulate the deque to build the final master string \(M[1..N]\) so that at any time \(t\), the current deque is a contiguous substring \(M[L_t .. R_t]\)!
  • In Subtask B (Encrypted): use the Online SCDAWG or coupled Heavy/Periodic Split + Suffix Balanced Tree!
NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

For Subtask B, observe how push_front(c) and push_back(c) can be unified using Two Coupled Suffix Balanced Trees Split at the Origin: If we split the deque at its initial origin \(0\) into a left-growing string \(S_L\) (push_front) and a right-growing string \(S_R\) (push_back), then any pattern \(P_q\) either lies entirely in \(S_L\), entirely in \(S_R\), or straddles the seam (\(|P_q|\) characters around the seam, checked by KMP in \(O(|P_q|)\) time)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Why does splitting at the origin \(0\) into \(S_L\) (maintained by a Suffix Balanced Tree \(\mathcal{T}_L\) under push_front) and \(S_R\) (maintained by a Suffix Balanced Tree \(\mathcal{T}_R\) on \(S_R^R\) under push_back, since push_back on \(S_R\) is push_front on \(S_R^R\)!) work immediately for arbitrary pattern occurrence queries \(\operatorname{occ}_S(P_q)\)? Because any occurrence of \(P_q\) in \(S = S_L \cdot S_R\) is:

  1. Entirely inside \(S_L\): counted in \(O(|P_q| + \log N)\) on \(\mathcal{T}_L\) (Problem 14.3)!
  2. Entirely inside \(S_R\) (i.e., \(P_q^R\) is entirely inside \(S_R^R\)): counted in \(O(|P_q| + \log N)\) on \(\mathcal{T}_R\)!
  3. Straddling the seam between \(S_L\) and \(S_R\): \(P_q\) can only straddle the seam inside the window of length \(\le 2|P_q| - 2\) taking the last \(|P_q| - 1\) characters of \(S_L\) and the first \(|P_q| - 1\) characters of \(S_R\), which is counted by running KMP on a string of length \(< 2|P_q|\) in \(O(|P_q|)\) time!
TipFull Derivation & Solution (W2B-46)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Recognising the Origin-Seam Decomposition turns an intimidating two-ended encrypted pattern-matching problem into two independent one-ended Suffix Balanced Trees plus a linear KMP seam check!
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (Origin-Seam Decomposition for Deque Pattern Matching):
    • Partition the deque string at its starting origin into \(S = S_L \cdot S_R\), where push_front(c) prepends \(c\) to \(S_L\) (\(S_L \leftarrow c S_L\)) and push_back(c) appends \(c\) to \(S_R\) (\(S_R \leftarrow S_R c \iff S_R^R \leftarrow c S_R^R\)).
    • Every occurrence of a pattern \(P\) of length \(m = |P|\) in \(S = S_L \cdot S_R\) falls into exactly one of three disjoint classes:
      1. Left-Only: Contained entirely in \(S_L\) (\(\operatorname{occ}_{S_L}(P)\)).
      2. Right-Only: Contained entirely in \(S_R\), which by reversal equals \(\operatorname{occ}_{S_R^R}(P^R)\).
      3. Seam-Straddling: Starts in the last \(m - 1\) characters of \(S_L\) and ends in the first \(m - 1\) characters of \(S_R\).
    • Crucially, push_front(c) on \(S_L\) and push_front(c) on \(S_R^R\) never modify the characters near the origin seam (once \(|S_L| \ge m-1\) and \(|S_R| \ge m-1\))!
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Subtask A (Unencrypted): Pre-simulate the deque into \(M[1..N]\) and answer substring queries \(M[l_q .. r_q]\) using Ch 10 Persistent Segment Tree Merge on \(\operatorname{SAM}(M)\) (query(rt[u], l_q + |P_q| - 1, r_q)) in \(O((N + \sum |P_q|) \log N)\) time.
    • Subtask B (Encrypted Online):
      • Maintain Suffix Balanced Tree \(\mathcal{T}_L\) on \(S_L\) (push_front(c) inserts into \(\mathcal{T}_L\)) and Suffix Balanced Tree \(\mathcal{T}_R\) on \(S_R^R\) (push_back(c) inserts into \(\mathcal{T}_R\)).
      • For query(P) of length \(m = |P|\):
        • If \(m == 1\): count occurrences of \(P[1]\) directly in \(O(1)\).
        • Otherwise: query \(\mathcal{T}_L\) for \(\operatorname{occ}_{S_L}(P)\), query \(\mathcal{T}_R\) for \(\operatorname{occ}_{S_R^R}(P^R)\), extract the seam string \(W = \operatorname{suffix}_{\min(|S_L|, m-1)}(S_L) \cdot \operatorname{prefix}_{\min(|S_R|, m-1)}(S_R)\) of length \(\le 2m - 2\), and add \(\operatorname{occ}_W(P)\) via KMP in \(O(m)\) time!
      • Total time: \(O(Q \log Q + \sum |P_q| \log Q)\), completely bypassing two-ended automaton splits!
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Taking \(\operatorname{suffix}_{m}(S_L) \cdot \operatorname{prefix}_{m}(S_R)\) (length \(m\) on each side instead of \(m - 1\)) includes occurrences of \(P\) that touch the seam without crossing it, double-counting them with \(\operatorname{occ}_{S_L}(P)\) and \(\operatorname{occ}_{S_R^R}(P^R)\)! Always take \(\min(|S_L|, m - 1)\) and \(\min(|S_R|, m - 1)\).
