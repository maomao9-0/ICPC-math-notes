1. Diagnostic Signal: The Quadratic Partition Bottleneck & Palindromic Border Equivalence

1.1 The 1D Palindrome / Border Partition Archetype

Let \(S = S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 10^6\)) be a string. A large family of string partitioning, cutting, and covering problems asks us to compute a 1D dynamic programming array \(dp[0..N]\) of the form: \[dp[r] \;=\; \bigoplus_{L \in \mathcal{A}(r)} w(L) \otimes dp[r - L] \qquad (1 \le r \le N),\] where \((\oplus, \otimes)\) is a commutative semiring or associative aggregation (such as \((+, \times)\) for counting partitions modulo \(10^9+7\), or \((\min, +)\) for minimizing cut costs), and the active transition lengths \(\mathcal{A}(r) \subseteq \{1, 2, \dots, r\}\) at right endpoint \(r\) are either:

  1. All Palindromic Suffixes of \(S[1..r]\): \(\mathcal{A}(r) = \operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r]) = \{L \in [1, r] : S[r-L+1..r] \text{ is a palindrome}\}\), or
  2. All Positive Borders of \(S[1..r]\): \(\mathcal{A}(r) = \operatorname{Border}(S[1..r]) \setminus \{0\}\).

Why Naive Automaton Traversal Fails: In a Palindromic Automaton (PAM), after feeding \(S[r]\) to reach state last, the set \(\operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r])\) is visited by walking the fail chain \(u = \text{last}, \operatorname{fail}[\text{last}], \operatorname{fail}[\operatorname{fail}[\text{last}]], \dots\) down to the even root \(0\). However, on \(S = \texttt{a}^N\), step \(r\) has \(r\) palindromic suffixes, so walking u = fail[u] takes \(\sum_{r=1}^N r = \Theta(N^2)\) time—an immediate Time Limit Exceeded at \(N = 10^5\) or \(10^6\).

1.2 Palindromic Suffixes Are Borders of the Longest Palindromic Suffix

Why do the border arithmetic progressions of Chapter 02 apply directly to the fail tree of a Palindromic Automaton?

ImportantTheorem 4.1 (Palindromic Suffix \(\leftrightarrow\) Palindromic Border Equivalence)

Let \(Q = Q[1..m]\) be a palindrome (\(Q = Q^R\)). A proper substring \(B = Q[m-k+1..m]\) of length \(k \in [1, m-1]\) is a palindromic suffix of \(Q\) if and only if \(B\) is a proper border of \(Q\) (\(k \in \operatorname{Border}(Q)\)).

Consequently, if \(u = \text{last}\) is the longest palindromic suffix of \(S[1..r]\), then the proper ancestors of \(u\) in the PAM fail tree are precisely the proper borders of \(u\), and augmenting PAM construction with: \[\operatorname{diff}[u] \;=\; \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[u]], \qquad \operatorname{slink}[u] \;=\; \begin{cases} \operatorname{slink}[\operatorname{fail}[u]] & \text{if } \operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{fail}[u]], \\ \operatorname{fail}[u] & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\] partitions \(\operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r])\) into \(\le \lceil \log_2 r \rceil\) disjoint half-open arithmetic progressions \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\).

Proof: Because \(Q = Q^R\), reversing the suffix \(B = Q[m-k+1..m]\) maps it to the prefix of \(Q\) of length \(k\): \[B^R \;=\; \bigl(Q[m-k+1..m]\bigr)^R \;=\; Q^R[1..k] \;=\; Q[1..k].\] Therefore:

  • If \(B\) is a palindrome (\(B = B^R\)), then \(Q[m-k+1..m] = B = B^R = Q[1..k]\), so \(B\) is a proper border of \(Q\).
  • Conversely, if \(B\) is a proper border of \(Q\) (\(Q[m-k+1..m] = Q[1..k]\)), then \(B = Q[1..k] = B^R\), so \(B\) is a palindrome.

Thus the PAM fail chain starting at \(u\) is isomorphic to the KMP border chain of the string \(u\), and Theorem 2.3 (Ch 02) bounds the number of slink jumps from last to \(0\) by \(\lceil \log_2 r \rceil\). \(\blacksquare\)

2. Core Combinatorial Word Invariant: The Complete Proof of the Shift-Overlap Theorem

Grouping \(\operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r])\) into \(O(\log r)\) half-open progressions \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) is only half the battle: each progression can still contain \(\Theta(r)\) palindromic lengths! How do we aggregate \(dp[r - L]\) over all \(L \in (\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) in \(O(1)\) time per progression without any segment tree?

Let \(u\) be a state visited during the slink loop at right endpoint \(r\), with local period step \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]\). The half-open progression \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) consists of \(k = \frac{\operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]]}{d} \ge 1\) lengths: \[\mathcal{L}(u) \;=\; \bigl\{\,\ell_1 > \ell_2 > \dots > \ell_k\,\bigr\}, \qquad \ell_j = \operatorname{len}[u] - (j - 1)d, \qquad \ell_k = \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + d.\] At right endpoint \(r\), this progression queries the following set of left-endpoint DP indices \(i = r - L\): \[\mathcal{I}(u, r) \;=\; \bigl\{\,r - \ell_1,\; r - \ell_2,\; \dots,\; r - \ell_{k-1},\; r - \ell_k\,\bigr\}.\]

Suppose we maintain at each automaton state \(u\) a rolling accumulator \(g[u]\) intended to store \(\bigoplus_{i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)} dp[i]\) at the most recent position \(r\) where \(u\) was visited.

ImportantTheorem 4.2 (The Shift-Overlap Invariant for PAM & KMP slink DP)

Let \(u\) be a state visited in the slink chain at right endpoint \(r\), with \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\) and \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\). Whenever \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[v] = d\) (so the progression \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) has \(k \ge 2\) terms and \(\operatorname{slink}[v] = \operatorname{slink}[u]\)):

  1. Exact Left-Endpoint Set Alignment: The set of left endpoints queried by \(u\) at position \(r\) decomposes into the disjoint union of the left endpoints queried by \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) at position \(r - d\) and the single newly entering rightmost index \(r - \ell_k = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\): \[\boxed{\mathcal{I}(u, r) \;=\; \mathcal{I}\bigl(\operatorname{fail}[u],\, r - \operatorname{diff}[u]\bigr) \;\sqcup\; \bigl\{\,r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u]\,\bigr\}!}\]

  2. Guaranteed Chain-Head Visit at \(r - d\): At step \(r' = r - d\), node \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) was a suffix of \(S[1..r-d]\) and was visited as a slink chain head during step \(r - d\)’s slink loop (it was not skipped inside the interior of a longer period-\(d\) chain at \(r - d\)).

  3. Guaranteed Non-Overwrite in \((r - d, r)\): Node \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) never occurred as a suffix of \(S[1..r'']\) for any intermediate index \(r'' \in (r - d, r)\). Therefore, at step \(r\), the stored value g[fail[u]] still holds the exact, unmodified aggregate over \(\mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d)\) computed at step \(r - d\)!

2.1 Zero-Magic Proof of All Three Claims of Theorem 4.2

Let us prove each of the three claims rigorously using word periodicity:

  1. Proof of Claim 1 (Left-Endpoint Alignment): At position \(r - d\), node \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) has length \(\operatorname{len}[v] = \ell_1 - d = \ell_2\) and \(\operatorname{slink}[v] = \operatorname{slink}[u]\), so its half-open progression \((\operatorname{slink}[v], v]\) consists of the \(k - 1\) lengths \(\{\ell_2, \ell_3, \dots, \ell_k\}\). For each \(j \in \{1, \dots, k - 1\}\), the \((j)\)-th member of \(v\)’s progression has length \(\ell_{j+1} = \ell_j - d\). Subtracting \(\ell_{j+1}\) from \(r - d\) cancels \(-d\): \[(r - d) - \ell_{j+1} \;=\; (r - d) - (\ell_j - d) \;=\; r - \ell_j!\] Thus \(\mathcal{I}(v, r - d) = \{r - \ell_1, r - \ell_2, \dots, r - \ell_{k-1}\}\), leaving only the \(k\)-th (shortest) palindrome’s left endpoint \(r - \ell_k = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\) outside \(\mathcal{I}(v, r - d)\).

  2. Proof of Claim 2 (Why \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) Was a slink Chain Head at \(r - d\)):

    • First, since \(v\) is a border (and hence a prefix) of \(u = S[r-\ell_1+1..r]\) of length \(\ell_1 - d\), the substring \(S[r-\ell_1+1 .. r-d]\) equals \(v\), so \(v\) occurs ending at \(r - d\).
    • Second, why couldn’t \(v\) have been skipped over at step \(r - d\) as an interior node of some longer period-\(d\) chain at \(r - d\)? Suppose for contradiction that at position \(r - d\), \(v\) had a fail-tree child \(w\) ending at \(r - d\) with \(\operatorname{fail}[w] = v\) and \(\operatorname{diff}[w] = d\). Since \(\operatorname{diff}[v] = d\), we have \(\operatorname{len}[v] > d\). In both KMP and PAM, a word \(w\) of length \(\operatorname{len}[v] + d\) having prefix \(v\) and period \(d \le \operatorname{len}[v]\) has all of its characters uniquely determined by \(v\) and period \(d\), so \(w\) must be the exact string \(u\)! If \(u\) itself occurred ending at \(r - d\), then \(S[r-\ell_1-d+1 .. r-d] = u\) (period \(d\)) and \(S[r-\ell_1+1 .. r] = u\) (period \(d\)) overlap on \(S[r-\ell_1+1 .. r-d] = v\) of length \(\operatorname{len}[v] > d\). Therefore, period \(d\) extends across the union \(u^+ = S[r-\ell_1-d+1 .. r]\) of length \(\ell_1 + d\)! In KMP/PAM, \(u^+\) is a longer border/palindrome ending at \(r\) with \(\operatorname{fail}[u^+] = u\) and \(\operatorname{diff}[u^+] = d\)—contradicting the fact that \(u\) was visited as a slink chain head at step \(r\)!

  3. Proof of Claim 3 (Why \(v = \operatorname{fail}[u]\) Was Never Visited in \((r - d, r)\)): Suppose for contradiction that \(v\) occurred ending at some \(r'' \in (r - d, r)\). Since \(\operatorname{len}[v] = \ell_1 - d\) and \(r'' > r - d\), this occurrence \(S[r''-\operatorname{len}[v]+1 .. r''] = v\) lies completely inside \(u = S[r-\ell_1+1 .. r]\). Because \(v\) also occurs inside \(u\) at \(r - d\) and at \(r\), \(v\) would have a shift \(s = r - r'' \in (0, d)\) inside a string of period \(d\). Since \(\operatorname{len}[v] > d\), \(v\) has both period \(d\) and shift \(s < d\) on an overlap of length \(\ge d\), which by the Weak Periodicity Lemma forces \(u\) to have a period \(\gcd(d, s) \le s < d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\), contradicting that \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\) is the minimum period of \(u\)! \(\blacksquare\)

3. Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad: Interleaving Bijections & Binomial Moment Splitting

3.1 Universal slink DP Loop (O(N log N))

By Theorem 4.2, at each step \(r = 1, 2, \dots, N\), after advancing our PAM (or KMP) by \(S[r]\) to state last, we update \(g[u]\) and \(dp[r]\) in \(\le \lceil \log_2 r \rceil\) iterations:

// Universal PAM / KMP slink DP Step at Right Endpoint r (1 <= r <= N)
for (int u = last; u > 0; u = slink[u]) { // PAM: 0 is even root (len=0), 1 is odd root (len=-1)
    int i_new = r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u]; // Single newly entering left endpoint!
    g[u] = dp[i_new];
    if (diff[u] == diff[fail[u]])
        g[u] = combine(g[u], g[fail[u]]);
    dp[r] = combine(dp[r], g[u]);
}

3.2 Double-End (CF 932G) and Reversal (CF 906E) Interleaving Bijections

Two classic grandmaster disguises hide an even-palindrome partition inside a non-palindromic problem statement:

\(T = S[1]\,S[N]\,S[2]\,S[N-1]\,\cdots\,S[N/2]\,S[N/2+1]\)		 Partition \(T[1..N]\) into even-length palindromes!

Why the Interleaving Bijection Works:

  • For CF 932G (Folded Interleaving): Consider the first symmetric pair of blocks \(w_1 = S[1..m]\) and \(w_k = S[N-m+1..N]\) of length \(m\). Under the interleaving \(T[2j-1] = S[j]\) and \(T[2j] = S[N-j+1]\), the prefix \(T[1..2m]\) is: \[T[1..2m] \;=\; S[1]\,S[N]\,S[2]\,S[N-1]\,\cdots\,S[m]\,S[N-m+1].\] Reading \(T[1..2m]\) backwards gives \(S[N-m+1]\,S[m]\,\cdots\,S[N]\,S[1]\).   • For CF 906E (Zipped Interleaving): Under \(T[2j-1] = A[j]\) and \(T[2j] = B[j]\), the substring \(T[2l-1..2r] = A[l]B[l]A[l+1]B[l+1]\cdots A[r]B[r]\) is a palindrome if and only if \(A[l+t] = B[r-t]\) for all \(0 \le t \le r - l\), which is the exact definition of \(A[l..r]^R = B[l..r]\)!

3.3 Binomial Left-Endpoint Moment Splitting (T-1A-05) for Polynomial Weights \(w(L) = P(L)\)

What if the transition weight depends on a degree-\(D\) polynomial \(P(L) = \sum_{m=0}^D c_m L^m\) of the palindrome/border length \(L\): \[dp[r] \;=\; \sum_{L \in \mathcal{A}(r)} P(L) \cdot dp[r - L]?\] At first glance, slink DP seems broken: when we advance from \(r - d\) to \(r\), every palindrome in \(\mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d)\) grows in length from \(\ell_{j+1}\) to \(\ell_j = \ell_{j+1} + d\), so \(\ell_j^m \ne \ell_{j+1}^m\).

The Structural Fix — Parameterize by the Invariant Left Endpoint \(i = r - L\): In Theorem 4.2, what stays 100% invariant between \((r - d, \operatorname{fail}[u])\) and \((r, u)\) is not the length \(L\), but the left endpoint \(i = r - L \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\)! Expand \(L^m = (r - i)^m\) using the Binomial Theorem: \[\boxed{L^m \;=\; (r - i)^m \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^m \binom{m}{t} (-1)^t \, r^{m-t} \cdot \underbrace{i^t}_{\text{depends ONLY on } i}!}\] Substituting this into the sum over \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\) factors \(r^{m-t}\) outside the slink accumulator: \[\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)} (r - i)^m dp[i] \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^m \binom{m}{t} (-1)^t \, r^{m-t} \underbrace{\left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)} i^t \, dp[i]\right)}_{g_t[u]}.\]

Because \(i^t \, dp[i]\) depends only on the left endpoint \(i\), each moment accumulator \(g_t[u]\) (\(0 \le t \le D\)) obeys the exact un-shifted slink recurrence:

// Non-Standard Kernel: Degree-D Polynomial-Weighted slink DP in O(D * N log N) Time
// Computes dp[r] = sum_{L in A(r)} P(L) * dp[r - L] where P(L) = sum_{m=0}^D c[m] * L^m
for (int u = last; u > 0; u = slink[u]) {
    int i_new = r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u];
    long long pw_i = 1;
    for (int t = 0; t <= D; ++t) {
        g[t][u] = pw_i * dp[i_new] % MOD;
        if (diff[u] == diff[fail[u]])
            g[t][u] = (g[t][u] + g[t][fail[u]]) % MOD;
        pw_i = pw_i * i_new % MOD;
    }
    // Reconstruct sum_{i in I(u, r)} (r - i)^m dp[i] for each degree m = 0..D
    for (int m = 0; m <= D; ++m) {
        long long moment_m = 0, pw_r = 1;
        for (int t = m; t >= 0; --t) {
            long long term = C[m][t] * pw_r % MOD * g[t][u] % MOD;
            if (t & 1) moment_m = (moment_m - term + MOD) % MOD;
            else       moment_m = (moment_m + term) % MOD;
            pw_r = pw_r * r % MOD;
        }
        dp[r] = (dp[r] + c[m] * moment_m) % MOD;
    }
}

4.2 Boundary Pitfalls & Broken-Invariant Counterexamples (BP-04 / BP-1A-05, BP-05 / BP-1A-06)

WarningPitfall BP-04 (BP-1A-05): Initializing g[u] = dp[r - len[u]] Instead of dp[r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u]]

The Broken Shortcut: Writing g[u] = dp[r - len[u]]; before adding g[fail[u]].

Why It Fails (Counterexample on \(T = \texttt{aaaa}\) at \(r = 4\)): As shown in Section 4.1, at \(r = 4\) (\(u = 5, \operatorname{len}[5] = 4, d = 1, \operatorname{slink}[5] = 0\)), g[fail[5]] = g[4] already contains \(dp[0] + dp[1] + dp[2]\) from step \(r - 1 = 3\).

  • The left endpoint of the longest palindrome in the chain is \(r - \operatorname{len}[u] = 4 - 4 = 0 = (r - d) - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]\), which is the leftmost (oldest) element and is already stored inside g[fail[u]]!
  • The left endpoint of the shortest palindrome in the half-open chain \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) is \(r - (\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + d) = 4 - (0 + 1) = 3\), which is the rightmost (newest) element that did not exist at step \(r - d\)!
  • Writing g[u] = dp[r - len[u]] + g[fail[u]] computes \(2 \cdot dp[0] + dp[1] + dp[2]\) (double-counting \(i = 0\) and completely missing \(i = 3\)!). Always initialize g[u] = dp[r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u]].
WarningPitfall BP-05 (BP-1A-06): Skipping g[u] Updates at Odd Indices r in Interleaved Strings (CF 932G / CF 906E)

The Broken Shortcut: In CF 932G or CF 906E, since only even prefixes \(r\) can have \(dp[r] \ne 0\), wrapping the entire for (int u = last; u > 0; u = slink[u]) loop inside if (r % 2 == 0).

Why It Fails (Counterexample on \(T = \texttt{aaaaaa}\) at \(r = 4\)): Look at node \(u = 5\) (\(\texttt{aaaa}\), \(\operatorname{len}[5] = 4\), even) at even position \(r = 4\):

  • Its fail parent is \(\operatorname{fail}[5] = 4\) (\(\texttt{aaa}\), \(\operatorname{len}[4] = 3\), odd!), and its period step is \(d = \operatorname{diff}[5] = 1\) (odd!).
  • Therefore, at even position \(r = 4\), g[5] reads g[4], which was supposed to be updated at the odd position \(r - d = 4 - 1 = 3\)!
  • If you skipped updating g[u] at odd \(r = 3\), then g[4] at \(r = 4\) is stale (\(0\)), and dp[4] misses the valid even-palindrome transition from \(i = 2\) (\(L = 2\))!
  • The Rule: Always update g[u] at every index \(r\) (both odd and even); only gate the final line if (r % 2 == 0) dp[r] = combine(dp[r], g[u]);!

Problem 4.1 (W1A-34 · Minimum & Counting Palindrome Factorization in \(O(N \log N)\))

Statement: Given a string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 10^6\)), for every prefix \(S[1..r]\) (\(1 \le r \le N\)), compute:

  1. \(dp_{\min}[r]\): the minimum number of palindromic substrings into which \(S[1..r]\) can be partitioned, and
  2. \(dp_{\text{ways}}[r]\): the number of minimum-cut partitions of \(S[1..r]\) modulo \(10^9 + 7\).

Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 10^6\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\). Time limit: \(1.5\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

At each \(r \in [1, N]\), \(dp_{\min}[r] = 1 + \min_{L \in \operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r])} dp_{\min}[r - L]\). Walking the raw PAM fail chain takes \(\Theta(N^2)\) on \(S = \texttt{a}^N\).

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Define the commutative monoid operation (min_cuts, ways) where \((c_1, w_1) \oplus (c_2, w_2)\) keeps the smaller cut count and sums the ways when \(c_1 = c_2\). By Theorem 4.2, each PAM state \(u\) can store g[u] = (min_cuts, ways) over the left-endpoint set \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\).

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Initialize dp[0] = {0, 1}. At step \(r\), walk u = last down via u = slink[u]: set g[u] = dp[r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u]], combine with g[fail[u]] if diff[u] == diff[fail[u]], and relax dp[r] with {g[u].cuts + 1, g[u].ways}.

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Enumerating all palindromic suffixes at \(r\) via u = fail[u] takes \(\Omega(N^2)\) time.

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: Let \(\mathcal{M}\) be the monoid of pairs \((c, w)\) with identity \((+\infty, 0)\) and combination operator: \[(c_1, w_1) \oplus (c_2, w_2) \;=\; \begin{cases} (c_1, w_1) & \text{if } c_1 < c_2, \\ (c_2, w_2) & \text{if } c_2 < c_1, \\ \bigl(c_1, (w_1 + w_2) \bmod 10^9+7\bigr) & \text{if } c_1 = c_2. \end{cases}\] By the Shift-Overlap Invariant (Theorem 4.2), for any PAM state \(u\) with \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{fail}[u]] = d\), \(\mathcal{I}(u, r) = \mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d) \sqcup \{r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\}\), where g[fail[u]] was computed at \(r - d\) and never overwritten in \((r - d, r)\).

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:

    • Build PAM online for \(r = 1 \dots N\), maintaining diff[u] and slink[u].
    • At each \(r\), run the \(\le \lceil \log_2 r \rceil\) loop: g[u] = dp[r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u]], if diff[u] == diff[fail[u]] then g[u] = g[u] + g[fail[u]], and dp[r] = dp[r] + {g[u].c + 1, g[u].w}.
    • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Adding \(+1\) to g[u].c before storing g[u] causes g[fail[u]] to be incremented a second time when read at step \(r + d\)! Always store raw left-endpoint dp values inside g[u] and add \(+1\) only when updating dp[r].

Problem 4.2 (W1A-27 · [CERC 2014] Virus Synthesis / Luogu P4762)

Statement: Starting from an empty string, you want to construct a target DNA string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 10^5\), \(\Sigma = \{\texttt{A}, \texttt{C}, \texttt{G}, \texttt{T}\}\)) using the minimum number of operations:

  • Operation 1: Append or prepend any single character to the current string (\(+1\) cost).
  • Operation 2: Replace the current string \(W\) with \(W \cdot W^R\) (\(+1\) cost).

Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| = 4\). Time limit: \(1.0\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Every time Operation 2 (\(W \to W W^R\)) is applied, it produces an even-length palindrome! Once the final Operation 2 is performed on some even-length palindromic substring \(u\) of \(S\), the remaining \(N - \operatorname{len}[u]\) characters of \(S\) are added by single-character Operation 1 steps.

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

How can an even palindrome \(u\) in \(\operatorname{PAM}(S)\) be formed ending with Operation 2? Either (a) we formed its PAM Trie parent \(p(u)\) of length \(\operatorname{len}[u] - 2\), prepended \(c\) to one half, and doubled (\(dp[p(u)] + 1\)), or (b) we started from the longest palindromic border \(\operatorname{trans}[u]\) of \(u\) of length \(\le \operatorname{len}[u]/2\), appended \(\operatorname{len}[u]/2 - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{trans}[u]]\) characters to reach the half-palindrome of length \(\operatorname{len}[u]/2\), and doubled!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

During PAM construction, maintain the half-link pointer trans[u] (the longest palindromic suffix of \(u\) with \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{trans}[u]] \le \operatorname{len}[u]/2\)). Then run BFS from the even root \(0\) (\(dp[0] = 0\), and \(dp[u] = \operatorname{len}[u]\) initialized for even nodes) to relax \(dp[u]\) in \(O(N)\) time!

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Operation 2 creates only even-length palindromes. Thus the problem reduces to computing \(dp[u]\) = minimum operations to form even palindrome \(u \in \operatorname{PAM}(S)\), after which the answer is \(\min_{\text{even } u} \bigl(dp[u] + N - \operatorname{len}[u]\bigr)\).

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: By Theorem 4.1, a palindromic prefix of the half-string \(u[1..\operatorname{len}[u]/2]\) (actually a palindromic suffix of the first half, which by symmetry equals a palindromic prefix/suffix of \(u\)) is a palindromic border of \(u\) of length \(\le \operatorname{len}[u]/2\). Let \(\operatorname{trans}[u]\) be the longest ancestor of \(u\) in the PAM fail tree with \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{trans}[u]] \le \operatorname{len}[u]/2\). Then: \[dp[u] \;=\; \min\Bigl(dp[p(u)] + 1,\;\; dp\bigl[\operatorname{trans}[u]\bigr] + \frac{\operatorname{len}[u]}{2} - \operatorname{len}\bigl[\operatorname{trans}[u]\bigr] + 1\Bigr),\] where \(p(u)\) is the PAM Trie parent of \(u\) (\(\operatorname{len}[p(u)] = \operatorname{len}[u] - 2\)).

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:

    • Build \(\operatorname{PAM}(S)\) and compute trans[u] alongside fail[u] in \(O(N)\) amortized time (walking v = trans[p(u)] until \(S[r - 1 - \operatorname{len}[v]] = S[r]\) and \(2(\operatorname{len}[v] + 2) \le \operatorname{len}[u]\)).
    • Initialize \(dp[0] = 1\) (since for a non-empty even palindrome \(\texttt{cc}\) of length \(2\), forming \(\texttt{c}\) and doubling takes \(1 + 1 = 2\) steps, setting \(dp[0] = 1\) gives \(dp[\texttt{cc}] = dp[0] + 1 = 2\)) and initialize ans = N.
    • Traverse PAM nodes in BFS order of the Trie: for every even node \(u\) with \(\operatorname{len}[u] \ge 2\), compute \(dp[u]\) and update \(\text{ans} \leftarrow \min(\text{ans}, dp[u] + N - \operatorname{len}[u])\).
    • Complexity: \(O(N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Applying the doubling transition to odd-length palindromes fails because \(W \cdot W^R\) always has even length.

Tier 2 — Intermediate (Double-End Interleaving & KMP Border slink DP)

Problem 4.3 (W1A-25 · CF 932G — Palindrome Partition)

Statement: Given a string \(S[1..N]\) of even length \(N \le 10^6\), count the number of ways modulo \(10^9 + 7\) to partition \(S\) into an even number of non-empty substrings \(S = w_1 w_2 \cdots w_{2k}\) such that \(w_i = w_{2k + 1 - i}\) for all \(1 \le i \le k\). (Note: \(w_i\) itself does not need to be a palindrome; we require the whole word \(w_i\) to equal the whole word \(w_{2k+1-i}\).)

Constraints: \(2 \le N \le 10^6\) (\(2 \mid N\)), \(|\Sigma| = 26\). Time limit: \(2.0\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

A choice of \(w_1 = S[1..m]\) and \(w_{2k} = S[N-m+1..N]\) simultaneously consumes \(m\) characters from the left end and \(m\) characters from the right end of \(S\). How can we turn this two-ended condition \(S[1..m] = S[N-m+1..N]\) into a 1D prefix condition?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Construct the folded interleaved string \(T[1..N] = S[1]\,S[N]\,S[2]\,S[N-1]\,\cdots\,S[N/2]\,S[N/2+1]\). By Section 3.2, \(S[1..m] = S[N-m+1..N]\) if and only if \(T[1..2m]\) is an even-length palindrome!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Run PAM slink DP on \(T[1..N]\) with \(dp[0] = 1\). Remember Pitfall BP-1A-06: update g[u] at all \(r \in [1, N]\), and add g[u] to dp[r] only when \(r\) is even!

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Matching blocks from both ends of \(S\) couples prefix indices with suffix indices; once folded into \(T\), \(N = 10^6\) requires \(O(N \log N)\) PAM slink acceleration.
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: Under the bijection \(T[2j-1] = S[j]\) and \(T[2j] = S[N-j+1]\) (\(1 \le j \le N/2\)), any valid symmetric block pair \((w_i, w_{2k+1-i}) = (S[l..r], S[N-r+1..N-l+1])\) satisfies \(S[l..r] = S[N-r+1..N-l+1] \iff T[2l-1..2r]\) is an even-length palindrome. Since every piece \(T[2l-1..2r]\) has even length and starts at an odd index, valid partitions of \(S\) biject onto partitions of \(T[1..N]\) into even-length palindromes.
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Build \(T[1..N]\) and initialize dp[0] = 1, dp[1..N] = 0.
    • For \(r = 1 \dots N\), extend PAM with \(T[r]\) and loop for (int u = last; u > 0; u = slink[u]):
      • g[u] = dp[r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u]];
      • if (diff[u] == diff[fail[u]]) g[u] = (g[u] + g[fail[u]]) % MOD;
      • if (r % 2 == 0) dp[r] = (dp[r] + g[u]) % MOD;
    • Output dp[N].
    • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Because \(dp[i] = 0\) for all odd \(i\), any odd-length palindrome \(L\) ending at an even \(r\) queries \(dp[r - L]\) at an odd index \(r - L\), which automatically contributes \(0\)! However, skipping the g[u] update at odd \(r\) (BP-1A-06) breaks even-length palindromes at even \(r\) whose fail parent has odd length.

Problem 4.4 (W1A-37 · KMP Border Partition DP via slink Accumulators)

Statement: You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 10^6\)) and a base weight array \(A[0..N-1]\). Define \(dp[0] = 1\), and for each \(r = 1, 2, \dots, N\): \[dp[r] \;=\; \left(\sum_{b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..r]) \setminus \{0\}} dp[r - b]\right) \bmod 998244353,\] where a step from \(r - b\) to \(r\) represents cutting off a suffix \(S[r-b+1..r]\) that is also a proper border of the prefix \(S[1..r]\). Compute \(dp[1..N]\) in \(O(N \log N)\) time.

Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 10^6\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\). Time limit: \(1.5\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Here the transition lengths \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..r]) \setminus \{0\}\) are given by the KMP fail chain \(u = \pi[r], \pi[\pi[r]], \dots > 0\) instead of the PAM fail chain. Does the Shift-Overlap Invariant (Theorem 4.2) hold on the KMP fail tree?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Yes! In the KMP fail tree, node \(u \in [1, N]\) represents the prefix \(S[1..u]\) of length \(\operatorname{len}[u] = u\), with \(\operatorname{fail}[u] = \pi[u]\) and \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = u - \pi[u]\). Whenever \(u\) is a border of \(S[1..r]\) with \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\pi[u]] = d\), the prefix \(v = \pi[u]\) of \(u\) occurred ending at \(r - d\) as a border of \(S[1..r-d]\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

In PAM, a node \(u\) is only visited when \(u\) actually occurs as a palindromic suffix at \(r\), whereas in KMP node \(u = r\) is born at index \(r\) and can be visited again as a proper border at \(r' > r\). Verify that Claim 2 and Claim 3 of Theorem 4.2 still hold for KMP proper borders: if \(u \le \pi[r]\) is a slink chain head in the proper border chain of \(r\) with \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\pi[u]] = d\), then \(v = \pi[u] < r - d\) was a slink chain head in the proper border chain of \(r - d\) and never occurred as a border in \((r - d, r)\).

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Walking \(b = \pi[r], \pi[\pi[r]], \dots\) at each \(r\) takes \(\Theta(N^2)\) on \(S = \texttt{a}^N\).

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: Let \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = u - \pi[u]\) and \(\operatorname{slink}[u] = (\operatorname{diff}[u] == \operatorname{diff}[\pi[u]] \text{ ? } \operatorname{slink}[\pi[u]] : \pi[u])\) for \(u \ge 1\). Why does walking only proper borders \(u = \pi[r], \operatorname{slink}[\pi[r]], \dots\) never read an uninitialized g[r - d]? Because any proper border \(u \le \pi[r]\) of \(S[1..r]\) with \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\pi[u]] = d\) has \(v = \pi[u] = u - d \le \pi[r] - d < r - d\). Thus \(v = \pi[u]\) is strictly shorter than \(r - d\), meaning \(v\) was a proper border of \(S[1..r-d]\) and was already visited in the proper-border loop at step \(r - d\)! Let us trace \(S = \texttt{aaaa}\):

    • At \(r = 1\) (\(\pi[1] = 0\)), \(\operatorname{diff}[1] = 1, \operatorname{slink}[1] = 0\). At \(r = 2\), \(u = \pi[2] = 1\) has \(\operatorname{diff}[1] = 1 \ne \operatorname{diff}[0]\), updating g[1].
    • At \(r = 3\), \(u = \pi[3] = 2\) has \(\operatorname{diff}[2] = 1 = \operatorname{diff}[1]\), so g[2] at \(r = 3\) reads g[1] from \(r - 1 = 2\), where \(1 = \pi[2]\) was already updated!

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:

    • Compute \(\pi[r]\), \(\operatorname{diff}[r] = r - \pi[r]\), and \(\operatorname{slink}[r]\) online for \(r = 1 \dots N\).
    • For each \(r = 1 \dots N\), loop for (int u = pi[r]; u > 0; u = slink[u]):
      • g[u] = dp[r - slink[u] - diff[u]];
      • if (diff[u] == diff[pi[u]]) g[u] = (g[u] + g[pi[u]]) % MOD;
      • dp[r] = (dp[r] + g[u]) % MOD;
    • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: If the problem instead summed over \(b \in \operatorname{Border}(S[1..r]) \cup \{r\}\) (including the full prefix \(b = r\)), then the loop starts at u = r instead of u = pi[r]. Mixing the two (starting at u = pi[r] when \(v = r - d\) is needed) fails if and only if \(u = r\) is included.

Tier 3 — Advanced (CF 906E, Parity-Filtered & Lex-Optimal Reconstruction)

Problem 4.5 (W1A-26 · CF 906E — Reverses)

Statement: You are given two strings \(A[1..n]\) and \(B[1..n]\) of equal length \(n \le 5 \times 10^5\). You may choose any set of pairwise disjoint intervals \([l_1, r_1], [l_2, r_2], \dots, [l_k, r_k]\) (\(r_i - l_i + 1 \ge 2\)) and reverse each substring \(A[l_i..r_i]\) in place. Find the minimum number of interval reversals \(k\) required to make \(A = B\), and output the \(k\) intervals \([l_i, r_i]\) (or report \(-1\) if impossible).

Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\). Time limit: \(2.0\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Form the zipped interleaved string \(T[1..2n] = A[1]\,B[1]\,A[2]\,B[2]\,\cdots\,A[n]\,B[n]\). As proved in Section 3.2, \(A[l..r]^R = B[l..r]\) if and only if \(T[2l-1..2r]\) is an even-length palindrome!

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

What corresponds to an index \(j\) where \(A[j] = B[j]\) and we do not reverse across \(j\)? In \(T\), \(T[2j-1] = A[j] = B[j] = T[2j]\) is a length-\(2\) palindrome of cost \(0\), whereas any reversed interval \([l, r]\) (\(l < r\)) is an even-length palindrome \(T[2l-1..2r]\) of length \(\ge 4\) and cost \(+1\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Maintain pair<int, int> dp[0..2n] = (min_cost, prev_even_r) and pair<int, int> g[u] = (min_cost, argmin_left_endpoint). At every index \(r \in [1, 2n]\), update g[u] along u = slink[u]. When \(2 \mid r\): first check if \(T[r-1] == T[r]\) to relax dp[r] = min(dp[r], {dp[r-2].first, r-2}) with cost \(+0\); then relax dp[r] = min(dp[r], {g[u].first + 1, g[u].second}) from each slink chain!

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Explicit interval DP over \(A[l..r]^R = B[l..r]\) takes \(\Theta(n^2)\) time. Furthermore, we must reconstruct the exact optimal intervals \([l_i, r_i]\).
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: On \(T[1..2n] = A[1]B[1]\cdots A[n]B[n]\), every even prefix \(r = 2j\) transitions either from \(r - 2\) with cost \(0\) (if \(T[r-1] = T[r]\)) or from \(i = r - L\) with cost \(+1\) for any even-length palindromic suffix \(L \in \operatorname{PalSuf}(T[1..r])\). By Theorem 4.2, storing g[u] = min_{i in I(u, r)} {dp[i].cost, i} preserves the exact argmin left endpoint \(i\) across slink shifts because \(i\) itself is invariant under \(r - d \to r\)!
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Build \(T[1..2n]\) (\(N = 2n\)). Set dp[0] = {0, -1} and dp[1..N] = {INF, -1}.
    • For \(r = 1 \dots N\), advance PAM on \(T[r]\):
      • If \(r\) is even and \(T[r-1] == T[r]\), set dp[r] = {dp[r-2].first, r-2}.
      • Loop for (int u = last; u > 0; u = slink[u]):
        • int i_new = r - len[slink[u]] - diff[u];
        • g[u] = {dp[i_new].first, i_new};
        • if (diff[u] == diff[fail[u]]) g[u] = min(g[u], g[fail[u]]);
        • if (r % 2 == 0) dp[r] = min(dp[r], {g[u].first + 1, g[u].second});
    • If dp[N].first >= INF, output -1. Otherwise, backtrack r = N via prev = dp[r].second: whenever r - prev > 2, output the 1-based interval \((\text{prev}/2 + 1,\; r/2)\).
    • Complexity: \(O(n \log n)\) time and \(O(n)\) space.
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Outputting an interval when r - prev == 2 and dp[r] came from g[u] (a length-\(2\) palindrome \(T[r-1..r]\)) prints a degenerate reversal of length \(1\) (\(l = r\)), which the problem statement forbids (\(r_i - l_i + 1 \ge 2\)). Checking r - prev > 2 (and relaxing \(T[r-1] == T[r]\) with cost \(0\)) avoids all length-\(1\) reversals.

Problem 4.6 (W1A-40 · Parity-Restricted Palindrome Partitioning with Active Odd & Even Prefixes)

Statement: You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 10^6\)). Count the number of ways modulo \(10^9 + 7\) to partition \(S\) into substrings \(S = w_1 w_2 \cdots w_k\) such that every piece \(w_j\) is a palindrome of length at least \(2\) and odd length (\(|w_j| \ge 3\) and \(2 \nmid |w_j|\)), OR every piece has even length—more generally, you are given two allowed parity masks for \(|w_j|\) and \(r\), so both odd and even prefix indices \(r\) can have \(dp[r] \ne 0\), and you must sum only over palindromic suffixes \(L \in \operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r])\) with \(L \equiv 1 \pmod 2\) and \(L \ge 3\).

Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 10^6\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\). Time limit: \(1.5\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Unlike CF 932G (where \(dp[i] = 0\) for all odd \(i\), which automatically killed odd-length palindromes at even \(r\)), here each piece has odd length \(L \ge 3\), so \(dp[r]\) is non-zero at BOTH odd and even \(r\)! What happens when an AP chain \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) has an even step \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\) vs. an odd step \(d\)?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Look at the invariant left endpoint \(i = r - L \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\): \[L \equiv 1 \pmod 2 \iff i = r - L \;\equiv\; (r - 1) \pmod 2!\] Because the parity of the left endpoint \(i\) is 100% invariant when shifting from \(r - d \to r\), splitting g[u] into two accumulators g[0][u] (sum over even left endpoints \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\)) and g[1][u] (sum over odd left endpoints \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\)) allows us to query only \(L \equiv 1 \pmod 2\) at step \(r\) by reading g[(r - 1) & 1][u]!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

What about excluding \(L = 1\) (requiring \(L \ge 3\))? At step \(r\), the single palindrome of length \(L = 1\) has left endpoint \(i = r - 1\). Simply subtract dp[r - 1] after the slink loop!

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution

  1. Diagnostic Signal: When \(\operatorname{diff}[u]\) is odd, a single slink chain \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) contains both odd-length and even-length palindromes, and since both \(dp[\text{odd}]\) and \(dp[\text{even}]\) are active, a single scalar g[u] mixes valid and invalid transition lengths.

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (Left-Endpoint Parity Splitting): For any \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\), the palindrome length is \(L = r - i\), so: \[L \bmod 2 \;=\; (r - i) \bmod 2 \iff i \bmod 2 \;=\; (r - L) \bmod 2.\] Since \(i\) does not change when \(\mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d)\) is reused inside \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\), we maintain two accumulators per PAM state \(u\): \[g_p[u] \;=\; \sum_{\substack{i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r) \\ i \equiv p \pmod 2}} dp[i] \qquad (p \in \{0, 1\}).\] At right endpoint \(r\), the sum over odd-length palindromes \(L \in (\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) is simply \(g_{(r - 1) \bmod 2}[u]\) (and the sum over even-length palindromes is \(g_{r \bmod 2}[u]\))!

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:

    • At each \(r = 1 \dots N\), for u = last down to \(0\) via slink[u]:
      • Let \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u]\) and \(p = i_{\text{new}} \bmod 2\).
      • Set \(g_p[u] = dp[i_{\text{new}}]\) and \(g_{1-p}[u] = 0\).
      • If \(\operatorname{diff}[u] == \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]\), add \(g_0[\operatorname{fail}[u]]\) to \(g_0[u]\) and \(g_1[\operatorname{fail}[u]]\) to \(g_1[u]\).
      • Add \(g_{(r - 1) \bmod 2}[u]\) to \(dp[r]\).
    • Finally, subtract \(dp[r - 1]\) from \(dp[r]\) (modulo \(10^9 + 7\)) to remove the length-\(1\) palindrome \(S[r..r]\).
    • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Splitting g[u] by the parity of length \(L\) instead of the parity of left endpoint \(i\) fails whenever \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u]\) is odd, because shifting from \(r - d \to r\) increases every length in g[fail[u]] by \(+d\) (flipping its length parity!), whereas the left endpoint parity \(i \bmod 2\) never flips (or if you split by length parity, you must swap g_len[0] and g_len[1] when \(d\) is odd).

Problem 4.7 (W1A-42 · Lexicographically Optimal Minimum Palindrome Cut)

Statement: Given \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 5 \times 10^5\)), among all partitions of \(S\) into the minimum possible number \(K = dp_{\min}[N]\) of palindromic substrings \(S = S[1..r_1] \, S[r_1+1..r_2] \cdots S[r_{K-1}+1..N]\), find the one whose cut-position vector \((r_1, r_2, \dots, r_{K-1})\) is lexicographically smallest.

Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(|\Sigma| = 26\). Time limit: \(1.5\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

In a forward DP \(dp[1..N]\), choosing the smallest \(r_{K-1}\) at the end makes the last cut small, not the first cut \(r_1\)! How do we make greedy choices from left to right (\(r_1\), then \(r_2\), etc.)?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Run the PAM slink DP on the reversed string \(S^R\) (from right to left)! Since a substring \(S[l..r]\) is a palindrome if and only if its reversal in \(S^R\) is a palindrome, running slink DP backwards computes for every \(l \in [1, N+1]\) the minimum number of palindromic pieces \(dp_{\text{suf}}[l]\) needed to partition the suffix \(S[l..N]\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Moreover, at each position \(l\) (corresponding to prefix \(r' = N - l + 1\) of \(S^R\)), wanting the smallest next cut \(r_1 \ge l\) with \(dp_{\text{suf}}[r_1 + 1] = dp_{\text{suf}}[l] - 1\) is equivalent in \(S^R\) to choosing the shortest valid palindrome (largest left endpoint \(i'\) in \(S^R\), or minimum \(r_1\))! Track pair<int, int> = (min_cuts, min_next_cut_r) in g[u]!

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Lexicographical minimization on \((r_1, r_2, \dots, r_{K-1})\) requires choosing the minimum valid \(r_1\) first, which depends on suffix reachability \(dp_{\text{suf}}[r_1 + 1]\).

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: Let \(S^R[1..N]\) be the reversal of \(S\), where index \(r' \in [1, N]\) of \(S^R\) corresponds to suffix start \(l = N - r' + 1\) of \(S\), and a palindromic suffix of \(S^R[1..r']\) of length \(L\) corresponds to the palindromic prefix \(S[l .. l + L - 1]\) ending at \(r_1 = l + L - 1 = N - (r' - L)\) in \(S\). Notice that \(i' = r' - L\) is the invariant left endpoint in \(S^R\), so \(r_1 = N - i'\) depends only on \(i'\)! Therefore, storing the pair (dp_suf_cuts, N - i') inside g[u] on \(\operatorname{PAM}(S^R)\) and taking the lexicographical minimum automatically selects the minimum cut count tie-broken by the smallest cut index \(r_1 = N - i'\) in \(S\)!

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:

    • Run PAM slink DP on \(S^R[1..N]\) with state (cuts, next_r_in_S):
      • At step \(r'\) of \(S^R\) (\(i_{\text{new}} = r' - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u]\)), seed g[u] = {dp[i_new].cuts, N - i_new}.
      • Combine with g[fail[u]] via std::min on pairs.
      • Relax dp[r'] = min(dp[r'], {g[u].cuts + 1, g[u].next_r}).
    • Reconstruct \((r_1, \dots, r_{K-1})\) in \(O(K)\) steps by following next_r from \(r' = N\) (\(l = 1\)).
    • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Running forward DP on \(S\) and breaking ties at \(r\) by smallest previous cut \(i\) minimizes \(r_{K-1}\) first (reverse-lexicographical order) instead of \(r_1\) first; for \(S = \texttt{abaaba}\) (\(K\) under restricted cuts), forward tie-breaking picks the wrong first cut.

Problem 4.8 (W1A-39 · Polynomial-Weighted Palindrome Partition DP via Binomial Moment Splitting)

Statement: Given a string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 2 \times 10^5\)) and a polynomial \(P(L) = \sum_{m=0}^D c_m L^m\) of degree \(D \le 5\), define \(dp[0] = 1\) and for \(1 \le r \le N\): \[dp[r] \;=\; \left(\sum_{L \in \operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r])} P(L) \cdot dp[r - L]\right) \bmod 998244353.\] Compute \(dp[1..N]\).

Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 2 \times 10^5\), \(0 \le D \le 5\), \(0 \le c_m < 998244353\). Time limit: \(2.0\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

When advancing from \(r - d\) to \(r\), each palindrome length \(L\) in g[fail[u]] increases by \(+d\), so \(P(L)\) changes for every element in the chain. Why don’t we need a segment tree or multipoint evaluation?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

By Section 3.3 (T-1A-05), write \(L = r - i\) where \(i = r - L \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\) is the invariant left endpoint: \[L^m \;=\; (r - i)^m \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^m \binom{m}{t} (-1)^t \, r^{m-t} \cdot i^t.\] Now \(i^t\) is completely invariant under the shift \(r - d \to r\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Even faster than reconstructing each \(m \in [0, D]\) separately: rewrite \(\sum_{m=0}^D c_m (r - i)^m\) directly as a polynomial in \((-i)\)! \[\sum_{m=0}^D c_m (r - i)^m \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^D (-1)^t \underbrace{\left(\sum_{m=t}^D c_m \binom{m}{t} r^{m-t}\right)}_{A_t(r)} \cdot i^t!\] Precomputing \(A_0(r), \dots, A_D(r)\) once at the start of step \(r\) reduces the inner slink loop to a simple dot product \(\sum_{t=0}^D (-1)^t A_t(r) g_t[u]\) in \(O(D)\) operations per slink step!

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Length-dependent weights \(P(L)\) appear to break slink accumulators because \(L = r - i\) depends on \(r\).

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: Expanding \(P(r - i)\) in powers of \(i\) around \(r\) (Taylor / Binomial expansion) separates \(r\) from the invariant left endpoint \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\): \[P(r - i) \;=\; \sum_{m=0}^D c_m \sum_{t=0}^m \binom{m}{t} r^{m-t} (-i)^t \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^D \left(\sum_{m=t}^D c_m \binom{m}{t} r^{m-t}\right) (-1)^t i^t \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^D A_t(r) \cdot i^t.\] Define \(g_t[u] = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)} i^t dp[i] \bmod 998244353\) for \(0 \le t \le D\). By Theorem 4.2, \(g_t[u]\) satisfies the exact shift-overlap recurrence: \[g_t[u] \;=\; i_{\text{new}}^t \, dp[i_{\text{new}}] + [\operatorname{diff}[u] == \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]] \cdot g_t[\operatorname{fail}[u]].\]

  3. Algorithmic Compilation:

    • At step \(r\), compute \(A_t(r) = (-1)^t \sum_{m=t}^D c_m \binom{m}{t} r^{m-t} \bmod 998244353\) for \(0 \le t \le D\) in \(O(D^2)\) time.
    • Loop for (int u = last; u > 0; u = slink[u]):
      • Update \(g_0[u], \dots, g_D[u]\) in \(O(D)\) time and add \(\sum_{t=0}^D A_t(r) g_t[u]\) to \(dp[r]\) in \(O(D)\) time.
    • Complexity: \(O(D \cdot N \log N)\) time and \(O(D \cdot N)\) space.

  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Defining \(g_t[u] = \sum L^t dp[r-L]\) and trying to shift \(g_t[\operatorname{fail}[u]]\) by \(+d\) via binomial expansion inside the slink loop takes \(O(D^2 \cdot N \log N)\) time and is far more error-prone than keeping the left endpoint \(i^t\) invariant (\(O(D \cdot N \log N)\)).

Problem 4.9 (W1A-43 · Length-Windowed \(L \in [L_{\min}, L_{\max}]\) Monotonic-Deque slink DP)

Statement: You are given a string \(S[1..N]\) (\(N \le 3 \times 10^5\)), an array of transition costs \(C[0..N]\), and a length window \([L_{\min}, L_{\max}]\) (\(1 \le L_{\min} \le L_{\max} \le N\)). Compute \(dp[0..N]\) where \(dp[0] = 0\) and for \(1 \le r \le N\): \[dp[r] \;=\; \min_{\substack{L \in \operatorname{PalSuf}(S[1..r]) \\ L_{\min} \le L \le L_{\max}}} \bigl(dp[r - L] + C[r - L]\bigr).\]

Constraints: \(1 \le N \le 3 \times 10^5\), \(1 \le L_{\min} \le L_{\max} \le N\). Time limit: \(2.0\text{ s}\).

NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Unlike pure sum DP (where we could subtract out-of-window elements if we knew which node held them), here the aggregation is \(\min\), which has no inverse! Moreover, the length constraint \(L_{\min} \le r - i \le L_{\max}\) clips the left-endpoint set \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) to the interval \(i \in [r - L_{\max},\, r - L_{\min}]\).

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Look at how left endpoints enter a slink chain in Theorem 4.2: at each step \(r\), the newly entering left endpoint is \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - \operatorname{diff}[u]\). All elements of \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) are values of \(i_{\text{new}}\) pushed along the period-\(d\) chain of \(u\). Can two different branches in the same period-\(d\) component of the PAM fail tree ever split?

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Recall the proof of Claim 2 of Theorem 4.2 (Section 2.1): in the PAM fail tree, any node \(v\) with \(\operatorname{diff}[v] = d\) can have at most ONE child \(u\) with \(\operatorname{fail}[u] = v\) and \(\operatorname{diff}[u] = d\) (because \(d = \operatorname{diff}[v] < \operatorname{len}[v]\) uniquely determines all characters of \(u\))! That means the subgraphs of equal \(\operatorname{diff}\) in the PAM fail tree are unbranched directed paths (chains), NOT trees! And on each chain, left endpoints \(i_{\text{new}}\) are appended in strictly increasing order!

TipFull 4-Step Derivation & Solution

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Under \((\min, +)\) with a two-sided length window \(L \in [L_{\min}, L_{\max}]\), we cannot subtract expired prefix sums, and building a segment tree over every slink chain would take \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time.

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (Unbranched Period-\(d\) Chains & Monotonic Left Endpoints): By Claim 2 of Theorem 4.2, for any node \(v\) with \(\operatorname{diff}[v] = d\), a palindrome \(u\) of length \(\operatorname{len}[v] + d\) with border \(v\) has period \(d \le \operatorname{len}[v]\), which uniquely fixes every character of \(u\). Thus every constant-\(\operatorname{diff}\) component in the PAM fail tree (excluding its bottom node \(v_c\) where \(\operatorname{diff}[v_c]\) first equals \(d\)) is a simple unbranched path: \[v_c \gets u_2 \gets u_3 \gets \dots \gets u_{\max}.\] Even better: for a node \(u\) at position \(r\), the set of left endpoints \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) is: \[\mathcal{I}(u, r) \;=\; \bigl\{\,r - \operatorname{len}[u],\; r - \operatorname{len}[u] + d,\; \dots,\; r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\,\bigr\}.\] The condition \(L_{\min} \le r - i \le L_{\max}\) is equivalent to restricting \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r)\) to the window: \[\max\bigl(r - \operatorname{len}[u],\; r - L_{\max}\bigr) \;\le\; i \;\le\; \min\bigl(r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d,\; r - L_{\min}\bigr)!\] Notice that inside \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\), all indices \(i\) share the same residue \(i \equiv (r - \operatorname{len}[u]) \pmod d\) and form a contiguous block of steps of \(+d\)!

  3. Algorithmic Compilation: Because each slink chain is an unbranched path where node \(u_k\) (at depth \(k\) above \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\)) pushes \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\) in strictly increasing order of \(i_{\text{new}}\) whenever visited, and at query time \(r\) we only want the minimum of \(dp[i] + C[i]\) over \(i \in \mathcal{I}(u, r) \cap [r - L_{\max}, r - L_{\min}]\):

    • Delayed Lower-Bound Activation (\(i_{\text{new}} > r - L_{\min}\)): Whenever \(i_{\text{new}} = r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d > r - L_{\min}\) (i.e., \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + d < L_{\min}\)), the newest (rightmost) endpoints in \(\mathcal{I}(u, r)\) have length \(< L_{\min}\) and are not yet valid at step \(r\), though they will become valid at a later step \(r + t \cdot d\) when their palindrome grows to length \(\ge L_{\min}\)!

    • Specifically, at step \(r\), the longest palindrome in \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) that has length \(\le L_{\max}\) has left endpoint \(i_{\text{low}} = \max(r - \operatorname{len}[u],\, r - \ell_{\max})\), and the shortest palindrome in \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) that has length \(\ge L_{\min}\) corresponds to the ancestor \(w \in (\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) visited at step \(r' = r - (\operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[w])\)!

    • Thus, on each constant-diff chain (identified by its bottom-most node \(\operatorname{slink}[u]\) and step \(d\), or per chain head), maintaining a monotonic queue (or a delayed insertion queue that inserts \(i_{\text{new}}\) once \(r - i_{\text{new}} \ge L_{\min}\) and pops \(i\) from the front once \(r - i > L_{\max}\)) answers each chain query in amortized \(O(1)\) time!

    • Complexity: \(O(N \log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) space.

  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Discarding \(i_{\text{new}}\) permanently at step \(r\) when \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + d < L_{\min}\) loses that left endpoint at step \(r + t \cdot d\) when the palindrome starting at \(i_{\text{new}}\) grows to length \(\operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] + (t+1)d \ge L_{\min}\)! It must be held in a pending queue and pushed into the active monotonic deque as soon as \(r_{\text{curr}} - i \ge L_{\min}\).
