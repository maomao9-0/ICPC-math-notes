  2. 1. Structural Anatomy of \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) & Blumer–Crochemore Reversal Duality

1.1 Diagnostic Signal: Why Black-Box Single-String SAM Breaks on Suffix-LCP & Multi-String Tasks

When competitive programmers treat the Suffix Automaton \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) as a black-box substring dictionary, two immediate structural mismatches appear:

  1. The Left-vs.-Right Extension Asymmetry (LCSuf vs. LCP): In \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\), walking forward along DAG transition edges \(\delta(u, c)\) appends a character on the right (\(w \to wc\)), whereas walking upward along suffix links \(\operatorname{link}(u)\) strips characters from the left (\(cw \to w\)). Consequently, the Lowest Common Ancestor (LCA) of two prefix states \(\operatorname{pos}_S[i]\) and \(\operatorname{pos}_S[j]\) on the Suffix Link Tree \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{SAM}(S))\) computes the Longest Common Suffix (LCSuf) of prefixes \(S[1..i]\) and \(S[1..j]\)—never the Longest Common Prefix (LCP) of suffixes \(S[i..N]\) and \(S[j..N]\)!

  2. The Multi-String Sentinel Explosion: Given a dictionary of strings \(\mathcal{S} = \{S_1, \dots, S_k\}\) with total length \(L = \sum_{i=1}^k |S_i|\) (or an explicit compressed Trie \(\mathcal{T}\) with \(V\) vertices), a naive folklore workaround concatenates them with distinct separators \(S_1 \#_1 S_2 \#_2 \cdots \#_{k-1} S_k\). This inflates the alphabet size from \(\sigma\) to \(\sigma + k - 1\) (blowing up static transition arrays from \(O(L\sigma)\) to \(\Theta(Lk)\) memory) and creates \(\Theta(L^2)\) artificial cross-sentinel substrings that pollute every state’s length interval \([\operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(u))+1, \operatorname{len}(u)]\).

To resolve both barriers without sentinels or ad-hoc patches, we begin from the exact combinatorial object that each state of \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) represents.

1.2 Equivalence Classes of \(\operatorname{endpos}\) and the Contiguous Length Interval

Fix a string \(S[1..N]\) (1-based closed indexing). For any nonempty substring \(w\) of \(S\), define its right-endpoint occurrence set \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(w)\) as the set of all 1-based ending indices where \(w\) appears in \(S\): \[\operatorname{endpos}_S(w) \;=\; \bigl\{\, r \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\} \;\big|\; S[r - |w| + 1 .. r] = w \,\bigr\}.\]

Two nonempty substrings \(u\) and \(v\) of \(S\) are called right-context equivalent (written \(u \equiv_S v\)) if and only if \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(u) = \operatorname{endpos}_S(v)\). Every state \(x > 1\) of \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) corresponds bijectively to one nonempty equivalence class \(E(x)\) under \(\equiv_S\), while the root state \(1\) represents the empty word \(\varepsilon\) with \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(\varepsilon) = \{0, 1, \dots, N\}\) and \(\operatorname{len}(1) = 0\).

ImportantLemma 9.1 (Laminar Nesting of \(\operatorname{endpos}\) & Contiguous Suffix-Length Intervals)

Let \(u\) and \(v\) be nonempty substrings of \(S[1..N]\) with \(|u| \le |v|\).

  1. Suffix Containment Characterization: If \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(u) \cap \operatorname{endpos}_S(v) \neq \emptyset\), then \(u\) is a suffix of \(v\) (\(u \sqsubseteq_{\text{suf}} v\)), and consequently \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(v) \subseteq \operatorname{endpos}_S(u)\). Conversely, if \(u \not\sqsubseteq_{\text{suf}} v\), then \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(u) \cap \operatorname{endpos}_S(v) = \emptyset\).
  2. Contiguous Length Interval per State: For every state \(x \in \operatorname{SAM}(S)\), let \(\operatorname{longest}(x)\) denote the unique longest string in \(E(x)\) of length \(\operatorname{len}(x) = |\operatorname{longest}(x)|\), and let \(\operatorname{shortest}(x)\) denote the unique shortest string in \(E(x)\) of length \(\operatorname{minlen}(x) = |\operatorname{shortest}(x)|\). Then:
    • \(E(x)\) consists of precisely the suffixes of \(\operatorname{longest}(x)\) whose lengths lie in the contiguous integer interval \([\operatorname{minlen}(x), \operatorname{len}(x)]\).
    • For every non-root state \(x > 1\), the longest proper suffix of \(\operatorname{longest}(x)\) whose \(\operatorname{endpos}_S\) set strictly contains \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(x)\) has length \(\operatorname{minlen}(x) - 1\) and is the longest string of the parent state \(\operatorname{link}(x)\). Thus: \[\boxed{\operatorname{minlen}(x) \;=\; \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(x)) + 1, \qquad |E(x)| \;=\; \operatorname{len}(x) - \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(x)).}\]
  3. Linear State and Transition Bounds: Because the family \(\{\operatorname{endpos}_S(x) \mid x \in \operatorname{SAM}(S)\}\) is laminar (any two sets are either disjoint or nested) and seeded by \(N\) singleton prefix endpoints \(\{1\}, \{2\}, \dots, \{N\}\), the Suffix Link Tree \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{SAM}(S))\) has at most \(2N - 1\) states (for \(N \ge 2\)) and the transition DAG has at most \(3N - 4\) directed edges.

Proof: For (1), suppose \(r \in \operatorname{endpos}_S(u) \cap \operatorname{endpos}_S(v)\). Then both \(u\) and \(v\) occur in \(S\) ending at index \(r\): \(u = S[r - |u| + 1 .. r]\) and \(v = S[r - |v| + 1 .. r]\). Since \(|u| \le |v|\), \(S[r - |u| + 1 .. r]\) is the length-\(|u|\) suffix of \(S[r - |v| + 1 .. r]\), so \(u \sqsubseteq_{\text{suf}} v\). Once \(u\) is a suffix of \(v\), any ending position \(r' \in \operatorname{endpos}_S(v)\) immediately places \(u\) at \(S[r' - |u| + 1 .. r']\), proving \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(v) \subseteq \operatorname{endpos}_S(u)\).

For (2), if \(w_1, w_2 \in E(x)\) have \(|w_1| \le |w_2|\), their shared nonempty \(\operatorname{endpos}_S\) set forces \(w_1 \sqsubseteq_{\text{suf}} w_2\) by (1). For any intermediate suffix \(u\) with \(w_1 \sqsubseteq_{\text{suf}} u \sqsubseteq_{\text{suf}} w_2\), suffix inclusion gives \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(w_2) \subseteq \operatorname{endpos}_S(u) \subseteq \operatorname{endpos}_S(w_1)\). Since \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(w_2) = \operatorname{endpos}_S(w_1)\), all three sets are equal, so \(u \in E(x)\). Taking \(w_2 = \operatorname{longest}(x)\) and \(w_1 = \operatorname{shortest}(x)\) shows that \(E(x)\) is the contiguous interval of suffixes of \(\operatorname{longest}(x)\) of lengths \([\operatorname{minlen}(x), \operatorname{len}(x)]\). Stripping one more character from the left of \(\operatorname{shortest}(x)\) produces a suffix \(z\) of length \(\operatorname{minlen}(x) - 1\) with \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(x) \subsetneq \operatorname{endpos}_S(z)\); no longer string can belong to \(z\)’s equivalence class without being a suffix of \(\operatorname{longest}(x)\) of length \(\ge \operatorname{minlen}(x)\) (which would force its \(\operatorname{endpos}_S\) set to equal \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(x)\)). Hence \(z = \operatorname{longest}(\operatorname{link}(x))\) and \(\operatorname{minlen}(x) = \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(x)) + 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

1.3 The Canonical Blumer–Crochemore Isomorphism: \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{SAM}(S)) \cong \operatorname{ST}(S^R)\)

Why does stripping characters from the left in \(S\) mirror a Suffix Tree? Consider the reversed word \(S^R = S[N]S[N-1]\cdots S[1]\). An occurrence of substring \(w\) ending at position \(r\) in \(S\) (\(S[r - |w| + 1 .. r] = w\)) is the exact mirror image of an occurrence of \(w^R\) starting at position \(l' = N - r + 1\) in \(S^R\) (\(S^R[l' .. l' + |w| - 1] = w^R\)).

ImportantTheorem 9.2 (Blumer–Crochemore Reversal Duality)

For any string \(S[1..N]\) and its reversal \(S^R[1..N]\), the Suffix Link Tree of \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) is canonically isomorphic to the compacted Suffix Tree (the compacted trie of all suffixes, without a terminal \(\$\) sentinel unless explicitly appended) of \(S^R\): \[\boxed{\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\bigl(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{ST}(S^R) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\bigl(\operatorname{SAM}(S^R)\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{ST}(S).}\]

Under the second isomorphism \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{SAM}(S^R)) \cong \operatorname{ST}(S)\), let \(v_i = \operatorname{pos}_{S^R}[N - i + 1]\) be the prefix state in \(\operatorname{SAM}(S^R)\) corresponding to the reversal of suffix \(S[i..N]\). Then for every pair of starting indices \(1 \le i, j \le N\): \[\boxed{\operatorname{LCP}\bigl(S[i..N],\, S[j..N]\bigr) \;=\; \operatorname{len}\!\Bigl(\operatorname{LCA}_{\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{SAM}(S^R))}(v_i, v_j)\Bigr).}\]

Proof: In the uncompacted Suffix Trie of \(S^R\), a node spelling \(w^R\) has a set of leaf descendants (and marked prefix nodes) corresponding to the starting positions \(\operatorname{startpos}_{S^R}(w^R)\) where \(w^R\) occurs as a prefix of a suffix of \(S^R\). Under the reflection \(l' = N - r + 1\), we have: \[\operatorname{startpos}_{S^R}(w^R) \;=\; \bigl\{\, N - r + 1 \;\big|\; r \in \operatorname{endpos}_S(w) \,\bigr\}.\] In the compacted Suffix Tree \(\operatorname{ST}(S^R)\), a node \(w^R\) is kept explicit if and only if right-extending \(w^R \to w^R c\) in \(S^R\) splits its occurrence set \(\operatorname{startpos}_{S^R}(w^R)\) (either because \(w^R\) has \(\ge 2\) outgoing character branches or because \(w^R\) itself is a full suffix of \(S^R\)). Under reversal, right-extending \(w^R \to w^R c\) in \(S^R\) is identical to left-extending \(w \to cw\) in \(S\). Therefore, the maximal unary chains compressed into a single edge of \(\operatorname{ST}(S^R)\) are precisely the equivalence classes \(E(u)\) of substrings sharing identical \(\operatorname{endpos}_S(u)\) in \(S\), and the explicit parent pointer in \(\operatorname{ST}(S^R)\) is \(\operatorname{link}(u)\)! \(\blacksquare\)

2. Core Combinatorial Word Invariants — Generalized SAM (广义 SAM): Online Reset vs. Offline Trie-BFS & The Zero-Length Ghost Clone Bug

Now suppose we are given a family of strings \(\mathcal{S} = \{S_1, S_2, \dots, S_k\}\) (or a Trie \(\mathcal{T}\) whose root-to-node paths are the prefixes of \(\mathcal{S}\)). We want a single minimal automaton \(\operatorname{GSAM}(\mathcal{S})\) whose states partition all substrings occurring in \(\bigcup_{i=1}^k S_i\) by their multi-string right-endpoint occurrence tuples \(\bigl(\operatorname{endpos}_{S_1}(w), \dots, \operatorname{endpos}_{S_k}(w)\bigr)\).

2.1 Why Naive last = 1 Reset + Single-String extend(c) Corrupts the Automaton

A widespread competitive programming implementation resets last = 1 at the start of each string \(S_i\) and calls the unguarded single-string extend(c) routine. Why does this fail?

In a single string \(S\), every call to extend(c) appends a character to the entire prefix read so far, creating a strictly longer prefix of length \(\operatorname{len}(\text{last}) + 1\) that has never appeared in the automaton before. Therefore, \(\delta(\text{last}, c)\) is guaranteed to be \(0\).

When we reset last = 1 to insert a second string \(S_2\) (or step down another branch of a Trie from state \(p\)), the transition \(q = \delta(p, c)\) may already be non-zero! Calling single-string extend(c) blindly allocates a new state cur with \(\operatorname{len}(\text{cur}) = \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) even when a state representing \(\operatorname{longest}(p) + c\) already exists.

WarningCounterexample 9.3 (Explicit Trace of the Zero-Length Ghost Clone Bug on \(\mathcal{S} = {\texttt{ab}, \texttt{b}}\) — BP-09)

Let \(\mathcal{S} = \{S_1 = \texttt{ab},\; S_2 = \texttt{b}\}\). Trace the automaton state table under the naive last = 1 reset vs. the 3-case Generalized SAM:

  1. After inserting \(S_1 = \texttt{ab}\) from root \(1\):
    • State \(1\) (\(\varepsilon\)): \(\operatorname{len}(1) = 0, \operatorname{link}(1) = 0, \delta(1, \texttt{a}) = 2, \delta(1, \texttt{b}) = 3\).
    • State \(2\) (\(\{\texttt{a}\}\)): \(\operatorname{len}(2) = 1, \operatorname{link}(2) = 1, \delta(2, \texttt{b}) = 3\).
    • State \(3\) (\(\{\texttt{b}, \texttt{ab}\}\)): \(\operatorname{len}(3) = 2, \operatorname{link}(3) = 1\).
  2. Now reset last = 1 and insert \(S_2[1] = \texttt{b}\) from \(p = 1\):
    • Broken Single-String extend('b') Execution:
      • Allocates cur = 4 with \(\operatorname{len}(4) = \operatorname{len}(1) + 1 = 1\).
      • Enters while (p && !ch[p]['b']): since \(\delta(1, \texttt{b}) = 3 \neq 0\), the loop terminates in \(0\) iterations with \(p = 1\) and \(q = \delta(1, \texttt{b}) = 3\).
      • Checks \(\operatorname{len}(q) == \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\): since \(\operatorname{len}(3) = 2 > 1\), it splits state \(3\) by allocating clone = 5 with \(\operatorname{len}(5) = \operatorname{len}(1) + 1 = 1\), sets \(\operatorname{link}(5) = \operatorname{link}(3) = 1\), redirects \(\delta(1, \texttt{b}) \leftarrow 5\), and sets \(\operatorname{link}(3) = \operatorname{link}(4) = 5\).
      • Returns last = cur = 4, so the caller marks \(\operatorname{cnt}_{S_2}[4] \mathrel{+}= 1\).
    • Why State \(4\) Is a Fatal Ghost State:
      • Zero Length Span: \(\operatorname{len}(4) - \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(4)) = \operatorname{len}(4) - \operatorname{len}(5) = 1 - 1 = 0\). Its length interval is \([\operatorname{len}(5)+1, \operatorname{len}(4)] = [2, 1] = \emptyset\)!
      • DAG Unreachability vs. Link-Tree Theft: In the transition DAG, \(\delta(1, \texttt{b}) = 5\), while no transition in the entire DAG points to state \(4\) (\(\operatorname{in-deg}_{\text{DAG}}(4) = 0\))! Yet \(S_2\)’s prefix marker was placed on state \(4\) instead of state \(5\). Any algorithm that walks \(S_2\) on the DAG visits state \(5\) (where \(\operatorname{cnt}_{S_2}\) was not directly marked), while any algorithm that queries state \(4\) sees a phantom node representing zero strings!

2.2 The 3-Case Generalized SAM Extension Invariant (extend_gsam)

When extending an existing GSAM state \(p\) by character \(c\), we must inspect \(q = \delta(p, c)\) before allocating any node:

  1. Case 1 (\(\delta(p, c) = 0\) — Transition Absent): No substring in the current automaton equals \(\operatorname{longest}(p) + c\). Execute the standard single-string SAM extension: allocate cur with \(\operatorname{len}(\text{cur}) = \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\), walk suffix links while \(\delta(p', c) = 0\) setting \(\delta(p', c) \leftarrow \text{cur}\), and link cur to \(1\), \(q\), or a newly split clone. Return cur.

  2. Case 2 (\(\delta(p, c) = q \neq 0\) and \(\operatorname{len}(q) = \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) — Exact State Match): State \(q\) already has \(\operatorname{longest}(q) = \operatorname{longest}(p) + c\). Allocate nothing and return \(q\) directly!

  3. Case 3 (\(\delta(p, c) = q \neq 0\) and \(\operatorname{len}(q) > \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) — Oversized State Split): The string \(\operatorname{longest}(p) + c\) of length \(\operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) is currently bundled inside state \(q\) alongside strictly longer strings (such as \(\texttt{ab}\) inside state \(3\) of Counterexample 9.3). Because the new prefix of length \(\operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) ends here without those longer left-extensions, we must split \(q\) at threshold \(\operatorname{len}(p) + 1\):

    • Allocate only ONE state clone (do not allocate cur!).
    • Set \(\operatorname{len}(\text{clone}) = \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\), copy \(\delta(\text{clone}, \cdot) \leftarrow \delta(q, \cdot)\) and \(\operatorname{link}(\text{clone}) \leftarrow \operatorname{link}(q)\).
    • Walk \(p' \leftarrow p, \operatorname{link}(p), \dots\) while \(\delta(p', c) = q\) and redirect \(\delta(p', c) \leftarrow \text{clone}\).
    • Set \(\operatorname{link}(q) \leftarrow \text{clone}\) and return clone directly as the state representing \(\operatorname{longest}(p) + c\)!

On \(\mathcal{S} = \{\texttt{ab}, \texttt{b}\}\), calling extend_gsam(1, 'b') enters Case 3 (\(\delta(1, \texttt{b}) = 3\) with \(\operatorname{len}(3) = 2 > 1\)), allocates only clone = 4 (\(\operatorname{len}(4) = 1, \operatorname{link}(4) = 1\)), sets \(\delta(1, \texttt{b}) = 4\) and \(\operatorname{link}(3) = 4\), and returns \(4\). Every non-root state \(u > 1\) strictly satisfies \(\operatorname{len}(u) > \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(u))\).

3. Why Trie-DFS Degrades to \(\Theta(V^2)\) on Binary Alphabets vs. Strict \(O(V\sigma)\) for Trie-BFS

Suppose the input is given either as:

  • Regime I (Explicit String List): \(\mathcal{S} = \{S_1, \dots, S_k\}\) with total character length \(L = \sum_{i=1}^k |S_i|\), or
  • Regime II (Compressed Input Trie): A rooted Trie \(\mathcal{T}\) with \(V\) vertices and edge labels in \(\Sigma\), where the sum of leaf depths \(\sum_{v \in \mathcal{T}} \operatorname{depth}(v)\) can be \(\Theta(V^2)\)!

A classic trap in Regime II is running a Depth-First Search (DFS) over the Trie \(\mathcal{T}\) while calling the 3-case extend_gsam(sam_node[parent], c). Even with the 3-case fix, Trie-DFS degrades to \(\Theta(V^2)\) time and work on a binary alphabet \(\Sigma = \{\texttt{a}, \texttt{b}\}\)!

ImportantTheorem 9.4 (Exact Complexity Dichotomy: Online String List \(O(L\sigma)\) vs. Trie-DFS \(\Theta(V^2)\) vs. Trie-BFS \(O(V\sigma)\))
  1. Online String List (\(L = \sum_{i=1}^k |S_i|\)): Resetting last = 1 at the start of each string \(S_i\) and calling the 3-case last = extend_gsam(last, c) constructs \(\operatorname{GSAM}(\mathcal{S})\) in \(O(L\sigma)\) time and \(\le 2L\) states.
  2. Compressed Trie-DFS Lower Bound (\(\Theta(V^2)\) on \(\Sigma = \{\texttt{a}, \texttt{b}\}\)): There exists a binary-alphabet Trie \(\mathcal{T}_m\) with \(V = 2m + 1\) vertices representing \(\mathcal{S}_m = \{\texttt{a}^k\texttt{b} \mid 1 \le k \le m\}\) on which DFS calling extend_gsam performs \(\frac{m(m+1)}{2} - 1 = \Theta(V^2)\) suffix-link redirections.
  3. Compressed Trie-BFS Theorem (\(O(V\sigma)\) Worst-Case Time): Traversing any Trie \(\mathcal{T}\) of \(V\) vertices in Breadth-First Search (BFS) order (non-decreasing Trie depth) constructs \(\operatorname{GSAM}(\mathcal{T})\) in strict \(O(V\sigma)\) time and \(\le 2V\) states, and never triggers Case 2 or Case 3 at the entry of extend_gsam.

Proof: Part 1 (Online String List \(O(L\sigma)\)): Along a single string \(S_i\) of length \(|S_i|\), let \(p_t\) be the state last after reading \(S_i[1..t]\) (so \(\operatorname{len}(p_t) = t\)). Consider the potential function \(\Phi(t) = \operatorname{minlen}(p_t) = \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(p_t)) + 1 \ge 1\). When extending \(p_{t-1}\) by \(c = S_i[t]\):

  • Every suffix-link step \(p' \leftarrow \operatorname{link}(p')\) in the while loops of Case 1 or Case 3 strictly decreases \(\operatorname{len}(p')\). If the loop takes \(h \ge 1\) steps and stops at \(p^*\), then the new state \(p_t\) (either cur or clone) has \(\operatorname{link}(p_t)\) at length \(\le \operatorname{len}(p^*) + 1\), whereas \(\operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(p_{t-1}))\) was walked through \(h - 1\) strict link steps before reaching \(p^*\). Thus \(\Phi(t) \le \Phi(t-1) - h + 2\), so \(h \le \Phi(t-1) - \Phi(t) + 2\).
  • Summing over \(t = 1, \dots, |S_i|\) telescopes to \(\sum_{t=1}^{|S_i|} h_t \le 2|S_i|\), and summing over all \(i = 1, \dots, k\) yields \(\le 2\sum |S_i| = 2L\) link steps!

Part 2 (Why Trie-DFS Explodes to \(\Theta(V^2)\) on \(\mathcal{T}_m = \{\texttt{a}^k\texttt{b} \mid 1 \le k \le m\}\)): In the compressed Trie \(\mathcal{T}_m\), all \(m\) strings share a single spine of \(m\) 'a'-edges (\(v_0 \xrightarrow{\texttt{a}} v_1 \xrightarrow{\texttt{a}} \cdots \xrightarrow{\texttt{a}} v_m\)), and each spine vertex \(v_k\) (\(1 \le k \le m\)) has a single leaf child \(u_k\) via edge \(v_k \xrightarrow{\texttt{b}} u_k\). Thus \(\mathcal{T}_m\) has \(V = 2m + 1\) vertices (even though \(\sum_{k=1}^m |\texttt{a}^k\texttt{b}| = \Theta(m^2)\)!). Suppose alphabetical DFS always visits child 'a' before child 'b':

  1. DFS first plunges all the way down the 'a'-spine to \(v_m\), creating SAM states \(s_k = \operatorname{state}(\texttt{a}^k)\) with \(\operatorname{len}(s_k) = k\) and \(\operatorname{link}(s_k) = s_{k-1}\) for \(1 \le k \le m\) (\(s_0 = 1\)).

  2. At the bottom of the spine, DFS visits the leaf \(v_m \xrightarrow{\texttt{b}} u_m\) (spelling \(\texttt{a}^m\texttt{b}\)). Since no 'b'-transition exists yet, Case 1 walks the entire suffix-link chain \(s_m \to s_{m-1} \to \dots \to s_0\), setting: \[\delta(s_j, \texttt{b}) \;=\; q_m \quad (\text{where } \operatorname{len}(q_m) = m + 1) \qquad \forall j \in \{0, 1, \dots, m\}.\]

  3. Next, DFS backtracks to \(v_{m-1}\) and visits leaf \(v_{m-1} \xrightarrow{\texttt{b}} u_{m-1}\) from \(p = s_{m-1}\). It inspects \(\delta(s_{m-1}, \texttt{b}) = q_m\), which is already non-zero with \(\operatorname{len}(q_m) = m + 1 > \operatorname{len}(s_{m-1}) + 1 = m\)! Thus Case 3 triggers, allocating \(\text{clone}_{m-1}\) of length \(m\) and executing while (p && ch[p]['b'] == q_m) { ch[p]['b'] = clone_{m-1}; p = link[p]; } across all \(m\) states \(s_{m-1}, s_{m-2}, \dots, s_0\)!

  4. Next, DFS backtracks to \(v_{m-2}\) and visits \(v_{m-2} \xrightarrow{\texttt{b}} u_{m-2}\) from \(p = s_{m-2}\). Now \(\delta(s_{m-2}, \texttt{b}) = \text{clone}_{m-1}\) has length \(m > m - 1\), triggering Case 3 again and redirecting \(s_{m-2}, s_{m-3}, \dots, s_0\) (\(m - 1\) steps)!

  5. Continuing down to \(k = 1\), every single backtrack step \(k \in \{m-1, m-2, \dots, 1\}\) triggers Case 3 and walks all \(k + 1\) ancestors \(s_k, s_{k-1}, \dots, s_0\): \[\text{Total Case-3 Redirections} \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{m-1} (k + 1) \;=\; \frac{m(m+1)}{2} - 1 \;=\; \Theta(m^2) \;=\; \Theta(V^2)!\] (Why didn’t the potential \(\Phi\) save DFS? Because whenever DFS backtracks from a deep branch of depth \(m\) to a shallow branch of depth \(k\), it resets the active state without paying for the \(\Theta(m)\) drop in potential!)

Part 3 (Why Trie-BFS Runs in Strict \(O(V\sigma)\) Time): In BFS over \(\mathcal{T}\), vertices are popped from the queue in non-decreasing order of Trie depth \(d = \operatorname{depth}(x) = \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{sam}(x))\).

  • First, if \(\mathcal{T}\) is a standard deterministic Trie (where each vertex \(x\) has at most one outgoing edge per character \(c \in \Sigma\)), when BFS processes edge \(x \xrightarrow{c} y\) at depth \(d + 1\), no state in the GSAM has \(\operatorname{len} > d + 1\) yet. Could \(\delta(\operatorname{sam}(x), c)\) already be non-zero? If \(\delta(\operatorname{sam}(x), c) = q \neq 0\), then since the maximum state length in the GSAM before layer \(d+1\) is at most \(d+1\) and \(\operatorname{len}(q) \ge \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{sam}(x)) + 1 = d + 1\), we must have \(\operatorname{len}(q) = d + 1\) (Case 2; Case 3 \(\operatorname{len}(q) > d + 1\) is impossible under BFS!).
  • Second, why is the total number of suffix-link steps in the while loops bounded by \(O(V)\) across the entire BFS? Define for each GSAM state \(u\) its longest non-empty transition depth \(M(u) = \max\bigl(\{0\} \cup \{\operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}(u)) + 1\} \cup \{\operatorname{len}(u) \mid \exists c, \delta(u, c) \neq 0\}\bigr)\). More simply, in the first loop while (p && !ch[p][c]), each step permanently fills an empty transition slot ch[p][c] = cur (at most \(V_{\text{GSAM}} \cdot \sigma \le 2V\sigma\) times total!). In the clone redirection loop while (p && ch[p][c] == q) ch[p][c] = clone, because BFS processes nodes in non-decreasing order of \(d = \operatorname{len}(\text{cur})\), any state \(q\) being split at step \(d+1\) has \(\operatorname{len}(q) \le d + 1\). Thus for every redirected state \(p'\), its target length \(\operatorname{len}(\delta(p', c))\) is set to \(\operatorname{len}(\text{clone}) = \operatorname{len}(p_0) + 1 \le \operatorname{len}(q)\), and crucially \(\operatorname{link}(q)\) increases from \(\operatorname{link}_{\text{old}}(q)\) (length \(< \operatorname{len}(p_0) + 1\)) to \(\text{clone}\) (length \(= \operatorname{len}(p_0) + 1\)). Specifically, every step of the clone loop visits an ancestor \(p'\) of the previous node whose \(\operatorname{len}(p') \ge \operatorname{len}(\operatorname{link}_{\text{old}}(q))\), advancing the pointer along the suffix-link chain by at most \(2\) amortised steps per BFS transition (exactly as in Blumer et al.’s DAWG potential proof when processed in non-decreasing length order!). Hence Trie-BFS runs in strict \(O(V\sigma)\) time and \(\le 2V\) states. \(\blacksquare\)

4. Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad, Algorithmic Contracts & Multi-String Aggregation Patterns

4.1 Contract A: The 3-Case extend_gsam & Offline Trie-BFS Engine

When building a Generalized SAM either from an explicit Trie \(\mathcal{T}\) (via BFS) or from a list of strings \(\mathcal{S} = \{S_1, \dots, S_k\}\) (online with last = 1), the 3-case extend_gsam(p, c) routine is the single unified primitive:

// Non-standard 3-Case Generalized SAM Extension + Trie-BFS Driver
// State 1 is the root (len[1] = 0, link[1] = 0). Total states <= 2 * V.
int extend_gsam(int p, int c) {
    if (ch[p][c]) {
        int q = ch[p][c];
        if (len[q] == len[p] + 1) return q; // Case 2: Exact length match
        int clone = ++tot;                  // Case 3: Split q -> clone WITHOUT cur
        len[clone] = len[p] + 1;
        memcpy(ch[clone], ch[q], sizeof(ch[q]));
        link[clone] = link[q];
        while (p && ch[p][c] == q) {
            ch[p][c] = clone;
            p = link[p];
        }
        link[q] = clone;
        return clone;
    }
    int cur = ++tot;                        // Case 1: Standard SAM extension
    len[cur] = len[p] + 1;
    while (p && !ch[p][c]) {
        ch[p][c] = cur;
        p = link[p];
    }
    if (!p) {
        link[cur] = 1;
    } else {
        int q = ch[p][c];
        if (len[q] == len[p] + 1) {
            link[cur] = q;
        } else {
            int clone = ++tot;
            len[clone] = len[p] + 1;
            memcpy(ch[clone], ch[q], sizeof(ch[q]));
            link[clone] = link[q];
            while (p && ch[p][c] == q) {
                ch[p][c] = clone;
                p = link[p];
            }
            link[q] = link[cur] = clone;
        }
    }
    return cur;
}

// Offline Trie-BFS Construction in strict O(V * sigma) time:
void build_gsam_from_trie() {
    queue<int> q;
    sam_pos[1] = 1; // Trie root 1 maps to GSAM root 1
    q.push(1);
    while (!q.empty()) {
        int u = q.front();
        q.pop();
        for (int c = 0; c < SIGMA; ++c) {
            int v = trie_ch[u][c];
            if (v) {
                sam_pos[v] = extend_gsam(sam_pos[u], c);
                q.push(v);
            }
        }
    }
}

4.2 Three Canonical Multi-String Aggregation Patterns on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{GSAM})\)

Once \(\operatorname{GSAM}(\mathcal{S})\) is constructed for \(\mathcal{S} = \{S_1, \dots, S_k\}\) (\(L = \sum |S_i|\)), how do we propagate multi-string occurrence information up the Link Tree \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\)?

  1. Pattern I — Small Fixed \(k\) (\(k \le 10\), e.g., CF 452E, CF 316G3): Maintain an explicit array \(\operatorname{cnt}[u][i]\) (\(1 \le i \le k\)) at every GSAM state \(u\). As each prefix \(S_i[1..r]\) lands at GSAM state \(u_{i,r}\), increment \(\operatorname{cnt}[u_{i,r}][i] \mathrel{+}= 1\). Bucket-sort all GSAM states by \(\operatorname{len}(u)\) in \(O(L)\) time and push counts bottom-up in decreasing order of \(\operatorname{len}(u)\): \[\operatorname{cnt}[\operatorname{link}(u)][i] \;\mathrel{+}=\; \operatorname{cnt}[u][i] \qquad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, k\}.\]

  2. Pattern II — Exclusive Ownership / At-Most-One-String Check ([USACO 2017 Dec Plat] Standing Out): For large \(k \le 10^5\), we want to know whether state \(u\)’s subtree in \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\) contains prefixes from only one string \(S_i\), or from \(\ge 2\) distinct strings. Maintain a single integer \(\operatorname{owner}[u] \in \{0, 1, \dots, k, -1\}\): \[\operatorname{merge\_owner}(a, b) = \begin{cases} a \text{ (or } b\text{)} & \text{if } b = 0 \text{ (or } a = 0\text{) or } a = b, \\ -1 & \text{otherwise (collision of } \ge 2 \text{ distinct strings).} \end{cases}\] Propagating \(\operatorname{owner}[\operatorname{link}(u)] \leftarrow \operatorname{merge\_owner}(\operatorname{owner}[\operatorname{link}(u)], \operatorname{owner}[u])\) takes \(O(1)\) time and space per state!

  3. Pattern III — Exact Number of Distinct Strings in Subtree (CF 204E — DFS-Order LCA Subtraction): Suppose \(k \le 10^5\) and we need \(\operatorname{distinct}[u] =\) the exact number of distinct strings \(S_i\) that appear in \(u\)’s subtree on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\). For a fixed string \(S_i\), let \(v_1, v_2, \dots, v_m\) be the GSAM states visited by the prefixes of \(S_i\), sorted by their Link-Tree DFS entry time \(\operatorname{dfn}[v_1] \le \operatorname{dfn}[v_2] \le \dots \le \operatorname{dfn}[v_m]\) (and deduplicated). By the standard Virtual Tree / Tree-Union identity:

    • Adding \(+1\) to \(\operatorname{diff}[v_t]\) for each \(1 \le t \le m\), and
    • Subtracting \(-1\) from \(\operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{LCA}_{\text{link}}(v_{t-1}, v_t)]\) for each \(2 \le t \le m\) ensures that the Link-Tree subtree sum \(\operatorname{distinct}[u] = \sum_{w \in \operatorname{subtree}(u)} \operatorname{diff}[w]\) counts string \(S_i\) at most once (and exactly once iff \(\ge 1\) node \(v_t\) lies in \(\operatorname{subtree}(u)\))! Summing over all \(S_i\) computes \(\operatorname{distinct}[u]\) for every state in \(O(L \log L)\) (or \(O(L)\) with \(O(1)\) RMQ LCA) time and \(O(L)\) space.

Problem 9.1 (W2A-05) — CF 204E — Little Elephant and Strings

  • Source & Constraints: Codeforces Round 129 Div. 1 E. Given \(n\) strings \(S_1, S_2, \dots, S_n\) over \(\Sigma = \{\texttt{a}, \dots, \texttt{z}\}\) with total length \(L = \sum_{i=1}^n |S_i| \le 10^5\) and a threshold integer \(1 \le k \le n\). For each string \(S_i\) (\(1 \le i \le n\)), compute how many of its \(\binom{|S_i|+1}{2}\) substrings \(S_i[l..r]\) (\(1 \le l \le r \le |S_i|\)) appear as a substring in at least \(k\) distinct strings among \(S_1, \dots, S_n\). Time limit: \(2.0\text{s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & What to Count per GSAM State

If two substrings \(w_1, w_2 \in E(u)\) belong to the same state \(u\) of \(\operatorname{GSAM}(S_1, \dots, S_n)\), they occur in the exact same subset of input strings! Let \(\operatorname{distinct}[u]\) be the number of distinct strings \(S_j\) (\(1 \le j \le n\)) that have at least one prefix in the Link-Tree subtree of \(u\). Why does every ancestor of a valid state \(u\) (\(\operatorname{distinct}[u] \ge k\)) also satisfy \(\operatorname{distinct}[\operatorname{link}(u)] \ge k\)?

NoteHint 2 — Computing \(\operatorname{distinct}[u]\) in \(O(L \log L)\) Time Without Bitsets

Since \(n\) can be up to \(10^5\), maintaining an \(n\)-bit mask per state takes \(\Theta(n^2)\) memory. Instead, for each string \(S_i\), collect the GSAM states \(v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{|S_i|}\) visited by its prefixes, sort them by their Link-Tree DFS entry time \(\operatorname{dfn}[v_t]\), and deduplicate identical states. How can you use \(\operatorname{LCA}_{\text{link}}(v_{t-1}, v_t)\) so that a bottom-up subtree sum counts string \(S_i\) at most once in every state \(u\)?

NoteHint 3 — Answering for Each Prefix \(S_i[1..r]\) in \(O(1)\)

Fix a string \(S_i\) and a right endpoint \(r \in [1, |S_i|]\), which lands at GSAM state \(u_{i,r}\) (with \(\operatorname{len}(u_{i,r}) \ge r\)). All suffixes of \(S_i[1..r]\) lie along the root-to-\(u_{i,r}\) path in \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\). Because \(\operatorname{distinct}[w]\) is monotonically non-increasing as \(w\) moves down from the root, the valid suffixes of \(S_i[1..r]\) are all suffixes of length \(\le \min\bigl(r, \operatorname{len}(u^*)\bigr)\), where \(u^*\) is the deepest ancestor of \(u_{i,r}\) satisfying \(\operatorname{distinct}[u^*] \ge k\)!

TipFull Mathematical Derivation & Algorithmic Contract

4-Step Zero-Magic Derivation:

  1. Diagnostic Signal: We must count, for every prefix \(S_i[1..r]\), how many of its \(r\) nonempty suffixes occur in \(\ge k\) distinct strings among \(\{S_1, \dots, S_n\}\).

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant: Build \(\operatorname{GSAM}(S_1, \dots, S_n)\) using the 3-case extend_gsam routine (Contract A), recording the state \(u_{i,r}\) reached after prefix \(S_i[1..r]\). For any state \(u \in \operatorname{GSAM}\), all strings \(w \in E(u)\) occur in string \(S_j\) if and only if at least one prefix state \(u_{j,r'}\) (\(1 \le r' \le |S_j|\)) lies in the Link-Tree subtree of \(u\). By Pattern III (§4.2), sorting the visited states of \(S_j\) in Link-Tree DFS order \(v_1, \dots, v_m\) (\(\operatorname{dfn}[v_1] < \dots < \operatorname{dfn}[v_m]\)) and updating: \[\operatorname{diff}[v_t] \mathrel{+}= 1 \quad (1 \le t \le m), \qquad \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{LCA}_{\text{link}}(v_{t-1}, v_t)] \mathrel{-}= 1 \quad (2 \le t \le m)\] guarantees that the subtree sum \(\operatorname{distinct}[u] = \sum_{w \in \operatorname{subtree}(u)} \operatorname{diff}[w]\) equals the exact number of distinct strings containing \(u\).

  3. Algorithmic Compilation (Top-Down Valid Ancestor Propagation): Because \(\operatorname{subtree}(u) \subseteq \operatorname{subtree}(\operatorname{link}(u))\), we have \(\operatorname{distinct}[u] \le \operatorname{distinct}[\operatorname{link}(u)]\). In a single top-down pass on \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\), compute for each state \(u\): \[\operatorname{best\_len}[u] \;=\; \begin{cases} \operatorname{len}(u) & \text{if } \operatorname{distinct}[u] \ge k, \\ \operatorname{best\_len}[\operatorname{link}(u)] & \text{if } \operatorname{distinct}[u] < k \quad (\text{with } \operatorname{best\_len}[1] = 0). \end{cases}\] Then for string \(S_i\) and right endpoint \(r\), the suffixes of \(S_i[1..r]\) occurring in \(\ge k\) strings are precisely those of lengths \(1, 2, \dots, \min(r, \operatorname{best\_len}[u_{i,r}])\). (In fact, since \(S_i[1..r]\) itself has length \(r\) and belongs to \(u_{i,r}\) or a descendant, if \(\operatorname{best\_len}[u_{i,r}]\) comes from a proper ancestor \(u^* \neq u_{i,r}\), then \(\operatorname{len}(u^*) < \operatorname{minlen}(u_{i,r}) \le r\); if \(u^* = u_{i,r}\), then all \(r\) suffixes of \(S_i[1..r]\) are valid, so \(\min(r, \operatorname{best\_len}[u_{i,r}])\) is exact in all cases!) Boundary Pitfall: Using naive single-string extend after last = 1: when a ghost clone is created with \(\operatorname{len}(\text{cur}) = \operatorname{len}(p) + 1\) and \(\operatorname{link}(\text{cur}) = \text{clone}\), if \(W(\text{cur}) > 0\) and \(W(q) < 0\), evaluating \(\operatorname{len}(\text{cur}) \cdot W(\text{cur})\) evaluates an unmerged partial sum on a non-existent equivalence class and outputs a strictly false positive maximum!

Problem 9.8 (W2A-31 / W2A-13) — [CTSC2012] 熟悉的文章 (Familiar Article) (Luogu P4022 / LOJ 2255)

  • Source & Constraints: CTSC 2012. Given a dictionary of \(m\) binary strings \(D_1, \dots, D_m \in \{\texttt{0}, \texttt{1}\}^+\) and \(Q\) query essays \(T_1, \dots, T_Q \in \{\texttt{0}, \texttt{1}\}^+\) with \(\sum |D_i| \le 1.1 \times 10^6\) and \(\sum |T_q| \le 1.1 \times 10^6\). For an essay \(T\) of length \(N = |T|\) and an integer threshold \(L_0 \ge 1\), a familiar partition selects a set of pairwise disjoint substrings of \(T\), each of length \(\ge L_0\) and each occurring as a substring in at least one dictionary string \(D_j\), such that the sum of their lengths is at least \(\lceil 0.9 N \rceil\) (i.e., \(10 \times \text{covered} \ge 9 N\)). Find the maximum integer \(L_0\) for which \(T\) admits a familiar partition (or \(0\) if none exists). Time limit: \(1.5\text{s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Precomputing the Longest Dictionary Suffix \(\operatorname{lim}[i]\) in \(O(N)\)

Build the 3-case Generalized SAM over the dictionary \(\mathcal{D} = \{D_1, \dots, D_m\}\). Walk the query essay \(T[1..N]\) on \(\operatorname{GSAM}(\mathcal{D})\) to compute, for every \(1 \le i \le N\), the maximum length \(\operatorname{lim}[i]\) of a suffix of \(T[1..i]\) that occurs in \(\mathcal{D}\). Why is this walk done once per query \(T\) outside the binary search on \(L_0\)?

NoteHint 2 — The Monotonic Left-Boundary Invariant \(i - \operatorname{lim}[i]\)

When we extend \(T[1..i-1]\) by one character \(T[i]\), the longest dictionary suffix can grow by at most \(+1\): \(\operatorname{lim}[i] \le \operatorname{lim}[i-1] + 1\). Rearranging this inequality gives: \[i - \operatorname{lim}[i] \;\ge\; (i - 1) - \operatorname{lim}[i-1]!\] Why does the monotonicity of the left boundary \(i - \operatorname{lim}[i]\) allow us to check a candidate \(L_0\) in \(O(N)\) time?

NoteHint 3 — Sliding Monotonic Deque on \(dp[j] - j\)

For a fixed \(L_0\), let \(dp[i]\) be the maximum covered characters in \(T[1..i]\). Choosing a valid dictionary block \(T[j+1..i]\) requires its length \(i - j\) to satisfy \(L_0 \le i - j \le \operatorname{lim}[i]\), i.e., \(j \in [i - \operatorname{lim}[i],\, i - L_0]\). Rewrite the transition as: \[dp[i] \;=\; \max\!\Bigl(\,dp[i-1],\;\; i + \max_{j \in [i - \operatorname{lim}[i],\, i - L_0]} \bigl(dp[j] - j\bigr)\Bigr).\] Since both interval endpoints \(i - \operatorname{lim}[i]\) and \(i - L_0\) are monotonically non-decreasing in \(i\), a sliding monotonic deque maintains the maximum in amortized \(O(1)\) per index!

TipFull Mathematical Derivation & Algorithmic Contract

4-Step Zero-Magic Derivation:

  1. Diagnostic Signal: Binary search on threshold \(L_0\) coupled with an \(O(N)\) interval-covering DP driven by GSAM suffix-match lengths.

  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (Left-Endpoint Monotonicity): Build \(\operatorname{GSAM}(D_1, \dots, D_m)\) in \(O(\sum |D_i|)\) time. For a query \(T[1..N]\), walk \(T\) on the GSAM to obtain \(\operatorname{lim}[i]\) for \(1 \le i \le N\). Because stripping the last character from a valid suffix of \(T[1..i]\) of length \(\operatorname{lim}[i]\) yields a valid dictionary suffix of \(T[1..i-1]\) of length \(\operatorname{lim}[i] - 1\), we have \(\operatorname{lim}[i] - 1 \le \operatorname{lim}[i-1]\), which is equivalent to: \[\boxed{L_{\text{left}}(i) \;\triangleq\; i - \operatorname{lim}[i] \quad \text{is monotonically non-decreasing in } i!}\]

  3. Algorithmic Compilation: Binary search \(L_0 \in [1, N]\). To test \(L_0\):

    • Maintain a monotonic decreasing deque of indices \(j\) ordered by \(g(j) = dp[j] - j\).
    • As \(i\) goes from \(L_0\) to \(N\):
      1. Push \(j = i - L_0\) into the back of the deque (popping indices \(j'\) from the back with \(g(j') \le g(i - L_0)\)).
      2. Pop indices \(j'\) from the front of the deque with \(j' < i - \operatorname{lim}[i]\).
      3. Set \(dp[i] = dp[i-1]\); if the deque is non-empty, set \(dp[i] = \max\bigl(dp[i],\, i + g(\text{dq.front()})\bigr)\).
    • \(L_0\) is feasible iff \(10 \cdot dp[N] \ge 9 N\). Total time: \(O\bigl(\sum |D_i| + \sum |T_q| \log |T_q|\bigr)\).

  4. Boundary Pitfall: Using a Segment Tree inside the binary search (\(O(N \log^2 N)\) TLE), or pushing \(j = i - L_0\) only when \(i - L_0 \ge i - \operatorname{lim}[i]\) (note that \(j = i - L_0\) might be \(< i - \operatorname{lim}[i]\) at step \(i\), yet become \(\ge i' - \operatorname{lim}[i']\) at a later step \(i' > i\); always push \(j = i - L_0\) unconditionally and let the front pop filter \(j' < i - \operatorname{lim}[i]\)!).
