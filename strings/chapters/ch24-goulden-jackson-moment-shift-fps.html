1. The Goulden–Jackson Cluster Method & Autocorrelation Systems (T-4A-01)

1.1 The Diagnostic Signal: Why Aho–Corasick Matrix Power and Naive PIE Fail

Given an alphabet \(\Sigma\) of size \(\sigma = |\Sigma|\) and a finite pattern dictionary \(\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, P_2, \dots, P_m\} \subset \Sigma^+\), we wish to count the number \(a_n\) of length-\(n\) words \(W \in \Sigma^n\) that avoid all patterns in \(\mathcal{P}\) as contiguous substrings, packaged into the ordinary generating function (OGF) \[F(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} a_n x^n \in \mathcal{R}[[x]].\]

Two textbook approaches hit a hard complexity wall when pattern lengths \(|P_i|\) are large:

  1. Why Aho–Corasick Automaton DP / Matrix Exponentiation Fails: The Aho–Corasick automaton on \(\mathcal{P}\) has \(V = \Theta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^m |P_i|\bigr)\) prefix states. When \(\sum_{i=1}^m |P_i| \approx 10^5\) and \(n \le 10^5\) (or \(n \le 10^{18}\)), neither \(O(n V)\) dynamic programming nor \(O(V^3 \log n)\) transition matrix exponentiation can finish within the time limit.
  2. Why Naive Inclusion–Exclusion (PIE) Fails: If we attempt to apply PIE over the set of positions where forbidden patterns occur, choosing \(k\) pattern occurrences \(e_1 = [l_1, r_1], \dots, e_k = [l_k, r_k]\) inside \(W[1..n]\) leaves \(n - \bigl|\bigcup_{q=1}^k [l_q, r_q]\bigr|\) unconstrained characters. Because the union size \(\bigl|\bigcup_{q=1}^k [l_q, r_q]\bigr|\) depends on the exact pairwise overlap lengths of adjacent intervals, we cannot simply pick \(k\) starting indices with a binomial coefficient.

The Goulden–Jackson Breakthrough (1979): Instead of tracking all \(O(\sum |P_i|)\) partial prefix states step-by-step, group overlapping designated occurrences into maximal connected chains called clusters (簇). Because two patterns in a reduced dictionary can overlap only where a suffix of \(P_i\) equals a prefix of \(P_j\), all internal prefix states disappear, compressing the entire \(V \times V\) automaton into an \(m \times m\) polynomial linear system!

1.2 The Combinatorial Word Invariant: Marked Words, The Antichain Precondition & Unique Cluster Factorization

Before writing any generating function, let us name the concrete combinatorial objects being counted and state the structural precondition on \(\mathcal{P}\).

  • The Antichain Precondition (反链条件): A pattern set \(\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, \dots, P_m\}\) is an antichain under the substring relation if no pattern \(P_i\) is a contiguous substring of another pattern \(P_j\) (\(i \ne j \implies P_i \not\subseteq P_j\)). For pure avoidance, if \(P_i \subseteq P_j\), then any word \(W\) containing \(P_j\) already contains \(P_i\); thus \(P_j\) is redundant and can be deleted in \(O\bigl(\sum |P_i| \cdot |\Sigma|\bigr)\) time using the Aho–Corasick failure tree before constructing the system.
  • Marked Words \((W, M)\): For any word \(W \in \Sigma^*\), let \(\operatorname{Occ}(W)\) denote the set of all occurrences of patterns from \(\mathcal{P}\) in \(W\), where each occurrence \(e = (i, [l, r])\) records the matched pattern \(P_i\) and its 1-based closed index interval \([l, r]\) in \(W\) (\(r - l + 1 = |P_i|\)). A marked word (标记串) is a pair \((W, M)\) where \(W \in \Sigma^*\) is an arbitrary word and \(M \subseteq \operatorname{Occ}(W)\) is a chosen subset of its pattern occurrences, assigned the signed formal weight \((-1)^{|M|} x^{|W|}\).

Summing the weight \((-1)^{|M|} x^{|W|}\) over all pairs \((W, M)\) double-counts each word \(W\) according to the subset sieve \(\sum_{M \subseteq \operatorname{Occ}(W)} (-1)^{|M|} = (1 - 1)^{|\operatorname{Occ}(W)|} = [\operatorname{Occ}(W) = \emptyset]\): \[\begin{aligned} \sum_{W \in \Sigma^*} \sum_{M \subseteq \operatorname{Occ}(W)} (-1)^{|M|} x^{|W|} &= \sum_{W \in \Sigma^*} x^{|W|} \sum_{k=0}^{|\operatorname{Occ}(W)|} \binom{|\operatorname{Occ}(W)|}{k} (-1)^k && \text{(group subsets } M \text{ by size } k = |M|\text{)} \\ &= \sum_{W \in \Sigma^*} x^{|W|} (1 - 1)^{|\operatorname{Occ}(W)|} = \sum_{\substack{W \in \Sigma^* \\ \operatorname{Occ}(W) = \emptyset}} x^{|W|} = F(x). && \text{(only } \mathcal{P}\text{-avoiding words survive)} \end{aligned}\]

Now look at any marked word \((W, M)\) from left to right. Under the Antichain Precondition, no interval in \(M\) can be strictly nested inside another, so sorting the marked occurrences \(M = \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_s\}\) by their left endpoints \(l_1 < l_2 < \dots < l_s\) automatically sorts them by their right endpoints \(r_1 < r_2 < \dots < r_s\). Two consecutive marked occurrences \(e_q = [l_q, r_q]\) and \(e_{q+1} = [l_{q+1}, r_{q+1}]\) overlap if and only if \(l_{q+1} \le r_q\), sharing a non-empty overlap subsegment \([l_{q+1}, r_q]\) of length \(k = r_q - l_{q+1} + 1 \ge 1\).

Taking the connected components of the intervals in \(M\) decomposes \((W, M)\) uniquely into a free sequence (\(\operatorname{SEQ}\)) of two kinds of atomic blocks:

  1. Unmarked free characters \(c \in \Sigma\) that do not belong to any interval in \(M\), with generating function \(\sigma x\).
  2. Clusters (簇): maximal connected chains \(e_1, e_2, \dots, e_s\) (\(s \ge 1\)) of overlapping marked occurrences where \(l_{q+1} \le r_q\) for every \(1 \le q < s\). Let \(C_j(x)\) be the generating function of all clusters whose rightmost marked pattern is \(P_j\), and let \(C(x) = \sum_{j=1}^m C_j(x)\) be the total cluster generating function.

Because every marked word \((W, M)\) is a unique sequence of free characters and clusters, the symbolic \(\operatorname{SEQ}\) construction gives: \[F(x) = \operatorname{SEQ}\bigl(\sigma x + C(x)\bigr) = \frac{1}{1 - \sigma x - \sum_{j=1}^m C_j(x)}. \tag{24.1}\]

1.3 Dual Derivations of the \(m \times m\) Overlap System & Ring Invertibility

To compute \(C_1(x), \dots, C_m(x)\), we define the proper overlap set \(\operatorname{Overlap}(P_i, P_j)\) and full overlap set \(\operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_i, P_j)\) between \(P_i\) on the left and \(P_j\) on the right: \[\operatorname{Overlap}(P_i, P_j) = \Bigl\{ k \in \{1, \dots, \min(|P_i|, |P_j|)\} \setminus \bigl\{|P_i| \text{ if } i = j\bigr\} \;\Big|\; P_i\bigl[|P_i|-k+1 .. |P_i|\bigr] = P_j[1..k] \Bigr\},\] \[\operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_i, P_j) = \Bigl\{ k \in \{1, \dots, \min(|P_i|, |P_j|)\} \;\Big|\; P_i\bigl[|P_i|-k+1 .. |P_i|\bigr] = P_j[1..k] \Bigr\},\] along with their Overlap (Cross-Correlation) Polynomials (互相关多项式): \[\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(x) = \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Overlap}(P_i, P_j)} x^{|P_j| - k}, \qquad C_{i,j}(x) = \delta_{i,j} + \widetilde{C}_{i,j}(x) = \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_i, P_j)} x^{|P_j| - k}. \tag{24.2}\] Notice the exponent \(|P_j| - k\): when \(P_j\) overlaps the tail of \(P_i\) by \(k\) characters, appending \(P_j\) extends the word length by the remaining \(|P_j| - k\) suffix characters \(P_j[k+1..|P_j|]\).

NoteTheorem 24.1 (Goulden–Jackson Cluster System & Guibas–Odlyzko End-Tagged Duality)

Let \(\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, \dots, P_m\}\) be an antichain of patterns over \(\Sigma\) (\(\sigma = |\Sigma|\)), and let \(\mathbf{C}(x) = [C_{i,j}(x)]_{1 \le i, j \le m}\).

  1. Signed Cluster Linear System (Goulden–Jackson, 1979): The cluster generating functions \(C_1(x), \dots, C_m(x)\) satisfy the \(m \times m\) polynomial linear system: \[\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^m C_i(x) C_{i,j}(x) = -x^{|P_j|} \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, m\}.} \tag{24.3}\]
  2. Unsigned First-Hit End-Tagged System (Guibas–Odlyzko, 1981): Let \(F_i(x)\) be the OGF of words that avoid \(\mathcal{P}\) until their very last character, where they complete their first occurrence of a pattern in \(\mathcal{P}\), namely \(P_i\). Then \(F(x)\) and \(F_1(x), \dots, F_m(x)\) satisfy: \[\boxed{(1 - \sigma x) F(x) + \sum_{i=1}^m F_i(x) = 1, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^m F_i(x) C_{i,j}(x) = F(x) x^{|P_j|} \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, m\},} \tag{24.4}\] and the exact duality identity \(\dfrac{F_i(x)}{F(x)} = -C_i(x)\) holds for every \(1 \le i \le m\).
  3. Commutative Ring Invertibility Invariant: Over any commutative ring \(\mathcal{R}\) (regardless of characteristic), \(\mathbf{C}(0) = I_m\), so \(\det \mathbf{C}(x) \in 1 + x \mathcal{R}[x]\) is a unit in \(\mathcal{R}[[x]]\) and the system (24.3) has a unique solution \(C_i(x) \in \mathcal{R}[[x]]\).

Dual Proof of Theorem 24.1

  • Proof of (1) via Signed Cluster Peeling: Every cluster ending with \(P_j\) falls into one of two disjoint cases:
    • Base Case (\(s = 1\)): The cluster consists of the single marked occurrence \(P_j\) alone, contributing \((-1)^1 x^{|P_j|} = -x^{|P_j|}\).
    • Inductive Step (\(s \ge 2\)): Stripping the rightmost marked occurrence \(P_j\) leaves a valid smaller cluster ending with some \(P_i\) (counted by \(C_i(x)\)), which overlaps \(P_j\) along a proper suffix-prefix match \(k \in \operatorname{Overlap}(P_i, P_j)\). Appending \(P_j\) adds \(|P_j| - k\) characters and multiplies the sign by \(-1\), contributing \(-C_i(x) x^{|P_j|-k}\). Summing over all \(i\) and \(k \in \operatorname{Overlap}(P_i, P_j)\) yields \(C_j(x) = -x^{|P_j|} - \sum_{i=1}^m C_i(x) \widetilde{C}_{i,j}(x)\). Moving the sum to the left and using \(\delta_{i,j} + \widetilde{C}_{i,j}(x) = C_{i,j}(x)\) gives (24.3).
  • Proof of (2) via Unsigned End-Tagging:
    • First, take any \(\mathcal{P}\)-avoiding word \(W\) (counted by \(F(x)\)) and append one character \(c \in \Sigma\) (total GF \(\sigma x F(x)\)). The extended word \(Wc\) is either a non-empty \(\mathcal{P}\)-avoiding word (GF \(F(x) - 1\)) or hits \(\mathcal{P}\) for the very first time at its right end as some \(P_i\) (GF \(\sum_{i=1}^m F_i(x)\)). Thus \(1 + \sigma x F(x) = F(x) + \sum_{i=1}^m F_i(x)\), proving the first identity of (24.4).
    • Second, take any \(\mathcal{P}\)-avoiding word \(W\) and append the entire pattern \(P_j\) (total GF \(F(x) x^{|P_j|}\)). Because \(W P_j\) ends with \(P_j\), it certainly contains at least one occurrence of \(\mathcal{P}\). Look at the earliest occurrence of any pattern \(P_i \in \mathcal{P}\) inside \(W P_j\): since \(W\) avoids \(\mathcal{P}\) and \(\mathcal{P}\) is an antichain, this first occurrence \(P_i\) must start inside \(W\) (or at the start of \(P_j\)) and end at some prefix \(P_j[1..k]\) of \(P_j\), where \(k \in \operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_i, P_j)\)! The prefix of \(W P_j\) up to the end of \(P_i\) is counted by \(F_i(x)\), and the remaining suffix \(P_j[k+1..|P_j|]\) is fixed of length \(|P_j| - k\) (weight \(x^{|P_j|-k}\)). Summing over \(i\) and \(k\) gives \(\sum_{i=1}^m F_i(x) C_{i,j}(x) = F(x) x^{|P_j|}\). Dividing by \(F(x)\) shows that \((-F_i(x)/F(x))_{i=1}^m\) satisfies (24.3), so \(F_i(x)/F(x) = -C_i(x)\) by uniqueness!
  • Proof of (3) (Ring Invertibility): Because \(\mathcal{P}\) is an antichain, for \(i \ne j\) no overlap \(k \in \operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_i, P_j)\) can equal \(|P_j|\) (otherwise \(P_j\) would be a suffix of \(P_i\)). Thus \(|P_j| - k \ge 1\) for all \(i \ne j\), meaning \(C_{i,j}(0) = 0\) for \(i \ne j\). For \(i = j\), \(k = |P_j|\) contributes \(x^0 = 1\) while proper borders \(k < |P_j|\) contribute positive powers of \(x\), so \(C_{j,j}(0) = 1\). Hence \(\mathbf{C}(0) = I_m\) and \(\det \mathbf{C}(0) = 1 \in \mathcal{R}^\times\). \(\blacksquare\)

1.4 Single-Pattern Closed Form & The Rank-1 Sherman–Morrison Reduction

For a single pattern \(P\) (\(m = 1, L = |P|\)), \(\operatorname{Overlap}^+(P, P) = \operatorname{Borders}^+(P) = \operatorname{Border}(P) \cup \{L\}\) by Period–Border Duality (Theorem 1.1), so the autocorrelation polynomial is \(C_P(x) = \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Borders}^+(P)} x^{L - k} = \sum_{p \in \operatorname{Per}^+(P)} x^p\) (with \(p = 0\) giving \(x^0 = 1\)). Solving \(C_1(x) C_P(x) = -x^L\) and substituting into (24.1) and (24.4) yields: \[\boxed{F(x) = \frac{C_P(x)}{(1 - \sigma x) C_P(x) + x^L}, \qquad F_1(x) = \frac{x^L}{(1 - \sigma x) C_P(x) + x^L}.} \tag{24.5}\]

For \(m \ge 2\) patterns, writing \(u_i(x) = -C_i(x) = F_i(x)/F(x)\) converts (24.3) into the vector equation \(\mathbf{u}(x)^T \mathbf{C}(x) = \mathbf{x}^T\), where \(\mathbf{x} = (x^{|P_1|}, \dots, x^{|P_m|})^T\). Substituting \(\sum_{i=1}^m u_i(x) = \mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{C}(x)^{-1} \mathbf{1}\) into \(F(x) = \bigl(1 - \sigma x + \sum_{i=1}^m u_i(x)\bigr)^{-1}\) and applying the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury Matrix Determinant Lemma (\(\det(A + \mathbf{v}\mathbf{w}^T) = \det(A)(1 + \mathbf{w}^T A^{-1} \mathbf{v})\)) gives the explicit rational determinantal form: \[\boxed{F(x) = \frac{\det \mathbf{C}(x)}{(1 - \sigma x)\det \mathbf{C}(x) + \mathbf{x}^T \operatorname{adj}\bigl(\mathbf{C}(x)\bigr)\mathbf{1}}, \qquad F_i(x) = \frac{\bigl(\mathbf{x}^T \operatorname{adj}(\mathbf{C}(x))\bigr)_i}{(1 - \sigma x)\det \mathbf{C}(x) + \mathbf{x}^T \operatorname{adj}\bigl(\mathbf{C}(x)\bigr)\mathbf{1}}.} \tag{24.6}\] Since the \(j\)-th column of \(\mathbf{C}(x)\) has polynomial degree at most \(|P_j|\), Equation (24.6) proves that the rational generating function \(F(x) = P(x)/Q(x)\) has denominator degree \(\deg Q(x) \le 1 + \sum_{j=1}^m (|P_j| - 1) \le \sum_{j=1}^m |P_j|\)!

2. Bivariate \((t-1)\) Cluster Sieve, Exact \(k\)-Occurrence FPS & Extensions (T-4A-02)

2.1 The Bivariate \((t-1)\) Cluster Sieve & Exact \(k\)-Occurrence Rational GF

What if we do not want to forbid \(\mathcal{P}\), but instead want to count words \(W \in \Sigma^n\) containing exactly \(k\) occurrences of a pattern \(P\) (or weight each occurrence of \(P_i\) by a formal variable \(t_i\))? Define the multivariate occurrence generating function \[F(x; t_1, \dots, t_m) = \sum_{W \in \Sigma^*} x^{|W|} \prod_{i=1}^m t_i^{\operatorname{occ}_i(W)}.\]

Instead of building a larger automaton that counts up to \(k\) occurrences, apply the \((t_i - 1)\) Binomial Taylor Shift (FPS Chapter 08): rewrite each occurrence weight \(t_{\operatorname{pat}(e)} = 1 + (t_{\operatorname{pat}(e)} - 1)\) and expand the product over all \(e \in \operatorname{Occ}(W)\): \[\prod_{e \in \operatorname{Occ}(W)} \Bigl(1 + \bigl(t_{\operatorname{pat}(e)} - 1\bigr)\Bigr) = \sum_{M \subseteq \operatorname{Occ}(W)} \prod_{e \in M} \bigl(t_{\operatorname{pat}(e)} - 1\bigr).\] This is the exact same sum over marked words \((W, M)\) as in Section 1.2, except that each marked occurrence of \(P_j\) in \(M\) carries weight \((t_j - 1)\) instead of \(-1\)! When \(\mathcal{P}\) is an antichain, replacing \(-1\) by \((t_j - 1)\) in (24.3) immediately gives the Weighted Goulden–Jackson System: \[\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^m C_i(x; \mathbf{t}) \Bigl(\delta_{i,j} - (t_j - 1)\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(x)\Bigr) = (t_j - 1) x^{|P_j|} \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, m\}.} \tag{24.7}\]

Now specialize (24.7) to a single pattern \(P\) of length \(L = |P|\) with autocorrelation polynomial \(C_P(x) = 1 + \widetilde{C}_P(x)\). Solving (24.7) for \(C_1(x, t) = \frac{(t - 1) x^L}{1 - (t - 1)(C_P(x) - 1)}\) and substituting into \(F(x, t) = \frac{1}{1 - \sigma x - C_1(x, t)}\) yields: \[F(x, t) = \frac{1 - (t - 1)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)}{(1 - \sigma x)\Bigl(1 - (t - 1)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)\Bigr) - (t - 1) x^L} = \frac{C_P(x) - t\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)}{D_0(x) - t D_1(x)}, \tag{24.8}\] where we define the two fundamental denominator polynomials of \(P\): \[D_0(x) = (1 - \sigma x) C_P(x) + x^L, \qquad D_1(x) = (1 - \sigma x)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr) + x^L = D_0(x) - (1 - \sigma x). \tag{24.9}\]

ImportantTheorem 24.2 (Exact \(k\)-Occurrence Rational OGF & The \(D_1(0) = 0\) Valuation Invariant)

For any single pattern \(P \in \Sigma^L\) and integer \(k \ge 1\), the ordinary generating function \(F_k(x) = [t^k] F(x, t)\) of words containing exactly \(k\) (possibly overlapping) occurrences of \(P\) is: \[\boxed{F_k(x) = [t^k] F(x, t) = \frac{x^L \, D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k+1}} = \frac{x^L \Bigl((1 - \sigma x)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr) + x^L\Bigr)^{k-1}}{\Bigl((1 - \sigma x) C_P(x) + x^L\Bigr)^{k+1}}.} \tag{24.10}\] Furthermore, while \(D_0(0) = 1\), the polynomial \(D_1(x)\) satisfies \(D_1(0) = 0\), and its lowest nonzero term has exact degree equal to the shortest period of \(P\): \[\boxed{\operatorname{val}_x\bigl(D_1(x)\bigr) = d_{\min} = \operatorname{per}(P) = L - \pi[L] \ge 1, \qquad [x^{d_{\min}}] D_1(x) = 1.}\] Consequently, \(F_k(x) = 0 \bmod x^{N+1}\) whenever \(L + (k - 1) d_{\min} > N\), and to compute \(D_1(x)^{k-1} \bmod x^{N+1}\) via FPS \(\ln/\exp\), one must first factor out \(x^{d_{\min}}\) by writing \(D_1(x) = x^{d_{\min}} \widehat{D}_1(x)\) with \(\widehat{D}_1(0) = 1\).

Dual Proof of Theorem 24.2 (Algebraic Extraction & Bijective 3-Piece Decomposition)

  1. Algebraic Proof: Expand (24.8) as a geometric series in \(t\): \[F(x, t) = \frac{C_P(x) - t(C_P(x) - 1)}{D_0(x)\bigl(1 - t \frac{D_1(x)}{D_0(x)}\bigr)} = \frac{C_P(x)}{D_0(x)} + \sum_{k \ge 1} t^k \, \frac{C_P(x) D_1(x) - (C_P(x) - 1) D_0(x)}{D_0(x)^2} \left(\frac{D_1(x)}{D_0(x)}\right)^{k-1}.\] Using \(D_0(x) - D_1(x) = 1 - \sigma x\), the numerator simplifies via a direct cancellation: \[C_P(x) D_1(x) - \bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr) D_0(x) = D_0(x) - C_P(x)\bigl(D_0(x) - D_1(x)\bigr) = D_0(x) - (1 - \sigma x)C_P(x) = x^L,\] which yields (24.10).
  2. Bijective 3-Piece Word Decomposition: Why is \(F_k(x) = \frac{x^L}{D_0(x)} \cdot \left(\frac{D_1(x)}{D_0(x)}\right)^{k-1} \cdot \frac{1}{D_0(x)}\)? Cut any word \(W\) containing exactly \(k \ge 1\) occurrences of \(P\) at the right endpoints \(r_1 < r_2 < \dots < r_k\) of those \(k\) occurrences:
    • Piece 1 (Prefix up to the 1st occurrence \(r_1\)): By (24.5), \(F_1^{\text{first}}(x) = \frac{x^L}{D_0(x)}\) counts prefixes ending at the first occurrence of \(P\).
    • Piece 2 (\(k - 1\) Inter-Occurrence Transitions \(r_q \to r_{q+1}\)): After an occurrence of \(P\) finishes at \(r_q\), how can we extend the string so that \(P\) occurs at least once more? Every non-empty extension either avoids \(P\) forever or hits a next occurrence of \(P\). By (24.5), after an occurrence of \(P\) on the tape, the generating function of extensions that never complete another copy of \(P\) is \(\frac{1}{D_0(x)}\) (since any proper border of \(P\) is already active on the tape!). Subtracting the \(P\)-free extensions \(\frac{1}{D_0(x)}\) from all unrestricted extensions \(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma x}\) and dividing by \(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma x}\) (to stop at the first subsequent copy of \(P\)) gives the exact inter-occurrence transition factor: \[T_{P \to P}(x) = 1 - \frac{1 - \sigma x}{D_0(x)} = \frac{D_0(x) - (1 - \sigma x)}{D_0(x)} = \frac{D_1(x)}{D_0(x)}!\]
    • Piece 3 (Suffix after the \(k\)-th occurrence \(r_k\)): After the \(k\)-th occurrence of \(P\), the remaining suffix must never complete a \((k+1)\)-th occurrence of \(P\) (even using borders of the \(k\)-th copy of \(P\)), which is counted by \(\frac{1}{D_0(x)}\). Multiplying the three factors gives \(\frac{x^L}{D_0(x)} \left(\frac{D_1(x)}{D_0(x)}\right)^{k-1} \frac{1}{D_0(x)} = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k+1}}\).
  3. Proof of the Valuation Invariant: Since \(C_P(x) - 1 = \sum_{b \in \operatorname{Border}(P) \setminus \{0\}} x^{L - b} = x^{d_{\min}} + \text{higher terms}\) where \(d_{\min} = L - \pi[L] = \operatorname{per}(P) \ge 1\), the lowest degree term in \((1 - \sigma x)(C_P(x) - 1) + x^L\) is \(1 \cdot x^{d_{\min}}\) (even if \(C_P(x) = 1\), where \(d_{\min} = L\) comes from \(+x^L\)). \(\blacksquare\)

2.2 Non-Antichain Embedded Substring Weight Absorption

When \(\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, \dots, P_m\}\) is not an antichain (\(P_i \subsetneq P_j\) for some \(i \ne j\)) and we are solving a weighted problem where \(t_i \ne 0\), we cannot delete the super-pattern \(P_j\)! If we naively ran (24.7) on a non-antichain, a marked copy of \(P_i\) strictly inside a marked copy of \(P_j\) would be missed whenever \(P_i\) is not a prefix or suffix of \(P_j\).

To restore the left-to-right chain property without extra automaton states, we restrict cluster chains to maximal marked intervals (no marked interval in the chain is a sub-interval of another) and absorb all subset choices of shorter patterns \(P_i \subsetneq P_j\) into the weight of the enclosing super-pattern \(P_j\):

  1. Absorbed Single-Pattern Weight \(\widetilde{W}_j\): Every time \(P_j\) appears as a maximal marked interval in a cluster, any occurrence \(e\) of a shorter pattern \(P_i \subsetneq P_j\) that lies completely inside \(P_j\) can independently be marked (weight \(t_i - 1\)) or unmarked (weight \(1\)), summing to \(1 + (t_i - 1) = t_i\). Thus the effective weight of marking \(P_j\) is: \[\boxed{\widetilde{W}_j = (t_j - 1) \prod_{P_i \subsetneq P_j} t_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i, P_j)}.} \tag{24.11}\]
  2. Overlap Double-Count & Straddling Correction \(\widetilde{C}_{a,b}^{\text{abs}}(x; \mathbf{t})\): When two maximal marked patterns \(P_a\) and \(P_b\) overlap by \(k \in \operatorname{Overlap}(P_a, P_b)\) characters (\(P_a[|P_a|-k+1..|P_a|] = P_b[1..k]\), with neither containing the other), two adjustments occur at the junction \(P_a P_b[k+1..|P_b|]\):
    • Any occurrence of a pattern \(P_i\) contained entirely inside the overlap segment \(P_b[1..k]\) was multiplied into both \(\widetilde{W}_a\) and \(\widetilde{W}_b\), so we must divide by \(t_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i,\, P_b[1..k])}\).
    • Any occurrence of a pattern \(P_i\) inside the merged word \(P_a P_b[k+1..|P_b|]\) that straddles the junction (starts before \(|P_a| - k + 1\) and ends after \(|P_a|\), so it lies inside neither \(P_a\) nor \(P_b\) alone) must be multiplied in by \(t_i\). Hence the \((a, b)\) proper overlap polynomial becomes: \[\boxed{\widetilde{C}_{a,b}^{\text{abs}}(x; \mathbf{t}) = \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Overlap}(P_a, P_b)} x^{|P_b| - k} \frac{\prod_{P_i \text{ straddling } (P_a, P_b, k)} t_i}{\prod_{P_i \subseteq P_b[1..k]} t_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i,\, P_b[1..k])}}.} \tag{24.12}\]

2.3 Cyclic / Necklace Goulden–Jackson Cluster Method

Suppose we want to count circular strings of length \(n\) (indexed \(1, 2, \dots, n \pmod n\)) that avoid \(\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, \dots, P_m\}\), including occurrences that wrap across the cyclic boundary \(n \to 1\). When \(V = \sum |P_i| \approx 10^5\), computing \(\operatorname{Tr}(M_{\text{AC}}^n)\) is impossible.

How do marked words behave on a circle of length \(n \ge \max |P_i|\)?

  1. Non-Wrapping Rooted Clusters (Logarithmic Derivative): Any linear cluster of length \(L \le n\) can be placed on the circle in \(n\) ways, and in \(L\) of those placements the cluster covers the root position \(1\). Thus, circular configurations where clusters do not wrap all the way around the circle are generated by applying the root-marking operator \(x \frac{d}{dx} \ln(\cdot)\) to \(F(x) = (1 - \sigma x - C(x))^{-1}\): \[x \frac{d}{dx} \ln \frac{1}{1 - \sigma x - C(x)} = \frac{\sigma x + x C'(x)}{1 - \sigma x - C(x)} = -x \frac{Q'(x)}{Q(x)},\] where \(Q(x) = (1 - \sigma x)\det \mathbf{C}(x) + \mathbf{x}^T \operatorname{adj}(\mathbf{C}(x))\mathbf{1}\) is the rational denominator of \(F(x)\) from (24.6)!
  2. All-Wrapping Circular Clusters (Seam Trace Correction): When a cluster of \(s \ge 1\) overlapping marked patterns wraps around the circle of length \(n\) so that the tail of the last pattern \(P_{i_s}\) overlaps the head of the first pattern \(P_{i_1}\) across the seam \(n \to 1\), the cluster is a closed cycle of proper overlap transitions \((-\widetilde{\mathbf{C}}(x))\)! Including the \(L - k\) rotation offset across the seam gives the trace \(\operatorname{Tr}\Bigl(\bigl(-x \frac{d}{dx} \widetilde{\mathbf{C}}(x)\bigr)\bigl(I_m + \widetilde{\mathbf{C}}(x)\bigr)^{-1}\Bigr) = -x \frac{d}{dx} \ln \det \mathbf{C}(x)\). Combining both pieces shows that for all \(n \ge \max |P_i|\), the cyclic avoidance count \(a_n^{\text{cyc}}\) is simply the negative power sum of the roots of the rational denominator \(Q(x)\): \[\boxed{\sum_{n \ge \max |P_i|} a_n^{\text{cyc}} x^n = -x \frac{Q'(x)}{Q(x)} \pmod{\text{terms of degree } < \max |P_i|}.} \tag{24.13}\]

3. The \(t = 1 + u\) Binomial Moment Shift for Huge \(n \le 10^{18}\) (T-4A-03)

3.1 The Diagnostic Signal: Why Expanding Around \(t = 0\) Fails for Moments at \(n \le 10^{18}\)

Consider the following Universal Cup / QOJ master problem: given a single pattern \(P\) of length \(L = |P| \le 10^5\), a small integer \(K \le 20\), and a massive string length \(n \le 10^{18}\), compute the \(K\)-th moment of pattern occurrences \[M_K(n) = \sum_{W \in \Sigma^n} \bigl(\operatorname{occ}_P(W)\bigr)^K \bmod 998244353.\]

Why does Theorem 24.2 (\([t^k] F(x, t) = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k+1}}\)) fail here?

  • Expanding around \(t = 0\) isolates words with exactly \(k\) occurrences, where \(k\) can be as large as \(n \approx 10^{18}\).
  • Even if we differentiate \(F(x, t)\) \(K\) times with respect to \(t\) at \(t = 1\), a naive rational-function differentiation keeps powers of \(D_0(x) - D_1(x)\) and \(D_1(x)\) mixed together unless we reorganize the expansion variable from the very beginning!

The \(t = 1 + u\) Binomial Moment Shift: Instead of expanding \(F(x, t)\) in powers of \(t\) around \(t = 0\), substitute \(t = 1 + u\) (\(u = t - 1\), expanding around \(t = 1\))!

  • Combinatorial Meaning: Because \(t^{\operatorname{occ}_P(W)} = (1 + u)^{\operatorname{occ}_P(W)} = \sum_{j \ge 0} \binom{\operatorname{occ}_P(W)}{j} u^j\), the coefficient \([x^n u^j] F(x, 1 + u)\) counts pairs \((W, S)\) where \(W \in \Sigma^n\) is a word and \(S \subseteq \operatorname{Occ}_P(W)\) is a designated subset of \(j\) occurrences of \(P\) in \(W\): \[B_j(n) = [x^n u^j] F(x, 1 + u) = \sum_{W \in \Sigma^n} \binom{\operatorname{occ}_P(W)}{j}.\]
  • Algebraic Denominator Collapse: In the first expression of (24.8), \((t - 1)\) is literally \(u\)! At \(u = 0\), all cluster terms vanish and the denominator reduces from \(D_0(x)\) (degree \(L\)) to the degree-1 polynomial \(1 - \sigma x\)!
ImportantTheorem 24.3 (The \(t = 1 + u\) Binomial Moment Shift & Denominator Collapse)

For any pattern \(P \in \Sigma^L\), substituting \(t = 1 + u\) into the bivariate generating function \(F(x, t)\) yields: \[\boxed{F(x, 1 + u) = \frac{1}{1 - \sigma x} + \sum_{j \ge 1} u^j \, \frac{x^L \, D_1(x)^{j-1}}{(1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}},} \tag{24.14}\] where \(D_1(x) = (1 - \sigma x)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr) + x^L\) has degree \(L\). Consequently, for every \(j \ge 1\), the \(j\)-th binomial moment \(B_j(n) = \sum_{W \in \Sigma^n} \binom{\operatorname{occ}_P(W)}{j}\) is extracted from a polynomial \(A_j(x) = x^L D_1(x)^{j-1}\) of degree \(jL\) divided by \((1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}\): \[\boxed{B_j(n) = [x^n] \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{j-1}}{(1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}} = \sum_{d=L}^{\min(n,\, jL)} \bigl([x^d] x^L D_1(x)^{j-1}\bigr) \binom{n - d + j}{j} \sigma^{n - d},} \tag{24.15}\] and the \(K\)-th power moment \(M_K(n)\) is recovered via Stirling numbers of the second kind \(\left\{\begin{matrix} K \\ j \end{matrix}\right\}\) in \(O\bigl(KL \log(KL)\bigr)\) total time, completely independent of \(n \le 10^{18}\): \[\boxed{M_K(n) = \sum_{W \in \Sigma^n} \bigl(\operatorname{occ}_P(W)\bigr)^K = \sum_{j=0}^K \left\{\begin{matrix} K \\ j \end{matrix}\right\} j! \, B_j(n).} \tag{24.16}\]

Proof of Theorem 24.3

Set \(u = t - 1\) in Equation (24.8): \[\begin{aligned} F(x, 1 + u) &= \frac{1 - u\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)}{(1 - \sigma x)\Bigl(1 - u\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)\Bigr) - u x^L} = \frac{1 - u\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)}{(1 - \sigma x) - u\Bigl((1 - \sigma x)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr) + x^L\Bigr)} \\ &= \frac{1 - u\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)}{(1 - \sigma x) - u D_1(x)} = \frac{1}{1 - \sigma x} \cdot \frac{1 - u\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)}{1 - u \frac{D_1(x)}{1 - \sigma x}}. \end{aligned}\] Expanding \(\bigl(1 - u \frac{D_1(x)}{1 - \sigma x}\bigr)^{-1} = \sum_{j \ge 0} u^j \frac{D_1(x)^j}{(1 - \sigma x)^j}\), the coefficient of \(u^j\) for \(j \ge 1\) is: \[[u^j] F(x, 1 + u) = \frac{D_1(x)^j - (1 - \sigma x)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr) D_1(x)^{j-1}}{(1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}} = \frac{\Bigl(D_1(x) - (1 - \sigma x)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)\Bigr) D_1(x)^{j-1}}{(1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}} = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{j-1}}{(1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}}.\] Why does this make combinatorially intuitive sense? Choosing \(j \ge 1\) occurrences of \(P\) in \(W[1..n]\) places the first chosen copy of \(P\) (\(x^L\)), then steps \((j - 1)\) times from one chosen copy of \(P\) to the next—either overlapping along a proper border of \(P\) without free intermediate characters (\((1 - \sigma x)(C_P(x) - 1)\) cancels one \((1 - \sigma x)\) gap!) or jumping across a gap of arbitrary free characters (\(x^L\))—leaving \((j + 1)\) gaps of completely unrestricted characters \((1 - \sigma x)^{-(j+1)}\) before, between, and after the \(j\) clusters! Since \([x^{n-d}](1 - \sigma x)^{-(j+1)} = \binom{n - d + j}{j} \sigma^{n - d}\), Equation (24.15) follows immediately, and using \(m^K = \sum_{j=0}^K \left\{\begin{matrix} K \\ j \end{matrix}\right\} j! \binom{m}{j}\) yields (24.16). \(\blacksquare\)

// Non-Standard Kernel 1: Exact k occurrences of P in length <= N via Theorem 24.2
// Assumes Poly supports .inv(deg), .ln(deg), .exp(deg), and operator* (NTT).
Poly exact_k_occurrences_ogf(const vector<int>& borders_plus, int L, int N, long long k, Mint sigma) {
    Poly C_P(L + 1, 0);
    for (int b : borders_plus) C_P[L - b] += 1; // b = L gives C_P[0] = 1
    Poly one_minus_sx = {1, -sigma};
    Poly D0 = (one_minus_sx * C_P).sliced(L + 1);
    D0[L] += 1;
    if (k == 0) return (C_P * D0.inv(N + 1)).sliced(N + 1);

    Poly C_proper = C_P; C_proper[0] -= 1;
    Poly D1 = (one_minus_sx * C_proper).sliced(L + 1);
    D1[L] += 1;
    // CRITICAL INVARIANT: D1[0] == 0; find d_min = per(P) >= 1 before poly_ln!
    int d_min = 1;
    while (d_min <= L && D1[d_min].v == 0) ++d_min;
    if (k - 1 > (N - L) / d_min) return Poly(N + 1, 0); // degree exceeds N

    int shift = L + (int)((k - 1) * d_min);
    int rem = N - shift;
    Poly D1_hat(rem + 1, 0);
    for (int i = 0; i <= rem && i + d_min <= L; ++i) D1_hat[i] = D1[i + d_min];

    // Compute D1_hat^{k-1} * D0^{-(k+1)} mod x^{rem+1} via single ln + exp
    Poly log_num = (D1_hat.ln(rem + 1)) * Mint(k - 1);
    Poly log_den = (D0.sliced(rem + 1).ln(rem + 1)) * Mint(k + 1);
    Poly core = (log_num - log_den).exp(rem + 1);

    Poly F_k(N + 1, 0);
    for (int i = 0; i <= rem; ++i) F_k[i + shift] = core[i];
    return F_k;
}

// Non-Standard Kernel 2: Sum_{W in Sigma^n} occ_P(W)^K for huge n <= 10^18 (Theorem 24.3)
Mint kth_occurrence_moment_huge_n(const Poly& D1, int L, long long n, int K, Mint sigma) {
    if (K == 0) return sigma.pow(n);
    if (n < L) return 0;
    vector<vector<Mint>> S2(K + 1, vector<Mint>(K + 1, 0));
    S2[0][0] = 1;
    for (int i = 1; i <= K; ++i)
        for (int j = 1; j <= i; ++j)
            S2[i][j] = S2[i - 1][j - 1] + Mint(j) * S2[i - 1][j];

    Poly cur = {1}; // cur = D1(x)^{j-1}, degree (j-1)*L
    Mint ans = 0, fact_j = 1, inv_sigma = sigma.inv();
    for (int j = 1; j <= K; ++j) {
        fact_j *= Mint(j);
        if (j > 1) cur = cur * D1; // degree <= (K-1)*L
        int max_d = (int)min<long long>(n, 1LL * j * L);
        // Maintain binom(n - d + j, j) and sigma^{n - d} incrementally from d = L..max_d
        Mint binom_val = 1;
        for (int r = 1; r <= j; ++r)
            binom_val = binom_val * Mint((n - L + r) % MOD) * Mint(r).inv();
        Mint sig_pow = sigma.pow(n - L);
        Mint B_j = 0;
        for (int d = L; d <= max_d; ++d) {
            Mint coeff = cur[d - L]; // [x^d] (x^L * D1(x)^{j-1})
            if (coeff.v != 0) B_j += coeff * binom_val * sig_pow;
            // Step d -> d + 1: binom(n - d - 1 + j, j) = binom(n - d + j, j) * (n - d) / (n - d + j)
            binom_val = binom_val * Mint((n - d) % MOD) * Mint((n - d + j) % MOD).inv();
            sig_pow *= inv_sigma;
        }
        ans += S2[K][j] * fact_j * B_j;
    }
    return ans;
}

  1. The Non-Antichain Ghost-Cluster Bug (BP-22 / BP-24-01 / BP-4A-01): Suppose \(\mathcal{P} = \{P_1 = \texttt{ab},\; P_2 = \texttt{aabb}\}\) over \(\Sigma = \{\texttt{a}, \texttt{b}\}\) (\(\sigma = 2\)), and you build the \(2 \times 2\) avoidance system (24.3) without deleting \(P_2 \supset P_1\). Because \(P_1 = \texttt{ab}\) is strictly internal to \(P_2 = \texttt{aabb}\) (a[ab]b), \(\operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_1, P_2) = \emptyset\) and \(\operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_2, P_1) = \emptyset\)! Thus the unreduced matrix is \(\mathbf{C}(x) = I_2\), giving \(C_1(x) = -x^2, C_2(x) = -x^4\), and \(F_{\text{bug}}(x) = (1 - 2x + x^2 + x^4)^{-1} = 1 + 2x + 3x^2 + 4x^3 + \mathbf{4}x^4 + \cdots\). At \(n = 4\), the true number of \(\texttt{ab}\)-avoiding binary words is \(5\) (\(\texttt{aaaa}, \texttt{baaa}, \texttt{bbaa}, \texttt{bbba}, \texttt{bbbb}\)), because on \(W = \texttt{aabb}\) the pair \(M = \{P_1, P_2\}\) cannot be formed by suffix-prefix overlaps, leaving \(\texttt{aabb}\) with net weight \(1 + (-1)_{P_1} + (-1)_{P_2} = -1 \ne 0\)! Rule: Always filter \(\mathcal{P}\) to an antichain (for \(t_i = 0\)) or apply Section 2.2’s weight absorption (for \(t_i \ne 0\)).
  2. The Off-Diagonal Polynomial Pivot Trap (BP-24-02 / BP-4A-02): When solving \(\sum_{i=1}^m C_i(x) C_{i,j}(x) = -x^{|P_j|}\) via Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb{F}_p[[x]] \bmod x^{N+1}\), never swap rows/columns to pivot on an off-diagonal entry \(C_{i,j}(x)\) (\(i \ne j\))! By Theorem 24.1(3), \(C_{i,j}(0) = 0\) for all \(i \ne j\) whereas \(C_{j,j}(0) = 1\), so only the diagonal entry \(C_{j,j}(x)\) is invertible in \(\mathbb{F}_p[[x]]\).
  3. The \(D_1(0) = 0\) poly_ln Crash (BP-24-03 / BP-4A-03): In Theorem 24.2, \(D_0(0) = 1\) is immediately ready for poly_ln, but \(D_1(0) = (1 - 0)(1 - 1) + 0 = 0\) always has zero constant term! Calling D1.ln() without shifting by \(d_{\min} = \operatorname{per}(P) = L - \pi[L]\) divides by zero at index \(0\).

Problem 24.1 (W4A-01) — CSES 1112 / [HNOI2008] GT考试 — Single-Pattern Avoidance OGF & Bostan–Mori Extraction

  • Mathematical Statement: Given an alphabet of size \(\sigma\), a target length \(N\), and a single forbidden pattern \(P[1..L]\) (\(L \le 10^5\)), compute the number of length-\(N\) words \(W \in \Sigma^N\) containing at least one occurrence of \(P\) (or avoiding \(P\)) modulo \(998244353\), under two regimes: (a) for all \(1 \le n \le N \le 10^5\), and (b) for a single huge \(N \le 10^{18}\) with \(L \le 5 \times 10^4\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le L \le 10^5\), \(N \le 10^5\) (Regime a) or \(N \le 10^{18}\) (Regime b), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

For \(L = 10^5\), building a KMP transition matrix of size \(L \times L\) or running \(O(N L)\) KMP DP is far too slow. How does the single-pattern Guibas–Odlyzko formula (24.5) compress all \(L\) prefix states into a single rational function of degree \(L\)?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

By Theorem 24.1 and Equation (24.5), the OGF of \(P\)-avoiding words is \(F(x) = \frac{C_P(x)}{(1 - \sigma x)C_P(x) + x^L}\), where \(C_P(x) = 1 + \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Border}(P) \setminus \{0\}} x^{L - k}\). The number of words of length \(n\) containing \(P\) at least once is \(\sigma^n - [x^n] F(x)\).

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Compute \(\pi[1..L]\) via KMP in \(O(L)\) time and walk \(k = \pi[L], \pi[\pi[L]], \dots > 0\) to populate \(C_P(x)\). In Regime (a), compute \(C_P(x) \cdot D_0(x)^{-1} \bmod x^{N+1}\) via NTT polynomial inversion in \(O(N \log N)\). In Regime (b), extract \([x^N] \frac{C_P(x)}{D_0(x)}\) in \(O(L \log L \log N)\) via Bostan–Mori.

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-01)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: \(O(N L)\) KMP DP and \(O(L^3 \log N)\) matrix exponentiation both fail when \(L \le 10^5\).
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • Let \(a_n = [x^n] F(x)\) count \(P\)-avoiding words of length \(n\). By complementation, \(\#\{W \in \Sigma^n : P \subseteq W\} = \sigma^n - a_n\).
    • Every cluster of marked copies of \(P\) is a chain where each copy overlaps the previous copy at a proper border \(k \in \operatorname{Border}(P) \setminus \{0\}\), extending the length by \(p = L - k \in \operatorname{Per}(P)\). Thus \(C_P(x) = 1 + \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Border}(P)\setminus\{0\}} x^{L-k}\), and Equation (24.5) gives: \[F(x) = \frac{C_P(x)}{D_0(x)}, \qquad D_0(x) = (1 - \sigma x) C_P(x) + x^L.\]
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Run \(O(L)\) KMP on \(P\) to extract all borders \(k = L, \pi[L], \pi[\pi[L]], \dots > 0\) and set [x^{L-k}] C_P(x) = 1.
    • Form \(D_0(x) = (1 - \sigma x) C_P(x) + x^L\) of degree \(L\) with \(D_0(0) = 1\).
    • For Regime (a) (\(N \le 10^5\)), call C_P * D0.inv(N + 1) in \(O(N \log N)\) time.
    • For Regime (b) (\(N \le 10^{18}\)), pass \((C_P(x), D_0(x), N)\) to bostan_mori(C_P, D0, N) in \(O(L \log L \log N)\) time.
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: When \(L = 1\), \(C_P(x) = 1\) and \(D_0(x) = (1 - \sigma x) + x = 1 - (\sigma - 1)x\). Make sure the \(+x^L\) addition uses D0[L] += 1 rather than overwriting D0[L] = 1!

Problem 24.2 (W4A-04) — QOJ / UCup — Multi-Pattern Antichain Avoidance via m × m Goulden–Jackson System

  • Mathematical Statement: Given \(m \le 15\) forbidden patterns \(P_1, \dots, P_m \in \Sigma^+\) with each \(|P_i| \le 5000\) and target length \(N \le 5 \times 10^4\), compute the number of length-\(n\) words avoiding all \(P_i \in \mathcal{P}\) modulo \(998244353\) for every \(1 \le n \le N\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le m \le 15\), \(|P_i| \le 5000\), \(N \le 5 \times 10^4\), time limit \(3.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

The Aho–Corasick automaton has up to \(\sum |P_i| = 7.5 \times 10^4\) states, so \(O(N \sum |P_i|)\) DP takes \(\approx 3.75 \times 10^9\) operations. However, \(m \le 15\) is tiny!

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

First filter \(\mathcal{P}\) into an antichain by deleting any \(P_j\) that contains some \(P_i\) (\(i \ne j\)) as a substring. Then by Theorem 24.1, the cluster generating functions \(C_1(x), \dots, C_m(x)\) satisfy the \(m \times m\) polynomial system \(\sum_{i=1}^m C_i(x) C_{i,j}(x) = -x^{|P_j|}\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p[[x]] \bmod x^{N+1}\), with \(\mathbf{C}(0) = I_m\).

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Key Observation: Do you need to run Gaussian elimination on degree-\(N\) polynomials (costing \(O(m^3 N \log N)\)), or can you solve the \(m \times m\) polynomial matrix system using polynomials of degree at most \(L_{\max} = \max |P_i|\)? Since \(\deg C_{i,j}(x) \le |P_j| \le 5000\), running fraction-free or rational Gaussian elimination (or solving modulo \(x^{N+1}\) via NTT) takes only \(O(m^2 \sum |P_i| + m^3 N \log N)\)!

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-04)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Large \(\sum |P_i| \le 7.5 \times 10^4\) vs. tiny pattern count \(m \le 15\).
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • Step 1 (Antichain Filtering): Remove duplicate patterns, then check pairwise substring containment via KMP in \(O(m^2 L)\) time and discard any \(P_j\) containing another \(P_i\).
    • Step 2 (Cross-Correlation Matrix): For each ordered pair \((i, j)\), run KMP on \(P_j \# P_i\) to find all overlap lengths \(k \in \operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_i, P_j)\) and build \(C_{i,j}(x) = \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Overlap}^+(P_i, P_j)} x^{|P_j| - k}\).
    • Step 3 (Diagonal-Pivot Elimination): By Theorem 24.1(3), \(C_{j,j}(0) = 1\) and \(C_{i,j}(0) = 0\) (\(i \ne j\)). Solve \(\mathbf{C}(x)^T \mathbf{c}(x) = -\mathbf{x}\) modulo \(x^{N+1}\) by pivoting strictly on the \(j\)-th diagonal entry \(C_{j,j}(x)\) via C[j][j].inv(N + 1)!
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • In the \(j\)-th step of Gaussian elimination (\(1 \le j \le m\)), compute \(I_j(x) = C_{j,j}(x)^{-1} \bmod x^{N+1}\) once, normalize row \(j\) of \(\mathbf{C}^T\), and eliminate column \(j\) from rows \(r > j\) using NTT multiplication truncated to \(x^{N+1}\).
    • Finally, compute \(F(x) = \bigl(1 - \sigma x - \sum_{i=1}^m C_i(x)\bigr)^{-1} \bmod x^{N+1}\). Total time: \(O(m^2 L + m^3 N \log N)\).
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample (BP-4A-01 & BP-4A-02): Skipping Step 1 (antichain filtering) or pivoting on an off-diagonal entry \(C_{i,j}(x)\) (\(i \ne j\), which has \(C_{i,j}(0) = 0\)) breaks the solver.

Problem 24.3 (W4A-08) — UOJ / QOJ — Counting Words of Length N with Exactly k Pattern Occurrences

  • Mathematical Statement: Given alphabet size \(\sigma\), pattern \(P\) of length \(L \le 5 \times 10^4\), maximum length \(N \le 10^5\), and target count \(k\) (\(0 \le k \le N\)), output for every \(n \in [1, N]\) the number of words \(W \in \Sigma^n\) containing exactly \(k\) (possibly overlapping) occurrences of \(P\), modulo \(998244353\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le L \le 5 \times 10^4\), \(1 \le N \le 10^5\), \(0 \le k \le N\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Tracking the number of occurrences \(0 \dots k\) alongside the KMP state requires \(O(N L k)\) time. Instead, we need the coefficient \([t^k] F(x, t)\) of the bivariate cluster generating function.

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

By Theorem 24.2 (Equation 24.10), for \(k \ge 1\), \(F_k(x) = [t^k] F(x, t) = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k+1}}\), where \(D_0(x) = (1 - \sigma x)C_P(x) + x^L\) and \(D_1(x) = (1 - \sigma x)(C_P(x) - 1) + x^L\). Remember that \(D_1(0) = 0\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Let \(d_{\min} = \operatorname{per}(P) = L - \pi[L] \ge 1\) be the lowest degree with \([x^{d_{\min}}] D_1(x) = 1\). Factor \(D_1(x) = x^{d_{\min}} \widehat{D}_1(x)\) so \(\widehat{D}_1(0) = 1\). Then \(F_k(x) = x^{L + (k-1)d_{\min}} \exp\bigl((k-1)\ln \widehat{D}_1(x) - (k+1)\ln D_0(x)\bigr) \bmod x^{N+1}\).

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-08)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: 2D KMP DP over (length, kmp_state, count) is \(O(N L k)\), whereas FPS \(\ln/\exp\) computes all \(n \le N\) in \(O(N \log N)\) independent of \(k\).
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • For \(k = 0\), \(F_0(x) = C_P(x) / D_0(x)\).
    • For \(k \ge 1\), Theorem 24.2 gives \(F_k(x) = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k+1}}\). Since \(\operatorname{val}_x(D_1) = d_{\min} = L - \pi[L] \ge 1\), the minimum length of a word with \(k\) occurrences of \(P\) is \(s = L + (k - 1) d_{\min}\) (achieved by overlapping \(k\) copies of \(P\) at its longest proper border \(\pi[L]\)!).
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Directly execute exact_k_occurrences_ogf from Section 4.2: if \(L + (k - 1) d_{\min} > N\), return the zero polynomial; otherwise shift \(D_1(x)\) down by \(d_{\min}\), evaluate \((k-1)\ln \widehat{D}_1(x) - (k+1)\ln D_0(x) \bmod x^{N - s + 1}\), exponentiate once, and shift up by \(x^s\). Time: \(O(L + N \log N)\).
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample (BP-4A-03): Calling poly_ln on \(D_1(x)\) without shifting by \(d_{\min}\) divides by \(D_1(0) = 0\).

Problem 24.4 (W4A-22) — QOJ / AtCoder Style — At-Least-k & Modulo-r Pattern Occurrence Counting

  • Mathematical Statement: Given a pattern \(P \in \Sigma^L\) (\(L \le 5 \times 10^4\)) and \(N \le 10^5\):
    • Part A: Compute for all \(n \le N\) the number of words \(W \in \Sigma^n\) containing at least \(k\) occurrences of \(P\) (\(k \ge 1\)).
    • Part B: Given a small modulus \(r \ge 2\) dividing \(998244353 - 1\) (e.g., \(r = 2^m\)), compute for all \(n \le N\) the number of words \(W \in \Sigma^n\) whose number of occurrences of \(P\) satisfies \(\operatorname{occ}_P(W) \equiv c \pmod r\).
  • Constraints: \(L \le 5 \times 10^4\), \(N \le 10^5\), \(r \le 16\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Summing \(F_k(x) + F_{k+1}(x) + \cdots\) term-by-term takes \(O(N)\) polynomial operations. Can we sum the geometric tail \(\sum_{m \ge k} F_m(x)\) in closed rational form for Part A, and apply a Roots-of-Unity Filter (FPS Chapter 07) to \(F(x, t)\) for Part B?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

For Part A, summing \(F_m(x) = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{m-1}}{D_0(x)^{m+1}}\) over all \(m \ge k\) is a geometric series with ratio \(q(x) = \frac{D_1(x)}{D_0(x)}\) and \(1 - q(x) = \frac{D_0(x) - D_1(x)}{D_0(x)} = \frac{1 - \sigma x}{D_0(x)}\)! For Part B, evaluating \(F(x, \omega^j)\) at the \(r\)-th roots of unity \(\omega = g^{(p-1)/r}\) projects onto \(\operatorname{occ}_P(W) \equiv c \pmod r\).

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Key Simplification: In Part A, simplifying \(\sum_{m \ge k} \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{m-1}}{D_0(x)^{m+1}} = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k+1}} \cdot \frac{1}{1 - D_1(x)/D_0(x)}\) cancels one power of \(D_0(x)\) and replaces it with \((1 - \sigma x)\)! Check what happens at \(k = 1\): \(\frac{x^L}{(1 - \sigma x) D_0(x)}\), which is just \(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma x} - \frac{C_P(x)}{D_0(x)}\)!

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-22)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Explicit summation over \(m \ge k\) is slow; geometric tail summation collapses at-least-\(k\) to a single rational power.
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • Part A (Closed-Form At-Least-\(k\) OGF): Since \(D_0(x) - D_1(x) = 1 - \sigma x\), summing (24.10) over \(m \ge k\) gives: \[\boxed{F_{\ge k}(x) = \sum_{m=k}^\infty \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{m-1}}{D_0(x)^{m+1}} = \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k+1}\bigl(1 - \frac{D_1(x)}{D_0(x)}\bigr)} = \frac{x^L \, D_1(x)^{k-1}}{(1 - \sigma x) \, D_0(x)^k}!}\] Notice the combinatorial meaning: a word with at least \(k\) occurrences of \(P\) consists of the prefix up to the \(1\)-st occurrence (\(\frac{x^L}{D_0(x)}\)), followed by \(k - 1\) inter-occurrence steps (\(\frac{D_1(x)^{k-1}}{D_0(x)^{k-1}}\)), followed by an unrestricted suffix (\(\frac{1}{1 - \sigma x}\)) after the \(k\)-th occurrence completes!
    • Part B (Roots-of-Unity Filter on \(F(x, t)\)): Let \(\omega \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\) be a primitive \(r\)-th root of unity. By orthogonality of roots of unity, \[\sum_{\substack{W \in \Sigma^* \\ \operatorname{occ}_P(W) \equiv c \pmod r}} x^{|W|} = \frac{1}{r} \sum_{j=0}^{r-1} \omega^{-j c} F(x, \omega^j) = \frac{1}{r} \sum_{j=0}^{r-1} \omega^{-j c} \, \frac{C_P(x) - \omega^j\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr)}{D_0(x) - \omega^j D_1(x)}.\]
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Part A: Shift \(D_1(x) = x^{d_{\min}} \widehat{D}_1(x)\), compute \(\widehat{D}_1(x)^{k-1} D_0(x)^{-k} (1 - \sigma x)^{-1} \bmod x^{N+1}\) via one ln/exp in \(O(N \log N)\).
    • Part B: Perform \(r\) polynomial inversions of \(D_0(x) - \omega^j D_1(x) \bmod x^{N+1}\) in \(O(r N \log N)\) time.
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: In Part B at \(j = 0\) (\(\omega^0 = 1\)), \(D_0(x) - D_1(x) = 1 - \sigma x\), and \(C_P(x) - (C_P(x) - 1) = 1\), so \(F(x, 1) = \frac{1}{1 - \sigma x}\) (all words of length \(n\)).

Tier 3 — Advanced (W4A-28, W4A-34)

Problem 24.5 (W4A-28) — THUPC / QOJ — Non-Antichain Weighted Multi-Pattern Cluster GF with Embedded Factors

  • Mathematical Statement: Given \(m \le 8\) patterns \(P_1, \dots, P_m \in \Sigma^+\) (\(|P_i| \le 2000\), where shorter patterns \(P_a\) may be contiguous substrings of longer patterns \(P_b\)), occurrence weights \(w_1, \dots, w_m \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{0\}\), and length \(N \le 5 \times 10^4\), compute for all \(1 \le n \le N\): \[Z_n = \sum_{W \in \Sigma^n} \prod_{i=1}^m w_i^{\operatorname{occ}_{P_i}(W)} \bmod 998244353.\]
  • Constraints: \(1 \le m \le 8\), \(|P_i| \le 2000\), \(N \le 5 \times 10^4\), time limit \(3.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

Because \(w_i \ne 0\), a word containing \(P_b\) (which internally contains \(P_a\)) still contributes nonzero weight \(w_b w_a^{\operatorname{occ}(P_a, P_b)}\), so we cannot delete \(P_b\)! Yet if we keep both \(P_a\) and \(P_b\) in a standard overlap matrix, internal occurrences of \(P_a\) inside \(P_b\) are missed whenever \(P_a\) is neither a prefix nor a suffix of \(P_b\).

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Apply Section 2.2’s Non-Antichain Embedded Substring Weight Absorption (Equations 24.11–24.12)! Only allow maximal marked intervals in a cluster chain (so if \(P_a\) lies strictly inside a marked \(P_b\), \(P_a\)’s two choices \(1 + (w_a - 1) = w_a\) are absorbed into \(\widetilde{W}_b = (w_b - 1) \prod_{P_i \subsetneq P_b} w_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i, P_b)}\)). What if two marked occurrences \(P_a\) and \(P_b\) overlap by \(k\) characters without either containing the other?

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Crucial Boundary Case: What if \(P_a\) is a suffix of \(P_b\) (or prefix of \(P_b\))? Any occurrence of \(P_a\) aligned with the suffix/prefix of a marked \(P_b\) is also contained inside \(P_b\), so absorb all occurrences \(P_i \subsetneq P_b\) (including boundary-aligned ones!) into \(\widetilde{W}_b\), and allow an overlap transition \(P_a \xrightarrow{k} P_b\) only when neither interval contains the other (\(k < \min(|P_a|, |P_b|)\))! Then for each valid overlap \(k\), multiply by \(\prod_{P_i \text{ straddling}} w_i\) and divide by \(\prod_{P_i \subseteq P_b[1..k]} w_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i, P_b[1..k])}\).

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-28)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Non-antichain pattern dictionary with nonzero weights \(w_i \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\).
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • Merge identical patterns by multiplying their weights \(w_i\).
    • In any marked word \((W, M)\), call an interval \(e \in M\) dominant if no other interval \(e' \in M\) contains \(e\) as a sub-interval. Every non-dominant occurrence \(e \subseteq e'\) of \(P_i\) inside a dominant \(P_j\) can independently be in \(M\) (weight \(w_i - 1\)) or not in \(M\) (weight \(1\)), summing to \(w_i\).
    • When dominant intervals \(e_1 < e_2 < \dots < e_s\) form a cluster, no \(e_q\) contains \(e_{q+1}\) or vice versa, so \(1 \le k = r_q - l_{q+1} + 1 < \min(|P_{i_q}|, |P_{i_{q+1}}|)\).
    • Any occurrence of a pattern \(P_r\) inside the union \(e_1 \cup \dots \cup e_s\) that is not one of the dominant intervals \(e_q\) itself is absorbed with weight \(w_r\). By Inclusion–Exclusion on contiguous intervals of the chain (and assuming \(\max |P_r|\) cannot span across three dominant intervals without being contained in two adjacent ones, or by keeping only maximal chains), for two overlapping patterns \(P_a, P_b\) at overlap \(1 \le k < \min(|P_a|, |P_b|)\): \[\widetilde{W}_b = (w_b - 1) \prod_{P_i \subsetneq P_b} w_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i, P_b)}, \qquad T_{a, b}(x) = \sum_{k} \widetilde{W}_b \, x^{|P_b|-k} \frac{\prod_{P_i \text{ straddling } (P_a, P_b, k)} w_i}{\prod_{P_i \subseteq P_b[1..k]} w_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i, P_b[1..k])}}.\]
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Precompute \(\operatorname{occ}(P_i, U)\) on all pattern substrings via KMP in \(O(m^2 L)\).
    • Solve \(\bigl(I_m - \mathbf{T}(x)^T\bigr)\mathbf{C}(x) = \bigl(\widetilde{W}_j x^{|P_j|}\bigr)_{j=1}^m\) modulo \(x^{N+1}\) in \(O(m^3 N \log N)\) and invert \(1 - \sigma x - \sum_{j=1}^m C_j(x)\).
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Forgetting to divide by \(\prod_{P_i \subseteq P_b[1..k]} w_i^{\operatorname{occ}(P_i, P_b[1..k])}\) counts a short pattern \(P_i\) lying inside the overlap \(P_b[1..k]\) twice (once in \(\widetilde{W}_a\) and once in \(\widetilde{W}_b\)).

Problem 24.6 (W4A-34) — AtCoder AGC 058 D — Yet Another ABC String (Multivariate Cyclic Cluster Cancellation)

  • Mathematical Statement: Given \(A, B, C \le 10^6\), count the number of strings over \(\{\texttt{A}, \texttt{B}, \texttt{C}\}\) containing exactly \(A\) copies of \(\texttt{'A'}\), \(B\) copies of \(\texttt{'B'}\), and \(C\) copies of \(\texttt{'C'}\) that avoid all three cyclic substrings \(\mathcal{P} = \{\texttt{ABC},\; \texttt{BCA},\; \texttt{CAB}\}\), modulo \(998244353\).
  • Constraints: \(1 \le A, B, C \le 10^6\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

We have a 3-variable exact character count \([x^A y^B z^C]\) with \(A, B, C \le 10^6\). A 3D DP is \(O(ABC) = 10^{18}\). What does a Goulden–Jackson cluster of \(\mathcal{P} = \{\texttt{ABC}, \texttt{BCA}, \texttt{CAB}\}\) look like?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Two patterns in \(\{\texttt{ABC}, \texttt{BCA}, \texttt{CAB}\}\) can overlap only if they continue the cyclic period \(\texttt{ABCABC...}\)! Thus every cluster is a cyclic substring of \(\texttt{(ABC)}^\omega\) of length \(L \ge 3\). By symmetry, in the univariate projection \(x = y = z = t\), \(C_{i,j}(t)\) sums to \(\frac{1}{1 - t}\) across each column! What is the total univariate cluster GF \(C(t) = C_1(t) + C_2(t) + C_3(t)\)?

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Key Cancellation: For any cluster of length \(L \ge 3\), how many subset markings \(M\) cover all \(L\) positions of \(\texttt{ABCABC...}[1..L]\)? Adding \(1\) character extends the cluster either by marking the new length-\(3\) window (\(-t\)) or not, giving \(C(t) = \frac{-3 t^3}{1 + t + t^2} = \sum_{k \ge 1} \bigl(3 (-1)^k t^{3k} - 3 (-1)^k t^{3k+1}\bigr)\)! Every cluster of length \(L \equiv 2 \pmod 3\) has net coefficient \(0\), and every surviving cluster has length \(3k\) (using \((x y z)^k\)) or \(3k + 1\) (using \((x y z)^k (x + y + z)\))!

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-34)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: 3-variable generating function \([x^A y^B z^C]\) where naive 3D multinomial expansion is impossible, but the forbidden set \(\mathcal{P} = \{\texttt{ABC}, \texttt{BCA}, \texttt{CAB}\}\) is invariant under cyclic letter shifts.
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (The \(1 + t + t^2\) Cluster Cancellation):
    • In the univariate projection (\(x = y = z = t\)), each pattern \(P_i \in \mathcal{P}\) has length \(3\). Because \(\texttt{ABC}, \texttt{BCA}, \texttt{CAB}\) cyclic-shift into one another at every step, for any \(i \in \{1, 2, 3\}\), summing the overlap polynomials across all three predecessor patterns \(r \in \{1, 2, 3\}\) gives every shift length \(3 - k \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}\): \[\sum_{r=1}^3 C_{r, j}(t) = 1 + t + t^2. \quad (\text{from } k = 3, 2, 1\text{!})\]
    • Summing the Goulden–Jackson system \(\sum_{r=1}^3 C_r(t) C_{r,j}(t) = -t^3\) over \(j \in \{1, 2, 3\}\) yields: \[C(t) (1 + t + t^2) = -3 t^3 \implies \boxed{C(t) = \frac{-3 t^3}{1 + t + t^2} = \frac{-3 t^3 (1 - t)}{1 - t^3} = \sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^k \bigl(3 t^{3k} - 3 t^{3k+1}\bigr)!}\]
    • Now restore the 3 variables \((x, y, z)\)! A cyclic substring of \(\texttt{ABCABC...}\) of length \(3k\) always contains exactly \(k\) \(\texttt{A}\)’s, \(k\) \(\texttt{B}\)’s, and \(k\) \(\texttt{C}\)’s (monomial \((xyz)^k\)), regardless of which of the 3 letters it starts with! And a cyclic substring of length \(3k + 1\) starts and ends with the same letter, contributing \((xyz)^k(x + y + z)\). Meanwhile, all length-\((3k+2)\) clusters cancel to \(0\)!
    • Therefore, the exact 3-variable cluster generating function is: \[C(x, y, z) = \sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^k (xyz)^k \bigl(3 - (x + y + z)\bigr) = \frac{-xyz\bigl(3 - (x + y + z)\bigr)}{1 + xyz}!\]
    • Substituting \(C(x, y, z)\) into \(F(x, y, z) = \frac{1}{1 - (x + y + z) - C(x, y, z)}\) gives: \[\boxed{F(x, y, z) = \frac{1 + xyz}{(1 + xyz)\bigl(1 - (x + y + z)\bigr) + xyz\bigl(3 - (x + y + z)\bigr)} = \frac{1 + xyz}{1 - (x + y + z) + 3xyz - 2xyz(x + y + z)}!}\]
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Let \(S = x + y + z\) and \(P = xyz\). Write \(F(x, y, z) = (1 + P) \sum_{k \ge 0} (-P)^k \bigl(3 - 2S\bigr)^k (1 - S)^{-(k+1)}\), or directly expand in powers of \(P = xyz\): \[[x^A y^B z^C] F(x, y, z) = [x^A y^B z^C] (1 + xyz) \sum_{k=0}^{\min(A,B,C)} (-xyz)^k \frac{(3 - 2S)^k}{(1 - S)^{k+1}}.\]
    • Since \([x^{A-k} y^{B-k} z^{C-k}] S^N = \frac{N!}{(A-k)!(B-k)!(C-k)!}\) where \(N = (A + B + C) - 3k\), and \([S^N] \frac{(3 - 2S)^k}{(1 - S)^{k+1}} = [S^N] \frac{(1 + 2(1 - S))^k}{(1 - S)^{k+1}} = \sum_{r=0}^k \binom{k}{r} 2^{k-r} \binom{N + r}{r}\) (which simplifies via a \(1\)-step recurrence in \(k\)), the entire coefficient \([x^A y^B z^C] F(x, y, z)\) is computed in \(O\bigl(\min(A, B, C)\bigr)\) time!
  4. Broken-Invariant Counterexample: Trying to solve AGC 058 D without noticing that \([t^{3k+2}] C(t) = 0\) leaves \(xy + yz + zx\) terms that cannot be factored into powers of \(P = xyz\) and \(S = x + y + z\).

Problem 24.7 (W4A-39) — Universal Cup / QOJ — Expected K-th Power of Pattern Occurrences for n ≤ 10^{18} via t = 1 + u Shift

  • Mathematical Statement: Given alphabet size \(\sigma\), a pattern \(P \in \Sigma^L\) of length \(L \le 10^5\), a small integer \(K \le 20\), and a huge word length \(n \le 10^{18}\), compute the expected \(K\)-th power of the number of occurrences of \(P\) in a uniformly random word \(W \in \Sigma^n\): \[\mathbb{E}\bigl[\operatorname{occ}_P(W)^K\bigr] = \sigma^{-n} \sum_{W \in \Sigma^n} \bigl(\operatorname{occ}_P(W)\bigr)^K \bmod 998244353.\]
  • Constraints: \(1 \le L \le 10^5\), \(1 \le K \le 20\), \(1 \le n \le 10^{18}\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

At \(L = 10^5, K = 20, n = 10^{18}\), running Bostan–Mori on a denominator \(D_0(x)^{K+1}\) of degree \(2.1 \times 10^6\) takes \(O(KL \log(KL) \log n)\) (\(\approx 60\) polynomial multiplications of size \(4 \times 10^6\)), which TLEs. How can we eliminate the \(\log n\) Bostan–Mori loop completely?

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

Invoke Theorem 24.3 (The \(t = 1 + u\) Binomial Moment Shift)! By expanding \(F(x, 1 + u)\) in powers of \(u = t - 1\), the \(j\)-th binomial moment \(B_j(n) = \sum_{W \in \Sigma^n} \binom{\operatorname{occ}_P(W)}{j}\) is the coefficient of \(x^n\) in \(\frac{x^L D_1(x)^{j-1}}{(1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}}\), whose denominator is simply \((1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}\)!

NoteHint 3 — Algorithmic Assembly

Since \(x^L D_1(x)^{j-1}\) is a polynomial of degree \(j L \le K L \le 2 \times 10^6\), we never need polynomial division or Bostan–Mori! Simply compute \(D_1(x)^{j-1}\) for \(j = 1 \dots K\) via NTT, extract \(B_j(n) = \sum_{d=L}^{\min(n, jL)} ([x^{d-L}] D_1(x)^{j-1}) \binom{n - d + j}{j} \sigma^{n - d}\), and combine with Stirling numbers \(\left\{\begin{matrix} K \\ j \end{matrix}\right\} j!\).

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-39)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Huge \(n \le 10^{18}\) and long pattern \(L \le 10^5\), but low moment order \(K \le 20\).
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant:
    • By Theorem 24.3 (Equations 24.14–24.16), setting \(t = 1 + u\) collapses the denominator at \(u = 0\) from \(D_0(x)^{j+1}\) to \((1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}\): \[B_j(n) = [x^n u^j] F(x, 1 + u) = [x^n] \frac{x^L D_1(x)^{j-1}}{(1 - \sigma x)^{j+1}}, \qquad D_1(x) = (1 - \sigma x)\bigl(C_P(x) - 1\bigr) + x^L.\]
    • Dividing \(B_j(n)\) by \(\sigma^n\) normalizes the binomial moment directly to expectation: \[\mathbb{E}\left[\binom{\operatorname{occ}_P(W)}{j}\right] = \sum_{d=L}^{\min(n,\, jL)} \bigl([x^{d-L}] D_1(x)^{j-1}\bigr) \binom{n - d + j}{j} \sigma^{-d}.\]
    • Sanity check at \(j = 1\): \(D_1(x)^0 = 1\), so the only term is \(d = L\), giving \(\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{occ}_P(W)] = \binom{n - L + 1}{1} \sigma^{-L} = (n - L + 1)\sigma^{-L}\) (linearity of expectation over the \(n - L + 1\) window positions!).
  3. Algorithmic Compilation:
    • Build \(C_P(x)\) via KMP in \(O(L)\) and form \(D_1(x)\).
    • Run kth_occurrence_moment_huge_n from Section 4.2, multiplying the final sum by \(\sigma^{-n \bmod (p-1)} \bmod p\). Total time: \(O\bigl(K L \log(K L)\bigr)\), with zero dependence on \(\log n\)!

Problem 24.8 (W4A-41) — UCup / QOJ — Bivariate Multi-Pattern Moment Shift & Cross-Covariance at n ≤ 10^{18}

  • Mathematical Statement: Given two distinct patterns \(P_1, P_2 \in \Sigma^+\) (\(|P_1|, |P_2| \le 10^5\), neither a substring of the other) and \(n \le 10^{18}\), compute the exact covariance \(\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(\operatorname{occ}_{P_1}(W),\, \operatorname{occ}_{P_2}(W)\bigr) = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{occ}_{P_1}(W)\operatorname{occ}_{P_2}(W)] - \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{occ}_{P_1}(W)]\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{occ}_{P_2}(W)]\) (and more generally \([x^n u_1^{k_1} u_2^{k_2}] F(x; 1 + u_1, 1 + u_2)\) for \(k_1 + k_2 \le K\)) modulo \(998244353\).
  • Constraints: \(|P_1|, |P_2| \le 10^5\), \(n \le 10^{18}\), time limit \(2.0\text{ s}\).
NoteHint 1 — Diagnostic Signal

NoteHint 2 — Combinatorial Word Invariant

TipFull Derivation & Solution (W4A-41)
  1. Diagnostic Signal: Cross-covariance and mixed moments of multiple long patterns (\(|P_i| \le 10^5\)) at \(n \le 10^{18}\).
  2. Combinatorial Word Invariant (Multi-Pattern \(t_i = 1 + u_i\) Neumann Expansion):
    • Let \(U = \operatorname{diag}(u_1, \dots, u_m)\). Rewriting (24.7) with \(t_j - 1 = u_j\) gives: \[C(x; \mathbf{1} + \mathbf{u}) = \sum_{s \ge 1} \mathbf{x}^T U \bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{C}}(x) U\bigr)^{s-1} \mathbf{1}.\]
    • Look at the coefficient \([u_1^1 u_2^1]\) (one designated copy of \(P_1\) and one designated copy of \(P_2\)):
