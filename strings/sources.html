Multilingual Stringology Bibliography & Terminology Rosetta Stone

Primary Academic Papers & Monographs [EN], Chinese IOI National Team Theses (国家集训队论文, [ZH]), Japanese Competitive Programming Treatises [JA], and the 84-Entry Trilingual Rosetta Stone Mapped to Ch 01–Ch 30

Modern competitive programming stringology draws from three deeply interconnected traditions:

  • [EN] Theoretical Stringology & Combinatorics on Words: Established the foundational algebraic and structural theorems of words—Fine–Wilf periodicity (1965), Chen–Fox–Lyndon factorization (1958) and Duval’s linear algorithm (1983), Suffix Automata and Compact DAWGs (Blumer et al. 1985–1987, Crochemore–Rytter 1994–2002), Palindromic Trees / Eertrees (Rubinchik–Shur 2015–2018), the Significant Suffixes Theorem and Basic Substring Dictionary / Internal Pattern Matching (Kociumaka, Radoszewski, Rytter, Waleń 2014–2024), the Runs Theorem (Kolpakov–Kucherov 1999, Bannai et al. 2017), Locally Consistent Parsing & Recompression SLPs (Cole–Vishkin 1986, Mehlhorn–Sundar–Uhrig 1997, Jeż 2015–2016), String Attractors & \(\tau\)-Synchronizing Sets (Kempa–Prezza 2018, Kempa–Kociumaka 2019), Goulden–Jackson Cluster Generating Functions (1979), and Tiskin’s Seaweed Matrices (2008–2015).
  • [ZH] Chinese Olympiad (NOI / WC / CTSC / 国家集训队论文, 2004–2024): Pioneered the competitive programming synthesis of word combinatorics with advanced data structures—most notably SAM + Persistent Segment Tree Merging on \(\operatorname{endpos}\) ([NOI2018] 你的名字), Dynamic SAM + Link-Cut Tree access Color-Chain Sweeping (陈立杰 2012, 张天扬 2015), 3-Case Trie-BFS Generalized SAM (刘研绎 2015), PAM & Range Distinct Palindrome slink Telescoping (徐毅 2014), Border AP Modulo Shortest Path ([WC2016] 论战捆竹竿, 翁文涛 / 钟知闲 2017), Fibonacci Singular Words (周任飞 2017), Significant Suffixes, Lyndon Trees & Runs 2D Sweep-Lines (金策 2019), Internal Pattern Matching (王修涵 2020), and Unit-Monge Seaweed Matrices (潘佳奇 / 戴江齐 2021).
  • [JA] Japanese AtCoder (AGC / ARC) & Kyopro Ecosystem: Standardized algebraic and unamortized string formulations—including \(O(\sigma)\) Matrix-Free Invertible Subsequence Automaton Queries (maspypy), Trace-Zero \(\det = -1\) Involution Matrices in \(GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\) & Stack-Trie Path LCE (noshi91, snuke), 1-FFT / 3-in-2 Wildcard Convolutions (Nyaan), 4-Way Double-Ended Deque PAM (tatyam), and \(O(N)\) Double-Order Lyndon-Root Runs Enumeration (henkoku, noshi91).

1. Academic Stringology & Combinatorics on Words Canon ([EN])

1.1 Foundational Monographs & Textbooks

  1. [Lothaire83] / [Lothaire97] — M. Lothaire (1983; 1997). Combinatorics on Words. Encyclopedia of Mathematics and its Applications, Vol. 17 (2nd ed., Cambridge University Press).
    • Core Scope: Free monoids \(\Sigma^*\), primitive and conjugate words (\(uv \sim_{\text{conj}} vu\)), the Defect Theorem, Fine and Wilf’s Periodicity Theorem, the Chen–Fox–Lyndon Factorization Theorem, and the Critical Factorization Theorem (Césari–Vincent–Duval).
    • Textbook Mapping: Ch 01–Ch 02, Ch 05, Ch 30.
  2. [Lothaire02] — M. Lothaire (2002). Algebraic Combinatorics on Words. Encyclopedia of Mathematics and its Applications, Vol. 90, Cambridge University Press.
    • Core Scope: Definitive treatment of Sturmian words (Ch. 2, by Jean Berstel & Patrice Séébold: 6-way equivalence of subword complexity \(p(n) = n+1\), 1-balancedness, mechanical rotation words \(s_{\alpha,\rho}\), and central Sturmian words), episturmian words, and Plandowski’s theory of word equations (Ch. 9, by Christian Choffrut & Juhani Karhumäki).
    • Textbook Mapping: Ch 01, Ch 26, Ch 30.
  3. [Lothaire05] — M. Lothaire (2005). Applied Combinatorics on Words. Encyclopedia of Mathematics and its Applications, Vol. 105, Cambridge University Press.
    • Core Scope: Algorithmic word combinatorics, suffix automata, Lempel–Ziv factorizations, and autocorrelation analysis of pattern occurrences and waiting times in random strings.
    • Textbook Mapping: Ch 09, Ch 21, Ch 24–Ch 25.
  4. [CR94] / [CR02] — Maxime Crochemore & Wojciech Rytter (1994; 2002). Text Algorithms (Oxford University Press, 1994) & Jewels of Stringology: Text Algorithms (World Scientific, 2002).
    • Core Scope: Canonical exposition of the Weak Periodicity Lemma, dyadic \(O(\log N)\) border arithmetic progressions, the Three-Squares Lemma, Duval’s Lyndon factorization, Suffix Automata (SAM), Compact DAWGs (CDAWG), Two-Way \(O(1)\)-space matching, and Rytter’s Singular Words decomposition of Fibonacci strings.
    • Textbook Mapping: Ch 01–Ch 02, Ch 05, Ch 08–Ch 09, Ch 14, Ch 30.
  5. [Gusfield97] — Dan Gusfield (1997). Algorithms on Strings, Trees, and Sequences: Computer Science and Computational Biology. Cambridge University Press.
    • Core Scope: Suffix trees (Weiner, McCreight, Ukkonen), \(O(1)\) RMQ-LCA Longest Common Extensions (\(\operatorname{LCE}\)), Landau–Vishkin \(k\)-mismatch Kangaroo jumps and diagonal wavefronts, and generalized suffix structures.
    • Textbook Mapping: Ch 09, Ch 13, Ch 15, Ch 19.
  6. [FS09] / [GKP94] — Philippe Flajolet & Robert Sedgewick (2009), Analytic Combinatorics (Cambridge University Press, Ch. I.4 & III.7); Ronald L. Graham, Donald E. Knuth, & Oren Patashnik (1994), Concrete Mathematics (2nd ed., §8.4).
    • Core Scope: Symbolic language equations for pattern avoidance, autocorrelation polynomials \(C_{P,P}(z)\), bivariate cluster generating functions, and Conway’s Penney-Ante / Casino Betting Martingale (\(\mathbb{E}[T_S] = \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Borders}(S)} \sigma^k\)).
    • Textbook Mapping: Ch 24–Ch 25.

1.2 Periodicity, Border Theory, Lyndon Words & The Runs Theorem (Parts I–II, Ch 01–Ch 08)

  1. [FW65] — Nathan J. Fine & Herbert S. Wilf (1965). “Uniqueness theorems for periodic functions”, Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society, 16(1), 109–114.
    • Core Theorem: If \(S\) has periods \(p, q\) and length \(|S| \ge p + q - \gcd(p, q)\), then \(\gcd(p, q) \in \operatorname{Per}(S)\); at \(|S| = p + q - \gcd(p, q) - 1\), \(\gcd(p, q)\) fails uniquely on central Sturmian words, and below threshold there are \(p + q - |S|\) independent character orbits (Ch 01, Ch 26, Ch 30).
  2. [CMR99] — Maria Gabriella Castelli, Filippo Mignosi, & Antonio Restivo (1999). “Fine and Wilf’s theorem for three periods and a generalization of Sturmian words”, Theoretical Computer Science, 218(1), 83–94.
    • Core Theorem: Characterizes the exact three-period threshold \(f(p_1, p_2, p_3)\) via a three-way Euclidean step and introduces episturmian words as the extremal counterexamples (Ch 01, Ch 17).
  3. [Lyndon54] — Roger C. Lyndon (1954). “On Burnside’s problem”, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society, 77(2), 202–215.
    • Core Theorem: Introduces standard lexicographical words (Lyndon words \(w \in \mathcal{L}\)), defined as primitive words strictly smaller than all of their nontrivial cyclic shifts (equivalently, \(\forall v \sqsubset_{\text{suf}} w,\, w \prec_{\text{lex}} v\), Ch 05).
  4. [CFL58] — Kuo-Tsai Chen, Ralph H. Fox, & Roger C. Lyndon (1958). “Free differential calculus, IV. The quotient groups of the lower central series”, Annals of Mathematics, 68(1), 81–95.
    • Core Theorem: Proves the Chen–Fox–Lyndon (CFL) Theorem: every \(S \in \Sigma^+\) admits a unique factorization \(S = w_1 w_2 \cdots w_k\) (\(w_i \in \mathcal{L}\), \(w_1 \ge_{\text{lex}} \cdots \ge_{\text{lex}} w_k\)), where \(w_k\) is the smallest nonempty suffix of \(S\), and establishes the standard factorization \(w = uv\) (Ch 05, Ch 07).
  5. [FM78] / [FK86] — Harold Fredricksen & James Maiorana (1978), “Necklaces of beads in \(k\) colors and \(k\)-ary de Bruijn sequences”, Discrete Mathematics, 23(3), 207–210; Harold Fredricksen & Irving Kessler (1986), Discrete Mathematics, 61, 181–188.
    • Core Theorem: The FKM Algorithm—concatenating all Lyndon words of length dividing \(n\) in lexicographical order generates the lexicographically smallest \(k\)-ary De Bruijn sequence in \(O(1)\) amortized time per symbol (Ch 05, Ch 28).
  6. [Duval83] / [Duval88] — Jean-Pierre Duval (1983; 1988). “Factorizing words over an ordered alphabet”, Journal of Algorithms, 4(4), 363–381; “Génération d’une section des classes de conjugaison…”, Theoretical Computer Science, 60(3), 255–283.
    • Core Theorem: Computes \(\operatorname{CFL}(S)\) in \(< 2N\) comparisons and \(O(1)\) space via a 3-pointer pre-Lyndon invariant \(S[i..k-1] = w^d w'\), yielding \(O(N)\) Smallest Cyclic Shift (最小表示法) and next-Lyndon word stepping (Ch 05).
  7. [KRRW14] — Tomasz Kociumaka, Jakub Radoszewski, Wojciech Rytter, & Tomasz Waleń (2014 / 2016). “Maximum motif and minimal suffix queries in a text” / “Minimal Suffixes and Lyndon Words in Substrings”, CPM 2014 / Theoretical Computer Science.
    • Core Theorem: Proves the Significant Suffixes (显著后缀) Theorem: if suffixes \(u, v\) of \(S[l..r]\) satisfy \(|u| < |v| < 2|u|\) and \(u \sqsubset v\), then \(u = w u'\) and \(v = w^2 u'\) force \(\min(u'T, vT) < uT < \max(u'T, vT)\) for every \(T \in \Sigma^*\), bounding \(|\operatorname{Cand}_{\min}(S[l..r])| \le \lfloor\log_2(r-l+1)\rfloor + 1\) (Ch 06).
  8. [Barcelo90] / [HR03] — Hélène Barcelo (1990), “On the action of the symmetric group on the free Lie algebra”, JCTA 55, 93–129; Christophe Hohlweg & Christophe Reutenauer (2003), “Lyndon words, permutations and trees”, Discrete Mathematics 260, 153–158.
    • Core Theorem: Establishes the structural isomorphism between the Lyndon Tree of \(\$S\) (built by recursive standard factorization \(w = uv\)) and the Min-Cartesian Tree of \(\operatorname{rk}[2..N+1]\) (Ch 07).
  9. [FIRS16] / [Bille20] — Frantisek Franek, A. S. M. Sohidull Islam, M. Sohel Rahman, & William F. Smyth (2016), “Algorithms to compute the Lyndon Array”, Stringology 2016, 172–184; Philip Bille et al. (2020), CPM 2020.
    • Core Theorem: Proves the Next-Smaller-Suffix identity \(i + \lambda[i] = \operatorname{nsv}_{\operatorname{rk}}[i]\) for the Lyndon array, enabling \(O(N)\) monotonic-stack construction over \(\operatorname{rk}_<\) and \(\operatorname{rk}_>\) (Ch 07).
  10. [CR95] / [FS98] / [BL22] — Maxime Crochemore & Wojciech Rytter (1995), “Squares, cubes, and time-space efficient string searching”, Algorithmica, 13(5), 405–425; Aviezri S. Fraenkel & Jamie Simpson (1998), JCTA 82, 112–120; Srećko Brlek & Shuo Li (2022).
    • Core Theorem: Proves the Three-Squares Lemma (\(u^2 \sqsubset v^2 \sqsubset w^2\) with \(u\) primitive \(\implies |w| \ge |u| + |v|\)), implying at most \(2\) distinct squares have their rightmost occurrence starting at index \(i\) and bounding distinct squares by \(\le 2N - 2\) (sharpened to \(< N\) by Brlek–Li, Ch 08).
  11. [KK99] — Roman Kolpakov & Gregory Kucherov (1999). “Finding maximal repetitions in a word in linear time”, Proceedings of FOCS 1999, 596–604.
    • Core Theorem: Proves \(|\mathcal{R}(S)| = O(N)\) and \(\sum_{R \in \mathcal{R}(S)} e(R) = O(N)\), and gives the first \(O(N)\) Runs enumeration algorithm (Ch 08).
  12. [Bannai17] — Hideo Bannai, Tomohiro I, Shunsuke Inenaga, Yuto Nakashima, Masayuki Takeda, & Kohei Tsuruta (2015 / 2017). “The ‘Runs’ Theorem”, SODA 2015, 324–332 / SIAM Journal on Computing, 46(5), 1501–1514.
    • Core Theorem: Resolves the Runs Conjecture (\(|\mathcal{R}(S)| \le N - 1 < N\) and \(\sum_{R \in \mathcal{R}(S)} e(R) < 3N\)) by assigning each run \(R = (l, r, p)\) a unique interior Lyndon root \(i \in [l+1, l+p]\) under mismatch order \(S[r+1] <_R S[r+1-p]\), yielding \(O(N)\) Lyndon-array + LCE Runs enumeration (Ch 07–Ch 08).

1.3 Suffix Structures, SA-IS, Internal Pattern Matching & Palindromic Automata (Parts III–IV, Ch 09–Ch 17)

  1. [Weiner73] / [McCreight76] / [Ukkonen95] — Peter Weiner (1973), SWAT (FOCS) 1973, 1–11; Edward M. McCreight (1976), JACM 23(2), 262–272; Esko Ukkonen (1995), Algorithmica 14(3), 249–260.
    • Core Theorem: The classic triad of \(O(N)\) suffix tree constructions; Weiner’s right-to-left suffix links correspond directly to the Link Tree transitions of \(\operatorname{SAM}(S^R)\) (Ch 09, Ch 14).
  2. [Blumer85] — Anselm Blumer, J. Blumer, David Haussler, Andrzej Ehrenfeucht, M. T. Chen, & Joel Seiferas (1985). “The smallest automaton recognizing the subwords of a text”, Theoretical Computer Science, 40, 31–55.
    • Core Theorem: Establishes the Suffix Automaton (SAM / DAWG) state bound \(|V| \le 2N - 1\), transition bound \(|E| \le 3N - 4\), laminar \(\operatorname{endpos}\) hierarchy, and exact reversal duality \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}(\operatorname{SAM}(S)) \cong \mathcal{T}_{\text{ST}}(S^R)\) (Ch 09–Ch 11).
  3. [Blumer87] / [Inenaga05] / [Stoye00] / [Maass00] — Anselm Blumer et al. (1987), JACM 34(3), 578–595; Shunsuke Inenaga et al. (2001 / 2005), Discrete Applied Math. 146(2), 156–179; Jens Stoye (2000) & Moritz G. Maaß (2000), CPM 2000.
    • Core Theorem: Formalizes the Compact DAWG (CDAWG) (contracting unary SAM states into maximal repeats \(\overleftrightarrow{w}\)) and the bi-directional Symmetric Compact DAWG (SCDAWG) via two-sided closure commutativity \(\operatorname{cl}_L(\operatorname{cl}_R(u)) = \operatorname{cl}_R(\operatorname{cl}_L(u))\) (Ch 14).
  4. [DS87] — Paul F. Dietz & Daniel D. Sleator (1987). “Two algorithms for maintaining order in a list”, Proceedings of STOC 1987, 365–372.
    • Core Theorem: Provides the \(O(1)\) dynamic order-maintenance tag invariant (realized via Scapegoat BSTs with 64-bit interval tags \([L_u, R_u]\)) powering hash-free Suffix Balanced Trees (后缀平衡树) (Ch 14).
  5. [Kasai01] / [AKO04] — Toru Kasai, Gunho Lee, Hiroki Arimura, Setsuo Arikawa, & Kunsoo Park (2001), CPM 2001, LNCS 2089, 181–192; Mohamed Ibrahim Abouelhoda, Stefan Kurtz, & Enno Ohlebusch (2004), J. Discrete Algorithms, 2(1), 53–86.
    • Core Theorem: Proves Kasai’s \(h[\operatorname{rk}[i]] \ge h[\operatorname{rk}[i-1]] - 1\) for \(O(N)\) LCP construction and the Three-Way Isomorphism between internal nodes of \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{ST}}(S)\) and the Min-Cartesian Tree of \(\operatorname{height}[2..N]\) (Ch 12–Ch 13).
  6. [NZC09] / [Fischer11] — Ge Nong, Sen Zhang, & Wai Hong Chan (2009 / 2011), “Two efficient algorithms for linear time suffix array construction”, DCC 2009 / IEEE Trans. Computers 60(10), 1471–1484; Johannes Fischer (2011), “Inducing the LCP-Array”, WADS 2011, LNCS 6844, 374–385.
    • Core Theorem: Introduces SA-IS (\(L/S\) and LMS Induced Sorting) in \(O(N)\) time via prefix-free LMS substrings and 3-pass bucket inducing, and augments the inducing passes to compute height (LCP) simultaneously in \(O(N)\) time (Ch 12).
  7. [Kociumaka15] / [Kociumaka18] / [KRRW24] — Tomasz Kociumaka, Jakub Radoszewski, Wojciech Rytter, & Tomasz Waleń (2015 / 2024), “Internal Pattern Matching Queries in a Text and Applications”, SODA 2015, 532–551 / SIAM J. Comput., 53(5), 2024; Tomasz Kociumaka (2018), PhD Dissertation, University of Warsaw.
    • Core Theorem: Establishes the Basic Substring Dictionary (BSD) and Bounded-Ratio Internal Pattern Matching (IPM) theorem: when \(|V| < 2|U|\), \(\operatorname{Occ}(U, V)\) forms a single arithmetic progression of step \(\operatorname{per}(U)\), enabling \(O(\log N)\) internal border queries, \(O(1)\) internal period queries, and \(O(1)\) substring rank/concatenation comparisons (Ch 15).
  8. [DJP01] / [GJWZ09] / [FGKK14] — Xavier Droubay, Jacques Justin, & Giuseppe Pirillo (2001), TCS 255, 539–553; Amy Glen et al. (2009), EJC 30(2), 510–531; Gabriele Fici, Travis Gagie, Juha Kärkkäinen, & Dominik Kempa (2014), “A subquadratic algorithm for minimum palindromic factorization”, J. Discrete Algorithms, 28, 41–48.
    • Core Theorem: Proves the Droubay–Pirillo \(N+1\) Rich Word Bound (\(|\operatorname{Pal}(S)| \le |S| + 1\)) and the Shift-Overlap Invariant accelerating palindromic factorization DP to \(O(N \log N)\) via slink AP chains (Ch 04, Ch 17).
  9. [RS15] / [RS17] / [RS18] / [BKRS17] — Mikhail Rubinchik & Arseny M. Shur (2015 / 2017 / 2018), “EERTREE: An efficient data structure for processing palindromes in strings”, European J. Combinatorics, 68, 249–265 (arXiv:1506.04862) & “Counting palindromes in substrings”, SPIRE 2017; Kirill Borozdin et al. (2017), CPM 2017.
    • Core Theorem: Formalizes the dual-root Palindromic Tree (PAM / Eertree), unamortized \(O(1)\) direct fail links (quick_link[u][c]), 4-Way Deque PAM, and Range Distinct Palindrome Queries via slink periodic occurrence telescoping (Ch 04, Ch 16–Ch 17).
