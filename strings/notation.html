1. Indexing Conventions, Alphabets, and Fundamental Word Operations

Stringology sits at the crossroads of combinatorics on words—where 1-based closed intervals \(S[l..r]\) align prefix lengths with right endpoints—and polynomial/modular algorithms—where 0-based indices \(0, \dots, N-1\) align with FFT/NTT exponents and residue classes. We standardize on 1-based closed indexing \(S[1..N]\) throughout the textbook, with explicit 0-based exceptions only where polynomial degrees or modular residues require them.

ImportantCanonical Textbook Indexing Contract (1-Based Closed \(S[1..N]\) vs. 0-Based Polynomial/Residue Offsets)
  1. Default 1-Based Closed Indexing (\(S[1..N]\)):
    • A string \(S\) of length \(N = |S|\) is indexed as \(S = S[1]S[2]\cdots S[N]\).
    • For \(1 \le l \le r \le N\), the contiguous substring \(S[l..r] = S[l]S[l+1]\cdots S[r]\) has length \(|S[l..r]| = r - l + 1\).
    • Whenever \(l > r\), \(S[l..r] = \varepsilon\) denotes the unique empty word of length \(|\varepsilon| = 0\).
    • Why 1-based closed intervals eliminate off-by-one bugs:
      • The \(i\)-th prefix \(S[1..i]\) has length exactly \(i\) (so \(\pi[i]\) is the longest proper border length of the length-\(i\) prefix).
      • A proper border of length \(b \in [0, i-1]\) of \(S[1..i]\) satisfies \(S[1..b] = S[i-b+1..i]\), corresponding to period \(p = i - b\).
      • A substring of length \(L\) ending at position \(e \in \operatorname{endpos}_S(u)\) starts at \(s = e - L + 1\), so it lies completely inside \(S[l..r]\) if and only if \(e \in [l + L - 1, r]\) (Ch 10).
  2. When 0-Based Indexing Is Used:

    • Polynomial / FFT String Matching (Ch 18–Ch 19): Pattern \(P[0..M-1]\) and text \(T[0..N-1]\) are indexed from \(0\) so character \(P[j]\) maps directly to monomial \(x^j\), and index-reflected pattern \(P^R[k] = P[M-1-k]\) places alignment shift \(i\) at Cauchy product degree \(M - 1 + i\): \[\sum_{j=0}^{M-1} f(P[j])\,g(T[i+j]) = [x^{M-1+i}] \left(\sum_{k=0}^{M-1} f(P[M-1-k]) x^k\right)\left(\sum_{t=0}^{N-1} g(T[t]) x^t\right).\]

    • Modular Residue Classes (Ch 01, Ch 03): Residue vertices in \(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\) are indexed \(0, 1, \dots, m-1\).

    • 2-Adic Popcount Sequences & Word-RAM Bit-Blocks (Ch 02, Ch 23, Ch 30): The Prouhet–Thue–Morse infinite word \(T[0..\infty)\) with \(T[n] = \operatorname{popcount}(n) \bmod 2\), 64-bit machine word-block arrays VP[0..W-1], and \(0\)-indexed Unit-Monge permutation border ports \(P[0..K-1]\).

2. Periodicity, Border Algebra, and Series-Link (slink) Notation (Part I)

Every proper border of \(S[1..N]\) is complementary to a weak period of \(S[1..N]\). All periodicity and border DP chapters (Ch 01–Ch 04, Ch 15) build upon three foundational identities:

  1. Period–Border Duality (Ch 01): For \(b \in [0, N-1]\) and \(p = N - b \in [1, N]\), \[b \in \operatorname{Border}(S) \iff S[1..b] = S[N-b+1..N] \iff \forall i \in [1, N-p],\; S[i] = S[i+p] \iff p = N - b \in \operatorname{Per}(S).\]

  2. Fine–Wilf Threshold & Sub-Threshold Orbit Identity (Ch 01): If \(p, q \in \operatorname{Per}(S)\) and \(N \ge p + q - \gcd(p, q)\), then \(\gcd(p, q) \in \operatorname{Per}(S)\). Below threshold (\(\max(p, q) \le N < p + q - \gcd(p, q)\)), the period constraints partition \(\{1, \dots, N\}\) into exactly \(C(N; p, q) = p + q - N\) independent character orbits.

  3. Half-Open slink Partition & Shift-Overlap Identity (Ch 02, Ch 04): By the Weak Periodicity Lemma, all borders/palindromic suffixes of state \(u\) of length \(\ge \lceil\operatorname{len}[u]/2\rceil\) share period step \(d = \operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{len}[u] - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{fail}[u]]\). Grouping contiguous ancestors with equal \(\operatorname{diff}\) into half-open chains \((\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\) yields \(\le \lceil\log_2 N\rceil\) chains whose left endpoints \(\mathcal{I}(u, r) = \{r - \ell : \ell \in (\operatorname{slink}[u], u]\}\) satisfy the Shift-Overlap Identity: \[\operatorname{diff}[u] = \operatorname{diff}[\operatorname{fail}[u]] = d \implies \mathcal{I}(u, r) = \mathcal{I}(\operatorname{fail}[u], r - d) \sqcup \bigl\{r - \operatorname{len}[\operatorname{slink}[u]] - d\bigr\}.\]

3. Lyndon Theory, Significant Suffixes, and Runs Notation (Part II)

In Ch 05–Ch 08, lexicographical comparisons are decomposed into prefix containment (\(\sqsubset_{\text{pref}}\)) and strong mismatch order (\(\prec\)):

  • Standard vs. Strong Lexicographical Order (Ch 05–Ch 06): \[U <_{\text{lex}} V \iff (U \sqsubset_{\text{pref}} V) \;\lor\; (U \prec_{\text{lex}} V), \qquad U \prec_{\text{lex}} V \iff \exists k \le \min(|U|, |V|) : U[1..k-1] = V[1..k-1] \land U[k] < V[k].\] When clear from context, \(\prec_{\text{lex}}\) is written \(\prec\). Crucially, strong order is invariant under arbitrary right-extensions (\(U \prec V \implies UT \prec VT\;\forall T \in \Sigma^*\)), whereas proper-prefix containment \(U \sqsubset_{\text{pref}} V\) can flip when a right-extension \(T\) is appended.

  • Reverse-Alphabet Order \(\overline{\prec}\) (or \(<_>, \prec_>\)) (Ch 07–Ch 08): \[U \;\overline{\prec}\; V \quad(\text{also written } U \prec_> V) \iff V \prec_{\text{lex}} U, \qquad U <_> V \iff (U \sqsubset_{\text{pref}} V) \;\lor\; (U \;\overline{\prec}\; V).\] Note that under \(<_>\), a proper prefix \(U \sqsubset_{\text{pref}} V\) is still smaller than \(V\)! Thus \(\operatorname{rk}_>\) must be computed on the character-inverted string \(\overline{S}[t] = \sigma + 1 - S[t]\) with terminal sentinel \(\$ = 0 = -\infty\) fixed, never by negating \(-\operatorname{rk}_<\) (BP-07).

  • Tandem-Annihilation of Candidate Suffixes (Ch 06): If \(u, v\) are suffixes of \(S[l..r]\) with \(|u| < |v| < 2|u|\) and \(u \sqsubset_{\text{pref}} v\), then \(u = w u'\) and \(v = w^2 u'\) (\(|w| = |v|-|u|\)), so for every right-extension \(T \in \Sigma^*\): \[\min(u'T, vT) <_{\text{lex}} uT <_{\text{lex}} \max(u'T, vT),\] eliminating \(u\) and bounding \(|\operatorname{Cand}_{\min}(S[l..r])| \le \lfloor\log_2(r-l+1)\rfloor + 1\).

4. Suffix Structures, Palindromic Automata, and Internal Queries (Parts III–IV)

Across Ch 09–Ch 17, suffix trees, suffix automata, enhanced suffix arrays, and internal dictionaries are unified by three structural dualities:

  1. Three-Way Suffix Tree Isomorphism (Ch 09, Ch 13): \[\mathcal{T}_{\text{ST}}(S) \;\cong\; \mathcal{T}_{\text{link}}\bigl(\operatorname{SAM}(S^R)\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Min-CartesianTree}\bigl(\operatorname{height}[2..N]\bigr).\]

  2. Substring Containment Clipping on \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) (Ch 10): A string \(P\) of length \(L\) belonging to \(\operatorname{SAM}(S)\) state \(u\) occurs inside \(S[l..r]\) if and only if \[L \le r - l + 1 \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{endpos}_S(u) \cap [l + L - 1,\, r] \neq \emptyset.\]

  3. Bounded-Ratio Internal Pattern Matching (IPM) Single-AP Theorem (Ch 15): For any substrings \(U, V\) of \(S\) with \(|U| \le |V| < 2|U|\), all occurrences of \(U\) inside \(V\) overlap on a common core of length \(> 0\) and form a single arithmetic progression with common difference \(d = \operatorname{per}(U)\).
