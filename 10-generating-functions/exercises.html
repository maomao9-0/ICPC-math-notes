10 · Practice — Generating Functions

Each solved exercise below isolates one modelling decision from the chapter: sequence versus multiset, where the initial conditions live in a rational function, and extracting a single Lagrange coefficient. They are small enough to check by hand, which is the point — a wrong model is invisible in code.

Solved exercises

A. Sequences and multisets

Count objects of total size four whose parts have sizes one or two, first when order matters and then when it does not.

TipHint

Compare \(1/(1-x-x^2)\) with \(1/((1-x)(1-x^2))\).

NoteSolution

Ordered sequences are 1111, 112, 121, 211, 22: five. Unordered objects are 1111, 112, 22: three. The first GF chooses a sequence of parts, while the second independently chooses the multiplicity of each part size. Coefficients through degree four are \((1,1,2,3,5)\) and \((1,1,2,2,3)\) respectively.

B. Recover initial conditions

Find the first four coefficients of \((2+x)/(1-x-x^2)\) and state the recurrence.

TipHint

Compare coefficients in \((1-x-x^2)A=2+x\) before using the recurrence.

NoteSolution

\(a_0=2\) and \(a_1-a_0=1\), so \(a_1=3\). For \(n\ge2\), \(a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}\). The requested prefix is \(2,3,5,8\). For a huge \(n\), use Bostan–Mori with this numerator and denominator; replacing the numerator by the Fibonacci numerator would change the sequence.

C. Ordered forests

Let \(T=x/(1-T)\) count nonempty plane rooted trees by vertices. Count ordered pairs of such trees with a total of five vertices.

TipHint

Use Lagrange with \(n=5,k=2\), or convolve the small tree counts.

NoteSolution

\([x^5]T^2=\frac25[t^3](1-t)^{-5}=\frac25\binom73=14\). The independent check is \(T=x+x^2+2x^3+5x^4+\cdots\), giving \(1\cdot5+1\cdot2+2\cdot1+5\cdot1=14\). Modulo five the displayed division cannot be used directly; the integer answer may be reduced afterward, or the convolution may be computed there.

4. Codeforces 960G — Bandit Blues (Mastery)

  • Statement · official tutorial · English/Russian
  • Train: permutation records, unsigned Stirling numbers, balanced polynomial products.
Hint 1 Condition on the position/value of the global maximum.
Hint 2 Left-to-right maxima and cycles of a permutation share the same Stirling distribution.
Hint 3 For \(A,B\ge1\), the answer simplifies to \(\binom{A+B-2}{A-1}\left[{N-1\atop A+B-2}\right]\). Obtain the Stirling coefficient from the product \(x(x+1)\cdots(x+N-2)\) using a balanced product tree. Handle \(N=1\) with the empty product.
NoteWhy the simplification works

Cut at the global maximum. On each side, break the permutation at its records (reading the right side backwards). Each record starts a block with its largest element first; viewing the block cyclically is a permutation cycle. The remaining \(N-1\) labels therefore form \(A+B-2\) cycles. Choose which \(A-1\) cycles belong on the left; record order reconstructs the two sides uniquely. This gives the binomial factor and the first-kind Stirling coefficient. If either \(A\) or \(B\) is zero the answer is zero for a nonempty permutation.
