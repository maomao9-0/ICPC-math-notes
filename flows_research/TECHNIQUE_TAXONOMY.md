### 1.3 The 8 Major Cross-Cohort Convention & Discrepancy Reconciliations

A critical value of the Cohort 3 synthesis is resolving **eight notorious notation and convention conflicts** that cause silent off-by-one, sign-inversion, or orientation bugs when competitive programmers read editorials across English (`[EN]`), Chinese (`[ZH]`), and Japanese (`[JA]`) sources:

1. **Min-Cut Binary Polarity Convention ($x_S = 1, x_T = 0$ vs. `燃やす埋める` $y_S = 0, y_T = 1$)**:
   - **Conflict**: Standard English/Chinese closure literature (`Picard 1976`, `胡伯涛 2007`) sets $x_i = 1 \iff i \in S$ ($x_S = 1, x_T = 0$), so a directed edge $u \xrightarrow{c} v$ charges $c \cdot x_u (1 - x_v)$. Japanese `燃やす埋める` treatises (`noshi91`, `maspypy`, `tatyam`) often assign label $0$ to $S$ ("burn") and label $1$ to $T$ ("bury"), setting $y_i = 1 \iff i \in T$ ($y_S = 0, y_T = 1$), so $u \xrightarrow{c} v$ charges $c \cdot (1 - y_u) y_v$.
   - **Cohort 3 Standard**: We standardize on $x_i = [i \in S] \in \{0, 1\}$ ($x_S = 1, x_T = 0$), where the cut monomial is $x_u(1 - x_v)$ and the implication $x_u = 1 \implies x_v = 1$ compiles to $u \xrightarrow{\infty} v$. Section 2.3 provides the exact bijective Rosetta Stone ($y_i = 1 - x_i$).
2. **Multi-State Unary Threshold Indexing ($z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k]$ vs. $z_{i,k} = [x_i > k]$)**:
   - **Conflict**: Cohort 1 and `[HNOI2013] 切糕` editorials index chain nodes by strict inequality $z_{i,k} = [x_i > k]$ ($k \in \{0, \dots, K\}$), whereas Cohort 2 and `noshi91` index by weak inequality $z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k]$ ($k \in \{1, \dots, K+1\}$). Mixing the two shifts cross-chain distance constraints $x_i - x_j \le D$ by $1$ level!
   - **Cohort 3 Standard**: In weak-inequality indexing $z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k]$ with $z_{i,1} = 1$ ($S$) and $z_{i,K+1} = 0$ ($T$):
     - Series Chain (`切糕`): edge $u_{i,k} \to u_{i,k+1}$ has capacity $c_i(k)$ (severed when $z_{i,k} = 1, z_{i,k+1} = 0 \iff x_i = k$), with reverse $\infty$-edge $u_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} u_{i,k}$, and constraint $x_i - x_j \le D$ ($x_i \ge k \implies x_j \ge k - D$) compiles to $u_{i,k} \xrightarrow{\infty} u_{j, k-D}$.
     - Parallel Closure (`ABC 326 G`, `ARC 142 E`, Ishikawa): $K-1$ free threshold bits $z_{i,2}, \dots, z_{i,K}$ with $u_{i,k} \xrightarrow{\infty} u_{i,k-1}$ and marginal cost $\delta_i(k) = c_i(k) - c_i(k-1)$ on $u_{i,k} \to T$ (if $\delta_i(k) > 0$) or $S \to u_{i,k}$ (if $\delta_i(k) < 0$).
3. **Planar Dual $90^\circ$ Clockwise (CW) vs. Counter-Clockwise (CCW) Rotation in `[NOI2010] 海拔`**:
   - **Conflict**: Cohort 1 placed $S^*$ at Bottom-Left and $T^*$ at Top-Right, deriving a $90^\circ$ Counter-Clockwise rule; Cohort 2 placed $S^*$ at Top-Right and $T^*$ at Bottom-Left, deriving a $90^\circ$ Clockwise rule.
   - **Cohort 3 Standard (Universal Left/Right Bank Law)**: A directed planar $s$-$t$ cut only pays for primal arcs directed from $S$ ($h=0$) to $T$ ($h=1$). As you walk along the dual path from $S^*$ to $T^*$, if the primal source $s$ lies on your **Left Bank** and primal sink $t$ lies on your **Right Bank**, then a primal directed edge $e = (u \to v)$ is crossed in the forward $S \to T$ direction if and only if its dual arc $e^*$ crosses your path from **Left to Right** ($90^\circ$ CCW rotation of $u \to v$ when $S$ is Left; $90^\circ$ CW rotation when $S$ is Right).
4. **Two-Sided Interval Bounds $[L_t, R_t]$: Forward-DAG Upper-Slack vs. Backward-Cyclic Lower-Slack (`[NEERC 2016] Delight for a Cat`)**:
   - **Conflict**: Writing $\sum_{j \ni t} x_j - y_t = L_t$ with $y_t \in [0, R_t - L_t]$ gives $y_{t-1} - y_t$ in $E_t - E_{t-1}$, creating a **backward spine** $t \to t-1$ with cycles and negative costs requiring Mandatory Initial Saturation. Writing $\sum_{j \ni t} x_j + \tilde{y}_t = R_t$ with upper slack $\tilde{y}_t = R_t - \sum_{j \ni t} x_j \in [0, R_t - L_t]$ gives $\tilde{y}_t - \tilde{y}_{t-1}$ in $E_t - E_{t-1}$, creating a **forward spine** $t-1 \to t$ so the entire graph is an **acyclic DAG**!
   - **Cohort 3 Standard**: Both networks are algebraically isomorphic via variable substitution $\tilde{y}_t = (R_t - L_t) - y_t$ (which is literally the residual reversal of saturating $y_t$!). Whenever upper bounds $R_t < \infty$ exist, prefer the **Forward-DAG Upper-Slack** formulation so Johnson potentials $\pi_0$ can be initialized in $O(V + E)$ via topological DP without SPFA or negative-cycle elimination.
5. **Bipartite Walk Game Turn-Order Polarity (`[JSOI2009] 游戏` Variant A vs. Variant B)**:
   - **Conflict**: Does the starting player win when $v_0$ is in *every* maximum matching ($\mathcal{O} \cup \mathcal{U}$), or when $v_0$ is *omitted* from some maximum matching ($\mathcal{E}$)?
   - **Cohort 3 Standard**: Always check **who makes the first edge move** after $v_0$ is chosen:
     - **Variant A (Chooser picks $v_0$, Mover makes the first edge step — `[JSOI2009] 游戏`)**: Mover wins iff $v_0$ is in every maximum matching; thus **Chooser wins iff $v_0 \in \mathcal{E}$ (Avoidable)**!

6. **Imai–Asano Rectilinear Polygon Partitioning $\pm h$ Genus Sign Identity (`UVa 1711`)**:
   - **Conflict**: Cohort 1 wrote $R_{\min} = \frac{N}{2} - 1 + h - \lvert I^* \rvert$ ($+h$ for $h$ interior holes), whereas classical computational geometry papers write $R_{\min} = 1 - h + c_{\text{conc}} - \lvert I^* \rvert$ ($-h$).

Substituting $c_{\text{conc}}$ into $1 - h + c_{\text{conc}} - \lvert I^* \rvert$ yields $\frac{N}{2} - 1 + h - \lvert I^* \rvert$.
7. **Hu Botao's `[BZOJ 2039] 人员雇佣` $2E_{ij}$ Cross-Edge Coefficient**:

Summing over all $j$ gives $S \to i$ capacity $\sum_j E_{ij}$ and directed cross-edge $i \to j$ capacity $2 E_{ij}$.

### 2.1 Flow & Circulation Polyhedra, Cut Lattices, Disjoint Paths & Lower-Bound Feasibility Operators (Waves 1A & 1B)

The foundation of network flow theory rests on viewing a directed graph $G = (V, E)$ through its **node-arc incidence matrix** $B \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^{|V| \times |E|}$, where column $e = (u, v)$ has $B_{u,e} = +1$ (or $-1$ depending on outflow/inflow sign convention; we standardize on **Net Outflow**: $(Bf)_u = \sum_{e \in \delta^+(u)} f_e - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(u)} f_e$).

#### 2.1.2 Undirected Edges, Vertex Splitting & Menger's 4 Connectivity Operators

1. **Undirected Edge Compilation (`Antiparallel Pair` vs. `Single Residual Entry`)**:
   - *Algebraic Target*: An undirected edge $\{u, v\}$ of capacity $c$ permits net flow $f_{uv} \in [-c, +c]$ at cost/capacity $|f_{uv}| \le c$.
   - *Graph Compilation*: Replace $\{u, v\}$ with two directed arcs $(u, v)$ and $(v, u)$, each with capacity $c$. In an adjacency-list implementation, these can be fused by initializing forward edge $(u, v)$ with capacity $c$ and its paired reverse residual edge $(v, u)$ with initial capacity $c$ (instead of $0$).
   - *Cycle Cancellation Invariant*: If solver augments $f_1 > 0$ on $(u, v)$ and $f_2 > 0$ on $(v, u)$, the net physical flow is $|f_1 - f_2| \le c$ in the dominant direction, with $\min(f_1, f_2)$ cancelling as a zero-net-effect local 2-cycle.
2. **Vertex Capacity & Vertex-Cut Compilation (`In-Out Node Splitting`)**:
   - *Algebraic Target*: Vertex $v \in V \setminus \{s, t\}$ has throughput limit $\sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f_e \le c(v)$ (or can be deleted in a vertex cut for cost $c(v)$).
   - *Graph Compilation*: Split $v$ into $v_{\text{in}}$ and $v_{\text{out}}$ connected by a directed internal bridge $(v_{\text{in}}, v_{\text{out}})$ with capacity $c(v)$. Redirect all incoming edges $(u, v) \mapsto (u_{\text{out}}, v_{\text{in}})$ (capacity $\infty$ for pure vertex cuts, or edge capacity $c(u, v)$ for mixed cuts) and all outgoing edges $(v, w) \mapsto (v_{\text{out}}, w_{\text{in}})$.
   - *Undirected Vertex Cut Trap*: To model an undirected edge $\{u, v\}$ inside a vertex-split graph, you MUST add two directed infinite-capacity arcs $(u_{\text{out}}, v_{\text{in}})$ and $(v_{\text{out}}, u_{\text{in}})$—never connect $u_{\text{in}} \leftrightarrow v_{\text{in}}$ or make $(v_{\text{in}}, v_{\text{out}})$ undirected!
3. **Menger's Theorem (4 Variants)**:
   - **Directed Edge-Disjoint ($s$-$t$)**: Unit capacity $c(u, v) = 1$ on all directed arcs; max paths $=$ min edge cut. Complexity: $O(E\sqrt{E})$ or $O(V^{2/3}E)$.
   - **Undirected Edge-Disjoint ($s$-$t$)**: Antiparallel unit arcs $(u, v), (v, u)$ of capacity $1$. Extract paths from net flow $f_{\text{net}}(u, v) = \max(0, f(u, v) - f(v, u)) \in \{0, 1\}$.
   - **Directed Internally Vertex-Disjoint ($s$-$t$)**: Split every $v \notin \{s, t\}$ with $c(v_{\text{in}}, v_{\text{out}}) = 1$, and set all original edges $(u_{\text{out}}, v_{\text{in}})$ to capacity $\infty$ (or $1$). Complexity: $O(E\sqrt{V})$.
   - **Undirected Internally Vertex-Disjoint ($s$-$t$)**: Same vertex split $c(v_{\text{in}}, v_{\text{out}}) = 1$ for $v \notin \{s, t\}$, with directed arcs $(u_{\text{out}}, v_{\text{in}})$ and $(v_{\text{out}}, u_{\text{in}})$ of capacity $\infty$ for each undirected $\{u, v\} \in E$.

#### 2.1.3 Lower-Bound Demand Flows & Hoffman's Circulation Theorem (All 4 Variants)

Consider a network where every arc $e = (u, v) \in E$ has a mandatory lower bound $l(e)$ and upper bound $u(e)$ ($0 \le l(e) \le u(e)$), and optionally a node balance vector $b(v)$. We apply the **Affine Shift Substitution** $f(e) = l(e) + f'(e)$, where $0 \le f'(e) \le u(e) - l(e)$. Substituting into flow conservation $\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} f(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f(e) = 0$ yields the residual conservation law:
$$\sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} f'(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} f'(e) = \underbrace{\sum_{e \in \delta^-(v)} l(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(v)} l(e)}_{M(v) \;:=\; \text{in-lower}(v) - \text{out-lower}(v)}$$
Intuitively, $l(e)$ units of flow are pre-shipped along every edge $e = (u, v)$, depositing $M(v)$ net excess units at vertex $v$ that the residual flow $f'$ must now carry *out* of $v$ (if $M(v) > 0$) or absorb *into* $v$ (if $M(v) < 0$). Create a super-source $S_{\text{sup}}$ and super-sink $T_{\text{sup}}$:
- For each $v$ with $M(v) > 0$, add auxiliary demand edge $(S_{\text{sup}}, v)$ with capacity $M(v)$.
- For each $v$ with $M(v) < 0$, add auxiliary demand edge $(v, T_{\text{sup}})$ with capacity $-M(v)$.
- Define total positive demand $D_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{v : M(v) > 0} M(v) = \sum_{v : M(v) < 0} (-M(v))$.

| Variant | Construction & Auxiliary Return Edge | Feasibility & Optimality Criterion | Reconstruction & Deletion Trap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. No $s$ or $t$ exists. | Feasible iff $\text{MaxFlow}(S_{\text{sup}} \to T_{\text{sup}}) = D_{\text{tot}}$ (every auxiliary arc out of $S_{\text{sup}}$ is completely saturated). | Set $f(e) = l(e) + f'(e)$ for all $e \in E$. |

Total Min-Flow: $F_{\min} = f_0 - \Delta f_{\text{back}}$. | Equivalently, before adding $(t, s)$ in Phase 1, run $S_{\text{sup}} \to T_{\text{sup}}$, then add $(t, s; 0, \infty)$ and run $S_{\text{sup}} \to T_{\text{sup}}$ again; the flow on $(t, s)$ equals $F_{\min}$ directly! |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Hoffman's Circulation Theorem (1960) & Unfeasibility Certificates**:
> A circulation network $(V, E, l, u)$ admits a feasible circulation if and only if for every vertex subset $S \subseteq V$, the lower-bound demand entering $S$ does not exceed the upper-bound capacity leaving $S$:
> $$\sum_{e \in \delta^-(S)} l(e) \le \sum_{e \in \delta^+(S)} u(e) \quad \forall S \subseteq V \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \sum_{e \in \delta^+(S)} u(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(S)} l(e) \ge 0$$
> *Proof via Min-Cut on $(S_{\text{sup}}, T_{\text{sup}})$*: In Variant 1, any cut $(\{S_{\text{sup}}\} \cup S,\; \{T_{\text{sup}}\} \cup \bar{S})$ has capacity $\sum_{v \notin S} \max(0, M(v)) + \sum_{v \in S} \max(0, -M(v)) + \sum_{e \in \delta^+(S)} (u(e) - l(e)) = D_{\text{tot}} + \sum_{e \in \delta^+(S)} u(e) - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(S)} l(e)$. Thus the minimum $S_{\text{sup}}$-$T_{\text{sup}}$ cut is $\ge D_{\text{tot}}$ iff Hoffman's condition holds for all $S \subseteq V$. When infeasible ($\text{MaxFlow} < D_{\text{tot}}$), the reachable set $S = \{ v \in V \mid S_{\text{sup}} \rightsquigarrow_{G'_f} v \}$ in the final residual graph is an explicit $O(V + E)$ **Hoffman Violation Certificate** with $\sum_{e \in \delta^-(S)} l(e) > \sum_{e \in \delta^+(S)} u(e)$.

### 2.2 Bipartite, Poset & General Graph Duality Operators (Waves 2A & 2B)

#### 2.2.1 Bipartite Matching, König's Duality, Gallai Identities & Dulmage–Mendelsohn Decomposition

Let $G = (L \sqcup R, E)$ be a bipartite graph with vertex weights $w : L \sqcup R \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ (unit weights $w \equiv 1$ in the unweighted case). Build the canonical $s$-$t$ network by adding source $S$, sink $T$, arcs $(S, u)$ of capacity $w(u)$ for $u \in L$, arcs $(v, T)$ of capacity $w(v)$ for $v \in R$, and directed unit-or-infinite bridges $(u, v)$ of capacity $\infty$ for every $(u, v) \in E$.

1. **König's Theorem (1931) & Minimum Weighted Vertex Cover (MWVC)**:
   - Any finite-capacity $S$-$T$ cut $(V_S, V_T)$ cannot sever any $\infty$-capacity edge $(u, v) \in E$, forcing $[u \in V_S \implies v \in V_S]$. Thus the set of severed $S/T$-incident edges $C = (L \cap V_T) \cup (R \cap V_S)$ intercepts every edge $(u, v) \in E$ and forms a valid **Vertex Cover** of weight $\sum_{x \in C} w(x) = \text{cap}(V_S, V_T)$.
   - **Constructive Extraction Recipe**: Compute Max-Flow $f^*$. Let $Z = \{ x \in L \sqcup R \mid S \rightsquigarrow_{G_{f^*}} x \}$ be the set of vertices reachable from $S$ via residual paths (equivalently, alternating paths from unmatched vertices in $L$ in the unweighted case). Then:
     $$\text{MWVC } C^* = (L \setminus Z) \cup (R \cap Z), \qquad \text{MWIS } I^* = (L \cap Z) \cup (R \setminus Z)$$
2. **Gallai's Identities (1959)**:
   - For any graph $G = (V, E)$ without isolated vertices: $\alpha(G) + \tau(G) = |V|$ (holds in *all* graphs; $I$ is independent iff $V \setminus I$ is a vertex cover) and $\nu(G) + \rho(G) = |V|$ (holds in *all* graphs without isolated vertices; greedy extension of max matching $\nu(G)$ by covering $|V| - 2\nu(G)$ exposed vertices yields minimum edge cover $\rho(G) = |V| - \nu(G)$).
   - Combining with König's Theorem ($\nu(G) = \tau(G)$ in bipartite graphs) yields the **Bipartite Quartet**:
     $$\nu(G) = \tau(G), \qquad \alpha(G) = \rho(G) = |V| - \nu(G)$$
3. **Dulmage–Mendelsohn (DM) Canonical Decomposition (1958)**:
   - Given a maximum matching $M$ in $G = (L \sqcup R, E)$ (corresponding to residual network $G_{f^*}$), partition $L = L_S \sqcup L_T \sqcup L_0$ and $R = R_S \sqcup R_T \sqcup R_0$ into three canonical regions based on residual reachability from $S$ and to $T$:
     - **Source-Reachable Core $V_S = L_S \cup R_S$**: Vertices reachable from $S$ in $G_{f^*}$ ($S \rightsquigarrow_{G_{f^*}} x$, i.e., alternating paths from exposed $L$-nodes). Here $|L_S| > |R_S|$, every $R_S$ is matched strictly inside $L_S$, every $L_S$ is *always* in the Maximum Independent Set (never in any min vertex cover), and every $R_S$ is *always* in every Minimum Vertex Cover.
     - **Sink-Co-Reachable Core $V_T = L_T \cup R_T$**: Vertices from which $T$ is reachable in $G_{f^*}$ ($x \rightsquigarrow_{G_{f^*}} T$, i.e., alternating paths to exposed $R$-nodes). Symmetric to $V_S$: $|R_T| > |L_T|$, $L_T$ is matched strictly inside $R_T$, every $L_T$ is in every min vertex cover, and every $R_T$ is in every max independent set.
     - **Perfectly Matched Residual Core $V_0 = L_0 \cup R_0$**: Vertices unreachable from $S$ and unable to reach $T$ in $G_{f^*}$. $V_0$ is decomposed by the Strongly Connected Components (SCCs) of $G_{f^*}$ into fine blocks $V_0^{(1)}, \dots, V_0^{(k)}$ satisfying $|L_0^{(i)}| = |R_0^{(i)}|$, yielding a unique block-upper-triangular permutation of the biadjacency matrix.
   - **Critical / Always-Matched Classification**: An edge $e = (u, v) \in E$ belongs to *some* maximum matching iff $u$ and $v$ lie in the same fine block of $V_0$ ($\text{SCC}(u) = \text{SCC}(v)$), or within $V_S$, or within $V_T$. A vertex $v \in L \sqcup R$ belongs to *every* maximum matching iff $v \notin L_S \cup R_T$.
4. **Hall's Marriage Theorem (1935) & Ore's Defect Formula (1955)**:
   - In $G = (L \sqcup R, E)$, for any $A \subseteq L$, the cut removing $L \setminus A$ from $S$ and $N(A)$ from $T$ has capacity $(|L| - |A|) + |N(A)|$. By König's theorem, the maximum matching size is given by **Ore's Defect Formula**:
     $$\nu(G) = \min_{A \subseteq L} \bigl(|L| - |A| + |N(A)|\bigr) = |L| - \max_{A \subseteq L} \bigl(|A| - |N(A)|\bigr)$$
   - Setting $\nu(G) = |L|$ recovers **Hall's Marriage Theorem**: $L$ has a complete matching iff $|N(A)| \ge |A|$ for all $A \subseteq L$. For weighted supply/demand ($a_u$ on $L$, $b_v$ on $R$), generalized Hall states that all supply is saturable iff $\sum_{v \in N(A)} b_v \ge \sum_{u \in A} a_u$ for all $A \subseteq L$.

#### 2.2.2 Poset Duality, Dilworth's Theorem, Transitive Closure Trap & Greene–Kleitman $k$-Families

Let $(P, \prec)$ be a finite strict partially ordered set (poset) with $n = |P|$ elements (transitively closed: $u \prec v \land v \prec w \implies u \prec w$). Build the **Bipartite Split Graph** $G_{\text{split}}(P) = (P_{\text{out}} \sqcup P_{\text{in}}, E_\prec)$ where $(u_{\text{out}}, v_{\text{in}}) \in E_\prec \iff u \prec v$.

| Operator / Theorem | Algebraic / Combinatorial Statement | Mechanical Graph Compilation & Extraction Recipe | Critical Hazard / Complexity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Minimum Path Cover in a General DAG** (Disjoint Vertex Paths) | Partition a DAG $D = (V, E)$ into the minimum number of vertex-disjoint directed paths $P_1, \dots, P_k$. Each path merge $(u \to v)$ reduces the path count from $|V|$ by $1$, subject to in-degree $\le 1$ and out-degree $\le 1$ per node. | Build bipartite graph $B(D) = (V_{\text{out}} \sqcup V_{\text{in}}, E)$ directly from the DAG edges. Every matching $M$ of size $|M|$ merges $|M|$ endpoints into vertex-disjoint paths. Hence: $\text{MinDisjointPathCover}(D) = |V| - \nu(B(D))$. | **NO Transitive Closure** when paths must be strictly vertex-disjoint! Only when paths are allowed to share vertices (general path cover) do we first take the transitive closure $D^*$ (via Floyd–Warshall / bitset reachability in $O(V^3 / w)$) to reduce to Dilworth's theorem. |
| **Dilworth's Theorem (1950)** (Chain Cover $=$ Max Antichain) | In any finite poset $(P, \prec)$, the minimum number of chains (pairwise comparable subsets) covering $P$ equals the maximum cardinality of an antichain (pairwise incomparable subset). | Ensure $\prec$ is transitively closed! Compute maximum matching $M^*$ of size $\nu$ in $G_{\text{split}}(P)$. Minimum chain cover size $= |P| - \nu$. To reconstruct the **Maximum Antichain $A^*$**: find minimum vertex cover $C^* = (P_{\text{out}} \setminus Z) \cup (P_{\text{in}} \cap Z)$ in $G_{\text{split}}(P)$ of size $\nu$, then set $A^* = \{ x \in P \mid x_{\text{out}} \notin C^* \text{ and } x_{\text{in}} \notin C^* \}$. | **Proof of $|A^*| = |P| - \nu$**: For each $x \in P$, $x_{\text{out}}$ and $x_{\text{in}}$ cannot *both* be in $C^*$! If both $x_{\text{out}}, x_{\text{in}} \in C^*$, then in $G_{f^*}$ we have $S \not\rightsquigarrow x_{\text{out}}$ (so $x_{\text{out}}$ is matched to some $v_{\text{in}}$ with $S \not\rightsquigarrow v_{\text{in}}$) and $S \rightsquigarrow x_{\text{in}}$ (so $x_{\text{in}}$ is matched to some $u_{\text{out}}$ with $S \rightsquigarrow u_{\text{out}}$). Since $u \prec x \prec v$, transitivity gives edge $(u_{\text{out}}, v_{\text{in}})$, forcing $S \rightsquigarrow u_{\text{out}} \to v_{\text{in}}$, a contradiction! Thus $|C^*| = \nu$ covers $\nu$ distinct elements of $P$, leaving $|A^*| = |P| - \nu$. |
| **Weighted Dilworth Theorem** | Each element $x \in P$ has weight $w(x) > 0$. Maximize total weight of an antichain $A \subseteq P$. | In $G_{\text{split}}(P)$, assign capacity $w(x)$ to $(S, x_{\text{out}})$ and $(x_{\text{in}}, T)$, and capacity $\infty$ to $(u_{\text{out}}, v_{\text{in}})$ for $u \prec v$. By the same transitivity non-overlap proof, no min-cut severs both $(S, x_{\text{out}})$ and $(x_{\text{in}}, T)$. Thus $\text{MaxWeightAntichain}(P) = \sum_{x \in P} w(x) - \text{MinCut}(G_{\text{split}}(P))$. | Equivalently modeled via lower-bound flow: split $x \to (x_{\text{in}}, x_{\text{out}})$ with lower bound $w(x)$ (or $1$ in unweighted case) and find Min $S$-$T$ Flow. |
| **Greene–Kleitman $k$-Chain / $k$-Antichain Duality (1976)** | Maximize the size of a union of $k$ chains ($k$-chain family) in poset $(P, \prec)$. Dual equals $\min_{A_1, \dots, A_t} \sum_{j=1}^t \min(k, |A_j|)$ over antichain partitions, or $\min_{S \subseteq P} (|S| + k \cdot \text{width}(P \setminus S))$. | In $G_{\text{split}}(P)$, assign cost $-1$ and capacity $1$ to internal node bridges $(x_{\text{in}}, x_{\text{out}})$ (with $x_{\text{out}} \to y_{\text{in}}$ for $x \prec y$), and send $k$ units of flow from $S$ to $T$ via Min-Cost Flow (with a zero-cost bypass $(S, T)$ of capacity $k$). | The slope convexity of MCMF recovers the entire **Greene–Kleitman Young Diagram** of shape $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots)$, where $\sum_{i=1}^k \lambda_i$ is the maximum size of a $k$-chain union and the conjugate partition $\lambda'_1$ is the maximum antichain width. |

#### 2.2.3 Degree-Constrained Factors, Eulerian Orientation & General Non-Bipartite Matching

1. **Birkhoff–von Neumann Theorem (1946) & König's $k$-Coloring**: Every doubly stochastic matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{n \times n}$ ($\sum_j A_{ij} = \sum_i A_{ij} = 1$) lies in the convex hull of $n \times n$ permutation matrices (proved by inductively extracting a Hall perfect matching from the positive support graph and subtracting $\min_{e \in M} A_e$). Consequently, any bipartite graph with maximum degree $\Delta$ can be regularized and edge-colored in exactly $\Delta$ matchings in $O(E\sqrt{V} \log \Delta)$ time (or $O(E \log \Delta)$ when $\Delta = 2^k$ via **Eulerian Circuit Halving**—splitting every Eulerian tour into alternating even/odd edges).
2. **Mixed Graph Eulerian Orientation**: Given a graph with directed edges $E_D$ and undirected edges $E_U$, orient $E_U$ so $\text{in-deg}(v) = \text{out-deg}(v)$ for all $v \in V$.
   - *Necessary parity check*: Total degree $\deg(v)$ must be even for all $v \in V$.
   - *Arbitrary Baseline Orientation + Flipping*: Arbitrarily orient each $\{u, v\} \in E_U$ as $(u \to v)$. Now vertex $v$ has current imbalance $\Delta(v) = \text{out}(v) - \text{in}(v)$, which must be even! Reversing an oriented edge $(u \to v)$ changes $\Delta(u)$ by $-2$ and $\Delta(v)$ by $+2$. Thus each reversed edge carries $1$ unit of flow from $u$ to $v$, and node $v$ requires net outflow $\Delta(v) / 2$. Connect $S \to v$ (cap $\Delta(v)/2$) if $\Delta(v) > 0$ and $v \to T$ (cap $-\Delta(v)/2$) if $\Delta(v) < 0$; feasible iff Max-Flow saturates all $S$-edges.
3. **General Non-Bipartite Matching (Edmonds' Blossom, Tutte–Berge, Gallai–Edmonds, Tutte Matrix)**:
   - **Why Bipartite Flow Fails on Odd Cycles**: In a non-bipartite graph $G = (V, E)$, building the symmetric bipartite double cover $(V_{\text{out}} \sqcup V_{\text{in}}, E)$ allows fractional half-integral cycles ($f_e = 1/2$ around an odd cycle $C_{2k+1}$) or pairs of disjoint odd cycles in $V_{\text{out}} \sqcup V_{\text{in}}$ that project to half-edges in $G$. The bipartite matching LP relaxation is **half-integral** ($x_e \in \{0, 1/2, 1\}$; Balinski 1965).
   - **Edmonds' Blossom Contraction (1965)**: During BFS from exposed roots (even distance $=$ `outer` / $+$ state; odd distance $=$ `inner` / $-$ state), encountering an edge $(u, v)$ between two `outer` ($+$) vertices reveals an odd-length alternating **blossom** $B$ ($|V(B)| = 2k+1$) rooted at their lowest common ancestor $b$ (the *base*). **Blossom Contraction Theorem**: $G$ has an $M$-augmenting path iff $G / B$ has an $(M / B)$-augmenting path, because any alternating path entering $B$ through its unique unmatched/even base $b$ can traverse the odd cycle $B$ in either direction (even-length route or odd-length route) to exit from *any* vertex $x \in B$ with the required matching parity! Using DSU for lazy contraction yields $O(V^3)$ or $O(VE)$.
   - **Tutte–Berge Formula (1958)**: For any general graph $G = (V, E)$, letting $\text{odd}(G \setminus U)$ denote the number of connected components of odd cardinality in $G \setminus U$:
     $$\nu(G) = \frac{1}{2} \min_{U \subseteq V} \Bigl( |V| + |U| - \text{odd}(G \setminus U) \Bigr)$$
     When $\nu(G) = |V|/2$, this reduces to **Tutte's 1-Factor Theorem (1947)**: $G$ has a perfect matching iff $\text{odd}(G \setminus U) \le |U|$ for all $U \subseteq V$.
   - **Gallai–Edmonds Structure Theorem (1964)**: Partition $V(G)$ intrinsically into:
     - $D(G) = \{ v \in V \mid \exists \text{ max matching } M \text{ leaving } v \text{ exposed} \}$ (the `outer` / deficient vertices);
     - $A(G) = N(D(G)) \setminus D(G)$ (the `inner` barrier neighbors of $D(G)$);
     - $C(G) = V \setminus (D(G) \cup A(G))$ (the perfectamente matchable neutral core).
     Then $U^* = A(G)$ uniquely achieves the Tutte–Berge minimum: every connected component of $C(G)$ is even and has a perfect matching, while every connected component $K$ of $D(G)$ is **factor-critical** ($|K|$ is odd and $K \setminus \{v\}$ has a perfect matching for every $v \in K$).
   - **Algebraic Tutte Matrix & Rabin–Vazirani Inverse ($T(G)^{-1}$)**: Define the $n \times n$ skew-symmetric symbolic matrix $T_{ij} = +x_{ij}$ ($i < j$, $(i, j) \in E$), $T_{ji} = -x_{ij}$, and $0$ otherwise. By the Pfaffian expansion ($\det T = (\text{Pf } T)^2$ where $\text{Pf } T = \sum_{M \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{perf}}} \text{sgn}(M) \prod_{e \in M} x_e$), $\text{rank}(T(G)) = 2\nu(G)$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p \approx 10^9 + 7$) with probability $\ge 1 - |V|/p$ (Schwartz–Zippel). Furthermore, when $T(G)$ is invertible, **Rabin–Vazirani (1989)** proves that edge $(i, j) \in E$ belongs to a perfect matching iff $(T^{-1})_{ji} \neq 0$, allowing full matching reconstruction via rank-2 Sherman–Morrison updates in $O(V^3)$ or $O(V^\omega)$ time.

### 2.3 Pseudo-Boolean Cut Polynomials, Submodular & Multi-State Min-Cut Operators (Waves 3A, 3B & 4A)

#### 2.3.1 Binary Cut Rosetta Stone, Regret Accounting & Picard Maximal Closure

Fix the **Canonical Binary Cut Convention**: for binary variables $x_1, \dots, x_n \in \{0, 1\}$, assign $x_S = 1$ (Source $S$ is the `True` / $1$ side) and $x_T = 0$ (Sink $T$ is the `False` / $0$ side). Any directed arc $(u \to v)$ of capacity $c \ge 0$ is severed iff $x_u = 1$ and $x_v = 0$, contributing the exact quadratic cut monomial $c \cdot x_u(1 - x_v)$:

| Directed Edge $(u \to v)$ with Capacity $c \ge 0$ | Cut Monomial ($x_S = 1, x_T = 0$) | Japanese `燃やす埋める` Dual ($y_i = 1 - x_i$: $y_S = 0, y_T = 1$) | Logical / Penalty Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$(S \to i)$**, capacity $c$ | $c \cdot x_S(1 - x_i) = c(1 - x_i)$ | $c \cdot (1 - y_S)y_i = c \cdot y_i$ | Penalty $c$ incurred if $x_i = 0$ ($i \in T$). |
| **$(i \to T)$**, capacity $c$ | $c \cdot x_i(1 - x_T) = c \cdot x_i$ | $c \cdot (1 - y_i)y_T = c(1 - y_i)$ | Penalty $c$ incurred if $x_i = 1$ ($i \in S$). |
| **$(u \to v)$**, capacity $c$ | $c \cdot x_u(1 - x_v)$ | $c \cdot (1 - y_u)y_v = c \cdot y_v(1 - y_u)$ | Penalty $c$ incurred if $x_u = 1$ and $x_v = 0$. |
| **$(u \to v)$**, capacity $\infty$ | $\infty \cdot x_u(1 - x_v)$ | $\infty \cdot (1 - y_u)y_v$ | Hard implication $[x_u = 1 \implies x_v = 1]$ ($x_u \le x_v$). |

1. **Regret Accounting (`先拿后退` / Pre-Claim and Refund) & Unary Normalization**:
   - **Rule 1 (Maximization $\to$ Minimization)**: To maximize $\text{Profit}(x)$, pre-claim all positive potential profits $P_{\text{base}}$ into a constant baseline and minimize total lost/refunded profit: $\max_x \text{Profit}(x) = P_{\text{base}} - \min_x \text{Regret}(x)$.
   - **Rule 2 (Negative Unary Elimination)**: After expanding any pseudo-Boolean polynomial into $C_0 + \sum_i \alpha_i x_i + \sum_{u \neq v} c_{uv} x_u(1 - x_v)$ (with $c_{uv} \ge 0$), any linear term with $\alpha_i \ge 0$ compiles directly to $(i \to T, \alpha_i)$. Any negative linear term $\alpha_i x_i$ with $\alpha_i < 0$ CANNOT be a negative-capacity edge; apply the identity $x_i = 1 - (1 - x_i)$ to rewrite $\alpha_i x_i = \alpha_i + (-\alpha_i)(1 - x_i)$, shifting $\alpha_i$ into the constant baseline $C_0$ and compiling $(-\alpha_i)(1 - x_i)$ into $(S \to i, -\alpha_i)$ with positive capacity $-\alpha_i > 0$!
2. **Picard's Maximal Closure Theorem (1976)**:
   - Given vertex weights $w : V \to \mathbb{R}$ and prerequisite DAG $E$ ($u \in C \implies v \in C$), we maximize $\sum_{u \in C} w_u$ subject to $x_u \le x_v$ for all $(u, v) \in E$.
   - Writing $w_u = w_u^+ - w_u^-$ ($w_u^+ = \max(0, w_u)$, $w_u^- = \max(0, -w_u)$):
     $$\max_x \sum_{u \in V} (w_u^+ - w_u^-) x_u = \sum_{u : w_u > 0} w_u - \min_x \left( \sum_{u : w_u > 0} w_u(1 - x_u) + \sum_{u : w_u < 0} |w_u| x_u + \sum_{(u,v) \in E} \infty \cdot x_u(1 - x_v) \right)$$
   - Compiles to $(S \to u, w_u)$ for $w_u > 0$, $(u \to T, |w_u|)$ for $w_u < 0$, and $(u \to v, \infty)$ for $(u, v) \in E$. Optimal closure is $C^* = \{ u \in V \mid S \rightsquigarrow_{G_{f^*}} u \}$.

#### 2.3.2 Pairwise Submodularity ($A + D \le B + C$), Bipartite Variable Flip, QPBO & Higher-Degree Hubs

For any pairwise interaction table $E_{uv}(x_u, x_v)$ with $E_{uv}(0, 0) = A$, $E_{uv}(0, 1) = B$, $E_{uv}(1, 0) = C$, $E_{uv}(1, 1) = D$:
$$E_{uv}(x_u, x_v) = A + (C - A)x_u + (B - A)x_v + (A + D - B - C)x_u x_v$$
Substituting $-x_u x_v = x_u(1 - x_v) - x_u$ or $-x_u x_v = x_v(1 - x_u) - x_v$ yields the **1-Parameter Family of Kolmogorov–Zabih Splits** ($\forall \lambda \in [0, B + C - A - D]$):
$$E_{uv}(x_u, x_v) = A + (C - A - \lambda)x_u + (B - A - \Delta + \lambda)x_v + \lambda x_u(1 - x_v) + (\Delta - \lambda)x_v(1 - x_u), \quad \Delta := B + C - A - D$$
- **Submodularity Theorem (Kolmogorov & Zabih 2004)**: $E_{uv}$ is graph-representable by non-negative cut edges (even allowing arbitrary auxiliary binary variables $z_1, \dots, z_k \in \{0, 1\}$ via set projection $f(x) = \min_z \hat{f}(x, z)$) **if and only if** $\Delta = B + C - A - D \ge 0$, i.e.:
  $$\underbrace{E_{uv}(0, 0) + E_{uv}(1, 1)}_{\text{Same-State Cost } A + D} \;\le\; \underbrace{E_{uv}(0, 1) + E_{uv}(1, 0)}_{\text{Different-State Cost } B + C}$$
- **Bipartite Variable Inversion (Supermodular Rescue)**: When a pairwise term is **supermodular** ($A + D > B + C$, rewarding different states $x_u \neq x_v$ or penalizing same states $x_u = x_v$, such as maximum cut / grid checkerboard conflicts), and the conflict graph $G_{\text{conflict}}$ is **bipartite** ($V = L \sqcup R$), apply the substitution $y_j = 1 - x_j$ for all $j \in R$ (keeping $y_i = x_i$ for $i \in L$). Then:
  - A same-state penalty $c \cdot [x_i = x_j]$ ($i \in L, j \in R$) becomes $c \cdot [y_i \neq y_j] = c \cdot y_i(1 - y_j) + c \cdot y_j(1 - y_i)$, which is **submodular** in $y$!
  - By **Harary's Balance Theorem (1953)**, a mixed system of submodular edges ($E^+$) and supermodular edges ($E^-$) can be converted into a purely submodular system via variable flips $y_i \in \{x_i, 1 - x_i\}$ iff the signed graph $(V, E^+ \cup E^-)$ contains no cycle with an odd number of supermodular ($E^-$) edges.
- **QPBO Roof Duality & Half-Integrality (Boros–Hammer 2002)**: When $(V, E^+ \cup E^-)$ has odd negative cycles (non-bipartite supermodular terms), introduce both literal $x_i$ and negated literal $\bar{x}_i = 1 - x_i$ for every node $i \in V$, creating a $2n$-vertex skew-symmetric network between $(S, T)$. A min-cut computes the **LP Roof Dual** $x_i^* \in \{0, 1/2, 1\}$:
  - If $x_i \in S$ and $\bar{x}_i \in T$, then $x_i^* = 1$; if $x_i \in T$ and $\bar{x}_i \in S$, then $x_i^* = 0$; if both lie on the same side, $x_i^* = 1/2$.
  - **Strong Persistency Theorem**: Every variable receiving $x_i^* \in \{0, 1\}$ is guaranteed to take that exact binary value in at least one globally optimal integer assignment of the NP-hard quadratic pseudo-Boolean minimization!
- **Higher-Degree Monomial Reductions (`All-in-S` and `All-in-T` Hubs)**:
  - **Negative All-Ones Monomial $-R \prod_{i \in U} x_i$ ($R > 0$, Synergy Reward if all $u \in U$ have $x_u = 1$)**: Introduce auxiliary node $p_U \in \{0, 1\}$:
    $$-R \prod_{i \in U} x_i = -R + \min_{p_U \in \{0, 1\}} \left( R(1 - p_U) + \sum_{i \in U} \infty \cdot p_U(1 - x_i) \right)$$
    Compiles to constant $-R$, edge $(S \to p_U, R)$, and edges $(p_U \to i, \infty)$ for all $i \in U$.
  - **Negative All-Zeros Monomial $-R \prod_{i \in U} (1 - x_i)$ ($R > 0$, Synergy Reward if all $u \in U$ have $x_u = 0$)**: Introduce auxiliary node $q_U \in \{0, 1\}$:
    $$-R \prod_{i \in U} (1 - x_i) = -R + \min_{q_U \in \{0, 1\}} \left( R \cdot q_U + \sum_{i \in U} \infty \cdot x_i(1 - q_U) \right)$$
    Compiles to constant $-R$, edge $(q_U \to T, R)$, and edges $(i \to q_U, \infty)$ for all $i \in U$.
  - **Positive Cubic Obstacle**: Positive higher-degree penalties $+P \cdot x_1 x_2 x_3$ ($P > 0$) are non-submodular and **cannot** be represented by any positive-capacity $s$-$t$ cut network without variable flips.

#### 2.3.3 Multi-State Unary Chains, Ishikawa Monge Compilation, Coarea Layer-Cake & Parametric Cuts

1. **Multi-State Unary Threshold Encoding ($z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k]$)**:
   - For a discrete variable $x_i \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$, define $K-1$ binary threshold indicators $z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k] \in \{0, 1\}$ for $k \in \{2, \dots, K\}$ (with fixed boundary constants $z_{i,1} \equiv 1$ via $S$ and $z_{i,K+1} \equiv 0$ via $T$).
   - **Monotonicity Invariant**: Since $x_i \ge k \implies x_i \ge k-1$, we must enforce $z_{i,k} \le z_{i,k-1}$, compiled as reverse infinite-capacity constraint arcs $(z_{i,k} \to z_{i,k-1}, \infty)$.
   - **Series vs. Parallel Equivalence (Abel Summation)**:
     - *Series Chain*: Connect $S = z_{i,1} \xrightarrow{\theta_i(1)} z_{i,2} \xrightarrow{\theta_i(2)} \dots \xrightarrow{\theta_i(K)} z_{i,K+1} = T$ with forward capacity $\theta_i(k) \ge 0$ and reverse capacity $(z_{i,k+1} \to z_{i,k}, \infty)$. Because reverse $\infty$-edges prevent $0 \to 1$ inversions, the cut severs *exactly one* forward link $(z_{i,x_i} \to z_{i,x_i+1})$ at cost $\theta_i(x_i)$.
     - *Parallel Marginal Form*: By Abel partial summation $\theta_i(x_i) = \theta_i(1) + \sum_{k=2}^K (\theta_i(k) - \theta_i(k-1)) z_{i,k}$, the series chain is algebraically identical to attaching unary marginal deltas $\Delta \theta_i(k) = \theta_i(k) - \theta_i(k-1)$ from $S$ or to $T$ alongside $(z_{i,k} \to z_{i,k-1}, \infty)$.
2. **Ishikawa (2003) Monge / Convex Difference Compilation**:
   - A pairwise potential $\Phi_{uv}(a, b)$ over $x_u, x_v \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ is **2D submodular (Monge)** iff all $2 \times 2$ finite differences satisfy:
     $$w_{a,b} := -\Delta_a \Delta_b \Phi_{uv}(a, b) = \Phi_{uv}(a, b-1) + \Phi_{uv}(a-1, b) - \Phi_{uv}(a, b) - \Phi_{uv}(a-1, b-1) \ge 0 \quad \forall a, b \in \{2, \dots, K\}$$
   - By discrete integration by parts, $\Phi_{uv}(x_u, x_v) = \text{Unary}(x_u) + \text{Unary}(x_v) + \sum_{a=2}^K \sum_{b=2}^K w_{a,b} \cdot z_{u,a}(1 - z_{v,b}) + \dots$, which compiles into cross-chain directed edges $(z_{u,a} \to z_{v,b})$ of capacity $w_{a,b} \ge 0$.
   - Specifically, for a **Convex Difference Penalty** $\Phi_{uv}(x_u - x_v) = g(x_u - x_v)$ where $g$ is convex ($g''(d) = g(d+1) - 2g(d) + g(d-1) \ge 0$):
     - **Hard Bounded Offset $x_u \le x_v + d$**: Add $(z_{u, k+d} \to z_{v, k}, \infty)$ for all $k$.
     - **Linear Absolute Deviation $c \cdot |x_u - x_v|$**: Since $g''(0) = 2c$ and $g''(d) = 0$ ($d \neq 0$), add bidirectional cross-edges $(z_{u,k} \leftrightarrow z_{v,k})$ of capacity $c$ at the **same** threshold level $k \in \{2, \dots, K\}$.
     - **General Convex $g(x_u - x_v)$**: Connect every pair $(z_{u,a} \to z_{v,b})$ with capacity $g''(a - b - 1) = g(a - b) - 2g(a - b - 1) + g(a - b - 2) \ge 0$.
3. **Discrete Coarea Formula (Layer-Cake Thresholding) & Isotonic / Nested Cut Envelopes**:
   - Any multi-label function with linear $L_1$ interaction $E(x) = \sum_{i \in V} \theta_i(x_i) + \sum_{(u,v) \in E} w_{uv} |x_u - x_v|$ over $x_i \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ satisfies the **Coarea Identity** $|x_u - x_v| = \sum_{k=2}^K |z_{u,k} - z_{v,k}|$.
   - When the unary costs $\theta_i(x_i)$ are **convex** in $x_i$ ($\Delta \theta_i(k) = \theta_i(k) - \theta_i(k-1)$ is non-decreasing in $k$, as in $L_p$ Isotonic Regression $\theta_i(x_i) = w_i |x_i - y_i|^p$ for $p \ge 1$), the $K-1$ threshold levels **decouple completely**: the inclusion-minimal optimal cuts $S_{\min}(k)$ of the independent binary problems $\mathcal{P}(k) = \min_{z_{\cdot, k} \in \{0,1\}^V} \sum_i \Delta \theta_i(k) z_{i,k} + \sum_{(u,v)} w_{uv} |z_{u,k} - z_{v,k}|$ automatically satisfy **Monotone Cut Nesting** $S_{\min}(2) \supseteq S_{\min}(3) \supseteq \dots \supseteq S_{\min}(K)$ ($z_{i,2}^* \ge z_{i,3}^* \ge \dots \ge z_{i,K}^*$)!
   - This eliminates the $(K-1)$-layer Ishikawa grid entirely and allows **Divide-and-Conquer on Threshold Levels (`整体二分`)** in $O(\text{MaxFlow}(V, E) \log K)$ time!
4. **0-1 Fractional Programming (Dinkelbach 1967), Goldberg Max-Density Subgraph & Advanced Cut Topologies**:
   - **Dinkelbach's Parametric Transformation**: To optimize $\lambda^* = \max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \frac{N(x)}{D(x)}$ ($D(x) > 0$), define $h(\lambda) = \max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} (N(x) - \lambda D(x))$, which is continuous, strictly decreasing, and convex in $\lambda$. Then $\lambda \le \lambda^* \iff h(\lambda) \ge 0$. For **Goldberg's Maximum Density Subgraph** ($\max_{\emptyset \neq S \subseteq V} \frac{|E(S)|}{|S|}$), compile $h(\lambda) = \max_x \bigl(\sum_{e=(u,v)} \min(x_u, x_v) - \lambda \sum_v x_v\bigr)$ either via edge-nodes $(S \to e, 1), (e \to u, \infty), (e \to v, \infty), (v \to T, \lambda)$ or degree-scaling $(S \to v, \deg(v)), (u \leftrightarrow v, 1), (v \to T, 2\lambda)$ in $O(\log V)$ binary search steps (exact rationality denominator gap $\ge \frac{1}{|V|(|V|-1)}$).
   - **Planar Dual Shortest Path (Hassnin 1981 / Reif 1983)**: In an $s$-$t$ planar graph ($s, t$ on outer face), split the outer face at $s$ and $t$ into $f_{\text{top}}$ and $f_{\text{bot}}$; then $\text{MinCut}_G(s, t) = \text{ShortestPath}_{G^*}(f_{\text{top}} \to f_{\text{bot}})$ in $O(V \log V)$ via Dijkstra. For general planar $s$-$t$ cuts (`[ICPC WF 2016 C]` / Reif's separator), slice along a shortest $s$-$t$ path $P$ to double $P$ into upper and lower boundaries and solve via Divide-and-Conquer / MSSP in $O(V \log^2 V)$ or $O(V \log V)$.
   - **Stoer–Wagner Global Min-Cut ($O(VE + V^2 \log V)$)**: Maximum Adjacency Search orders vertices $v_1, \dots, v_n$ by greedily picking $v_i = \arg\max_{u \notin A_{i-1}} w(A_{i-1}, u)$. The last two vertices $(s, t) = (v_{n-1}, v_n)$ satisfy $\lambda(G; s, t) = w(A_{n-1}, t)$, after which $s$ and $t$ are contracted.
   - **Gomory–Hu / Gusfield Cut Tree ($n-1$ Max-Flows) & DKL Min-Cut Cactus**: Any undirected weighted graph $G$ admits a weighted tree $T_{\text{GH}}$ on $V(G)$ such that for all $u, v \in V$, the minimum weight edge on the $u$-$v$ tree path in $T_{\text{GH}}$ equals $\lambda_G(u, v)$ and its removal gives an exact minimum $u$-$v$ cut in $G$. Furthermore, **Dinits–Karzanov–Lomonosov (1976)** proves that the family of *all* global minimum cuts of $G$ is compactly represented by a tree-of-cycles (**Cactus Graph**) with $\le 2|V|$ vertices and $\le \binom{|V|}{2}$ global min-cuts.

### 2.4 Min-Cost Flow, Consecutive Equation-Differencing & LP Duality Operators (Waves 4B, 5A & 5B)

#### 2.4.1 SSP Johnson Potentials, Zkw Primal–Dual, Slope Convexity & Negative-Cycle Handling

In a Min-Cost Max-Flow (MCMF) network $G = (V, E, c, w)$, each edge $e = (u, v)$ has capacity $c(e) \ge 0$ and unit cost $w(e) \in \mathbb{R}$. A flow $f$ of value $|f|$ is cost-optimal iff its residual network $G_f$ contains **no negative-cost directed cycle** (Klein's Theorem 1967).

1. **Johnson Node Potentials $\pi : V \to \mathbb{R}$ & Reduced Costs $w^\pi(u, v)$**:
   - Define the **Reduced Cost** of residual edge $(u, v) \in E_f$ as $w^\pi(u, v) := w(u, v) + \pi(u) - \pi(v)$.
   - **Telescoping Invariant**: Along any $s \to t$ path $P$, $\sum_{e \in P} w^\pi(e) = \sum_{e \in P} w(e) + \pi(s) - \pi(t)$; around any cycle $C$, $\sum_{e \in C} w^\pi(e) = \sum_{e \in C} w(e)$. Thus maintaining **Dual Feasibility** $w^\pi(u, v) \ge 0$ ($\forall (u, v) \in E_f$) guarantees zero negative cycles and makes every residual edge non-negative for **Dijkstra's algorithm** ($O(E \log V)$ or $O(V^2 + E)$).
   - **Potential Update Rule**: After computing shortest reduced-cost distances $d(v) = \text{dist}_{w^\pi}(s, v)$ in $G_f$ via Dijkstra, update $\pi'(v) \gets \pi(v) + d(v)$ for all reachable $v$. By triangle inequality $d(v) \le d(u) + w^\pi(u, v) \implies w^{\pi'}(u, v) \ge 0$, and along shortest paths $d(v) = d(u) + w^\pi(u, v) \implies w^{\pi'}(u, v) = 0$, so new reversed residual edges $(v, u)$ created by augmentation also have $w^{\pi'}(v, u) = -w^{\pi'}(u, v) = 0$!
   - **Early-Exit Potential Cap (Unreachable Nodes)**: If Dijkstra terminates early when popping $t$ (or if some nodes are unreachable), update $\pi'(v) \gets \pi(v) + \min(d(v), d(t))$ so unvisited vertices ($d(v) > d(t)$) retain $w^{\pi'}(u, v) \ge 0$.
   - **The 4 Potential Initialization Regimes**:
     1. *Non-negative initial costs ($w_e \ge 0$)*: Set $\pi(v) = 0$ in $O(V)$.
     2. *Negative edges on a DAG*: Compute $\pi(v)$ via **Topological Sort DP** $\pi(v) = \min_{u \to v} (\pi(u) + w(u, v))$ in $O(V + E)$—**never run SPFA on a DAG**!
     3. *Structured DP / Assignment layers*: Set $\pi(v)$ analytically in $O(V)$ from prefix sums or bipartite layer offsets.
     4. *General graph with negative edges (no negative cycles)*: Run Bellman–Ford / SPFA **once** at initialization ($O(VE)$), then use Dijkstra exclusively for all subsequent augmentations.
2. **Zkw Primal–Dual Algorithm (Zero-Reduced-Cost Blocking Flow)**:
   - After updating potentials $\pi(v)$, the subgraph $G_f^{(0)} = \{ (u, v) \in E_f \mid w^\pi(u, v) = 0 \}$ of tight edges is a DAG (unless zero-cost cycles exist). Run a Dinic-style multi-path DFS with `vis[u]` cycle-guard and current-arc optimization on $G_f^{(0)}$ to push an entire **blocking flow** per Dijkstra phase.
   - *Strength*: Dramatically faster than single-path SSP when graph has unit/small cost layers and large capacities. *Weakness*: Degrades on long narrow chains with distinct edge costs where each phase augments along a single path.
3. **Slope Convexity of $C(f)$, Minimum-Cost Feasible Flow (Early Stopping) & WQS Binary Search**:
   - Let $C(f)$ be the minimum cost to send $f$ units of flow from $s$ to $t$. Because SSP augments along shortest paths of monotonically non-decreasing marginal cost $\mu_1 \le \mu_2 \le \dots \le \mu_{F_{\max}}$ ($\mu_k = \pi_k(t) - \pi_k(s)$), the function $C(f)$ is **piecewise-linear and convex** on $[0, F_{\max}]$.
   - **Min-Cost Feasible Flow (Unconstrained Volume)**: To minimize cost over *all* valid $s$-$t$ flows of any volume, stop SSP the instant the shortest path distance $\mu_k = \pi(t) - \pi(s)$ becomes $\ge 0$!
   - **Parametric WQS / Aliens Penalty Connection**: Minimizing $C(f) - \lambda f$ (adding a $-\lambda$ rebate per unit of flow, or a feedback arc $(t \to s)$ of cost $-\lambda$) finds the point on the convex hull $C(f)$ where the subgradient $\partial C(f) \ni \lambda$.
4. **Negative Cycles & Mandatory Initial Saturation (`负权边强制满流消圈法`)**:
   - When a circulation or MCMF network already contains negative-cost cycles (or negative-cost edges with lower bounds), SPFA/Bellman–Ford loops infinitely.
   - **Compilation Recipe**: For every negative-cost edge $e = (u, v)$ with $w(e) < 0$ and capacity $[l(e), u(e)]$:
     1. **Pre-saturate $e$ to its upper bound $u(e)$**: Add $u(e) \cdot w(e)$ to the base cost, update node balances $M(v) \mathrel{+}= u(e)$ and $M(u) \mathrel{-}= u(e)$, and replace $e = (u, v)$ with its reversed refund edge $e_{\text{rev}} = (v, u)$ of capacity $u(e) - l(e)$ and **positive cost** $-w(e) > 0$.
     2. Now **every edge in the network has non-negative cost $w'(e) \ge 0$**! Connect $S_{\text{sup}} \to v$ (cap $M(v)$, cost $0$) for $M(v) > 0$ and $v \to T_{\text{sup}}$ (cap $-M(v)$, cost $0$) for $M(v) < 0$, and run standard non-negative Dijkstra-SSP from $S_{\text{sup}}$ to $T_{\text{sup}}$ with initial potentials $\pi \equiv 0$!

#### 2.4.2 Convex Marginal Splitting, Consecutive Equation Differencing & LP / Matroid Duality

1. **Convex Separable Cost Splitting ($\Delta g(k) = g(k) - g(k-1)$)**:
   - Any integer flow variable $f_e \in \{0, 1, \dots, U\}$ with a **convex** cost function $g(f_e)$ satisfying $\Delta g(1) \le \Delta g(2) \le \dots \le \Delta g(U)$ compiles into $U$ parallel arcs $(u \to v)$, where the $k$-th arc has capacity $1$ and unit cost $\Delta g(k) = g(k) - g(k-1)$. Because $\Delta g(k)$ is non-decreasing, any min-cost solver automatically saturates arc $1, 2, \dots, f_e$ in exact prefix order!
   - For piecewise-linear convex costs $g(f) = \max_{1 \le j \le m} (a_j f + b_j)$ with breakpoints $0 = u_0 < u_1 < \dots < u_m = U$ and slopes $s_1 < s_2 < \dots < s_m$, replace $e$ with $m$ parallel arcs of capacity $u_j - u_{j-1}$ and unit cost $s_j$.
   - *Concave Trap*: If $g(f_e)$ is strictly concave ($\Delta g(1) > \Delta g(2)$), parallel arcs fail completely because the solver greedily picks the last (cheapest) arc without saturating earlier arcs.
2. **Consecutive Equation Differencing (`差分法` / $E_t - E_{t-1}$)**:
   - Given a system of linear constraints $A x = b$ (or $A x \le b$ converted via non-negative slack variables $s_t \ge 0$ to $A x + s = b$) where every variable $x_j$ appears in a **contiguous interval of rows** $[L_j, R_j]$ with coefficient $+1$ (the **Consecutive-Ones Property**):
     - Pad boundary equations $E_0 : 0 = 0$ and $E_{n+1} : 0 = 0$.
     - Replace the system by the $n+1$ telescope differences $\Delta E_t := E_t - E_{t-1}$ for $t = 1, \dots, n+1$ (so $\sum_{t=1}^{n+1} \Delta E_t \equiv 0$).
     - Now $x_j$ cancels out of every interior row $t \in (L_j, R_j)$ and survives in **only two rows**: with coefficient $+1$ in $\Delta E_{L_j}$ and coefficient $-1$ in $\Delta E_{R_j + 1}$!
     - **Compilation Rule (Net Outflow = RHS $\Delta b_t = b_t - b_{t-1}$)**:
       - Each variable $x_j \in [l_j, u_j]$ (cost $w_j$) becomes a **Forward Arc** $(L_j \to R_j + 1)$ of capacity $[l_j, u_j]$ and cost $w_j$.
       - Each slack variable $s_t \ge 0$ in $E_t : (\text{sum}_t) + s_t = b_t$ appears $+s_t$ in $\Delta E_t$ and $-s_t$ in $\Delta E_{t+1}$, compiling into a **Unit-Step Forward Spine Arc** $(t \to t+1)$ of capacity $\infty$ and cost $0$ (or reverse spine arc $(t+1 \to t)$ if the inequality is $\ge b_t$, i.e., $-s_t$ in $E_t$).
       - Each constant RHS $\Delta b_t = b_t - b_{t-1}$ becomes a node supply/demand: connect $(S_{\text{sup}} \to t, \Delta b_t)$ if $\Delta b_t > 0$ and $(t \to T_{\text{sup}}, -\Delta b_t)$ if $\Delta b_t < 0$.
3. **LP Duality Compilation, Virtual Super-Root $Q$ & Complementary Slackness**:
   - When an LP has **2 variables per inequality** ($\pi_v - \pi_u \le w_e$, the Dual of Flow) rather than 2 occurrences per variable, take the LP Dual or interpret $\pi_v$ directly as MCMF node potentials!
   - **Virtual Ground Potential $Q$**: In flow duals, potentials $\pi_v$ are shift-invariant ($\pi_v \gets \pi_v + C$). To model absolute bounds on variables such as $0 \le x_v \le U_v$ or objective terms $\sum_v d_v x_v$, introduce a **Reference Ground Node $Q$** with fixed potential $\pi_Q \equiv 0$ and encode every unary variable $x_v$ as the potential difference $x_v = \pi_v - \pi_Q$ (or $\pi_Q - \pi_v$).
4. **Total Unimodularity (TU) Recognition Hierarchy & Murota's Discrete Convex Duality**:
   - **Hoffman–Kruskal (1956)**: $A$ is TU ($\det(A_{\text{sub}}) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$) iff $\mathcal{P}(b, d, l, u) = \{ x \mid b \le Ax \le d,\; l \le x \le u \}$ has integral vertices for all integral $b, d, l, u$.
   - **Ghouila–Houri Equitable Bicoloring Criterion (1962)**: $A \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^{m \times n}$ is TU iff every subset of rows $R \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}$ (and columns) can be partitioned into $R = R^+ \sqcup R^-$ such that $\sum_{i \in R^+} A_{ij} - \sum_{i \in R^-} A_{ij} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ for all $j$.
   - **The 4 Graph-Compiled TU Subclasses**:
     1. *Directed Node-Arc Incidence Matrices*: 1 entry $+1$ and 1 entry $-1$ per column $\to$ Standard Network Flow.
     2. *Bipartite Undirected Incidence Matrices*: 2 entries $+1$ per column, rows partitioned into $L \sqcup R$ $\to$ Negate rows in $R$ to reduce to Class 1.
     3. *Interval / Consecutive-Ones Matrices*: contiguous $1$s per column (or row) $\to$ Multiply by unimodular row-difference matrix $D$ ($E_t - E_{t-1}$) to reduce to Class 1.
     4. *Network Matrices (Tutte 1965 / Tree-Path Subtree Matrices)*: Given reference tree $T$ and directed arcs $D$, $M_{e, a} \in \{+1, -1, 0\}$ records whether tree edge $e \in T$ is traversed forward or backward along the fundamental tree path of $a \in D$ $\to$ Equivalent to flow conservation across fundamental tree cuts of $T$. (By **Seymour's Decomposition Theorem 1980**, every TU matrix is built from Network Matrices and two $5 \times 5$ sporadic matrices via 1-, 2-, and 3-sums.)
   - **Murota's Discrete Convex Analysis ($L^\natural$-Convex vs. $M^\natural$-Convex Duality)**:
     - **$L^\natural$-Convex Functions (Potential Space $\pi$)**: Functions $g(\pi)$ satisfying Discrete Midpoint Convexity $g(\pi) + g(\mu) \ge g(\lceil (\pi+\mu)/2 \rceil) + g(\lfloor (\pi+\mu)/2 \rfloor)$ (equivalently, submodular with translation invariance $g(\pi + \mathbf{1}) = g(\pi)$). Minimized via **Project Selection / Min-Cut / Ishikawa / Coarea**.
     - **$M^\natural$-Convex Functions (Flow/Supply Space $x$)**: Functions $f(x)$ satisfying the Symmetric Exchange Axiom: for $\forall i$ with $x_i > y_i$, $\exists j$ with $x_j < y_j$ (or $j = 0$) s.t. $f(x) + f(y) \ge f(x - \vec{e}_i + \vec{e}_j) + f(y + \vec{e}_i - \vec{e}_j)$. Minimized via **Min-Cost Flow / Matroid Intersection / Slope Trick**.
     - **Discrete Legendre–Fenchel Conjugate Theorem**: $f(x)$ is $M^\natural$-convex $\iff$ its conjugate $f^*(\pi) = \max_x (\langle \pi, x \rangle - f(x))$ is $L^\natural$-convex! This single theorem unifies Min-Cost Flow ($M^\natural$-primal) with Project Selection / Potentials ($L^\natural$-dual).

## 3. The "Zero Magic Gadgets" English-to-Flow Manipulation Discovery Playbook (Direction A: Non-Graph $\to$ Flow)

In competitive programming, the hardest part of a flow problem is almost never running Dinic or SSP—it is recognizing that a problem phrased in terms of grids, sequences, intervals, permutations, strings, or games is secretly an integer linear program over a Totally Unimodular or submodular polyhedron, and then compiling that English statement into a graph **without guessing gadgets**.

This section provides the complete **4-Step Diagnostic-to-Graph Compilation Playbook** across all **9 Non-Graph Problem Sub-Domains (`3.1`–`3.9`)**.

---

### 3.1 Sub-Domain 1: Grid, Chessboard & Parity Conflict Problems

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Place non-overlapping dominoes / L-trominoes / tetrominoes on a grid with holes", "Select cells on a $R \times C$ grid ($R, C \le 50$ or $R \cdot C \le 10^4$) with no two orthogonally adjacent cells chosen to maximize sum", "Color cells black/white where adjacent same-color cells pay a penalty and adjacent different-color cells earn a bonus", "Place laser towers on a grid where beams cannot cross".
- **Constraint Signature**: Grid dimensions $R, C \le 50$ (for weighted min-cut / MCMF, $V \le 2500$) or $R, C \le 200$ (for unit-capacity bipartite matching, $V \le 4 \cdot 10^4$).

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Greedy Failure**: Picking the heaviest cell or locally valid domino creates odd-length cavities that strand high-value neighbors globally.
- **Bitmask / Profile DP Failure**: Frontier profile DP requires $O(R \cdot C \cdot 2^{\min(R, C)})$ time and space, which explodes the moment $\min(R, C) \ge 22$. Even when $\min(R, C)$ is small, cross-row ray interactions (e.g., laser beams spanning an entire row/column) make the state space non-local.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. **Standard Orthogonal Adjacency (2-Coloring Parity Split)**:
   - Partition grid cells $(r, c)$ by chessboard parity $p(r, c) = (r + c) \bmod 2 \in \{0, 1\}$ into $L = \{ u \mid p(u) = 0 \}$ and $R = \{ v \mid p(v) = 1 \}$. Every orthogonal adjacency $(u, v)$ crosses between $L$ and $R$.
   - For **Maximum Weight Independent Set** ($x_u + x_v \le 1$ for adjacent $u \in L, v \in R$), apply **Bipartite Variable Inversion**: set $y_u = x_u \in \{0, 1\}$ for $u \in L$ (1 means chosen, placed in $S$) and $y_v = 1 - x_v \in \{0, 1\}$ for $v \in R$ (0 means chosen, placed in $T$).
   - Then the conflict constraint $[x_u = 1 \land x_v = 1]$ transforms into the submodular cut condition $[y_u = 1 \land y_v = 0]$:
     $$\max_{x} \sum_{u \in L} w_u x_u + \sum_{v \in R} w_v x_v - \sum_{(u,v) \in E} \infty \cdot x_u x_v \;=\; \sum_{w \in L \sqcup R} w_w - \min_{y \in \{0,1\}^V} \left( \sum_{u \in L} w_u(1 - y_u) + \sum_{v \in R} w_v y_v + \sum_{(u,v) \in E} \infty \cdot y_u(1 - y_v) \right)$$
2. **Multi-Cell Polyomino / L-Tromino / 4-Phase Modular Layering**:
   - When valid shapes consist of 3 or 4 connected cells (e.g., L-trominoes or $1 \times 3$ / $2 \times 2$ blocks where every valid shape must pass through a unique "pivot" cell of a specific modular residue class), partition the grid into $k \in \{3, 4\}$ residue classes $V_0 \to V_1 \to V_2 (\to V_3)$ based on $(r \bmod 2, c \bmod 2)$ or $(r + c) \bmod 3$.
   - Express each valid polyomino $P$ as a directed path $v_0 \to v_1 \to v_2 (\to v_3)$ passing through one vertex of each layer in strict sequence.

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **2-Layer Bipartite Conflict / Synergy Compilation**:
  - For $u \in L$ (Even parity): $(S \to u, w_u)$ represents the regret of NOT choosing $u$ ($y_u = 0 \iff x_u = 0$).
  - For $v \in R$ (Odd parity): $(v \to T, w_v)$ represents the regret of NOT choosing $v$ ($y_v = 1 \iff x_v = 0$).
  - Mutual exclusion $[x_u = 1 \land x_v = 1]$: directed edge $(u \to v, \infty)$ ($y_u(1 - y_v)$).
  - Same-state penalty $c \cdot [x_u = x_v]$ (supermodular in $x$, submodular in $y$): bidirectional edges $(u \to v, c)$ and $(v \to u, c)$ ($c \cdot y_u(1 - y_v) + c \cdot y_v(1 - y_u)$).
  - Both-chosen synergy bonus $b \cdot x_u x_v$ (supermodular in $x$, submodular in $y$ since $x_u x_v = y_u(1 - y_v)$): pre-claim $+b$, add regret $b(1 - x_u x_v) = b(1 - y_u) + b \cdot y_u y_v$ via auxiliary hub or direct edge adjustment!
- **4-Layer Modular DAG Compilation (`[AtCoder ARC 085E]`, `[Tsinghua Camp 2014] Blocks`, `[CF 1519F]`)**:
  - Split pivot layer nodes $v_{\text{in}} \to v_{\text{out}}$ with capacity $1$ (and cost/weight $w_v$) so each cell is used by at most one polyomino; connect $S \to V_0 \to V_1^{\text{in}} \to V_1^{\text{out}} \to V_2 \to T$.
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[POJ 2446]` Chessboard ($1 \times 2$ dominoes), `[Luogu P2774]` Grid Max Weight Independent Set, `[Luogu P3227 / HNOI2013]` Cake (3D grid column cut), `[Luogu P4174 / NOI2006]` Maximum Profit, `[QOJ / CCPC]` L-Trominoes & Grid Laser Cuts.

### 3.2 Sub-Domain 2: Subset Selection with Prerequisites, Synergies & Mutual Exclusions

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Choose a subset of projects/experiments/items; project $u$ requires equipment $v$ (or prerequisite $v$)", "If all items in subset $U$ are selected, earn bonus $B_U$; if both $u$ and $v$ are selected in different modes, pay penalty $P_{uv}$", "Assign each element to Team A or Team B with pairwise affinity/conflict scores".
- **Constraint Signature**: Number of items $N \le 500$–$2000$, number of dependency/synergy rules $M \le 5000$, arbitrary positive/negative weights $W_i \le 10^9$.

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Greedy Failure**: An item with negative individual profit $w_u < 0$ may unlock a massive downstream positive cluster $v_1, v_2, v_3$, while two overlapping positive clusters may share a single expensive prerequisite.
- **Tree DP Failure**: Dependencies form a general DAG (or contain cycles that must be contracted into SCCs), where shared prerequisites have in-degree $\ge 2$, creating arbitrary treewidth.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. Assign binary indicator $x_i \in \{0, 1\}$ ($1 =$ selected / Team A, $0 =$ rejected / Team B).
2. Convert maximization of $\sum_i w_i x_i + \sum_{U} B_U \prod_{i \in U} x_i + \sum_{U'} B'_{U'} \prod_{i \in U'} (1 - x_i) - \sum_{(u,v)} P_{uv} x_u(1 - x_v)$ into **Regret Minimization** by pre-claiming all positive rewards:
   $$P_{\text{base}} = \sum_{i : w_i > 0} w_i + \sum_U B_U + \sum_{U'} B'_{U'}$$
3. Express every lost reward and incurred penalty as a sum of cut monomials:
   $$\text{Regret}(x) = \sum_{w_i > 0} w_i(1 - x_i) + \sum_{w_i < 0} |w_i| x_i + \sum_{(u,v) \in E_{\text{prereq}}} \infty \cdot x_u(1 - x_v) + \sum_{U} \min_{p_U \in \{0,1\}} \left( B_U(1 - p_U) + \sum_{i \in U} \infty \cdot p_U(1 - x_i) \right) + \sum_{U'} \min_{q_{U'} \in \{0,1\}} \left( B'_{U'} q_{U'} + \sum_{i \in U'} \infty \cdot x_i(1 - q_{U'}) \right)$$
4. For any $2 \times 2$ pairwise cost matrix $\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}$ on $(x_u, x_v)$, check **Submodularity** $A + D \le B + C$. If satisfied, decompose into unary adjustments plus $\lambda x_u(1 - x_v) + (B + C - A - D - \lambda) x_v(1 - x_u)$ with $\lambda \in [0, B + C - A - D]$.

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **Unary Edges**: $(S \to i, w_i)$ if $w_i > 0$; $(i \to T, |w_i|)$ if $w_i < 0$.
- **Prerequisite $x_u \le x_v$**: $(u \to v, \infty)$. (**Cycle Trap**: If prerequisites have cycles, e.g., mutual dependencies in `[NOI2009] Plants vs. Zombies`, first run Tarjan SCC / topological pruning to delete all vertices in or reachable from cycles before adding $\infty$ edges!)
- **All-in-$S$ Synergy Hub $p_U$**: $(S \to p_U, B_U)$ and $(p_U \to i, \infty)$ for all $i \in U$.
- **All-in-$T$ Synergy Hub $q_{U'}$**: $(q_{U'} \to T, B'_{U'})$ and $(i \to q_{U'}, \infty)$ for all $i \in U'$.
- **Pairwise Cut Edges**: $(u \to v, \lambda)$ and $(v \to u, B + C - A - D - \lambda)$ after folding unary shifts into $S \to u/v$ or $u/v \to T$.
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[CF 1082G]` Petya and Graph, `[Luogu P2762]` Space Flight Plan, `[Luogu P2805 / NOI2009]` Plants vs. Zombies, `[Luogu P4313 / 文理分科]` Arts and Sciences, `[AtCoder ARC 085E]` MUL, `[CF 311E]` Biologist.

### 3.3 Sub-Domain 3: Discrete Multi-Choice Variables with Smoothness / Convex Difference Penalties

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Assign each variable $x_i$ an integer level from $\{1, 2, \dots, K\}$", "Choosing level $k$ for variable $i$ costs $\theta_i(k)$", "For pairs $(u, v)$, require $x_u \le x_v + d$ (or pay convex penalty $g(x_u - x_v)$ such as $c \cdot |x_u - x_v|$ or $c \cdot (x_u - x_v)^2$)".
- **Constraint Signature**: $N \le 100$, $K \le 100$ (so total unary threshold nodes $N \cdot K \le 10^4$), arbitrary unary cost tables $\theta_i(k)$ (NOT required to be convex!), and **Monge / submodular** pairwise cost $\Phi_{uv}(a, b)$ satisfying $\Phi_{uv}(a, b-1) + \Phi_{uv}(a-1, b) - \Phi_{uv}(a, b) - \Phi_{uv}(a-1, b-1) \ge 0$.

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Unary Non-Convexity**: Because individual cost tables $\theta_i(k)$ can oscillate arbitrarily (multiple local minima), continuous convex optimization and greedy local Descent get trapped immediately.
- **Cyclic Graph Topology**: Pairwise constraints $(u, v) \in E$ form an arbitrary graph with cycles, so Tree DP ($O(N K^2)$) is impossible.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. Introduce $K-1$ unary threshold binary indicators $z_{i,k} = [x_i \ge k] \in \{0, 1\}$ for $k \in \{2, \dots, K\}$, with boundary constants $z_{i,1} \equiv 1$ ($S$) and $z_{i,K+1} \equiv 0$ ($T$).
2. Enforce the **Monotonicity Chain Invariant** $z_{i,1} \ge z_{i,2} \ge \dots \ge z_{i,K} \ge z_{i,K+1}$ via penalty $\sum_{k=2}^K \infty \cdot z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k-1})$, ensuring a unique transition index $x_i \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ where $z_{i,x_i} = 1$ and $z_{i,x_i+1} = 0$.
3. Write unary cost via series transition indicators $z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1}) = [x_i = k]$:
   $$\theta_i(x_i) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_i(k) \cdot z_{i,k}(1 - z_{i,k+1})$$
   (Shifting $\theta_i(k) \gets \theta_i(k) + M$ by a large constant $M$ if any $\theta_i(k) < 0$, and subtracting $N \cdot M$ at the end!)
4. Expand each Monge pairwise potential $\Phi_{uv}(x_u, x_v)$ using second-order cross-differences $w_{a,b} = -\Delta_a \Delta_b \Phi_{uv}(a, b) \ge 0$:
   $$\Phi_{uv}(x_u, x_v) = \text{Unary}_u(x_u) + \text{Unary}_v(x_v) + \sum_{a=2}^K \sum_{b=2}^K w_{a,b} \cdot z_{u,a}(1 - z_{v,b})$$

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **Series Chain for Variable $i$**: Create vertices $z_{i,2}, \dots, z_{i,K}$. Add forward edges $S \xrightarrow{\theta_i(1)+M} z_{i,2} \xrightarrow{\theta_i(2)+M} \dots \xrightarrow{\theta_i(K)+M} T$, and **mandatory reverse $\infty$-edges** $z_{i,k+1} \xrightarrow{\infty} z_{i,k}$ for $k = 2, \dots, K-1$.
- **Hard Offset Constraint $x_u \le x_v + d$**: Equivalent to $[x_u \ge k + d \implies x_v \ge k]$, i.e., $z_{u, k+d} \le z_{v, k}$. Add directed edge $(z_{u, k+d} \to z_{v, k}, \infty)$ for all valid $k$.
- **Soft Convex Penalty $g(x_u - x_v)$**: Add directed edge $(z_{u,a} \to z_{v,b})$ of capacity $g(a - b) - 2g(a - b - 1) + g(a - b - 2) \ge 0$. For $c \cdot |x_u - x_v|$, simply add bidirectional edges $(z_{u,k} \leftrightarrow z_{v,k}, c)$ at each level $k \in \{2, \dots, K\}$.
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[Luogu P3227 / HNOI2013]`切糕 (Cake), `[AtCoder ARC 107F]` Sum of Abs, `[Project Selection with Multi-Level Upgrades]`, `[Ishikawa 2003]` Markov Random Field Exact Optimization.

### 3.4 Sub-Domain 4: Monotone Sequence / Tree / DAG Fitting & Isotonic Regression

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Modify array / tree / DAG values $a_i$ into $x_i$ at cost $\sum_i w_i |x_i - a_i|^p$ (or convex $f_i(x_i)$) so that $x_u \le x_v$ for all $(u, v) \in E$", "Modify edge weights of a graph so a given spanning tree $T$ becomes a Minimum/Maximum Spanning Tree", "Modify array elements so a given index subset becomes the lexicographically first LIS".
- **Constraint Signature**: $N \le 1000$, $M \le 5000$, but the value range $V_{\max} \le 10^9$ is **huge** (so an explicit $N \times V_{\max}$ Ishikawa chain of $10^{12}$ nodes is impossible!). Crucially, each unary cost $f_i(x_i)$ is **convex** in $x_i$.

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Explicit Multi-State Graph Blowup**: $V_{\max} = 10^9$ makes building $K = 10^9$ threshold nodes per variable impossible.
- **DAG Topology**: Partial order constraints $(u, v) \in E$ form a general DAG (e.g., tree vs. non-tree edge inequalities $w(e_{\text{tree}}) \le w(e_{\text{non-tree}})$ in Inverse MST), preventing 1D slope-trick heap merging (`Leftist Heap` only works when the dependency graph is a rooted tree or line!).

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. Apply the **Discrete Coarea / Layer-Cake Decomposition**: for any threshold $t$, let $z_i(t) = [x_i > t] \in \{0, 1\}$. Then $x_i = \int_0^\infty z_i(t)\,dt$ (or $\sum_{t} z_i(t)$), and any convex unary cost $f_i(x_i)$ decomposes into marginal threshold slopes $\Delta f_i(t) = f_i(t+1) - f_i(t)$ (or derivative $f'_i(t)$):
   $$f_i(x_i) = f_i(0) + \sum_{t} \Delta f_i(t) \cdot z_i(t), \qquad \text{subject to } z_u(t) \le z_v(t) \;\;\forall (u, v) \in E$$
2. Because $f_i$ is **convex**, $\Delta f_i(t)$ is monotonically non-decreasing in $t$. By the **Topkis / Picard–Queyranne Monotone Cut Nesting Theorem**, solving the independent binary closure problem at each threshold $t$:
   $$\mathcal{P}(t) : \min_{z(t) \in \{0,1\}^V} \sum_{i \in V} \Delta f_i(t) \cdot z_i(t) + \sum_{(u,v) \in E} \infty \cdot z_u(t)(1 - z_v(t))$$
   automatically produces nested inclusion-minimal source sets $S_{\min}(t_1) \supseteq S_{\min}(t_2)$ for $t_1 < t_2$, meaning $z_i^*(t_1) \ge z_i^*(t_2)$ holds **without needing cross-level edges**!

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **Overall Binary Search (`整体二分` / Divide-and-Conquer on Thresholds)**:
  - Define recursive procedure `solve(node_subset U, threshold_range [L, R])`.
  - Set midpoint $M = \lfloor (L + R) / 2 \rfloor$ and build the **single-layer binary closure graph** on $U$ using marginal cost $\Delta f_i(M) = f_i(M+1) - f_i(M)$:
    - If $\Delta f_i(M) < 0$ (prefer $x_i \ge M+1$, i.e., $z_i(M) = 1$): add $(S \to i, -\Delta f_i(M))$.
    - If $\Delta f_i(M) > 0$ (prefer $x_i \le M$, i.e., $z_i(M) = 0$): add $(i \to T, \Delta f_i(M))$.
    - For $(u, v) \in E$ with both $u, v \in U$: add $(u \to v, \infty)$ ($x_u \le x_v \iff z_u(M) \le z_v(M)$).
  - Run Dinic on $U$. Nodes unreachable from $S$ ($z_i^*(M) = 0 \implies x_i^* \le M$) recurse on `[L, M]`; nodes reachable from $S$ ($z_i^*(M) = 1 \implies x_i^* \ge M+1$) recurse on `[M+1, R]`.
  - Total complexity: $O(\text{MaxFlow}(N, M) \log V_{\max})$ (or $O(\text{MaxFlow}(N, M) \log N)$ after coordinate-compressing $L_1$ breakpoints)!
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[Luogu P4548 / Provincial Selection 2018]` 保序回归 (Isotonic Regression), `[UOJ #212 / BZOJ 1937]` Inverse MST, `[Luogu P6621]` Linear Basis Weight Modification.

### 3.5 Sub-Domain 5: Interval Covering, Staffing, Caching & Machine Scheduling

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "At each time step $t \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, at least (or at most) $b_t$ workers/machines/volunteers must be active", "Type-$j$ worker covers contiguous interval $[L_j, R_j]$ at cost $w_j$ per worker", "Every contiguous window of length $K$ must contain between $L$ and $R$ chosen elements", "$k$-server cache of size $K$: serving request sequence $r_1, \dots, r_n$ where loading item $c$ costs $w_c$".
- **Constraint Signature**: $n, m \le 1000$–$5000$, contiguous interval action $[L_j, R_j]$ on time horizon $1 \dots n$.

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **DP State Explosion**: Tracking $K$ active intervals or $K$ cache slots requires $O(n^K)$ states.
- **Greedy Failure**: Earliest-deadline or cheapest-interval greedy fails when intervals have arbitrary weights $w_j$, capacities $u_j$, and both lower and upper coverage bounds at each time step.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. Write the coverage equation at time $t \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ by introducing a non-negative slack/surplus variable $s_t \ge 0$:
   - For **At-Least Demand ($\ge b_t$)**: $\sum_{j : t \in [L_j, R_j]} x_j - s_t = b_t$.
   - For **At-Most Capacity ($\le b_t$)**: $\sum_{j : t \in [L_j, R_j]} x_j + s_t = b_t$.
2. Pad boundary equations $E_0 : 0 = 0$ and $E_{n+1} : 0 = 0$, and take **Consecutive Differences** $\Delta E_t = E_t - E_{t-1}$ for $t = 1, \dots, n+1$:
   $$\sum_{j : L_j = t} x_j - \sum_{j : R_j + 1 = t} x_j \mp s_t \pm s_{t-1} = b_t - b_{t-1} =: \Delta b_t$$
3. **Dual Alternative ($k$-Server Caching / Interval Packing)**: Set baseline where every request $i$ pays load cost $w_{r_i}$, and keeping the same item from request $i$ to its next occurrence $n(i)$ saves $w_{r_i}$ across the open interval $(i, n(i))$ while consuming $1$ cache slot out of $K$.

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **Consecutive Differencing Graph ($V = \{1, \dots, n+1\}$)**:
  - **Interval Variable $x_j \in [0, u_j]$ (cost $w_j$)**: Forward arc $(L_j \to R_j + 1)$ with capacity $u_j$ and cost $w_j$ (since $+x_j$ appears in $\Delta E_{L_j}$ and $-x_j$ appears in $\Delta E_{R_j+1}$).
  - **Slack $s_t \ge 0$ (cost $0$, cap $\infty$)**:
    - For $\le b_t$ ($+s_t$ in $E_t$): Forward spine arc $(t \to t+1, \text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } 0)$.
    - For $\ge b_t$ ($-s_t$ in $E_t$): Backward spine arc $(t+1 \to t, \text{cap } \infty, \text{cost } 0)$.
  - **RHS Balance $\Delta b_t = b_t - b_{t-1}$**: Connect $(S_{\text{sup}} \to t, \text{cap } \Delta b_t, \text{cost } 0)$ if $\Delta b_t > 0$, or $(t \to T_{\text{sup}}, \text{cap } -\Delta b_t, \text{cost } 0)$ if $\Delta b_t < 0$.
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[NOI2008 / Luogu P3980]` Volunteer Recruitment, `[Luogu P3358]` Longest $k$-Interval Set, `[Luogu P4001 / BZOJ 1283]` Sequence, `[CF 802C]` Heidi and Library ($k$-server caching), `[Luogu P1251]` Napkin Plan.

### 3.6 Sub-Domain 6: Matrix, Permutation & Degree-Sequence Reconstruction

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Construct an $R \times C$ matrix $A_{ij} \in [l_{ij}, u_{ij}]$ whose row sums lie in $[L_i^{\text{row}}, U_i^{\text{row}}]$ and column sums lie in $[L_j^{\text{col}}, U_j^{\text{col}}]$", "Round a real matrix $A_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}$ to integers $\lfloor A_{ij} \rfloor$ or $\lceil A_{ij} \rceil$ preserving rounded row and column sums", "Orient undirected edges of a mixed graph to make it Eulerian (or minimize out-degree squares $\sum_v d^+(v)^2$)", "Count or win tournaments where each team beats another".
- **Constraint Signature**: $R, C \le 300$, total cells $R \cdot C \le 10^5$.

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Greedy Row-by-Row Filling**: Filling row 1 greedily starves specific columns needed by later rows with tight lower/upper bounds.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. **Bounded Matrix Rounding / Reconstruction**:
   - View cell $A_{ij}$ as the flow $f(r_i \to c_j)$ on the directed edge from Row node $r_i$ to Column node $c_j$:
     $$L_i^{\text{row}} \le \sum_{j=1}^C f(r_i \to c_j) \le U_i^{\text{row}}, \qquad L_j^{\text{col}} \le \sum_{i=1}^R f(r_i \to c_j) \le U_j^{\text{col}}, \qquad l_{ij} \le f(r_i \to c_j) \le u_{ij}$$
   - For **Baranyai Matrix Rounding**, shift $f'_{ij} = A_{ij} - \lfloor A_{ij} \rfloor \in [0, 1]$, reducing bounds to $\{0, 1\}$ capacities!
2. **Tournament $3$-Cycles (`Landau's Theorem` & Degree Convexity)**:
   - In an $n$-vertex tournament, a triad $\{u, v, w\}$ is **non-cyclic (transitive)** iff one vertex beats the other two ($\binom{d^+(v)}{2}$ transitive triples where $v$ beats two others). Thus the number of 3-cycles is:
     $$\#(\text{3-cycles}) = \binom{n}{3} - \sum_{v=1}^n \binom{d^+(v)}{2}$$
     Maximizing 3-cycles is equivalent to **minimizing the convex separable sum** $\sum_{v=1}^n \binom{d^+(v)}{2}$, where marginal cost $\Delta \binom{k}{2} = \binom{k}{2} - \binom{k-1}{2} = k - 1$ is strictly increasing ($0, 1, 2, \dots, n-2$)!

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **Bounded Matrix Compilation**:
  - Add $S \to r_i$ with lower/upper bound $[L_i^{\text{row}}, U_i^{\text{row}}]$, $r_i \to c_j$ with $[l_{ij}, u_{ij}]$, and $c_j \to T$ with $[L_j^{\text{col}}, U_j^{\text{col}}]$, then solve Feasible / Min / Max $S$-$T$ Flow with Lower Bounds (Section 2.1.3).
- **Tournament / Win-Loss Convex Degree Compilation (`[WC2007]` Scissors-Paper-Stone)**:
  - Each undecided match $e = \{u, v\}$ is a supply node $(S \to e, \text{cap } 1, \text{cost } 0)$ with two choices $(e \to u, \text{cap } 1)$ or $(e \to v, \text{cap } 1)$ (whoever wins gets $+1$ out-degree).
  - From each player $v$, add $n-1$ parallel convex-split arcs $(v \to T)$ of capacity $1$ and marginal costs $0, 1, 2, \dots, n-2$.
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[Luogu P5192]` Shoot the Bullet (Lower-bound max flow), `[CF 704D]` Captain America (Bipartite bounded discrepancy), `[UVa 10735]` Mixed Graph Eulerian Circuit, `[Luogu P4249 / WC2007]` Scissors-Paper-Stone.

### 3.7 Sub-Domain 7: String, Subsequence, LIS & Poset Partitioning Problems

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Find the maximum number of disjoint Longest Increasing Subsequences (LIS) of length $L$", "Partition sequence into minimum number of monotone / valid chains", "Choose $k$ disjoint subsequences where adjacent elements satisfy $|a_{i_t} - a_{i_{t+1}}| = 1$ or $a_{i_t} \equiv a_{i_{t+1}} \pmod 7$".
- **Constraint Signature**: Sequence length $N \le 1000$–$5000$ (or $N \le 500$ for $O(N^2)$ transitions).

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Greedy Subsequence Extraction**: Greedily extracting the first LIS (or longest valid chain) and deleting its elements can shatter two other valid chains, leaving $k-1$ instead of $k$ paths (the exact failure that residual backward arcs undo!).
- **Naive $O(N^2)$ Edge Blowup**: Connecting every pair $i < j$ satisfying $a_i + 1 = a_j$ or $a_i \equiv a_j \pmod 7$ creates $\Theta(N^2)$ edges, causing TLE when $N = 3000$–$5000$.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. **Layered LIS Disjoint Packing**:
   - First compute $dp[i] =$ length of LIS ending at $i$, and let $L = \max_i dp[i]$.
   - An element $i$ can be the $dp[i]$-th vertex of a valid LIS iff it transitions to $j > i$ with $a_i \le a_j$ and $dp[j] = dp[i] + 1$.
   - Vertex-disjointness requires throughput $\le 1$ (or $\le c_i$) at each index $i$: split $i \mapsto (i_{\text{in}} \to i_{\text{out}})$ with capacity $c_i$.
2. **Prefix Value-Hub Optimization ($O(N^2) \to O(N)$ Edges)**:
   - Instead of adding directed edges $(i_{\text{out}} \to j_{\text{in}})$ for all $j > i$ with $a_j = v$, create a **Persistent Prefix / Suffix Value Chain** $H_{v, \text{time}}$ for each value/residue class $v$:
   - At step $i$, transition $H_{v, i_{\text{prev}}} \to H_{v, i}$ (cap $\infty$, cost $0$) and $H_{v, i} \to i_{\text{in}}$ (cap $\infty$, cost $0$), while $i_{\text{out}}$ feeds into $H_{a_i, i}$. This compresses $\Theta(N^2)$ pairwise transitions into $O(N)$ DAG edges!

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **LIS Layer Graph**: $S \to i_{\text{in}}$ (if $dp[i] = 1$), $i_{\text{in}} \to i_{\text{out}}$ (cap $1$), $i_{\text{out}} \to j_{\text{in}}$ (if $i < j, a_i \le a_j, dp[j] = dp[i] + 1$), and $i_{\text{out}} \to T$ (if $dp[i] = L$).
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[Luogu P2766]` Longest Non-Decreasing Subsequence Problem, `[CF 818G]` Four Melodies (Value-hub $O(N)$ MCMF), `[Luogu P3308 / SDOI2014]` LIS (Lexicographically smallest min-cut via residual reachability test).

### 3.8 Sub-Domain 8: Ratio / Average Optimization & Parametric Bottlenecks

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Maximize the ratio $\frac{\text{Total Profit}(S)}{\text{Total Cost}(S)}$ or $\frac{|E(S)|}{|S|}$", "Minimize the maximum load / time $T$ required to evacuate all tokens or complete all jobs with preemption", "Maximize the minimum edge weight in a valid matching / cycle cover".
- **Constraint Signature**: Fractional output (precision $10^{-6}$ or exact fraction $\frac{p}{q}$ with $q \le N^2$), or bottleneck time $T \le 10^9$.

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Non-Additivity of Ratios**: $\frac{a_1 + a_2}{b_1 + b_2} \neq \frac{a_1}{b_1} + \frac{a_2}{b_2}$; local optimality does not compose along subproblems.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. **Dinkelbach's 0-1 Fractional Linearization**:
   $$\max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \frac{N(x)}{D(x)} \ge \lambda \iff h(\lambda) := \max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \bigl( N(x) - \lambda D(x) \bigr) \ge 0$$
   When $N(x)$ is supermodular (closure / edge count $\sum_{e=(u,v)} w_e x_u x_v$) and $D(x)$ is modular/submodular ($\sum_v c_v x_v + \sum_{(u,v)} d_{uv} x_u(1 - x_v)$), $N(x) - \lambda D(x)$ is a standard **Picard / Submodular Min-Cut** for every fixed $\lambda > 0$!
2. **Preemptive Job Scheduling ($P \mid pmtn, r_i, d_i \mid L_{\max}$)**:
   - Sort all distinct release times $r_i$ and deadlines $d_i + \Delta$ into $2N$ elementary time intervals $I_k = [t_k, t_{k+1})$ of length $L_k = t_{k+1} - t_k$.
   - On interval $I_k$, at most $M$ machines can work in parallel, and each single job $i$ can receive at most $L_k$ units of processing (no self-concurrency):
     $$\sum_{k : I_k \subseteq [r_i, d_i + \Delta]} f_{i, k} = p_i, \qquad 0 \le f_{i, k} \le L_k, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^N f_{i, k} \le M \cdot L_k$$

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **Goldberg Max-Density Subgraph ($\max \frac{\sum_{e \in E(S)} w_e}{\sum_{v \in S} c_v}$)**:
  - Degree-scaling formulation (no edge-nodes needed!): Let $D_v = \sum_{e \ni v} w_e$. Using $2 \sum_{e \in E(S)} w_e = \sum_{v \in S} D_v - \sum_{(u,v) \in E} w_{uv} |x_u - x_v|$, connect $(S \to v, D_v)$, undirected $(u \leftrightarrow v, w_{uv})$, and $(v \to T, 2\lambda c_v)$. Then $h(\lambda) \ge 0 \iff \text{MinCut}(\lambda) < \sum_{v \in V} D_v$.
- **Preemptive Elementary Interval Graph**:
  - $S \to \text{Job}_i$ (cap $p_i$), $\text{Job}_i \to \text{Interval}_k$ (cap $L_k$ if $I_k \subseteq [r_i, d_i + \Delta]$), and $\text{Interval}_k \to T$ (cap $M \cdot L_k$). Feasible iff Max-Flow $= \sum_i p_i$.
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[UVA 1389 / POJ 3155]` Hard Life, `[Luogu P3425 / POI2005]` Koszulki, `[Luogu P3324 / SDOI2015]` Star Wars (Preemptive Laser Attack).

### 3.9 Sub-Domain 9: Game Theory, Adversarial Blocking & Minimax Cut/Matching Games

#### Step 1: Raw English Phrase & Constraint Signal
- **Surface Clues**: "Two players alternately move a token along edges of a graph / grid without revisiting any vertex; last player to move wins", "Attacker chooses $k$ edges to block/increase cost, Defender routes flow/shortest path".
- **Constraint Signature**: Impartial/partizan walk on a graph ($N \le 10^4$) with **no revisited vertices** (converting a cyclic graph game into a matching game!), or continuous/integral adversarial budget.

#### Step 2: Why Naive DP / Greedy Fails
- **Visited-Set Exponential State**: Because vertices cannot be revisited, the game is finite, but the state space is $(u, V_{\text{visited}})$ of size $N \cdot 2^N$, so standard Sprague–Grundy DAG DP is impossible.

#### Step 3: Exact Algebraic Intermediate Representation
1. **Vertex-Deletion Walk Game $\iff$ Dulmage–Mendelsohn / Gallai–Edmonds Matching Core**:
   - **Theorem**: Starting from vertex $v_0$ in graph $G = (V, E)$, the first player (Alice) has a forced winning strategy in the no-revisit walk game **if and only if** $v_0$ belongs to **every** maximum matching of $G$ (i.e., $\nu(G \setminus \{v_0\}) = \nu(G)$)!
   - *Proof*:
     - ($\impliedby$) Suppose $v_0$ is in every maximum matching $M^*$. Alice moves along $v_0$'s matched edge $(v_0, u_1) \in M^*$. Now Bob is at $u_1$ in $G \setminus \{v_0\}$. Bob cannot move to an $M^*$-unmatched vertex $w$, because then $P = (v_0 \to u_1 \to w)$ would allow replacing $(v_0, u_1) \in M^*$ with $(u_1, w)$, producing a maximum matching $M'$ that leaves $v_0$ exposed! Thus Bob is always forced to move to another $M^*$-matched vertex $v_1$, and Alice immediately replies along $v_1$'s matched edge $(v_1, u_2) \in M^*$.
     - ($\implies$) Suppose there exists a maximum matching $M^*$ leaving $v_0$ exposed. Then any first move $(v_0, u_1)$ by Alice must land on a vertex $u_1$ matched in $M^*$ (otherwise $M^* \cup \{(v_0, u_1)\}$ is a larger matching!), so Bob adopts the exact mirror strategy using $M^*$ and wins!
2. **Zero-Sum Matrix Game $\iff$ Primal-Dual LP**:
   - By **von Neumann's Minimax Theorem**, $\max_{p \in \Delta_m} \min_{q \in \Delta_n} p^T A q = \min_{q \in \Delta_n} \max_{p \in \Delta_m} p^T A q$. When the action space $\mathcal{X}$ is the flow/matching polytope, LP duality compiles the adversary's best response directly into the Dual Cut / Potential LP!

#### Step 4: Mechanical Graph Compilation Rule & Canonical Exemplars
- **Bipartite Game All-Winning-Starts Extraction ($O(E\sqrt{V})$ in a Single Max-Flow!)**:
  - Never run $N$ separate Max-Flows! Compute ONE maximum matching $M^*$ in $G = (L \sqcup R, E)$ and extract the **Dulmage–Mendelsohn Reachable Sets** (Section 2.2.1):
    - From exposed vertices in $L$, run alternating BFS ($L \xrightarrow{\notin M^*} R \xrightarrow{\in M^*} L$). Every visited $u \in L$ can be left exposed in some maximum matching $\implies$ **Bob (Second Player) wins** starting at $u$; for unvisited $u \in L$, **Alice (First Player) wins**!
    - Symmetrically, from exposed vertices in $R$, run reverse alternating BFS ($R \xrightarrow{\notin M^*} L \xrightarrow{\in M^*} R$) to classify all $v \in R$.
- **General Non-Bipartite Game Extraction ($O(V^3)$)**:
  - Run Edmonds' Blossom algorithm to compute the **Gallai–Edmonds Decomposition** $V = D(G) \sqcup A(G) \sqcup C(G)$. Then $v_0$ is a Second-Player win iff $v_0 \in D(G)$, and a First-Player win iff $v_0 \in A(G) \cup C(G)$!
- **Canonical Exemplars**: `[Luogu P4055 / JSOI2009]` Game (Bipartite grid game), `[Luogu P3171 / SCOI2015]` Network Throughput, `[Luogu P1963 / NOI2009]` Transform Sequence, `[General Graph Matching Game via Gallai–Edmonds]`.

## 4. The Direction B Flow-Elimination Playbook (When $N \le 10^5$–$5 \cdot 10^5$: Flow $\to$ Fast Non-Flow Algorithm)

When a problem has all the structural signatures of Network Flow, Bipartite Matching, or Min-Cost Max-Flow (Section 3), **but the constraint bounds are $N \le 10^5$–$5 \cdot 10^5$**, running Dinic or SSP directly is guaranteed to TLE. In this regime, the flow network is not an algorithm to be executed—it is an **algebraic bridge** whose Dual (Min-Cut, Hall's Defect, Johnson Potentials, or Residual Augmentation Cycle) collapses into a $O(N \log N)$ or $O(N \log^2 N)$ data structure, DP, or greedy priority-queue simulation.

This section codifies the **5 Canonical Flow-Elimination Compilers (`4.1`–`4.5`)**.

---

### 4.1 Compiler 1: Max-Flow $\to$ Min-Cut Dualization $\to$ DP / Segment Tree / Convex Optimization

Whenever a problem asks for the Maximum Flow on a highly regular graph (a DAG on $1 \dots N$, a Complete Bipartite Graph $K_{N, M}$ with structured capacities, a Tree, or a Grid strip) with $N \le 2 \cdot 10^5$:

#### The 5-Step Dual Elimination Recipe
1. **Formulate the Exact Primal Max-Flow**: Write down the source $S$, sink $T$, and all directed edge capacities $c(u, v)$ as if $N \le 100$.
2. **Apply Strong Duality ($\text{MaxFlow} = \text{MinCut}$)**: Replace the flow maximization with minimizing the cut capacity $C(S_{\text{cut}}, T_{\text{cut}}) = \sum_{u \in S_{\text{cut}}, v \in T_{\text{cut}}} c(u, v)$ over all binary assignments $x_v \in \{0, 1\}$ ($1 \iff v \in T_{\text{cut}}$, $0 \iff v \in S_{\text{cut}}$).
3. **Exploit Symmetry or Sequential Structure to Parametrize the Cut**:
   - *Sequential / Prefix DAGs*: Express $C(x_1, \dots, x_N)$ as a sequential transition where each node $i$ chooses $x_i \in \{0, 1\}$ and interacts only with previous choices via a summary statistic (such as the count $k = \sum_{j < i} (1 - x_j)$ of prior nodes kept in $S_{\text{cut}}$!).
   - *Bipartite $L \sqcup R$ Networks*: Fix the subset $A = L \cap S_{\text{cut}}$ (or just its cardinality $k = |L \cap S_{\text{cut}}|$). Once $A \subseteq L$ is fixed, each right vertex $v \in R$ makes a **completely independent** binary choice $v \in S_{\text{cut}}$ vs. $v \in T_{\text{cut}}$!
4. **Eliminate the Independent Side Analytically**:
   $$\min_{A \subseteq L,\; B \subseteq R} C(A, B) = \min_{A \subseteq L} \left( \sum_{u \notin A} c(S, u) + \sum_{v \in R} \min\Bigl( c(v, T),\; \sum_{u \in A} c(u, v) \Bigr) \right)$$
5. **Optimize via DP, Sorting, Two-Pointers, or Segment Tree**:
   - If $c(u, v) = c$ is uniform for all $u \in L, v \in R$ (as in `[CF 724E]` or `[ARC 125E]`), then $\sum_{u \in A} c(u, v)$ depends **only on the cardinality $k = |A|$**! For each fixed $k \in \{0, \dots, |L|\}$, we greedily put the $k$ largest-$c(S, u)$ vertices into $A$, and evaluate $\sum_{v \in R} \min(c(v, T), k \cdot c_v)$ using sorted prefix sums in $O(N \log N)$!

#### Canonical Worked Compilations

| Archetype & Problem | Primal Flow Network ($N \le 2 \cdot 10^5$) | Min-Cut Algebraic Collapse | Final $O(N \log N)$ / $O(N^2)$ Non-Flow Algorithm |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prefix-to-Suffix DAG Transport** (`[CF 724E]` Goods Transportation, $N \le 10^4$) | $(S \to i, p_i)$, $(i \to T, s_i)$, and $(i \to j, c)$ for all $1 \le i < j \le N$. | Node $i$ put in $S_{\text{cut}}$ pays $s_i$; node $i$ put in $T_{\text{cut}}$ pays $p_i + c \cdot |\{ j < i : j \in S_{\text{cut}} \}|$. | $O(N^2)$ DP (or $O(N \log N)$ slope-trick): $dp[i][k] =$ min cut of prefix $1 \dots i$ with $k$ nodes in $S_{\text{cut}}$: $dp[i][k] = \min(dp[i-1][k-1] + s_i,\; dp[i-1][k] + p_i + k \cdot c)$. |
| **Complete Bipartite Supply-Demand** (`[ARC 125E]` Snack, $N, M \le 2 \cdot 10^5$) | $(S \to i, A_i)$ for $i \in [1, N]$, $(j \to T, C_j)$ for $j \in [1, M]$, and $(i \to j, B_j)$ for all $(i, j)$. | Fix $k = |L \cap S_{\text{cut}}| \in [0, N]$. To minimize $\sum_{i \notin S_{\text{cut}}} A_i$, put the $k$ largest $A_i$ into $S_{\text{cut}}$. Each $j \in R$ independently pays $\min(C_j, k \cdot B_j)$. | Sort $A_1 \ge \dots \ge A_N$ and sort $j \in [1, M]$ by ratio $C_j / B_j$. Sweep $k = 0 \dots N$ with two pointers / prefix sums in $O((N + M) \log(N + M))$. |
| **2-Row / Strip Grid Min-Cut** (`[CF 1368H]` Breadboard, $N, M \le 10^5$) | Grid cells $(r, c)$ connected by capacity-$1$ grid edges; boundary ports connected to $S$ (red) or $T$ (blue). | By convexity of min-cut on high-aspect grids, the optimal cut never switches color more than once along the short side, reducing each column to a monochromatic state $x_c \in \{S, T\}$. | $2 \times 2$ Matrix Multiplication Segment Tree (`Dynamic DP`) maintaining column states $(S, T)$ across boundary port flips in $O((N + M + Q) \log(N + M))$. |

### 4.2 Compiler 2: Bipartite Matching $\to$ Hall's Marriage Theorem $\to$ Interval Segment Tree

Whenever a problem asks whether a bipartite graph $G = (L \sqcup R, E)$ with $N \le 2 \cdot 10^5$ has a perfect matching (or asks for the maximum matching size $\nu(G)$ via **Ore's Defect Formula** $\nu(G) = |L| - \max_{A \subseteq L} (|A| - |N(A)|)$) and every vertex $u \in L$ connects to a **contiguous interval** $N(u) = [l_u, r_u] \subseteq R$ (or complement of an interval, or circular arc):

#### The Contiguous Violator Reduction Theorem
1. **Why $2^{|L|}$ Subsets Collapse to $\binom{|R|+1}{2}$ Intervals**:
   - Suppose $N(u) = [l_u, r_u]$ is an interval for every $u \in L$. Take any subset $A \subseteq L$. Decompose the union $N(A) = \bigcup_{u \in A} [l_u, r_u]$ into its connected interval components $I_1 \sqcup I_2 \sqcup \dots \sqcup I_m$.
   - Partition $A = A_1 \sqcup \dots \sqcup A_m$ where $A_k = \{ u \in A \mid [l_u, r_u] \subseteq I_k \}$. Then $|A| - |N(A)| = \sum_{k=1}^m (|A_k| - |I_k|)$, so at least one component $I_k = [L, R]$ achieves at least as large a Hall defect!
   - Furthermore, for a fixed target interval $[L, R] \subseteq R$, to maximize $|A| - (R - L + 1)$ subject to $N(A) \subseteq [L, R]$, we MUST greedily include **every** $u \in L$ whose neighborhood $[l_u, r_u]$ is completely contained inside $[L, R]$!
2. **Exact Algebraic Formula**:
   $$\max_{A \subseteq L} \bigl( |A| - |N(A)| \bigr) = \max_{1 \le L \le R \le |R|} \Bigl( \underbrace{\#\{ u \in L \mid L \le l_u \le r_u \le R \}}_{\text{Intervals } [l_u, r_u] \subseteq [L, R]} \;-\; (R - L + 1) \Bigr)$$
3. **Sweep-Line + Lazy Segment Tree Compilation ($O(N \log N)$)**:
   - Sweep the right endpoint $R$ from $1$ to $|R|$. Maintain in a Segment Tree over $L \in [1, R]$ the value $V(L) = \text{count}(L, R) - (R - L + 1) = \text{count}(L, R) + L - R - 1$.
   - When advancing to $R$, for each $u \in L$ with $r_u = R$, add $+1$ (or weight $w_u$) to the prefix range $L \in [1, l_u]$, and query the global maximum $\max_{1 \le L \le R} V(L)$!
4. **Complement-of-Interval Variant (`[ARC 076F]` Exhausted?)**:
   - When $u \in L$ connects to $[1, l_u] \cup [r_u, M]$ (a prefix and a suffix), $N(A)$ is always a prefix $[1, L]$ union a suffix $[R, M]$ ($L < R$). A node $u \in L$ has $N(u) \subseteq [1, L] \cup [R, M]$ iff $l_u \le L$ and $r_u \ge R$, which is a 2D dominance box $[l_u \le L \land r_u \ge R]$ solved identically by sweeping $L$ and maintaining $R$ in a Segment Tree!
5. **Circular / Torus Hall Variant (`[CF 981F]` Round Table)**:
   - Unroll the circle of circumference $C$ by duplicating the arrays $2$–$3$ times (so every circular arc of length $< C$ becomes a contiguous linear interval), and enforce $R - L < C$ via a sliding-window Segment Tree query!

### 4.3 Compiler 3: MCMF $\to$ Simulated Cost Flow (`模拟费用流`)

When an MCMF network has a structured topology (a 1D line, a Tree, or a $k$-disjoint interval chain) with $N \le 2 \cdot 10^5$, **Simulated Cost Flow (`模拟费用流`)** executes the exact Successive Shortest Path (SSP) or Cycle-Cancelling steps using priority queues or segment trees—explicitly maintaining the **reverse residual cancellation edges** without ever building the graph.

#### Archetype A: 1D Line / Stock Trading & Mouse-Hole Matching (`Buy Low Sell High` / `雪灾与外卖`)

1. **Unidirectional 1D Prefix Regret (`[CF 865D]` Buy Low Sell High)**:
   - At day $i$ (price $p_i$), we can match $i$ with the cheapest available earlier day $j < i$ (cost $p_j$) to earn $p_i - p_j$.
   - **Residual Regret Insertion**: If we later decide on day $k > i$ to sell at $p_k$ instead of $p_i$, the net gain is $(p_k - p_j) - (p_i - p_j) = p_k - p_i$. Thus, matching $(j, i)$ pushes **two** copies of $p_i$ into the min-heap: one representing the unused share at day $i$, and one representing the **residual cancellation option** of revoking the sale at $i$ and selling at $k$ instead!
2. **Bidirectional 1D Line Mouse-Hole Matching (`[UOJ #455]` 雪灾与外卖, $N, M \le 10^5$)**:
   - Mice at $x_i$ and holes at $y_j$ ( capacity $c_j$, cost $w_j$) move left and right along a 1D line with distance cost $|x_i - y_j|$.
   - Sweep left-to-right along coordinates $z$. Maintain two min-heaps: $Q_{\text{mouse}}$ (effective cost of taking an unmatched mouse or stealing a mouse from a left hole) and $Q_{\text{hole}}$ (effective cost of matching a mouse into a left hole or cancelling a right-moving mouse).
   - **Symmetric Refund Principle**: Whenever mouse $i$ at $x_i$ matches with left hole $j$ at $y_j$ paying $(x_i - y_j) + v_j$:
     - Push a **synthetic hole** into $Q_{\text{hole}}$ at cost $-(x_i - y_j + v_j) - x_i = -2x_i + y_j - v_j$ so a future hole $j'$ at $y_{j'} > x_i$ can steal mouse $i$ for net change $(y_{j'} - x_i) - (x_i - y_j + v_j) = y_{j'} - 2x_i + y_j - v_j$!
     - Push a **synthetic mouse** into $Q_{\text{mouse}}$ at cost $-(x_i - y_j) - y_j = -x_i$ so a future mouse $i'$ at $x_{i'}$ can steal hole $j$ from mouse $i$!

#### Archetype B: Tree Metric MCMF via LCA Lazy-Heap Merging (`[ICPC WF 2018]` Conquer the World)

- On a weighted tree $T$ with edge weights $w_e \ge 0$, tree distance splits at $u = \text{LCA}(x, y)$:
  $$\text{dist}(x, y) = \text{dep}(x) + \text{dep}(y) - 2\,\text{dep}(\text{LCA}(x, y))$$
- Run bottom-up Tree DP: at each vertex $u$, merge the min-heaps $H_{\text{supply}}(u)$ and $H_{\text{demand}}(u)$ of its children in $O(\log^2 N)$ time using **Leftist Heaps** or **Small-to-Large Merging (`启发式合并`)**.
- Whenever the top of $H_{\text{supply}}(u)$ (value $s$) and the top of $H_{\text{demand}}(u)$ (value $d$) satisfy $s + d - 2\,\text{dep}(u) < 0$ (using a $-\infty$ mandatory penalty $M$ on demand nodes to force full saturation):
  - Pair $\Delta = \min(\text{cnt}_s, \text{cnt}_d)$ units at cost $\Delta \cdot (s + d - 2\,\text{dep}(u))$.
  - **Residual Refund**: Push $\Delta$ units of **cancelled supply** back into $H_{\text{supply}}(u)$ with value $2\,\text{dep}(u) - d$, and $\Delta$ units of **cancelled demand** back into $H_{\text{demand}}(u)$ with value $2\,\text{dep}(u) - s$!
  - *Verification*: If an ancestor $u' = \text{anc}(u)$ later pairs this cancelled supply $(2\,\text{dep}(u) - d)$ with a new demand $d'$, the total paid cost is $(s + d - 2\,\text{dep}(u)) + ((2\,\text{dep}(u) - d) + d' - 2\,\text{dep}(u')) = s + d' - 2\,\text{dep}(u')$—the intermediate match $(s, d)$ at $u$ cancels out algebraically!

#### Archetype C: $k$-Disjoint Subarrays via Segment Tree Sign-Flipping (`[CF 280D]` $k$-Maximum Subsequences)

- **Flow Model**: Choosing at most $k$ disjoint subarrays inside query range $[L, R]$ to maximize total sum is equivalent to sending at most $k$ units of flow through a vertex-split chain $L \to L+1 \to \dots \to R$ where edge $(i_{\text{in}} \to i_{\text{out}})$ has capacity $1$ and weight $+a_i$ (and bypass edge has capacity $\infty$, weight $0$).
- **Residual Augmentation Invariant**: Pushing $1$ unit of flow along the maximum-sum subarray $[l^*, r^*] \subseteq [L, R]$ saturates the forward $+a_i$ edges on $[l^*, r^*]$ and activates their reverse residual edges of weight **$-a_i$**!
- **Segment Tree Simulation ($O(k \log N)$ per query)**: Maintain in each Segment Tree node both the **Maximum Subarray Sum** (with endpoints) and the **Minimum Subarray Sum** (with endpoints). To augment $k$ times on $[L, R]$:
  1. Query the maximum subarray $[l_1, r_1] \subseteq [L, R]$ of sum $S_1$. If $S_1 \le 0$, stop early!
  2. Otherwise, add $S_1$ to the answer and apply a **lazy range sign-flip ($\times (-1)$)** to $[l_1, r_1]$ (which swaps max-subarray and negated min-subarray in $O(1)$ per node!). Repeat up to $k$ times, then undo the $k$ range flips to restore the array.

### 4.4 Compiler 4: MCMF Slope Convexity $\to$ WQS Binary Search (`Aliens Trick`) & Slope Trick

When an MCMF network forces exactly $k$ units of flow from $S$ to $T$ ($N, k \le 5 \cdot 10^5$), the minimum cost function $C(k)$ is guaranteed to be **discrete convex** ($C(k+1) - C(k) \ge C(k) - C(k-1)$) by the Shortest Augmenting Path Theorem.

1. **WQS Binary Search / Lagrangian Relaxation (`Aliens Trick`)**:
   - Instead of enforcing the global flow budget $\sum_i f_i = k$ (which requires $O(Nk)$ DP or $k$ augmenting paths), attach a Lagrangian penalty $-\lambda$ to every unit of flow reaching $T$:
     $$g(\lambda) = \min_{f \ge 0} \bigl( C(f) - \lambda f \bigr)$$
   - Once the global constraint $f = k$ is relaxed into a per-unit penalty $-\lambda$, the inner minimization $g(\lambda)$ drops the dimension $k$ completely and reduces to an unconstrained 1D DP, greedy heap (`[CF 802O]` April Fools' Problem in $O(N \log N \log V)$), or nested WQS binary search (`[CF 739E]` Gosha is Hunting, reducing $O(N K_1 K_2)$ two-item matching to $O(N \log^2 V)$)!
   - **Collinear Tie-Breaking Contract**: If $C(f)$ has flat collinear segments where multiple flow volumes $f \in [f_{\min}(\lambda), f_{\max}(\lambda)]$ tie for the minimum of $C(f) - \lambda f$, consistently break ties in favor of the **minimum** (or maximum) item count $f_{\min}(\lambda)$, find the largest $\lambda^*$ with $f_{\max}(\lambda^*) \ge k$, and recover $C(k) = g(\lambda^*) + \lambda^* \cdot k$.
2. **Slope Trick (`Leftist Heap` / Priority Queue Envelope Maintenance)**:
   - For 1D or Tree $L_1$ Isotonic Regression ($\min_{x_1 \le \dots \le x_n} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i |x_i - a_i|$), instead of running `整体二分` + Max-Flow in $O(\text{MaxFlow} \log V)$, maintain the piecewise-linear convex DP function $F_i(x) = \min_{x_1 \le \dots \le x_i \le x} \sum_{j=1}^i w_j |x_j - a_j|$ directly via its **slope-change breakpoints** in a max-heap!
   - Adding $|x - a_i|$ adds slope $-1$ on $(-\infty, a_i)$ and $+1$ on $(a_i, \infty)$ (pushing $a_i$ twice), and taking the prefix minimum $\tilde{F}_i(x) = \min_{y \le x} F_i(y)$ zeroes out the rightmost positive slope by **popping the maximum element** of the max-heap in $O(N \log N)$ total time!

### 4.5 Compiler 5: Data-Structure Graph Sparsification (Virtual Hub Compression for Dense Flow Graphs)

When the number of vertices $N \le 5 \cdot 10^4$ is small enough for Dinic / MCMF **provided the edge count is $O(N \log N)$**, but the problem statement specifies $\Theta(N^2)$ transitions (e.g., every $i$ connects to a contiguous range $[L_i, R_i]$, a 2D rectangle, a tree path, or all bitwise submasks):

> [!TIP]
> **The Virtual Hub Sparsification Principle**:
> Never add $\Theta(N^2)$ infinite-capacity edges directly! Whenever a bipartite biclique $A \times B$ of capacity $\infty$ (and additive cost $w_u + w_v$) exists, insert a single **Virtual Hub $H_{A,B}$** with $|A|$ incoming edges $(u \to H_{A,B}, \infty)$ and $|B|$ outgoing edges $(H_{A,B} \to v, \infty)$, compressing $|A| \cdot |B|$ edges into $|A| + |B|$. Decompose any structured adjacency relation into a small collection of canonical hubs!

| Dense Adjacency Pattern ($\Theta(N^2)$ Naive Edges) | Virtual Hub Data Structure | Vertex Count $|V'|$ & Edge Count $|E'|$ | Critical Implementation Contract |

## 5. Canonical Gadget Hazard & Pitfall Checklist (`H-01` through `H-24`) & Master Complexity Reference

Even when a solver identifies the right flow paradigm, a single broken gadget invariant—an omitted reverse $\infty$-edge, an unpruned prerequisite cycle, a swapped lower-bound balance sign, or a naive SPFA call on a dense graph—will silently corrupt the polyhedron or cause TLE.

This section catalogues the **24 Canonical Gadget Hazards (`H-01`–`H-24`)** audited across Cohorts 1, 2, and 3, followed by the **Master Algorithmic Complexity & Precondition Reference**.

### 5.1 The 24 Canonical Gadget Hazards (`H-01` through `H-24`)

| **`H-18`: Uncapped Johnson Potential Update on Early-Exit Dijkstra** | Wave 4B | Breaking out of Dijkstra as soon as `u == t` is popped from the priority queue, and updating `pi[v] += dist[v]` only for visited nodes while leaving unvisited nodes unchanged (or adding `INF`). | If node $x$ has `dist[x] > dist[t]` and is not yet popped when `t` exits, leaving `pi[x]` unchanged while increasing `pi[u]` by `dist[u]` for an explored neighbor $u \to x$ can make $w(u, x) + \pi'(u) - \pi'(x) < 0$, creating a **negative reduced cost** in the next phase! | Either run Dijkstra to completion over all reachable vertices before updating `pi[v] += dist[v]`, OR cap every potential update at `dist[t]`: `pi[v] += min(dist[v], dist[t])`. |

To send $1$ unit of flow, MCMF greedily routes along the second arc (cost $2$) instead of the first arc (cost $10$), paying $2$ instead of $g(1) = 10$! | Parallel marginal splitting is valid **if and only if** $g(f)$ is **convex** ($\Delta g(1) \le \Delta g(2) \le \dots \le \Delta g(U)$). Concave flow costs are NP-hard in general (Fixed-Charge Network Flow) and require min-cut binary indicator gadgets or DP when small. |
| **`H-21`: Off-by-One Boundary Shift in Equation Differencing (`R_j` vs. `R_j + 1`)** | Wave 5A | Compiling an interval variable $x_j$ active on contiguous rows $[L_j, R_j]$ into a flow arc $(L_j \to R_j)$ instead of $(L_j \to R_j + 1)$. | Under $\Delta E_t = E_t - E_{t-1}$, an variable $x_j$ present in $E_{L_j}, \dots, E_{R_j}$ cancels in $\Delta E_{R_j} = E_{R_j} - E_{R_j-1}$ and disappears only in $\Delta E_{R_j+1} = E_{R_j+1} - E_{R_j}$! Connecting $(L_j \to R_j)$ drops $x_j$'s coverage of row $R_j$ completely! | Always pad boundary equation $E_{n+1} : 0 = 0$ (creating $n+1$ vertices $1 \dots n+1$) and compile every interval $[L_j, R_j]$ into the directed arc **$(L_j \to R_j + 1)$**! |
| **`H-22`: Shared In-Tree / Out-Tree Hub Short-Circuit in Segment Tree Sparsification** | Wave 5B | Using a single Segment Tree of virtual hubs where both incoming range edges $([L_1, R_1] \to u)$ and outgoing range edges $(v \to [L_2, R_2])$ attach to the same tree nodes with bidirectional $\infty$ links. | If $v$ links down to hub $H$ and $u$ links up from hub $H$, any parent-child bidirectional $\infty$-edge allows flow to travel down to a child and immediately back up to an unrelated ancestor, connecting all intervals indiscriminately! | Build **two strictly separate directed trees** sharing only the $N$ leaf vertices: an **Out-Tree** (directed parent $\to$ child, for $u \to [L, R]$) and an **In-Tree** (directed child $\to$ parent, for $[L, R] \to v$). |
| **`H-23`: Missing Synthetic Cancellation Pair in Simulated MCMF (`模拟费用流`)** | Wave 5B | In 1D Mouse-Hole (`[UOJ #455]`) or Tree LCA (`[ICPC WF 2018]`) heap simulation, pushing only the synthetic mouse (or only the synthetic hole) after a match $(x_i, y_j)$ instead of **both** residual cancellation options. | Matching mouse $x_1$ to hole $y_1$ creates TWO residual paths in $G_f$: a future hole $y_2$ can steal $x_1$ (cancelling $y_1$), OR a future mouse $x_2$ can steal $y_1$ (re-matching $x_1$ elsewhere). Omitting either synthetic heap entry misses half of the augmenting paths! | Every time a supply unit $s$ and demand unit $d$ are paired at LCA $u$ (cost $s + d - 2\,\text{dep}(u)$), push **both** a synthetic supply of value $2\,\text{dep}(u) - d$ into $H_{\text{supply}}(u)$ AND a synthetic demand of value $2\,\text{dep}(u) - s$ into $H_{\text{demand}}(u)$! |
| **`H-24`: Collinear Slope Ambiguity in WQS Binary Search (`Aliens Trick`)** | Wave 5B | Binary searching on penalty $\lambda$ for $C(k)$ without deterministic tie-breaking when $C(f)$ has flat collinear segments ($C(f+1) - C(f) = \lambda^*$ for multiple $f \in [f_{\min}, f_{\max}]$). | If $C(f)$ has constant slope $\lambda^*$ across $f \in [3, 7]$ and target is $k = 5$, an arbitrary tie-breaker returns $f = 3$ for $\lambda^*$ and $f = 7$ for $\lambda^* - 1$, never hitting $f = 5$ and reconstructing the wrong intercept! | Always break ties consistently (e.g., minimize item count $f$ on ties), binary search for the largest $\lambda^*$ where $f(\lambda^*) \le k$ (or smallest where $f_{\max}(\lambda^*) \ge k$), and reconstruct $C(k) = g(\lambda^*) + \lambda^* \cdot k$ using the exact target $k$ (NOT $f(\lambda^*)$!). |

| Algorithm / Paradigm | Worst-Case Asymptotic Bound | Practical Contest Bound ($1\text{s} \approx 10^8\text{ ops}$) | Mandatory Structural Preconditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dinic's Algorithm (General Capacities)** | $O(V^2 E)$ | $V \le 5000,\; E \le 5 \cdot 10^4$ | Non-negative capacities $c(e) \ge 0$; requires BFS level graph + `cur[u]` arc pointer + dead-end pruning. |
| **Dinic on Unit Networks (Bipartite Matching / Hopcroft–Karp)** | $O(E \sqrt{V})$ | $V \le 2 \cdot 10^5,\; E \le 5 \cdot 10^5$ | Every $v \notin \{s, t\}$ has either $(\text{in-cap} = 1)$ or $(\text{out-cap} = 1)$, and all capacities are $1$. |
| **Dinic on Simple Unit-Capacity Graphs** | $O(\min(V^{2/3}, E^{1/2}) E)$ | $V \le 5 \cdot 10^4,\; E \le 2 \cdot 10^5$ | All edge capacities $c(e) = 1$ and no parallel edges (Karzanov 1973 / Even–Tarjan 1975). |
| **Highest-Label Push-Relabel (HLPP)** | $O(V^2 \sqrt{E})$ | $V \le 2 \cdot 10^4,\; E \le 2 \cdot 10^5$ | Needs Global Relabeling heuristic (every $V$ relabels) and Gap Relabeling heuristic to beat Dinic on dense graphs. |
| **Gusfield All-Pairs Min-Cut Tree** | $(N - 1) \times T_{\text{MaxFlow}}(N, M)$ | $N \le 500,\; M \le 5000$ | **Undirected** graph $G = (V, E, c)$ only (directed graphs do not admit a cut tree!). |
| **Stoer–Wagner Global Min-Cut** | $O(V E + V^2 \log V)$ | $V \le 1500,\; E \le 5 \cdot 10^5$ | **Undirected** non-negative weighted graph $G$; computes $\min_{\emptyset \neq S \subsetneq V} C(S, \bar{S})$. |
| **Planar $s$-$t$ Min-Cut $\iff$ Dual Dijkstra** | $O(V \log V)$ ($s$-$t$ planar) / $O(V \log^2 V)$ (general planar) | $V, E \le 5 \cdot 10^5$ | Planar embedding $G$ with Euler's formula $|F| = |E| - |V| + 2$. |
| **Edmonds' Blossom Algorithm (General Matching)** | $O(V^3)$ or $O(V E)$ | $V \le 1000,\; E \le 10^5$ | Unweighted general graph $G = (V, E)$ (may contain odd cycles). |
| **Algebraic Tutte Matrix ($T(G)^{-1}$)** | $O(V^3)$ or $O(V^\omega)$ | $V \le 500$ | Randomized over $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p = 10^9 + 7$); failure probability $\le |V|/p$ by Schwartz–Zippel. |
| **Ishikawa Multi-State Min-Cut** | $T_{\text{MaxFlow}}(N K, N K + M K^2)$ | $N \le 80,\; K \le 80$ ($N K \le 6400$) | Pairwise potentials must satisfy **Monge / Submodularity** $-\Delta_a \Delta_b \Phi_{uv}(a, b) \ge 0$; unary $\theta_i(k)$ can be arbitrary! |
| **Isotonic Regression via `整体二分` + Coarea** | $O(T_{\text{MaxFlow}}(N, M) \log V_{\max})$ | $N \le 1000,\; M \le 5000,\; V_{\max} \le 10^9$ | Unary cost functions $f_i(x_i)$ MUST be **convex**; pairwise constraints are partial order $x_u \le x_v$ (or $L_1$ smooth). |
| **MCMF via SSP + Johnson Potentials + Dijkstra** | $O(F \cdot E \log V)$ or $O(F \cdot V^2)$ | $V \le 3000,\; E \le 3 \cdot 10^4,\; F \le 5000$ | No negative-cost cycles in initial network (or remove via Mandatory Initial Saturation `负权边强制满流`). |
| **Simulated Cost Flow (`模拟费用流` on Line / Tree)** | $O((N + M) \log(N + M))$ or $O(N \log^2 N)$ | $N, M \le 2 \cdot 10^5$ | Cost metric must decompose on a **1D Line** ($|x - y|$), **Rooted Tree** ($\text{dep}(x) + \text{dep}(y) - 2\,\text{dep}(\text{LCA})$), or **Disjoint Intervals**. |
