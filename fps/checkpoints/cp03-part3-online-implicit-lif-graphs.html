  1. Axis 1 — Dependency Topology (Explicit vs. Semi-Online DP vs. Self-Referential Implicit Grammar):
    • Does the state at size \(n\) depend linearly on all previous sizes \(0 \le k < n\) via fixed transition weights \(P(x)\) (or a small \(d \times d\) matrix of coupled boundary states), or via an interval-monotonicity constraint on a given input array?
    • Or is the combinatorial object defined recursively in terms of itself through non-linear branching, tree children, excursion nesting, or block inflation (\(F(x) = x \Phi(F(x))\) or \(H(x, F(x)) = 0\))?
  2. Axis 2 — Extraction Regime & Arithmetic Field (\([x^0 \dots x^N]\) All-Coefficients vs. Single \([x^N]\) vs. Arbitrary / Small Prime \(p\)):

    • Or do you need a single coefficient at an extreme index (\(N \le 10^9\) or \(N \le 10^{18}\)), or modulo an arbitrary prime \(p\) (not NTT-friendly) or a small prime \(p \le 10^5 \ll N\) where \(p \mid N\) causes division by \(N\) and \(\exp/\ln\) to crash with division by zero?
  3. Axis 3 — Combinatorial Symmetry & Graph-Theoretic Incidence Structure:
    • Does the problem exhibit cyclic shift invariance on a circle of length \(n+1\) (where every shift orbit contains a unique valid prefix walk), or edge-sharing / vertex-pair interactions (\(2^{\binom{k}{2}}\) edge subsets, spanning forests \(S \subseteq E(T_1) \cap E(T_2)\) of connected component sizes \(s_1, \dots, s_k\) weighted by \(\prod_{i=1}^k s_i\))?

Problem CP-III.1 (#090) — Days of Floral Colours

  • Source: Codeforces 848E — Days of Floral Colours (Codeforces Round 431, Div. 1, Problem E)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Around the perimeter of a circular clock there are \(2n\) cells numbered \(0, 1, \dots, 2n - 1\) in clockwise order. You wish to partition the \(2n\) cells into \(n\) disjoint unordered pairs of identical flowers (two pairs are not distinguished by color label; only the matching of the \(2n\) positions matters) subject to the following rules:

  1. Allowed Pair Distances: For every matched pair \((u, v)\), the circular distance \(\min\big((u - v) \bmod 2n,\, (v - u) \bmod 2n\big)\) must belong to \(\{1, 2, n\}\). A pair at distance \(n\) (directly across the center of the circle) is called an opposite pair.
  2. Central Symmetry: The matching must be centrally symmetric: for every pair \((u, v)\), the diametrically opposite cells \(\big((u + n) \bmod 2n,\, (v + n) \bmod 2n\big)\) must also be matched to each other.
  3. Arc Beauty: There must be at least one opposite pair. When all opposite pairs are removed from the circle, the remaining unmatched cells split into \(2m\) contiguous arcs along the circle (since opposite pairs come in antipodal pairs \((i_r, i_r + n)\), these \(2m\) arcs consist of \(m\) antipodal pairs of arcs of equal lengths \(L_1, L_1, L_2, L_2, \dots, L_m, L_m\)). The beauty of the matching is the product of the lengths of all \(2m\) arcs: \[\text{Beauty} = \prod_{r=1}^{m} L_r^2.\] (If two opposite pairs are adjacent along the circle, the arc between them has length \(L_r = 0\), so the beauty of that matching is \(0\).)

Given \(n\), compute the sum of beauties over all valid matchings modulo \(998244353\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 5 \times 10^4\), modulus \(p = 998244353\).
  • Target Complexity: \(O(n \log^2 n)\) (or \(O(n)\)) time and \(O(n)\) space.

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n \le 4\))

  • \(n = 1\) (\(2n = 2\) cells): The only opposite pair is \((0, 1)\), leaving arc length \(L_1 = 0\). Sum of beauties \(= \mathbf{0}\).
  • \(n = 2\) (\(2n = 4\) cells): To avoid \(L_r = 0\), at most one opposite pair can be chosen, leaving two opposite arcs of odd length \(L_1 = 1\). Neither an internal pair nor a valid distance-\(2\) straddle can tile an arc of length \(1\) on a \(4\)-cell circle. Sum of beauties \(= \mathbf{0}\).
  • \(n = 3\) (\(2n = 6\) cells): To have \(L_r \ge 1\), there must be exactly \(m = 1\) opposite pair \((i_1, i_1 + 3)\) (\(3\) rotational choices \(i_1 \in \{0, 1, 2\}\)), leaving two opposite arcs of length \(L_1 = 2\) (beauty \(L_1^2 = 4\)). The \(4\) remaining cells can be paired in \(2\) ways: either two distance-\(1\) pairs inside the two arcs, or two distance-\(2\) pairs straddling across \(i_1\) and \(i_1 + 3\). Sum of beauties \(= 3 \times 4 \times 2 = \mathbf{24}\).
  • \(n = 4\) (\(2n = 8\) cells): With \(m = 1\) opposite pair, \(L_1 = 3\) is odd and cannot be paired (\(0\) ways). With \(m = 2\) opposite pairs at distance \(2\) (\(2\) rotational placements \(\{0, 2\}\) and \(\{1, 3\}\)), both arc lengths are \(L_1 = L_2 = 1\) (beauty \(1^2 \cdot 1^2 = 1\)), and we must straddle across one of the two opposite pairs so that both arcs of length \(1\) are consumed (\(2\) choices of which opposite pair is straddled). Sum of beauties \(= 2 \times 1 \times 2 = \mathbf{4}\).
Hint 1 — Structural Observation (Spoiler-Free)

Consider a single linear segment of \(L\) cells that contains no opposite pairs. If we match its cells using only distance-\(1\) and distance-\(2\) pairs, a distance-\(1\) pair at the left end consumes \(\{1, 2\}\) (length \(2\)), whereas a distance-\(2\) pair \((1, 3)\) forces cell \(2\) to pair with cell \(4\), consuming \(\{1, 2, 3, 4\}\) (an indivisible block of length \(4\)).

Now look at what happens at an opposite pair \((i_r, i_r + n)\): a distance-\(2\) pair can also straddle across \(i_r\) by matching \((i_r - 1, i_r + 1)\) (and by central symmetry, \((i_r + n - 1, i_r + n + 1)\)). Such a straddle consumes \(1\) cell from the right end of the preceding arc and \(1\) cell from the left end of the following arc, without changing the beauty factor \(L_r^2\) of either arc.

Hint 2 — Diagnostic Signal & Paradigm Selection
  • Diagnostic Signal: Cutting the circle at the opposite pairs decomposes the matching into a cyclic sequence of steps \(k_1 + k_2 + \dots + k_m = n\) (where \(L_r = k_r - 1\) is the arc length between \(i_r\) and \(i_{r+1}\)). At each opposite pair \(i_r\), there is a binary boundary state \(b_r \in \{0, 1\}\) indicating whether a distance-\(2\) pair straddles across \(i_r\).
  • Target Paradigm: Chapter 9 (§9.2 — Coupled Semi-Online Convolution via CDQ Divide-and-Conquer NTT) (or Chapter 5 rational matrix denominator clearing).
Hint 3 — Key Algebraic Equation

Let \(g_0 = 1, g_1 = 0, g_2 = 1, g_3 = 0\), and \(g_m = g_{m-2} + g_{m-4}\) (\(g_{2j} = F_{j+1}\), \(g_{2j+1} = 0\)) count the internal matchings of \(m\) cells. For \(c = b_{\text{left}} + b_{\text{right}} \in \{0, 1, 2\}\) straddled endpoints on an arc of step size \(k \ge 1\) (\(L = k - 1\) cells), define the three transition polynomials: \[P_c(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} (k - 1)^2 \, g_{k - 1 - c} \, x^k \qquad (c \in \{0, 1, 2\}),\] where \(g_m = 0\) for \(m < 0\). Tracking the open chain OGFs \(D_{a, b}(x)\) from initial boundary state \(a \in \{0, 1\}\) to current boundary state \(b \in \{0, 1\}\) gives the \(2 \times 2\) coupled semi-online system: \[\begin{bmatrix} D_{a, 0}(x) \\ D_{a, 1}(x) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} P_a(x) \\ P_{a+1}(x) \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} P_0(x) & P_1(x) \\ P_1(x) & P_2(x) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} D_{a, 0}(x) \\ D_{a, 1}(x) \end{bmatrix},\] and rooting the cycle at the unique arc of step \(k\) straddling cell \(0\) (with rotational multiplicity \(k\)) yields: \[\text{Ans} = [x^n] \sum_{k=1}^{n} k \, x^k \Big( (k-1)^2 g_{k-1} \big(1 + D_{0,0}(x)\big) + 2 (k-1)^2 g_{k-2} D_{0,1}(x) + (k-1)^2 g_{k-3} \big(1 + D_{1,1}(x)\big) \Big).\]

Problem CP-III.2 (#131) — Count BFS Graphs

  • Source: Codeforces 1906J — Count BFS Graphs (2023–2024 ICPC Asia Jakarta Regional Contest, Problem J)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

You are given a permutation \(a = (a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n)\) of \(\{1, 2, \dots, n\}\) with \(a_1 = 1\). Consider the standard Breadth-First Search (BFS) traversal on a simple, connected, undirected graph \(G = (V, E)\) with vertex set \(V = \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\) starting from vertex \(1\), where whenever a vertex \(u\) is popped from the front of the queue, all of its unvisited neighbors are pushed to the back of the queue in strictly increasing order of their vertex labels:

visited[1] = true; Q.push(1)
while Q is not empty:
    u = Q.pop_front()
    output.append(u)
    for v in sorted(neighbors(u)):
        if not visited[v]:
            visited[v] = true
            Q.push(v)

Count the number of simple undirected graphs \(G\) on \(\{1, \dots, n\}\) for which this BFS procedure visits all \(n\) vertices and produces the exact output sequence \(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n\), modulo \(998244353\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 5000\) (Original Version) or \(1 \le n \le 10^5\) (Strengthened Version), modulus \(p = 998244353\).
  • Target Complexity: \(O(n^2)\) time and \(O(n)\) space (or \(O(n \log^2 n)\) for the strengthened version).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n \le 4\))

  • \(n = 1\), \(a = [1]\): Only \(1\) graph on \(1\) vertex. Output \(= \mathbf{1}\).
  • \(n = 2\), \(a = [1, 2]\): The edge \((1, 2)\) must be present (\(1\) graph). Output \(= \mathbf{1}\).
  • \(n = 3\), \(a = [1, 2, 3]\):
    • If vertex \(1\) pushes only \([2]\), then when \(2\) is popped it must push \([3]\) (\(1\) graph: path \(1 - 2 - 3\)).
    • If vertex \(1\) pushes \([2, 3]\) (valid since \(2 < 3\)), then when \(2\) is popped the queue already contains \(3\), so the edge \((2, 3)\) can be either absent or present (\(2^1 = 2\) graphs).
    • Total for \(a = [1, 2, 3]\): \(1 + 2 = \mathbf{3}\).
  • \(n = 3\), \(a = [1, 3, 2]\):
    • Vertex \(1\) cannot push \([3, 2]\) together because \(3 > 2\) violates the sorted neighbor rule. Thus \(1\) pushes only \([3]\), and then \(3\) pushes \([2]\) (\(1\) graph: path \(1 - 3 - 2\)).
    • Total for \(a = [1, 3, 2]\): \(\mathbf{1}\).
Hint 1 — Structural Observation (Spoiler-Free)

At the moment vertex \(a_i\) is popped from the queue, suppose the queue currently contains \(a_{i+1}, a_{i+2}, \dots, a_j\) (for some \(j \ge i\)). The newly discovered neighbors of \(a_i\) are appended to the queue as a contiguous block \(a_{j+1}, a_{j+2}, \dots, a_k\) (\(k \ge j\)).

Ask two questions about this step:

  1. When can \(a_{j+1}, \dots, a_k\) be pushed together by \(a_i\)? Precisely when \(a_{j+1} < a_{j+2} < \dots < a_k\).
  2. Which other vertices can \(a_i\) have edges to without changing the BFS order? Vertex \(a_i\) cannot connect to any unvisited vertex outside \(\{a_{j+1}, \dots, a_k\}\), and edges to \(a_1, \dots, a_{i-1}\) were already decided when those vertices were popped. What about edges between \(a_i\) and the already-queued vertices \(a_{i+1}, \dots, a_j\)?
Hint 2 — Diagnostic Signal & Paradigm Selection
  • Diagnostic Signal: Each vertex \(a_i\) has \(2^{j - i}\) free edge choices to the already-queued segment \(a[i+1 \dots j]\), and extends the queue tail from \(j\) to \(k\) across any strictly increasing subarray \(a[j+1 \dots k]\).
  • Target Paradigm: Chapter 9 (§9.1 — Prefix/Interval-Sum Online DP Acceleration) combined with Chapter 13 (§13.1 — \(2^{\binom{k}{2}}\) / \(2^{j-i}\) Labeled Graphical Edge Factors).
Hint 3 — Key Algebraic Equation

Let \(\text{dp}[i][k]\) be the number of valid edge configurations among the edges incident to \(a_1, \dots, a_i\) such that after \(a_i\) is processed, the queue spans \(a[1 \dots k]\). Let \(L[k]\) be the smallest index \(\ge 2\) such that \(a[L[k] \dots k]\) is strictly increasing. Then for \(1 \le i \le k \le n\) (with \(k > i\) whenever \(i < n\) to keep the queue non-empty): \[\text{dp}[i][k] = \sum_{j = \max(i,\, L[k] - 1)}^{k} 2^{j - i} \, \text{dp}[i - 1][j],\] with base case \(\text{dp}[1][k] = 1\) for all \(2 \le k \le R[2]\) (where \(a[2 \dots R[2]]\) is the maximal increasing prefix starting at index \(2\)). Scaling \(\widetilde{\text{dp}}[i - 1][j] = 2^j \, \text{dp}[i - 1][j]\) reduces the transition for all \(k\) to a single sliding range sum multiplied by \(2^{-i}\).

Problem CP-III.3 (#133) — Counting Is Fun (Excursion-Majority Binary Strings)

  • Source: Adapted from Codeforces 1930I — Counting Is Fun (think-cell Round 1, Problem I)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

Consider a binary string \(s = s_1 s_2 \dots s_{2n} \in \{\mathtt{0}, \mathtt{1}\}^{2n}\) of even length \(2n\). Map \(\mathtt{1} \mapsto +1\) and \(\mathtt{0} \mapsto -1\), and let \(S_k = \sum_{j=1}^{k} (2 s_j - 1)\) be the prefix sums (\(S_0 = 0\)), with historical prefix minimum \(M_k = \min_{0 \le l \le k} S_l\).

A step \(i \in \{1, \dots, 2n\}\) is called a record-low step if \(s_i = \mathtt{0}\) and \(S_{i-1} = M_{i-1}\) (so \(S_i = M_{i-1} - 1\) sets a new strict minimum \(M_i = M_{i-1} - 1\)). We say that \(s \in \{\mathtt{0}, \mathtt{1}\}^{2n}\) is excursion-majority good if it satisfies two rules:

  1. Even Record-Low Depth: The total number of record-low steps in \(s\) is even (\(2h\) for some integer \(h \ge 0\), so the final minimum \(M_{2n} = -2h\) has the same parity as \(2n\), allowing the walk to return to or stay above \(M_{2n}\)).
  2. Local Excursion Recovery: Every record-low step \(i\) must be immediately followed (before the next record-low step, or before the end of the string if \(i\) is the last record-low step) by at least one \(\mathtt{1}\) step that lifts the walk back to \(S_r \ge S_i + 1 = M_{i-1}\) (so every record-low \(\mathtt{0}\) is locally covered by a balanced \(\mathtt{0}\dots\mathtt{1}\) excursion above \(M_i\)).

Given \(n\) and an arbitrary prime modulus \(p\) (which is not guaranteed to be NTT-friendly!), compute the number of excursion-majority good binary strings of length \(2n\) modulo \(p\).

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 5 \times 10^5\), \(10^8 < p < 10^9 + 9\) is an arbitrary prime given in the input.
  • Target Complexity: \(O(n)\) time and \(O(n)\) space, using zero NTTs and zero divisions by \(n\).

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n \le 4\))

  • \(n = 1\) (length \(2n = 2\), \(4\) strings total):
    • With \(2h = 0\) record lows (\(S_k \ge 0\) for all \(k\)): \(\mathtt{11}\) (\(S = [0, 1, 2]\)) and \(\mathtt{10}\) (\(S = [0, 1, 0]\)) are valid (\(2\) strings).
    • \(\mathtt{01}\) has \(1\) record low (odd), and \(\mathtt{00}\) has \(2\) record lows with no recovery after either.
    • Total for \(n = 1\): \(\mathbf{2}\).
  • \(n = 2\) (length \(2n = 4\), \(16\) strings total):
    • Any valid string with \(2h \ge 2\) record lows needs \(2\) record-low \(\mathtt{0}\) steps plus at least \(2\) recovery steps after each, requiring length \(\ge 6\). Thus for \(n = 2\), only \(2h = 0\) (walks of length \(4\) with \(S_k \ge 0\) for all \(k\)) can occur: \(\mathtt{1111}, \mathtt{1110}, \mathtt{1101}, \mathtt{1100}, \mathtt{1011}, \mathtt{1010}\).
    • Total for \(n = 2\): \(\mathbf{6}\).
  • \(n = 3\) (length \(2n = 6\), \(64\) strings total):
    • \(2h = 0\): \(\binom{6}{3} = 20\) walks of length \(6\) with \(S_k \ge 0\).
    • \(2h = 2\): The first record low (\(\mathtt{0}\) at \(i=1\)) is followed by \(\mathtt{10}\) (recovering to \(0\) and returning to \(-1\)), then the second record low (\(\mathtt{0}\) at \(i=4\), reaching \(-2\)) is followed by a recovery walk of length \(2\) staying \(\ge -2\), which must be \(\mathtt{10}\) or \(\mathtt{11}\). This gives \(2\) strings: \(\mathtt{010010}\) and \(\mathtt{010011}\).
    • Total for \(n = 3\): \(20 + 2 = \mathbf{22}\).
  • \(n = 4\) (length \(2n = 8\), \(256\) strings total):
    • \(2h = 0\): \(\binom{8}{4} = 70\) walks with \(S_k \ge 0\).
    • \(2h = 2\): \(12\) valid strings (distributing the \(3\) pairs of non-record steps among the pre-record excursion, the first recovery excursion, and the final recovery meander gives \(2 + 4 + 6 = 12\)).
    • Total for \(n = 4\): \(70 + 12 = \mathbf{82}\).
Hint 1 — Structural Observation (Spoiler-Free)

Factor any valid walk \(s \in \{\mathtt{0}, \mathtt{1}\}^{2n}\) at its \(2h\) record-low steps \(i_1 < i_2 < \dots < i_{2h}\):

  • Before \(i_1\), the walk starts at \(0\), stays \(\ge 0\), and ends at \(0\) (an even-length non-negative return walk).
  • Between \(i_r\) and \(i_{r+1}\) (\(1 \le r \le 2h - 1\)), the walk starts at \(-r\), stays \(\ge -r\), ends at \(-r\), and by Rule 2 must have positive length \(\ge 2\).
  • After \(i_{2h}\), the walk starts at \(-2h\), stays \(\ge -2h\), has even length, and if \(h \ge 1\) it must have positive length \(\ge 2\).
Hint 2 — Diagnostic Signal & Paradigm Selection
  • Diagnostic Signal: Every block between record lows is governed by the Catalan series \(C(z) = 1 + z C(z)^2\) (where \(z\) marks a pair of steps, i.e., semi-length \(1\)). Thus the OGF \(F(z)\) is a rational function \(R(u(z))\) of the shifted Catalan series \(u(z) = C(z) - 1 = z C(z)^2\), which satisfies \(u = z(1 + u)^2 \iff z = \frac{u}{(1+u)^2}\). Because the prime \(p\) is arbitrary, we cannot use NTT; we need an exact \(O(n)\) extraction in \(u\)-space.
  • Target Paradigm: Chapter 10 (§10.2 — Implicit Catalan Functional Equations) combined with Chapter 11 (§11.1–11.2 — Formal Residue Change of Variables / Canonical Form 4).
Hint 3 — Key Algebraic Equation

Problem CP-III.4 (#160) — Intrinsic Interval (Simple Permutations)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

A permutation \(\pi = (\pi_1, \pi_2, \dots, \pi_k)\) of \(\{1, 2, \dots, k\}\) has a framed interval (or block) on \([l \dots r]\) (\(1 \le l \le r \le k\)) if the contiguous subarray \(\pi[l \dots r]\) takes a contiguous set of values \(\{\min \pi[l \dots r], \dots, \max \pi[l \dots r]\}\), i.e., \[\max_{l \le i \le r} \pi_i - \min_{l \le i \le r} \pi_i = r - l.\] Every permutation of length \(k\) trivially has blocks of length \(1\) (singletons \([i, i]\)) and length \(k\) (the whole permutation \([1, k]\)). A permutation \(\pi \in S_k\) (\(k \ge 4\)) is called simple (or interval-indecomposable) if it has no block of length \(m\) with \(2 \le m \le k-1\).

Given \(n\), compute the number \(s_k\) of simple permutations of length \(k\) for every \(k \in \{4, 5, \dots, n\}\) modulo \(998244353\).

  • Constraints: \(4 \le n \le 10^5\), modulus \(p = 998244353\).
  • Target Complexity: \(O(n \log n)\) (or \(O(n \log^2 n)\)) time and \(O(n)\) space.

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(k \le 5\))

  • \(k = 1, 2, 3\):
    • For \(k = 2\), both \([1, 2]\) and \([2, 1]\) only have intervals of length \(1\) and \(2\), which are monotone two-element blocks rather than simple permutations of length \(\ge 4\).
    • For \(k = 3\), all \(3! = 6\) permutations contain an adjacent pair of consecutive values (\(\{1, 2\}\) or \(\{2, 3\}\)), i.e., a proper interval of length \(2\). Thus \(s_3 = \mathbf{0}\).
  • \(k = 4\) (\(4! = 24\) permutations):
    • Checking all \(24\) permutations of \(\{1, 2, 3, 4\}\), \(22\) of them contain a contiguous block of length \(2\) or \(3\), while exactly \(s_4 = \mathbf{2}\) permutations have no proper intervals of length \(2\) or \(3\): \[\pi^{(1)} = [2, 4, 1, 3], \qquad \pi^{(2)} = [3, 1, 4, 2].\]
  • \(k = 5\) (\(5! = 120\) permutations):
    • Exhaustive check (or subtracting block inflations of \([1, 2]\), \([2, 1]\), and \([2, 4, 1, 3], [3, 1, 4, 2]\) from \(5! = 120\)) yields \(s_5 = \mathbf{6}\) simple permutations of length \(5\): \[[2, 4, 1, 5, 3],\; [2, 5, 3, 1, 4],\; [3, 1, 5, 2, 4],\; [3, 5, 1, 4, 2],\; [4, 1, 3, 5, 2],\; [4, 2, 5, 1, 3].\]
Hint 1 — Combinatorial Decomposition (Zero Spoilers)

Instead of directly counting permutations that avoid all non-trivial contiguous value blocks, consider how an arbitrary permutation \(\pi \in S_n\) (\(n \ge 2\)) is built by inflating a smaller base permutation \(\sigma \in S_m\) (\(m \ge 2\)) with \(m\) contiguous value blocks \(B_1, B_2, \dots, B_m\). When is this inflation decomposition unique, and what special case arises when \(\sigma\) itself can be split into two monotone pieces?

Hint 2 — Diagnostic Signal & Paradigm Selection
  • Diagnostic Signal: Translating the three disjoint inflation cases into ordinary generating functions relates the known factorial OGF \(F(x) = \sum_{k \ge 1} k!\, x^k\) to the unknown simple-permutation OGF \(S(y) = \sum_{k \ge 4} s_k y^k\) evaluated at \(y = F(x)\). Solving for \(S(y)\) requires the compositional inverse \(G(y) = F^{\langle -1 \rangle}(y)\) satisfying \(F(G(y)) = y\).
  • Target Paradigm: Chapter 10 (§10.2–10.3 — Implicit Functional Equations & Newton Iteration for Compositional Inverse) (keeping strictly within Part III tools \(\le \text{Ch } 13\)).
Hint 3 — Key Algebraic Equation

Let \(F(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} k!\, x^k\) be the OGF of non-empty permutations. An indecomposable block inside an ascending join has OGF \(F_{\neq \text{asc}}(x) = \frac{F(x)}{1 + F(x)}\), so ascending joins of \(m \ge 2\) parts have OGF \(\sum_{m \ge 2} \left(\frac{F}{1+F}\right)^m = \frac{F(x)^2}{1 + F(x)}\), and likewise \(\frac{F(x)^2}{1 + F(x)}\) for descending joins. Thus: \[F(x) = x + \frac{2 F(x)^2}{1 + F(x)} + S\big(F(x)\big).\] Setting \(y = F(x) \iff x = F^{\langle -1 \rangle}(y) =: G(y)\) isolates \(S(y)\) immediately: \[S(y) = y - \frac{2y^2}{1 + y} - G(y) = \frac{y(1 - y)}{1 + y} - G(y),\] where \(G(y) \bmod y^{n+1}\) is computed by Newton iteration \(G_{\text{next}}(y) = G(y) - \frac{F(G(y)) - y}{F'(G(y))} \bmod y^{2d}\).

Problem CP-III.5 (#119) — AmShZ Farm

  • Source: Codeforces 1528F — AmShZ Farm (Codeforces Round 722, Div. 1, Problem F)

Problem Statement & Exact Constraints

There are \(n\) parking spaces numbered \(1, 2, \dots, n\) along a one-way street, and \(n\) cars arrive one by one. Car \(j\) (\(1 \le j \le n\)) has a preferred parking space \(a_j \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\): upon arrival, it drives to space \(a_j\) and parks in the first unoccupied space at an index \(\ge a_j\) (if all spaces \(a_j, a_j + 1, \dots, n\) are already occupied, car \(j\) cannot park).

A preference sequence \(a = (a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n) \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}^n\) is called valid if all \(n\) cars successfully park; equivalently, for every \(i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}\), at least \(i\) cars prefer a space \(\le i\): \[\big\lvert \{ j \in \{1, \dots, n\} : a_j \le i \} \big\rvert \ge i \qquad \forall\, i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}.\] For a valid sequence \(a\) and a spot \(v \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), let \(\operatorname{occ}(v, a) = \lvert \{ j : a_j = v \} \rvert\) denote the number of times \(v\) appears in \(a\). Given \(n\) and \(k\), compute: \[\sum_{a \text{ valid}} \sum_{v=1}^{n} \big(\operatorname{occ}(v, a)\big)^k \pmod{998244353}.\]

  • Constraints: \(1 \le n \le 10^9\), \(1 \le k \le 10^5\), modulus \(p = 998244353\).
  • Target Complexity: \(O(k \log k)\) time and \(O(k)\) space.

Small Hand-Checkable Trace (\(n \le 3\))

  • \(n = 1\), any \(k \ge 1\): Only \(a = (1)\) is valid, with \(\operatorname{occ}(1, a) = 1 \implies 1^k = \mathbf{1}\).
  • \(n = 2\), \(k = 2\):
    • Among \(\{1, 2\}^2\), the \(3\) valid sequences are \((1, 1), (1, 2), (2, 1)\) (only \((2, 2)\) fails because \(0 < 1\) cars have \(a_j \le 1\)).
    • For \(a = (1, 1)\): \(\operatorname{occ}(1, a)^2 + \operatorname{occ}(2, a)^2 = 2^2 + 0^2 = 4\).
    • For \(a = (1, 2)\): \(1^2 + 1^2 = 2\).
    • For \(a = (2, 1)\): \(1^2 + 1^2 = 2\).
    • Total for \(n = 2, k = 2\): \(4 + 2 + 2 = \mathbf{8}\).
  • \(n = 3\), \(k = 2\):
    • There are \((3 + 1)^{3 - 1} = 16\) valid sequences of length \(3\).
    • Counting pairs \((a, v)\) by frequency \(m = \operatorname{occ}(v, a) \in \{1, 2, 3\}\):
      • \(m = 3\): only \(a = (1, 1, 1), v = 1\) (\(1\) pair, contributing \(1 \times 3^2 = 9\)).
      • \(m = 2\): \(9\) pairs \((a, v)\) (contributing \(9 \times 2^2 = 36\)).
      • \(m = 1\): \(27\) pairs \((a, v)\) (contributing \(27 \times 1^2 = 27\)).
    • Total for \(n = 3, k = 2\): \(9 + 36 + 27 = \mathbf{72}\).
Hint 1 — Structural Observation (Spoiler-Free)

Add an extra parking space \(n + 1\) and arrange all \(n + 1\) spaces \(1, 2, \dots, n + 1\) around a circle so that cars wrap around modulo \(n + 1\). Now every sequence \(a \in \{1, 2, \dots, n + 1\}^n\) allows all \(n\) cars to park, leaving exactly one empty space on the circle!

If you shift every preference by \(+1 \bmod (n + 1)\) (sending \(a_j \mapsto (a_j \bmod (n+1)) + 1\)), the empty space also shifts by \(+1 \bmod (n + 1)\). Therefore, in every cyclic shift orbit of size \(n + 1\), there is exactly one sequence whose empty space is \(n + 1\) — which is precisely a valid parking sequence on \(\{1, \dots, n\}\)! Moreover, in that valid sequence, \(\operatorname{occ}(n + 1, a) = 0\). What does this tell you about the multiset of frequencies \(\{\operatorname{occ}(1, a), \dots, \operatorname{occ}(n + 1, a)\}\) across the entire orbit?

Hint 2 — Diagnostic Signal & Paradigm Selection
  • Diagnostic Signal: By Pollak’s circular symmetry / Raney’s Cycle Lemma, each cyclic orbit of size \(n + 1\) contributes the exact same nonzero frequency \(k\)-th powers \(\sum_{v=1}^{n+1} \operatorname{occ}(v, a)^k\) at its unique valid representative as the average over all \(n + 1\) shifts in the orbit! Because \(n \le 10^9\) is huge and \(k \le 10^5\) is small, we convert \(m^k\) into falling factorials \(m^{\underline{j}}\) via Stirling numbers of the second kind.
  • Target Paradigm: Chapter 11 (§11.3 — Raney’s Lemma & Circular Shift Orbit Bijection) combined with Chapter 6 (§6.4 — Stirling Numbers of the Second Kind & Binomial-Moment Collapse).
Hint 3 — Key Algebraic Equation

Summing \(\sum_{v=1}^{n+1} \operatorname{occ}(v, a)^k\) over all \((n + 1)^n\) unrestricted sequences \(a \in \{1, \dots, n + 1\}^n\) and dividing by the orbit size \(n + 1\) (since \(\operatorname{occ}(n + 1, a) = 0\) on every valid parking sequence) gives: \[\text{Ans} = \frac{1}{n + 1} \sum_{a \in \{1, \dots, n+1\}^n} \sum_{v=1}^{n+1} \operatorname{occ}(v, a)^k = \sum_{m=1}^{n} \binom{n}{m} n^{n - m} \, m^k.\] Expanding \(m^k = \sum_{j=1}^{\min(n, k)} \left\{\begin{matrix} k \\ j \end{matrix}\right\} m^{\underline{j}}\) and using \(\binom{n}{m} m^{\underline{j}} = n^{\underline{j}} \binom{n - j}{m - j}\) collapses the \(n\)-term sum into a \(k\)-term closed form: \[\text{Ans} = \sum_{j=1}^{\min(n, k)} \left\{\begin{matrix} k \\ j \end{matrix}\right\} n^{\underline{j}} \, (n + 1)^{n - j} \pmod{998244353}.\]

    • \([x^1] F(x) = 1\) (a single root leaf).

Hint 2 — Diagnostic Signal & Paradigm Selection
  • Diagnostic Signal: We have \(F(x) = x \Phi(F(x))\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) with \(p \le 10^5 \ll n \le 10^{18}\). Two standard tools fail when \(p \le n\):

Hint 3 — Key Algebraic Equation

1. Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad & Combinatorial Reduction

We want to count centrally symmetric matchings of \(2n\) circular cells into pairs of circular distance \(1, 2,\) or \(n\), weighted by the product of the lengths of the contiguous arcs remaining after all opposite pairs (distance \(n\)) are removed.

  1. Folding the Circle by Central Symmetry: Number the cells \(0, 1, \dots, 2n - 1\). An opposite pair matches \((i, i + n)\) for some \(i \in \{0, 1, \dots, n - 1\}\). Suppose a valid matching contains \(m \ge 1\) opposite pairs at indices \(0 \le i_1 < i_2 < \dots < i_m < n\) (along with their antipodal partners \(i_r + n\)). Let \(k_r = i_{r+1} - i_r \ge 1\) (with \(i_{m+1} := i_1 + n\)) be the circular step distances between consecutive opposite pairs along the upper semicircle, so \(\sum_{r=1}^{m} k_r = n\). Removing the \(m\) opposite pairs leaves \(m\) upper arcs of lengths \(L_r = k_r - 1\) and \(m\) antipodal lower arcs of the exact same lengths \(L_r = k_r - 1\). Thus the beauty of the matching is: \[\text{Beauty} = \prod_{r=1}^{m} L_r^2 = \prod_{r=1}^{m} (k_r - 1)^2.\] In particular, any step with \(k_r = 1\) (\(L_r = 0\)) contributes \(0\) to the beauty product, so we only need to consider steps \(k_r \ge 2\).

  2. Internal Tiling of an Arc & Endpoint Straddles: Because the matching is centrally symmetric, the pairing of the lower semicircle is the exact \(+n\) image of the pairing of the upper semicircle. What can happen to the cells on the upper semicircle?

    • Across each opposite pair \(i_r\), we may optionally place a straddling distance-\(2\) pair \((i_r - 1, i_r + 1)\) (together with its symmetric image \((i_r + n - 1, i_r + n + 1)\)). Let \(b_r \in \{0, 1\}\) be the indicator of whether \(i_r\) is straddled.
    • Inside the \(r\)-th upper arc (which has \(L_r = k_r - 1\) cells), the straddles at its left endpoint \(i_r\) and right endpoint \(i_{r+1}\) consume \(c_r = b_r + b_{r+1} \in \{0, 1, 2\}\) boundary cells, leaving \(m_r = k_r - 1 - c_r\) internal cells (note that the left and right straddles are disjoint whenever \(m_r \ge 0\)).
    • How many ways \(g_m\) can a contiguous linear segment of \(m\) cells be partitioned into pairs of distance \(1\) or \(2\)?
      • For \(m = 0\), the empty segment has \(g_0 = 1\) matching.
      • For odd \(m\), \(g_m = 0\) since every pair consumes \(2\) cells.
      • For even \(m \ge 2\), cell \(1\) must pair either with cell \(2\) (distance \(1\), leaving \(m - 2\) cells) or with cell \(3\) (distance \(2\), which forces cell \(2\) to pair with cell \(4\) since \(1\) and \(3\) are taken, consuming an indivisible block \(\{1, 2, 3, 4\}\) of length \(4\) and leaving \(m - 4\) cells). Hence \(g_m\) satisfies the Fibonacci recurrence on even indices: \[g_0 = 1, \quad g_1 = 0, \quad g_2 = 1, \quad g_3 = 0, \quad g_m = g_{m-2} + g_{m-4} \quad (m \ge 4),\] with \(g_m = 0\) for \(m < 0\).

2. Coupled Semi-Online System & Circular Closure

For each \(c \in \{0, 1, 2\}\), define the transition generating function for a single step \(k \ge 1\) with \(c\) straddled endpoints: \[P_c(x) = \sum_{k=2}^{n} (k - 1)^2 \, g_{k - 1 - c} \, x^k \qquad (c \in \{0, 1, 2\}).\] Note that \([x^0] P_c(x) = [x^1] P_c(x) = 0\), so \(\operatorname{val}(P_c) \ge 2\). For a linear chain of one or more steps starting with left boundary state \(a \in \{0, 1\}\) and ending with right boundary state \(b \in \{0, 1\}\), let \(D_{a, b}(x)\) be its OGF. Conditioning on the last step (which transitions from an intermediate boundary state \(s \in \{0, 1\}\) to \(b\) with weight \(P_{s + b}(x)\), or consists of a single step of weight \(P_{a + b}(x)\)) yields the coupled semi-online system: \[\begin{aligned} D_{a, 0}(x) &= P_a(x) + P_0(x) D_{a, 0}(x) + P_1(x) D_{a, 1}(x), \\ D_{a, 1}(x) &= P_{a+1}(x) + P_1(x) D_{a, 0}(x) + P_2(x) D_{a, 1}(x), \end{aligned} \qquad (a \in \{0, 1\}).\] By reversal symmetry of the steps, \(D_{0, 1}(x) = D_{1, 0}(x)\), so there are only three distinct series: \(D_0(x) := D_{0, 0}(x)\), \(D_1(x) := D_{0, 1}(x) = D_{1, 0}(x)\), and \(D_2(x) := D_{1, 1}(x)\), satisfying: \[\begin{aligned} D_0(x) &= P_0(x) + P_0(x) D_0(x) + P_1(x) D_1(x), \\ D_1(x) &= P_1(x) + P_1(x) D_0(x) + P_2(x) D_1(x), \\ D_2(x) &= P_2(x) + P_1(x) D_1(x) + P_2(x) D_2(x). \end{aligned}\]

To close the cycle of length \(n\), focus on the unique step of length \(k \in \{2, \dots, n\}\) that straddles cell \(0\) (or starts at cell \(0\)). A step of length \(k\) has \(k\) rotational placements covering cell \(0\):

  • If \(k = n\) (\(m = 1\) opposite pair, so both endpoints of the single arc are the same opposite pair \(b_1 \in \{0, 1\}\), giving \(c = 2b_1 \in \{0, 2\}\)), its contribution is: \[n \cdot \big( [x^n] P_0(x) + [x^n] P_2(x) \big) = n (n - 1)^2 (g_{n - 1} + g_{n - 3}).\]
  • If \(2 \le k \le n - 2\) (\(m \ge 2\) opposite pairs), choosing the boundary states \((b_{\text{left}}, b_{\text{right}}) \in \{0, 1\}^2\) of the distinguished step of length \(k\) and concatenating with an open chain of total length \(n - k\) gives: \[\sum_{k=2}^{n-2} k \Big( [x^k] P_0(x) \cdot [x^{n-k}] D_0(x) + 2 [x^k] P_1(x) \cdot [x^{n-k}] D_1(x) + [x^k] P_2(x) \cdot [x^{n-k}] D_2(x) \Big).\]

1. Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad & Queue-Tail Invariant

In a BFS starting at \(a_1 = 1\), vertices enter the queue in the exact order \(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n\) and are popped in that same order. Let us trace the state of the queue at the moment the \(i\)-th vertex \(a_i\) is popped:

  1. Queue State Before Popping \(a_i\): Because \(a_1, \dots, a_{i-1}\) have already been popped and processed, the vertices that have been discovered so far form a contiguous prefix \(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_j\) of the array \(a\), where \(j \ge i\) (so the queue right after \(a_i\) is popped consists of \(a_{i+1}, a_{i+2}, \dots, a_j\)).
  2. Newly Discovered Neighbors of \(a_i\): When \(a_i\) is popped, all of its unvisited neighbors are pushed to the back of the queue in strictly increasing order of their labels. Thus, the newly pushed neighbors of \(a_i\) must form the next contiguous block \(a_{j+1}, a_{j+2}, \dots, a_k\) (for some \(k \ge j\)), and this block is valid if and only if it is strictly increasing: \[a_{j+1} < a_{j+2} < \dots < a_k.\] (If \(k = j\), the block is empty, which is always vacuously increasing.)
  3. Counting All Edges Incident to \(a_i\):
    • Edges from \(a_i\) to \(a_1, \dots, a_{i-1}\): Already determined when \(a_1, \dots, a_{i-1}\) were popped.
    • Edges from \(a_i\) to \(a_{j+1}, \dots, a_k\): All \(k - j\) edges must be present (since these are the unvisited vertices discovered by \(a_i\)).
    • Edges from \(a_i\) to \(a_{k+1}, \dots, a_n\): All \(n - k\) edges must be absent (otherwise \(a_i\) would have discovered them now!).
    • Edges from \(a_i\) to \(a_{i+1}, \dots, a_j\): Because \(a_{i+1}, \dots, a_j\) were already in the queue before \(a_i\) was popped, adding or omitting an edge between \(a_i\) and any vertex in \(\{a_{i+1}, \dots, a_j\}\) has zero effect on the queue order! Since there are \(j - i\) such vertices, there are \(2^{j - i}\) independent choices for these edges.

2. Algebraic Recurrence & Sliding Interval-Sum Linearization

For each \(k \in \{2, \dots, n\}\), precompute the leftmost increasing-run boundary \(L[k] \in \{2, \dots, k\}\) defined by: \[L[k] = \begin{cases} 2 & \text{if } k = 2, \\ L[k - 1] & \text{if } k > 2 \text{ and } a_{k - 1} < a_k, \\ k & \text{if } k > 2 \text{ and } a_{k - 1} > a_k. \end{cases}\] Then the subarray \(a[j + 1 \dots k]\) (\(j < k\)) is strictly increasing if and only if \(j + 1 \ge L[k]\), i.e., \(j \ge L[k] - 1\) (and for \(j = k\), the empty subarray is always valid).

Let \(\text{dp}[i][k]\) be the number of valid edge choices incident to \(a_1, \dots, a_i\) such that after \(a_i\) pushes its new neighbors, the discovered prefix is \(a[1 \dots k]\).

  • Base case (\(i = 1\)): Vertex \(a_1 = 1\) starts with \(j = 1\) and must push a non-empty increasing prefix \(a[2 \dots k]\) (so \(L[k] = 2\)), giving \(\text{dp}[1][k] = [L[k] = 2]\) for \(2 \le k \le n\).
  • Transition (\(2 \le i \le n\)): For the graph to be connected, before \(a_i\) is popped, at least \(a_i\) itself must already be in the queue, so \(j \ge i\). Summing over all valid prior queue tails \(j \in [\max(i, L[k] - 1),\, k]\) gives: \[\text{dp}[i][k] = \sum_{j = \max(i,\, L[k] - 1)}^{k} 2^{j - i} \, \text{dp}[i - 1][j] = 2^{-i} \sum_{j = \max(i,\, L[k] - 1)}^{k} \Big( 2^j \, \text{dp}[i - 1][j] \Big).\] Defining the scaled prefix sums \(P_{i-1}[r] = \sum_{j=1}^{r} 2^j \, \text{dp}[i - 1][j] \pmod{998244353}\) evaluates each state \(\text{dp}[i][k]\) in \(O(1)\) time: \[\text{dp}[i][k] = 2^{-i} \Big( P_{i-1}[k] - P_{i-1}\big[\max(i,\, L[k] - 1) - 1\big] \Big) \pmod{998244353}.\]
  • Final Answer: \(\text{dp}[n][n] \pmod{998244353}\) (or equivalently \(\sum_{j=1}^{n} 2^{\binom{n - j}{2}} \text{dp}[j][n]\) if stopping as soon as \(k = n\) is reached, since once all \(n\) vertices are queued at step \(j\), the remaining \(n - j\) vertices \(a_{j+1}, \dots, a_n\) have \(\sum_{i=j+1}^{n} (n - i) = \binom{n - j}{2}\) completely free internal edges!).

  2. Closing the Street into a Circle of \(n + 1\) Spaces: Add a dummy parking space \(n + 1\) and arrange spaces \(1, 2, \dots, n + 1\) clockwise around a circle. Now consider the set \(\mathcal{U} = \{1, 2, \dots, n + 1\}^n\) of all \((n + 1)^n\) unrestricted preference sequences.

    • Since \(\gcd(n, n + 1) = 1\), every orbit \(\{a, \sigma(a), \dots, \sigma^n(a)\}\) has size \(n + 1\), and exactly one sequence \(a^*\) in each orbit leaves space \(n + 1\) empty!
