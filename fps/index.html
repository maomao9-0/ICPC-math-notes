Formal Power Series & Generating Functions

A 6-part, 24-chapter algebraic and combinatorial textbook for competitive programming

This textbook is a self-contained, zero-forward-reference treatise on Formal Power Series (FPS) and Generating Functions for competitive programming (Ch 01–Ch 24), paired with 6 Two-Stage Blind Topic-Group Checkpoints (CP-I–CP-VI), a 14-Problem Multi-Perspective Synthesis Reserve (Syn-01–Syn-14), and a 321-Problem Master Curated Archive (254 accepted problems across 192 chapter ladder slots, 48 blind checkpoint slots, and 14 synthesis reserve slots, plus 67 quarantined bare library templates R-01–R-67).

Every chapter is built from algebraic definitions (\(K[[x]]\), \(K((x))\), valuation topology \(\nu(F)\), formal residues \(\operatorname{Res}_{x=0}\), bivariate ring hierarchies, differential Weyl operators \(K[x]\langle D \rangle\), Tellegen transposition, Set Power Series \(\mathcal{R}_n\), and Dirichlet series \(\mathcal{D}_f\)) with explicit Diagnostic Signals, Reverse-Engineering Scratchpads, Characteristic-\(p\) & Valuation-Shift Failure Modes, and an inline 8-Problem 4-Tier Practice Ladder (2 Stepping Stones, 2 Intermediate, 2 Advanced, 2 Mastery).

Start with FPS Ch 01 · The Ring \(K[[x]]\), Valuation Topology & Newton Inversion, follow the study plan and mastery gates, and keep the notation, operators, and 16 failure modes open alongside your work. For general number theory, linear algebra, probability, and sieves outside the FPS track, switch to the companion Competitive Programming Mathematics textbook in the top navigation bar.

ImportantEntry Contract (Ch 01–Ch 24)

The 24-chapter curriculum is built on four explicit entry assumptions:

  1. Formal Power Series vs. Analytic Functions: An element \(F(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} f_n x^n \in K[[x]]\) is an infinite sequence \((f_0, f_1, f_2, \dots)\) over a commutative ring \(K\), never an analytic function of a complex variable. Infinite sums \(\sum_{k \ge 0} G_k(x)\) are governed purely by the \(x\)-adic valuation \(\nu(G_k) \to \infty\) (finitely many non-zero contributions to \([x^n]\) for each fixed \(n\)), not radius of convergence.
  2. Ring Units vs. Field Division: Division in \(K[[x]]\) is checked locally before every step: \(F(x)\) is invertible in \(K[[x]]\) if and only if its constant term is a unit \([x^0]F(x) = f_0 \in K^\times\). Operators that integrate \(\int x^{n-1}\,dx = x^n / n\) (ln, exp, pow, classical Lagrange inversion) additionally require the divided integers to be units in \(K\); over a field, characteristic zero or prime characteristic \(p > N\) suffices through degree \(N\).
  3. Black-Box mint & NTT Primitive Contract: In accordance with repository policy, chapters never dump boilerplate modular integer (mint), factorial sieve, or raw Cooley–Tukey butterfly NTT templates. You are assumed to have a working black-box polynomial multiplication primitive mul(A, B) running in \(O(N \log N)\) over NTT-friendly primes such as \(p = 998244353 = 119 \cdot 2^{23} + 1\).
  4. Notation & Defensive Contracts: Keep the Notation & Operator Reference open for the 16 Canonical Failure Modes (#1–#16) and operator contracts, and use the Study Plan & Diagnostic Guide to navigate the 24 chapters, 6 Two-Stage Blind Checkpoints (CP-I–CP-VI), Multi-Perspective Synthesis Reserve, and 321-Problem Master Archive.

How Each Chapter and Checkpoint Works

  1. Motivation & Diagnostic Signals: Each chapter opens with concrete contest statement shapes that defeat naive DP or combinatorial casework, and lists the exact algebraic recognition triggers.
  2. First-Principles Derivations & Hand-Checkable Examples: Every central identity is derived as an explicit sequence of ring equalities or a double count, followed by a small numerical example worked by hand to degree \(3\) or \(4\).
  3. Algorithm Contracts & Defensive Failure Modes: Every operator states its preconditions, exact index bounds, complexity in \(\mathcal{M}(N)\), and how it breaks when valuation shifts (\(\nu(F) \ge 1\)) or small characteristic (\(p \le N\) or \(p \mid n\)) occur.
  4. 8-Problem 4-Tier Practice Ladder: Two Stepping Stones, two Intermediate, two Advanced, and two Mastery problems per chapter, each with progressive hints and a complete self-contained algebraic derivation.
  5. Two-Stage Blind Checkpoints (CP-I–CP-VI): At the end of each Part, an 8-problem blind diagnostic assessment presents contest problems without chapter tags, training you to identify the right algebraic model before opening the stage-gated hints.

6-Part Dependency Architecture

Chapters & Checkpoints

Part I · Foundations of Formal Power Series & Combinatorial Constructions (Ch 01–Ch 04)

  • Ch 01 · The Ring \(K[[x]]\), Valuation Topology & Newton Inversion: Cauchy convolution, \(x\)-adic valuation \(\nu(F)\), unit criterion \([x^0]F \in K^\times\), quadratic Newton inv, valuation-shift precision loss, and min-heap product trees.
  • Ch 02 · Formal Calculus, 1D Newton Iteration & The \(O(NK)\) Sparse Engine: formal \(D_x\) and \(\int\), characteristic-\(p\) Frobenius endomorphism \(A(x)^p \equiv A(x^p) \pmod p\), 1D Newton sqrt/ln/exp/pow, 3-way modular reduction in \(P(x)^\alpha\), and \(O(NK)\) sparse_div/sparse_ln/sparse_exp/sparse_pow.
  • Ch 03 · The Unlabeled Symbolic Method: OGFs, Multisets & Partitions: \(\operatorname{SEQ}\), \(\operatorname{PSET}\), \(\operatorname{MSET}\) (Pólya \(\exp\)), Inverse Euler Transform via Möbius inversion, Ferrers conjugation, Euler’s Pentagonal Number Theorem, and Burnside/Pólya cycle index enumeration.
  • Ch 04 · The Labeled Symbolic Method: EGFs, Boxed Products & Prüfer Trees: relabeling binomial convolution, \(\operatorname{SET} \mapsto \exp\), \(\operatorname{CYC} \mapsto \ln(1-x)^{-1}\), Greene–Wilf Boxed Product \(\widehat{C}' = \widehat{A}'\widehat{B}\), Rota’s Twelvefold Way, and Prüfer degree-sequence EGFs.
  • Checkpoint I · Part I Blind Assessment (CP-I): 8 two-stage blind diagnostic problems testing Ch 01–Ch 04 recognition without topic tags.

Part II · Rational Sequences, Binomial Transforms, Roots of Unity & Inclusion–Exclusion (Ch 05–Ch 08)

  • Ch 05 · Rational Generating Functions \(K(x)\), Bostan–Mori & Berlekamp–Massey: rational series \(P(x)/Q(x)\), reversal Euclidean divmod, Graeffe parity-splitting Bostan–Mori in \(O(d \log d \log N)\), Heaviside partial fractions, and Berlekamp–Massey recurrence synthesis.
  • Ch 06 · Binomial Sums, Snake Oil, Taylor Shift & Stirling Engines: Golden Rule of Index Alignment, Wilf’s Snake Oil, Egorychev polynomial negative-exponent extraction, all 4 Stirling row/column \(O(N \log N)\) engines, falling-factorial polynomials (下降幂多项式), Taylor shift \(F(x+c)\), and the generalized \(\alpha\)-Euler transform.
  • Ch 07 · Point-Value Duality, Roots-of-Unity Filters, Cyclotomic Rings & Chirp-Z: root-of-unity modulo-\(m\) filter (单位根反演), cyclotomic quotient ring \(\mathbb{F}_p[\omega]/(\Phi_m(\omega))\) when \(m \nmid (p-1)\), 3-prime Garner NTT (MTT), and Bluestein’s binomial Chirp Z-Transform \(F(ac^i)\).
  • Ch 08 · Sieve & Inclusion–Exclusion Engines: Run-Length Transfer, Rook & Goulden–Jackson: bivariate Taylor shift \(G(x, y-1)\), universal run-length IE \(F(x) = W(x)/(1+W(x))\), harmonic sparsity of bounded runs, rook polynomials, and the Goulden–Jackson cluster overlap matrix \(\mathbf{C}(x)^{-1}\).
  • Checkpoint II · Part II Blind Assessment (CP-II): 8 two-stage blind diagnostic problems spanning Ch 01–Ch 08.

Part III · Online Convolutions, Implicit Equations, Formal Residues & Graph Species (Ch 09–Ch 13)

  • Ch 09 · Causal Recurrences: CDQ Divide-and-Conquer NTT & Online Relaxed Convolution: semi-online CDQ NTT, full-online self-convolution (\(l > 0\) factor-of-\(2\) symmetry invariant), van der Hoeven’s lowbit(q+1) RelaxedConvolution, and online Dirichlet updates.
  • Ch 10 · Implicit Functional Equations, Coupled Newton Iteration & Unlabeled Otter Trees: implicit equations \(A(x, F(x)) = 0\), Fréchet derivatives, \(2 \times 2\) Jacobian Newton systems, integrating-factor differential Newton, Pólya–Euler rooted trees, and Otter’s dissimilarity theorem \(U(x) = T(x) - \frac{1}{2}(T(x)^2 - T(x^2))\).
  • Ch 11 · Formal Laurent Series \(K((x))\), Residue Calculus, Raney’s Lemma & 6-Form LIF: formal residue invariance \(\operatorname{Res}_{x=0} A(x)\,dx = \operatorname{Res}_{u=0} A(G(u))G'(u)\,du\), Raney’s cycle lemma and Łukasiewicz paths, all 6 canonical forms of Lagrange Inversion (including char-\(p\)-safe Derivative-Free LIF), Elegia coupled binomial sum linearization, and base-\(p\) Frobenius LIF.
  • Ch 12 · Advanced Labeled Graph Species: Pointed Trees, Cacti, Deserts & Tutte Block-Cut Equations: pointing operator \(\Theta = xD_x\), Husimi cacti and deserts, Tutte’s pointed block-cut equation \(\ln(\widehat{R}(x)/x) = \widehat{B}'(\widehat{R}(x))\), bridge-tree 2-edge-connected graphs, and reverse LIF coefficient extraction.
  • Ch 13 · Graphical Generating Functions: Eulerian, Bipartite, DAGs, SCCs & Symbolic Matrix-Tree: Eulerian and bipartite graph EGFs, Robinson–Stanley layer IE for DAGs and strongly connected digraphs, Wright’s fixed-excess kernel decomposition, Kirchhoff Matrix-Tree, BEST theorem, symbolic determinants over \(\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^{k+1})\), and \(O(V^3)\) Hessenberg characteristic polynomials.
  • Checkpoint III · Part III Blind Assessment (CP-III): 8 two-stage blind diagnostic problems spanning Ch 01–Ch 13.

Part IV · Stochastic Processes, Lattice Walks, Bivariate Diagonals & The Kernel Method (Ch 14–Ch 17)

  • Ch 15 · 1D Lattice Walks: Reflection Principle, Chebyshev Strips & The Small-Root Kernel Method: André reflection, Kelvin alternating strip images and Chebyshev polynomials \(U_h\), Laurent step ring \((K[y,y^{-1}])[[x]]\), and the 1D Bivariate Kernel Method (single small root, multiple small roots, and Vieta’s large-root shortcut).
  • Ch 16 · Multivariate FPS: 5-Ring Hierarchy, 2D Bostan–Mori, Twisted Kronecker & Furstenberg Diagonals: the 5-ring bivariate hierarchy, all 4 regimes of 2D Bostan–Mori (Truncated, Eval-Interp, Kinoshita-Li, Frobenius), van der Hoeven’s \(\chi\)-graded twisted Kronecker substitution, Furstenberg’s diagonal residue theorem in \(K[[t]]((y))\), and Lipshitz 3D D-finite diagonals.
  • Ch 17 · Coupled Multivariate Systems: Quarter-Plane Obstinate Kernel, Good–Lagrange & MacMahon: Multivariate Good–Lagrange Inversion (\(\det(I-J)\) Jacobian and bipartite rank-1 shortcut), MacMahon’s Master Theorem \(\det(I-XA)^{-1}\), symmetric function ring \(\Lambda\) and Newton–Girard char-\(p\) asymmetry, 2D quarter-plane Obstinate Kernel Method (dihedral group \(\mathcal{G} = \langle \Phi, \Psi \rangle\)), and bivariate PDE companion matrix products.
  • Checkpoint IV · Part IV Blind Assessment (CP-IV): 8 two-stage blind diagnostic problems spanning Ch 01–Ch 17.
