For forty-six years after R. P. Brent and H. T. Kung introduced their \(O((N \log N)^{3/2})\) baby-step giant-step algorithm in 1978, fast composition \(H(x) = G(F(x)) \bmod x^N\) and compositional inversion \(G(x) = F^{\langle -1 \rangle}(x) \bmod x^N\) remained the last major bottlenecks in the formal power series library. While algebraic operations (inv, sqrt) and differential operations (ln, exp, pow) ran in \(O(N \log N)\) time via Newton iteration, composition and inversion of general dense series at \(N = 2 \times 10^5\) required hundreds of milliseconds of cache-unfriendly block multiplications.

In 2024, Yasunori Kinoshita and Baitian Li shattered this barrier with a remarkably simple insight: Power Projection—the exact matrix transpose of FPS Composition—is nothing more than extracting \([x^{N-1}]\) from a bivariate rational function \(\frac{P_0(x, y)}{1 - y F(x)} \bmod y^N\). Applying the 2D Bostan–Mori parity-splitting loop from Chapter 16 halves the \(x\)-degree while doubling the \(y\)-degree at each of \(\log_2 N\) levels, keeping the total bivariate coefficient volume invariant at \(\Theta(N)\) and solving Power Projection in \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time. Reversing the linear dataflow graph via Tellegen’s Transposition Principle from Chapter 20 immediately yields \(O(N \log^2 N)\) FPS Composition (compose), which in turn unlocks \(O(N \log^2 N)\) Compositional Inverse (revert) across all characteristics.

21.1 Power Projection (\(\text{proj}_k = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i [x^i] F(x)^k\)) via Kinoshita–Li 2D Bostan–Mori

Concrete Combinatorial & Algebraic Motivation

Suppose we are given a formal power series \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} f_i x^i \in K[[x]] / (x^N)\) (for instance, the step generator of a random walk or the branching kernel of a tree) and a weight vector \(\mathbf{w} = (w_0, w_1, \dots, w_{N-1})^\top \in K^N\). We want to compute a single linear functional of the \(k\)-th power \(F(x)^k\) for every \(k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1\) simultaneously: \[\text{proj}_k = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i [x^i] F(x)^k \qquad (0 \le k < N).\]

Notice what this asks in linear algebra terms. Define the \(N \times N\) composition matrix \(M_F\) whose \((i, k)\) entry is the \(i\)-th coefficient of the \(k\)-th power of \(F(x)\): \[(M_F)_{i, k} := [x^i] F(x)^k \qquad (0 \le i, k < N).\]

  • Multiplying \(M_F\) on the right by a column vector \(\mathbf{g} = (g_0, \dots, g_{N-1})^\top\) computes the coefficients of the composite series \(H(x) = G(F(x)) \bmod x^N\): \[h_i = [x^i] \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} g_k F(x)^k = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (M_F)_{i, k} g_k \iff \mathbf{h} = M_F \mathbf{g}.\]
  • Multiplying the transpose \(M_F^\top\) by a weight vector \(\mathbf{w} = (w_0, \dots, w_{N-1})^\top\) computes the power projection vector \(\mathbf{p} = (\text{proj}_0, \dots, \text{proj}_{N-1})^\top\): \[\text{proj}_k = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i [x^i] F(x)^k = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} (M_F^\top)_{k, i} w_i \iff \mathbf{p} = M_F^\top \mathbf{w}.\]

In particular, when \(\mathbf{w} = (0, \dots, 0, 1)^\top\) is the standard basis vector \(e_{N-1}\), Power Projection extracts the single coefficient \([x^{N-1}] F(x)^k\) for all \(k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1\) at once—which is the exact input required by Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11) to recover the compositional inverse \(F^{\langle -1 \rangle}(x) \bmod x^N\).

Bivariate Rational Conversion & The \(x\)-Halving / \(y\)-Doubling Loop

1. The Diagnostic Signal

Whenever you need a linear combination \(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i [x^i] F(x)^k\) for all exponents \(k = 0, \dots, N-1\), never compute the powers \(F(x)^2, F(x)^3, \dots, F(x)^{N-1}\) individually (\(O(N^2 \log N)\)). Instead, introduce a second formal indeterminate \(y\) to sum over all \(k \ge 0\) into a geometric series \(\frac{1}{1 - y F(x)}\).

2. The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

How do we turn the inner product \(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i [x^i] F(x)^k\) into a single coefficient extraction in \(x\)? Recall from Chapter 20 that if we define the reversed weight polynomial \[P_0(x) := \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i x^{N-1-i},\] then multiplying \(P_0(x)\) by \(F(x)^k\) and extracting \([x^{N-1}]\) pairs \(x^{N-1-i}\) from \(P_0(x)\) with \(x^i\) from \(F(x)^k\): \[[x^{N-1}] \Big( P_0(x) F(x)^k \Big) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i [x^i] F(x)^k = \text{proj}_k.\] Now multiply both sides by \(y^k\) and sum over \(0 \le k < N\). In the bivariate quotient ring \(K[[x]][y] / (y^N)\), the geometric series \(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} y^k F(x)^k\) is the exact inverse of \(Q_0(x, y) := 1 - y F(x)\) (which is a unit in \(K[[x, y]]\) because its constant term in \(y\) is \([y^0] Q_0(x, y) = 1 \in K^\times\)): \[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \text{proj}_k y^k = [x^{N-1}] \left( \frac{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i x^{N-1-i}}{1 - y F(x)} \right) \bmod y^N = [x^{N-1}] \frac{P_0(x, y)}{Q_0(x, y)} \bmod y^N.\]

3. Single-Step Verbalized Derivation of Regime 3 2D Bostan–Mori

Pad \(N\) up to the next power of two, \(N = 2^m\) (appending zeros to \(\mathbf{w}\) and \(F(x)\)). This simple padding confers a remarkable structural gift: the target \(x\)-exponent is \(n_0 = N - 1 = 2^m - 1 = (11\dots1)_2\) in binary, so at every halving step \(t = 0, 1, \dots, m-1\), the remaining \(x\)-index \(n_t = 2^{m-t} - 1\) is always odd (\(b_t = n_t \bmod 2 = 1\)).

At recursion level \(t \in \{0, 1, \dots, m\}\), we maintain bivariate polynomials \(P_t(x, y)\) and \(Q_t(x, y)\) satisfying three strict invariants:

  1. \(x\)-Degree Bound: \(\deg_x P_t < 2^{m-t}\) and \(\deg_x Q_t < 2^{m-t}\).
  2. \(y\)-Degree Bound: \(\deg_y P_t < 2^t\) and \(\deg_y Q_t \le 2^t\) (truncated modulo \(y^N\)).
  3. Unit-Constant Normalization: \(Q_t(0, y) = 1\) as a polynomial in \(y\).

Let us verify why these three invariants are preserved when transitioning from \(t\) to \(t + 1\):

  • Step 1 (Conjugate Multiplication): Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\frac{P_t(x, y)}{Q_t(x, y)}\) by \(Q_t(-x, y) \bmod x^{2^{m-t}}\): \[\frac{P_t(x, y)}{Q_t(x, y)} = \frac{P_t(x, y) Q_t(-x, y)}{Q_t(x, y) Q_t(-x, y)}.\]
  • Step 2 (Denominator Deflation): Since \(Q_t(x, y) Q_t(-x, y)\) is invariant under \(x \mapsto -x\), all of its odd \(x\)-powers vanish. Truncating to \(\deg_x < 2^{m-t}\) before substituting \(x^2 \gets x\) defines \[Q_{t+1}(x, y) := \mathcal{S}_0\big(Q_t(x, y) Q_t(-x, y) \bmod x^{2^{m-t}}\big) \bmod y^N,\] where \(\mathcal{S}_b\big(\sum_{i} A_i(y) x^i\big) := \sum_{i} A_{2i+b}(y) x^i\). Because \(\deg_y Q_t \le 2^t\), we have \(\deg_y Q_{t+1} \le 2^{t+1}\). Furthermore, at \(x = 0\), \(Q_0(0, y) = 1 - y f_0\) when \(f_0 = 0\) (or after stripping \(x^0 F\)), so \(Q_0(0, y) = 1\); by induction, \(Q_{t+1}(0, y) = Q_t(0, y) Q_t(0, y) = 1 \cdot 1 = 1\).
  • Step 3 (Numerator Parity Selection): Since \(n_t = 2^{m-t} - 1\) is odd (\(b_t = 1\)), only the odd \(x\)-powers of \(P_t(x, y) Q_t(-x, y) \bmod x^{2^{m-t}}\) contribute to \([x^{n_t}]\): \[P_{t+1}(x, y) := \mathcal{S}_1\big(P_t(x, y) Q_t(-x, y) \bmod x^{2^{m-t}}\big) \bmod y^N.\] Since \(\deg_y P_t < 2^t\) and \(\deg_y Q_t \le 2^t\), their product has \(y\)-degree \(< 2^t + 2^t = 2^{t+1}\), so \(\deg_y P_{t+1} < 2^{t+1}\) without even losing information up to \(y^{2^{t+1}-1}\).
  • Step 4 (Terminal Extraction at \(t = m\)): After \(m = \log_2 N\) steps, \(n_m = 0\), \(\deg_x P_m = 0\), and \(Q_m(0, y) = 1\). Thus zero final polynomial inversion is needed: \[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \text{proj}_k y^k = \frac{P_m(0, y)}{Q_m(0, y)} \bmod y^N = P_m(0, y).\]
TipNachia’s 2D-FFT Frequency Half-Swap Optimization

At level \(t\), a naive implementation performs four 2D NTTs of size \(2 L_x \times 2 L_y\) (where \(L_x = 2^{m-t}, L_y = 2^t\), so \(2 L_x \cdot 2 L_y = 4N\)): forward NTTs of \(P_t(x,y)\), \(Q_t(x,y)\), and \(Q_t(-x,y)\), plus two inverse NTTs.

As observed by Nachia (2024), we never need to compute a separate forward NTT for \(Q_t(-x, y)\):

  1. In a radix-2 NTT of length \(2 L_x\) along the \(x\)-dimension, the evaluation points are \(\omega_{2L_x}^r\). Because \(\omega_{2L_x}^{L_x} = -1\), negating \(x \mapsto -x\) simply shifts the \(x\)-frequency index \(r\) by \(L_x \pmod{2L_x}\): \[\widehat{Q_t(-x, y)}[r_x, r_y] = \widehat{Q_t(x, y)}[r_x \oplus L_x, r_y].\]
  2. Even better, in the frequency domain, the even-index decimation \(\mathcal{S}_0\) and odd-index decimation \(\mathcal{S}_1\) along \(x\) can be extracted before the inverse NTT by combining frequency pairs \((r_x, r_x \oplus L_x)\): \[\widehat{Q_{t+1}}[r_x, r_y] = \widehat{Q_t}[r_x, r_y] \cdot \widehat{Q_t}[r_x \oplus L_x, r_y],\] halving the \(x\)-length of the inverse NTT from \(2 L_x\) to \(L_x\) and cutting the total runtime by nearly \(50\%\).

Hand-Checkable Toy Trace (\(N = 4\))

Let \(N = 4 = 2^2\) (\(m = 2\)), \(F(x) = x + 2x^2\) (\(f_0 = 0, f_1 = 1, f_2 = 2, f_3 = 0\)), and \(\mathbf{w} = (1, 1, 1, 1)^\top\), so \(\text{proj}_k = \sum_{i=0}^3 [x^i] F(x)^k\).

  • Direct check:
    • \(k = 0\): \(F(x)^0 = 1 \implies \text{proj}_0 = 1\).
    • \(k = 1\): \(F(x)^1 = x + 2x^2 \implies \text{proj}_1 = 1 + 2 = 3\).
    • \(k = 2\): \(F(x)^2 = x^2 + 4x^3 + 4x^4 \equiv x^2 + 4x^3 \pmod{x^4} \implies \text{proj}_2 = 1 + 4 = 5\).
    • \(k = 3\): \(F(x)^3 = x^3 + 6x^4 + \dots \equiv x^3 \pmod{x^4} \implies \text{proj}_3 = 1\).
  • Kinoshita–Li 2D Bostan–Mori trace:
    • Level \(t = 0\) (\(n_0 = 3\), \(\deg_x < 4, \deg_y \le 1\)): \[P_0(x, y) = 1 + x + x^2 + x^3, \qquad Q_0(x, y) = 1 - y(x + 2x^2) = 1 - y x - 2 y x^2.\] Here \(Q_0(-x, y) = 1 + y x - 2 y x^2\). Multiply modulo \(x^4\): \[P_0(x, y) Q_0(-x, y) \equiv 1 + (1 + y)x + (1 - y)x^2 + (1 - y)x^3 \pmod{x^4},\] \[Q_0(x, y) Q_0(-x, y) \equiv (1 - 2y x^2)^2 - y^2 x^2 \equiv 1 - (4y + y^2) x^2 \pmod{x^4}.\] Extracting odd \(x\)-powers (\(\mathcal{S}_1\)) for \(P_1\) and even \(x\)-powers (\(\mathcal{S}_0\)) for \(Q_1\): \[P_1(x, y) = (1 + y) + (1 - y) x, \qquad Q_1(x, y) = 1 - (4y + y^2) x.\]
    • Level \(t = 1\) (\(n_1 = 1\), \(\deg_x < 2, \deg_y \le 2\)): Here \(Q_1(-x, y) = 1 + (4y + y^2) x\). Multiply modulo \(x^2\): \[P_1(x, y) Q_1(-x, y) \equiv (1 + y) + \Big((1 - y) + (1 + y)(4y + y^2)\Big) x = (1 + y) + (1 + 3y + 5y^2 + y^3) x \pmod{x^2},\] \[Q_1(x, y) Q_1(-x, y) \equiv 1 - (4y + y^2)^2 x^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{x^2}.\] Extracting odd \(x\)-powers (\(\mathcal{S}_1\)) for \(P_2\) and even \(x\)-powers (\(\mathcal{S}_0\)) for \(Q_2\): \[P_2(0, y) = 1 + 3y + 5y^2 + y^3, \qquad Q_2(0, y) = 1.\]
    • Level \(t = 2\) (\(n_2 = 0\)): Reading the coefficients of \(P_2(0, y)\) yields \(\mathbf{p} = (1, 3, 5, 1)^\top\), matching the direct expansion.
ImportantAlgorithm Contract: Kinoshita–Li Power Projection (power_projection)
  1. Inputs: \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} f_i x^i \in K[[x]]/(x^N)\) and weight vector \(\mathbf{w} = (w_0, \dots, w_{N-1})^\top \in K^N\).
  2. Output: Vector \(\mathbf{p} = (\text{proj}_0, \dots, \text{proj}_{N-1})^\top\) where \(\text{proj}_k = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i [x^i] F(x)^k\).
  3. Algebraic Preconditions: If \(c = f_0 \ne 0\), first compute the projection for \(\tilde{F}(x) = F(x) - c\) (so \([x^0]\tilde{F} = 0\)) and apply the binomial shift \(\text{proj}_k = \sum_{j=0}^k \binom{k}{j} c^{k-j} \widetilde{\text{proj}}_j\) via a single EGF convolution (Ch 06). Requires a ring/field supporting FFT/NTT of length \(4N\) (or 3-prime NTT + CRT).
  4. Complexity: \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time and \(O(N)\) auxiliary space.
  5. Core Invariant: At level \(t \in [0, \log_2 N]\), \([x^{2^{m-t}-1}] \frac{P_t(x, y)}{Q_t(x, y)} \equiv \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \text{proj}_k y^k \pmod{y^{2^t}}\) with \(\deg_x P_t, \deg_x Q_t < 2^{m-t}\), \(\deg_y P_t < 2^t\), \(\deg_y Q_t \le 2^t\), and \(Q_t(0, y) = 1\).
// Core loop of Kinoshita-Li Power Projection (N = 2^m, f[0] == 0)
Poly power_projection(Poly F, Poly w, int N) {
    // Pack (x, y) into 1D array of stride 2*Ly: index(i, j) = i * (2*Ly) + j
    vector<Mint> P(2 * N, 0), Q(2 * N, 0);
    for (int i = 0; i < N; ++i) P[2 * (N - 1 - i)] = w[i];
    for (int i = 1; i < N; ++i) Q[2 * i + 1] = -F[i];
    for (int Lx = N, Ly = 1; Lx > 1; Lx >>= 1, Ly <<= 1) {
        // Multiply P(x, y)*Q(-x, y) and Q(x, y)*Q(-x, y) via 2D NTT of size (2*Lx) x (2*Ly)
        // Extract odd x-powers S_1 into P_next and even x-powers S_0 into Q_next
        bostan_mori_2d_step(P, Q, Lx, Ly);
    }
    return Poly(P.begin(), P.begin() + N); // P_m(0, y) since Q_m(0, y) == 1
}
WarningFailure Mode & Remedy: Non-Zero Constant Term \(f_0 \ne 0\) in power_projection
  • The Failure Mode: Running the \(Q_t(0, y) = 1\) zero-division loop directly when \(f_0 = [x^0] F(x) \ne 0\). When \(f_0 \ne 0\), \(Q_0(0, y) = 1 - f_0 y \ne 1\), so at \(t = m\) the denominator is \(Q_m(0, y) = (1 - f_0 y)^{2^m} \ne 1\), and reading \(P_m(0, y)\) without dividing by \(Q_m(0, y)\) produces garbage for all \(k \ge 1\).
  • The Remedy: Either strip \(c = f_0\) upfront by setting \(\tilde{F}(x) = F(x) - c\) (so \(\tilde{F}(0) = 0\)) and convolving \(\widetilde{\text{proj}}_j / j!\) with \(c^r / r!\) at the end, or divide \(P_m(0, y)\) by \(Q_m(0, y) \bmod y^N\) via a single 1D polynomial inversion at \(t = m\). Stripping \(f_0 = 0\) upfront is cleaner because it also guarantees \(\deg_y P_t < 2^t\) without carry-over from \(x^0\).

21.2 Kinoshita–Li FPS Composition (\(H(x) = G(F(x)) \bmod x^N\)) via Tellegen Transposition

Transposing the Power Projection Circuit

Given \(F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} f_i x^i\) and \(G(y) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} g_k y^k\), we want to compute the composite series \[H(x) = G(F(x)) \bmod x^N = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} h_i x^i, \qquad h_i = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} g_k [x^i] F(x)^k.\]

1. The Diagnostic Signal

For a fixed inner series \(F(x)\), both Power Projection \(\mathbf{w} \mapsto \mathbf{p} = M_F^\top \mathbf{w}\) and FPS Composition \(\mathbf{g} \mapsto \mathbf{h} = M_F \mathbf{g}\) are linear maps in \(\mathbf{w}\) and \(\mathbf{g}\) respectively, and their matrices are exact transposes of one another. Notice that in power_projection, the denominator sequence \(Q_0(x, y), Q_1(x, y), \dots, Q_m(x, y)\) depends only on \(F(x)\) and is completely independent of \(\mathbf{w}\).

2. The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

Let us trace the linear operations applied to the vector \(\mathbf{w}\) in power_projection (§21.1) and invert their order and direction using the rules of Tellegen’s Transposition Principle from Chapter 20:

  1. Forward Map on \(\mathbf{w}\): \[\mathbf{w} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{R}_x} P_0(x, y) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_0} P_1(x, y) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_1} \cdots \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{m-1}} P_m(0, y) = \mathbf{p},\] where \(\mathcal{R}_x\) places \(w_i\) at \([x^{N-1-i} y^0] P_0(x, y)\), and each linear layer \(\mathcal{L}_t : P_t \mapsto P_{t+1}\) performs two elementary linear maps in sequence:

    • Multiplication by \(Q_t(-x, y)\): \(U_t(x, y) = P_t(x, y) \cdot Q_t(-x, y) \bmod (x^{2^{m-t}}, y^{2^{t+1}})\).
    • Odd-Index Decimation \(\mathcal{S}_1\) in \(x\): \(P_{t+1}(x, y) = \mathcal{S}_1(U_t(x, y))\), which selects \([x^{2i+1}] U_t(x, y)\) as \([x^i] P_{t+1}(x, y)\).

  2. Transposed Map on \(\mathbf{g}\) (fps_compose): \[\mathbf{g} = \hat{P}_m(0, y) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{m-1}^\top} \hat{P}_{m-1}(x, y) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{m-2}^\top} \cdots \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_0^\top} \hat{P}_0(x, y) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{R}_x^\top} \mathbf{h}.\] What is the transpose \(\mathcal{L}_t^\top = (\mathcal{S}_1 \circ \operatorname{mul}_{Q_t(-x, y)})^\top = \operatorname{mul}_{Q_t(-x, y)}^\top \circ \mathcal{S}_1^\top\)?

    • Transpose of Odd-Index Decimation \(\mathcal{S}_1^\top\): Decimation \(\mathcal{S}_1\) maps index \(2i + 1 \mapsto i\) and discards even indices \(2i\). Its transpose \(\mathcal{S}_1^\top\) interleaves zeros at even \(x\)-indices and places \([x^i] \hat{P}_{t+1}(x, y)\) at \([x^{2i+1}] \hat{U}_t(x, y)\): \[\hat{U}_t(x, y) := \mathcal{S}_1^\top\big(\hat{P}_{t+1}(x, y)\big) = x \hat{P}_{t+1}(x^2, y).\]
    • Transpose of Multiplication by \(Q_t(-x, y)\): By the Middle Product Theorem (Ch 20, Theorem 20.1), the transpose of multiplying \(P_t(x, y)\) by the fixed bivariate polynomial \(Q_t(-x, y)\) is the 2D Middle Product (cross-correlation) \(\operatorname{mulT}_{x, y}\): \[[x^i y^j] \hat{P}_t(x, y) = \sum_{a, b} \big([x^{i+a} y^{j+b}] \hat{U}_t(x, y)\big) \cdot \big([x^a y^b] Q_t(-x, y)\big),\] or in polynomial notation: \[\hat{P}_t(x, y) = \operatorname{mulT}_{x, y}\!\Big(\hat{U}_t(x, y),\, Q_t(-x, y)\Big) \bmod \big(x^{2^{m-t}}, y^{2^t}\big).\]

21.3 Elegia’s All-\(n\) Coupled Binomial Sum Linearization (炫酷反演魔术, Form 6 Case B)

In Chapter 11, we saw how the formal residue change-of-variables theorem extracts a single coefficient of an implicit series. Now that we possess \(O(N \log^2 N)\) fast composition (compose) and compositional inversion (revert), we can evaluate an entire family of coupled binomial sums across all \(n = 0, 1, \dots, N\) simultaneously.

1. The Diagnostic Signal

You are asked to compute, for all \(n = 0, 1, \dots, N\) (or for a single \(n\) when \(f_k\) is an arbitrary array), the sum \[S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} f_k \binom{\alpha n + \beta k + \gamma}{n - k},\] where the upper index \(\alpha n + \beta k + \gamma\) depends linearly on both the outer index \(n\) and the summation index \(k\), preventing a direct 1D convolution.

2. The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

How do we decouple \(n\) and \(k\) inside \(\binom{\alpha n + \beta k + \gamma}{n - k}\)? Write the binomial coefficient as a formal Laurent residue in a dummy variable \(u\): \[\binom{\alpha n + \beta k + \gamma}{n - k} = [u^{n-k}] (1 + u)^{\alpha n + \beta k + \gamma} = \operatorname{Res}_u \frac{(1 + u)^{\alpha n + \beta k + \gamma}}{u^{n - k + 1}}\,du.\] Group all factors according to whether their exponent is \(n\), \(k\), or constant: \[\frac{(1 + u)^{\alpha n + \beta k + \gamma}}{u^{n - k + 1}}\,du = (1 + u)^\gamma \cdot \Big(u (1 + u)^\beta\Big)^k \cdot \left(\frac{u}{(1 + u)^\alpha}\right)^{-n - 1} \frac{du}{(1 + u)^\alpha}.\] Look at the base raised to the power \(-n - 1\): it is \[z = G(u) := \frac{u}{(1 + u)^\alpha} = u (1 + u)^{-\alpha} \in \mathcal{G}_1(K).\] Let \(u = A(z) := G^{\langle -1 \rangle}(z)\) be its unique compositional inverse satisfying \(A(z) = z (1 + A(z))^\alpha\). What is the differential conversion factor between \(\frac{du}{(1+u)^\alpha}\) and \(dz\)? Differentiate \(z = u(1+u)^{-\alpha}\) logarithmically: \[\frac{dz}{z} = \left(\frac{1}{u} - \frac{\alpha}{1 + u}\right) du = \frac{1 - (\alpha - 1)u}{u(1 + u)}\,du \implies dz = \frac{1 - (\alpha - 1)u}{(1 + u)^{\alpha + 1}}\,du \implies \frac{du}{(1 + u)^\alpha} = \frac{1 + u}{1 - (\alpha - 1)u}\,dz.\] Substituting \(u = A(z)\) via the Formal Residue Change-of-Variables Theorem (Ch 11, Theorem 11.1) turns \(\operatorname{Res}_u (\dots) G(u)^{-n-1} G'(u)\,du\) directly into \([z^n]\)!

3. Single-Step Verbalized Derivation of the Master Identity

Let \(F(w) := \sum_{k=0}^N f_k w^k\) be the ordinary generating function of the sequence \((f_k)\). Summing over \(k = 0 \dots n\) (since terms with \(k > n\) have \(\operatorname{val}_u\big(u^{k-n-1}(1+u)^{\dots}\big) \ge 0\) and zero residue at \(u^{-1}\)):

  1. Express \(S_n\) as a single \(u\)-residue: \[S_n = \operatorname{Res}_u \left( F\big(u(1 + u)^\beta\big) \cdot (1 + u)^\gamma \cdot \left(\frac{u}{(1 + u)^\alpha}\right)^{-n-1} \frac{du}{(1 + u)^\alpha} \right).\]
  2. Substitute \(\frac{du}{(1+u)^\alpha} = \frac{1+u}{1 - (\alpha-1)u}\,dz\) where \(z = u(1+u)^{-\alpha}\): \[S_n = \operatorname{Res}_u \left( F\big(u(1 + u)^\beta\big) \cdot \frac{(1 + u)^{\gamma + 1}}{1 - (\alpha - 1) u} \cdot z(u)^{-n-1}\,dz(u) \right).\]
  3. Apply the Change-of-Variables Theorem \(u = A(z)\) (Ch 11): \[S_n = \operatorname{Res}_z \left( F\!\Big(A(z)\big(1 + A(z)\big)^\beta\Big) \cdot \frac{\big(1 + A(z)\big)^{\gamma + 1}}{1 - (\alpha - 1) A(z)} \cdot z^{-n-1}\,dz \right).\]
  4. Read off the coefficient \([z^n]\) for all \(0 \le n \le N\) simultaneously: \[\sum_{n=0}^{N} S_n z^n \equiv F\!\Big(A(z)\big(1 + A(z)\big)^\beta\Big) \cdot \frac{\big(1 + A(z)\big)^{\gamma + 1}}{1 - (\alpha - 1) A(z)} \pmod{z^{N+1}}.\]

21.4 Dual-Route Compositional Inverse (revert), Failure Mode #6 & Functional Square Roots \(G(G(x)) = F(x)\)

Given \(F(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} f_i x^i \in x K[[x]] / (x^N)\) with unit linear coefficient \(f_1 = [x^1] F(x) \in K^\times\), we want to compute its unique compositional inverse \(G(y) = F^{\langle -1 \rangle}(y) = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} g_k y^k \bmod y^N\) satisfying \(F(G(y)) \equiv y \pmod{y^N}\) and \(G(F(x)) \equiv x \pmod{x^N}\).

Depending on whether the field characteristic \(p = \operatorname{char}(K)\) satisfies \(p > N\) (or \(p = 0\)) versus \(p \le N\), there are two distinct \(O(N \log^2 N)\) algorithms for revert. Knowing both routes—and why Route A suffers a catastrophic division-by-zero crash when \(p \le N\)—is mandatory for mastery-tier competitive programming.

Route A (noshi91 LIF + Power Projection — When \(\operatorname{char}(K) = 0\) or \(p > N\))

1. The Diagnostic Signal & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

In §21.1, we saw that running power_projection with the single weight \(w_n = 1\) (where \(n = N - 1\) and \(w_i = 0\) for \(i \ne n\)) computes the sequence of coefficients \([x^n] F(x)^k\) for all \(k = 1, 2, \dots, n\) simultaneously in \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time. How are the coefficients \([x^n] F(x)^k\) related to the compositional inverse \(G(y) = F^{\langle -1 \rangle}(y)\)? By Classical Lagrange Inversion (Ch 11, Theorem 11.2, Variant C) applied with the roles of \(F\) and \(G\) swapped (\(G(F(x)) = x\), so \(F(x) = \frac{x}{G(F(x))/F(x)}\)): \[[x^n] F(x)^k = \frac{k}{n} [y^{n-k}] \left(\frac{y}{G(y)}\right)^n = \frac{k}{n} [y^{n-k}] \left(\frac{G(y)}{y}\right)^{-n} \qquad (1 \le k \le n).\]

2. Single-Step Verbalized Algorithm (noshi91, 2024)

Let \(n = N - 1\) and define the unit-constant series \(H(y) := \left(\frac{G(y)}{y}\right)^{-n} \in K[[y]] / (y^n)\).

  1. Power Projection: Call power_projection(F, e_n, N) with \(\mathbf{w} = e_n = (0, \dots, 0, 1)^\top\) to obtain \(p_k = [x^n] F(x)^k\) for \(1 \le k \le n\) in \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time.
  2. Lagrange Scaling & Index Reversal: For each \(k = 1, \dots, n\), set \[[y^{n-k}] H(y) = \frac{n}{k} p_k = \frac{n}{k} [x^n] F(x)^k.\] This determines all coefficients of \(H(y) \bmod y^n\).
  3. Route B (Newton Iteration + Kinoshita–Li compose — Mandatory When \(p \le N\))

What happens to Route A if we work modulo a small prime \(p \le N\) (or over an arbitrary characteristic-\(p\) field)? Both Step 2 (\(\frac{n}{k}\)) and Step 3 (\(\exp(-\frac{1}{n}\ln(\dots))\)) divide by every integer \(k \in \{1, 2, \dots, N-1\}\) and by \(n = N - 1\), crashing at \(k = p, 2p, \dots\).

1. The Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

To avoid dividing by \(1, 2, \dots, N-1\), view \(G(y)\) as the root of the functional equation \[\Phi(G)(y) := F(G(y)) - y = 0.\] Suppose we know \(G_s(y) \equiv G(y) \pmod{y^{2^s}}\) (starting from \(G_0(y) = f_1^{-1} y \bmod y^2\)). Write \(G_{s+1}(y) = G_s(y) + \Delta(y)\) where \(\operatorname{val}_y(\Delta) \ge 2^s\), so \(\Delta(y)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{y^{2^{s+1}}}\). By the Hasse–Taylor expansion of \(F\) around \(G_s(y)\) (which requires zero factorials, since \(\binom{j}{1} = j \in \mathbb{Z}\)): \[0 \equiv F(G_s(y) + \Delta(y)) - y \equiv F(G_s(y)) - y + F'(G_s(y)) \Delta(y) \pmod{y^{2^{s+1}}}.\] Solving for \(\Delta(y)\) yields the quadratic Newton step: \[G_{s+1}(y) \equiv G_s(y) - \frac{F(G_s(y)) - y}{F'(G_s(y))} \pmod{y^{2^{s+1}}}.\]

2. Why Route B Never Divides by \(1, 2, \dots, N-1\)

At precision \(L = 2^{s+1}\):

  1. We evaluate \(F(G_s(y)) \bmod y^L\) and \(F'(G_s(y)) \bmod y^{L/2}\) using Kinoshita–Li compose (§21.2), which is \(100\%\) free of divisions by \(1, \dots, L-1\) since \([y^0] G_s(y) = 0\).
  2. We invert \(F'(G_s(y)) \bmod y^{L/2}\) via multiplicative Newton inversion (fps_inv). What is the constant term of \(F'(G_s(y))\)? Since \(G_s(0) = 0\), \([y^0] F'(G_s(y)) = F'(0) = f_1 \in K^\times\), which is invertible by hypothesis!
  3. Total time across \(s = 0, 1, \dots, \log_2 N - 1\) satisfies the geometric sum \[T(N) = \sum_{s=1}^{\log_2 N} O(2^s \log^2(2^s)) = O(N \log^2 N).\]

ImportantAlgorithm Contract: Dual-Route Compositional Inverse (fps_revert)
  1. Input: \(F(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} f_i x^i \in x K[[x]]/(x^N)\) with \([x^0] F(x) = 0\) and \([x^1] F(x) = f_1 \in K^\times\).
  2. Output: \(G(y) = F^{\langle -1 \rangle}(y) \bmod y^N\) satisfying \(F(G(y)) \equiv y \pmod{y^N}\).
  3. Algebraic Preconditions:
    • Route A (revert_lif_proj): Requires \(f_1 \in K^\times\) and \(1, 2, \dots, N-1 \in K^\times\) (\(\operatorname{char}(K) = 0\) or \(p > N\)).
    • Route B (revert_newton_compose): Requires only \(f_1 \in K^\times\) and \(2 \in K^\times\) (for radix-2 FFT/NTT).
  4. Complexity: \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time and \(O(N)\) (Route A) or \(O(N \log N)\) (Route B) space.
  5. Core Invariant: In Route A, \(H(y) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{n}{k} ([x^n]F(x)^k) y^{n-k} \equiv (G(y)/y)^{-n} \pmod{y^n}\); in Route B, \(F(G_s(y)) \equiv y \pmod{y^{2^s}}\) doubles precision at every step \(s\).
// Route A (p > N): 1 Power Projection + pow(-1/n); Route B (all p != 2): Newton + compose
Poly fps_revert_routeA(const Poly& F, int N) { // Precondition: F[0] == 0, F[1] != 0, p > N
    int n = N - 1; Poly w(N, 0), H(n); w[n] = 1; Poly proj = power_projection(F, w, N);
    for (int k = 1; k <= n; ++k) H[n - k] = proj[k] * Mint(n) * Mint(k).inv();
    Poly G = fps_pow(H, -Mint(n).inv(), n); G.insert(G.begin(), 0); return G;
}
Poly fps_revert_routeB(const Poly& F, int N) { // Precondition: F[0] == 0, F[1] != 0 (p <= N!)
    Poly G = {0, F[1].inv()}, dF = fps_deriv(F);
    for (int L = 2; L < N; L <<= 1) {
        Poly FG = fps_compose(G, trunc(F, 2 * L), 2 * L); FG[1] -= 1; // F(G_s(y)) - y
        G = fps_sub(G, fps_mul_trunc(FG, fps_inv(fps_compose(G, trunc(dF, L), L), L), 2 * L));
    }
    G.resize(N); return G;
}

Functional Square Roots \(G(G(x)) = F(x) \bmod x^N\) & The Degree \(p + 2\) Artin–Schreier Obstruction

A closely related compositional problem is the Functional Square Root (AtCoder fps_24_x): given \(F(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} f_k x^k \in x \mathbb{F}_p[[x]] / (x^N)\) over an odd prime field \(\mathbb{F}_p\), find \(G(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} g_k x^k\) such that \[G(G(x)) \equiv F(x) \pmod{x^N}.\]

1. Degree-by-Degree Indeterminate Analysis

Comparing \([x^1]\) on both sides gives \(g_1^2 \equiv f_1 \pmod p\). Once we fix a square root \(g_1 \in \mathbb{F}_p\), what equation governs the \(k\)-th coefficient \(g_k\) (\(k \ge 2\)) in terms of \(g_1, \dots, g_{k-1}\)? In \(G(G(x)) = \sum_{m=1}^{N-1} g_m G(x)^m\), the unknown \(g_k\) appears in only two places at degree \(x^k\):

  • Inside the \(m = 1\) term \(g_1 G(x)\), which contributes \(g_1 g_k x^k\).
  • Inside the \(m = k\) term \(g_k G(x)^k = g_k (g_1 x + \dots)^k\), which contributes \(g_k g_1^k x^k\).

All terms with \(2 \le m \le k - 1\) depend solely on \(g_1, \dots, g_{k-1}\). Therefore: \[g_1 \big(1 + g_1^{k-1}\big) g_k = f_k - [x^k] \sum_{m=2}^{k-1} g_m G_{<k}(x)^m.\]

Look at the multiplier \(g_1(1 + g_1^{k-1})\):

  • Tangent-to-Identity Case (\(f_1 = 1\), choosing \(g_1 = 1\)): For every \(k \ge 2\), the multiplier is \(1 \cdot (1 + 1^{k-1}) = 2 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\) (since \(p\) is an odd prime)! Thus a unique tangent-to-identity functional square root \(G(x) = x + g_2 x^2 + \dots\) exists for every \(N\) and every odd prime \(p\), with zero division by \(k\)!
  • Non-Unit-Multiplier Obstructions: If \(g_1 = 0\) (\(f_1 = 0\)), \(\operatorname{val}(G) \ge 2 \implies \operatorname{val}(G \circ G) \ge 4\). If \(g_1 \not\equiv 0, 1 \pmod p\) has even multiplicative order \(2d\) so \(g_1^{k-1} \equiv -1 \pmod p\) for \(k = d + 1\), then \(1 + g_1^{k-1} \equiv 0 \pmod p\), creating a resonance obstruction unless the right-hand side vanishes.

2. maspy’s Period-\(p\) Shortcut vs. The Degree \(p + 2\) Artin–Schreier Obstruction

When \(g_1 = 1\) (\(F(x) = x + f_2 x^2 + \dots\)) and \(N \le p + 1\), maspy’s Jordan Block Theorem shows that the linear composition operator \(T_G : A(x) \mapsto A(G(x))\) on the \(p\)-dimensional vector space \(V = x\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^{p+1})\) is upper unitriangular (\(T_G(x^k) = x^k + k g_2 x^{k+1} + \dots\)), so \((T_G - I)^p = T_{G^{\circ p}} - I = 0\) on \(V\). Consequently, every tangent-to-identity series satisfies \(G^{\circ p}(x) \equiv x \pmod{x^{p+1}}\), and the unique functional square root modulo \(x^{p+1}\) is simply the \(\frac{p+1}{2}\)-th compositional iterate of \(F(x)\): \[G(x) \equiv F^{\circ \left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}(x) \pmod{x^{p+1}}.\]

WarningThe Degree \(p + 2\) Artin–Schreier Obstruction to maspy’s Period-\(p\) Shortcut

Why does the cyclic-group shortcut \(G^{\circ p}(x) \equiv x\) fail at degree \(N = p + 2\)?

  • Consider the Artin–Schreier generator \(G(x) = x + x^2 = \frac{x}{1 - x} \bmod x^4\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\). Since \(\frac{x}{1 - c x}\) composes additively in \(c\), the \(p\)-fold iterate of \(\frac{x}{1 - x}\) is \(\frac{x}{1 - p x} \equiv x \pmod p\).
  • However, if we perturb \(G(x)\) at degree \(3\), say \(G(x) = x + x^2 + c x^3\), the \(p\)-fold composition \(G^{\circ p}(x)\) still equals \(x\) modulo \(x^{p+1}\), and even modulo \(x^{p+2}\) only when \(c = 1\)! Specifically, expanding \(G^{\circ p}(x) \bmod x^{p+2}\) for a general \(G(x) = x + g_2 x^2 + g_3 x^3 + \dots\) reveals the Artin–Schreier residue obstruction at degree \(x^{p+2}\) (and already at \(p = 2\), \(G(x) = x + x^2 \implies G(G(x)) = (x+x^2) + (x+x^2)^2 \equiv x + x^4 \pmod 2\), which is non-zero at degree \(p + 2 = 4\)).
  • Remedy: For general \(N > p + 1\) (or whenever binary exponentiation of compose in \(O(N \log^2 N \log p)\) is slower than Newton iteration), solve \(G(G(x)) - F(x) = 0 \bmod x^N\) via Newton Iteration (Mas-2 below) or incremental degree-by-degree extraction.

Problem Statement & Constraints

  • Constraints: \(1 \le N, M \le 2 \times 10^5\), Time Limit \(4.0\text{ s}\), Modulo \(998244353\).
  • Sample Trace (\(N = 2, M = 2\)):
    • Waiting time \(T = 1 + G_{1/2}\) where \(G_{1/2}\) is geometric with success probability \(\frac{1}{2}\): \(P(x) = \sum_{t \ge 2} \mathbb{P}(T=t) x^t = x \cdot \frac{\frac{1}{2}x}{1 - \frac{1}{2}x} = \frac{x^2}{2 - x}\).
    • \(k = 1\): \(\mathbb{E}[T] = 1 + 2 = 3\).
    • \(k = 2\): \(\mathbb{E}[T^2] = \sum_{t \ge 2} t^2 2^{-(t-1)} = 4(\frac{1}{2}) + 9(\frac{1}{4}) + 16(\frac{1}{8}) + \dots = 11\).
Hint 1 — Diagnostic Signal & Combinatorial Model (Spoiler-Free)

By the standard coupon collector decomposition, when \(i\) distinct faces have already been seen (\(0 \le i \le N-1\)), the number of additional rolls \(T_i\) to see the \((i+1)\)-th new face is a geometric random variable with success probability \(\frac{N - i}{N}\), independent of previous phases. What is the Probability Generating Function \(P(x) = \mathbb{E}[x^T] = \prod_{i=0}^{N-1} \mathbb{E}[x^{T_i}]\)?

Hint 2 — Algebraic Setup & Reverse-Engineering Scratchpad

We have \[P(x) = \prod_{i=0}^{N-1} \frac{\frac{N - i}{N} x}{1 - \frac{i}{N} x} = \frac{\frac{N!}{N^N} x^N}{\prod_{i=1}^{N-1} \left(1 - \frac{i}{N} x\right)} = \frac{A(x)}{B(x)}.\] How do we extract \(\mathbb{E}[T^k]\) for all \(1 \le k \le M\) from \(P(x)\)? Recall that \((x \frac{d}{dx})^k P(x) \big|_{x=1} = \mathbb{E}[T^k]\). Under the exponential change of variables \(x = e^z\), the differential operator \(x \frac{d}{dx}\) becomes \(\frac{d}{dz}\), so the Moment Generating Function is the composition \[P(e^z) = \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \mathbb{P}(T = t) e^{tz} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \mathbb{E}[T^k] \frac{z^k}{k!} = \frac{A(e^z)}{B(e^z)} \pmod{z^{M+1}}.\]

Hint 3 — Coefficient Extraction & Complexity Contract

First expand \(B(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{N-1} (1 - \frac{i}{N} x) = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} b_j x^j\) via a binary D&C NTT product tree in \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time. Next, instead of calling general \(O(M \log^2 M)\) fps_compose to evaluate \(B(e^z)\), exploit the exponential structure of \(e^z\): \[B(e^z) = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} b_j e^{j z} = \sum_{k=0}^{M} \left( \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} b_j j^k \right) \frac{z^k}{k!},\] where the power sums \(s_k = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} b_j j^k = [z^k] \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \frac{b_j}{1 - j z}\) are computed in \(O((N + M) \log^2(N + M))\) via a single Rational Sum of Simple Fractions (Ch 20)! Finally, compute \(A(e^z) B(e^z)^{-1} \bmod z^{M+1}\).

NoteComplete Derivation & Solution

  1. Classification of Degree-\(0\) and Degree-\(1\) Coefficients: If \([x^0] F(x) = 0\):

    • Case A (\(f_1 = 1\), Tangent to Identity): Choose \(g_1 = 1\). As proved in §21.4, for every \(k \ge 2\), the degree-\(k\) coefficient equation is \[2 g_k = f_k - [x^k] \sum_{m=2}^{k-1} g_m G_{<k}(x)^m,\] which has a unique solution in \(\mathbb{F}_p\) for every odd prime \(p\).
    • Case B (\(f_1 \not\equiv 0, 1 \pmod p\)): Check whether \(f_1\) is a quadratic residue modulo \(p\) via Tonelli–Shanks / Cipolla (\(g_1^2 \equiv f_1 \pmod p\)). If so, test both roots \(\pm g_1\): at each degree \(k \ge 2\), \(g_1(1 + g_1^{k-1}) g_k = \text{RHS}_k\).
    • Case C (\(f_1 = 0\)): If \(F(x) \equiv 0 \pmod{x^N}\), then \(G(x) = 0\) works; otherwise, let \(v = \operatorname{val}_x(F) \ge 2\). Since \(\operatorname{val}_x(G \circ G) = (\operatorname{val}_x G)^2\), no solution can exist unless \(v = d^2\) is a perfect square (or \(v \ge N\), in which case \(G(x) = c x^{\lceil \sqrt{N} \rceil}\) has \(G(G(x)) \equiv 0 \pmod{x^N}\)).

  2. Method 1 — \(O(N^2)\) Indeterminate Extraction / Online Relaxed Convolution (for \(N \le 8000\)): Maintain the powers \(G(x)^2, G(x)^3, \dots, G(x)^{N-1} \bmod x^N\) incrementally: as soon as \(g_k\) is solved from \(g_1(1 + g_1^{k-1}) g_k = f_k - [x^k] \sum_{m=2}^{k-1} g_m G(x)^m\), update \([x^j] G(x)^m\) for \(m \le j \le N-1\), running in \(O(N^2)\) operations (\(\approx 15\text{ ms}\) for \(N = 8000\)).

  3. Method 2 — \(O(N \log^2 N)\) Newton / Lie-Flow Iteration via Kinoshita–Li compose (for \(N \le 2.5 \times 10^5, p > N\)): When \(f_0 = 0, f_1 = 1\) and \(\operatorname{char}(K) = 0\) or \(p > N\), every tangent-to-identity series \(F(x) = x + f_2 x^2 + \dots \in \mathcal{G}_{1,1}(K)\) is the time-\(1\) flow \(\exp(v(x) \frac{d}{dx})(x)\) of a unique formal vector field \(v(x) \frac{d}{dx}\) (\(v(x) = v_2 x^2 + v_3 x^3 + \dots\)), and its functional square root \(G(x)\) is the time-\(\frac{1}{2}\) flow \(\exp(\frac{1}{2} v(x) \frac{d}{dx})(x)\)! Even better, in Newton’s method \(G_{s+1} = G_s + \Delta\) (\(\Delta \equiv 0 \pmod{x^L}, L = 2^s\)), the linearized equation is \[G_s'(G_s(x)) \Delta(x) + \Delta(G_s(x)) \equiv F(x) - G_s(G_s(x)) \pmod{x^{2L}}.\] Because \(\Delta(x) = \delta_L x^L + \dots + \delta_{2L-1} x^{2L-1}\) has valuation \(\ge L\) and \((G_s(x))^k = x^k (G_s(x)/x)^k\) has a \(k\)-dependent exponent across \(L \le k < 2L\), we factor \(\Delta(x) = A_s(x) U(x)\) where \(U(x) \equiv 0 \pmod{x^L}\) and \(A_s(x)\) is the Julia / Lie generator satisfying \(A_s(G_s(x)) = G_s'(x) A_s(x)\). Equivalently, parameterizing \(F(x) = \Psi^{\langle -1 \rangle}(\Psi(x) + 1)\) via Abel’s equation \(\Psi(F(x)) = \Psi(x) + 1\) (solved by Newton iteration + Kinoshita–Li compose) yields \(G(x) = \Psi^{\langle -1 \rangle}\!\big(\Psi(x) + \frac{1}{2}\big) \bmod x^N\) in \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time!

Algorithm Contract

  • Input & Preconditions: \(F(x) \in \mathbb{F}_p[[x]]/(x^N)\) (\(N \le 8000\) for \(O(N^2)\) all-case solver; \(N \le 2.5 \times 10^5\) for \(O(N \log^2 N)\) tangent-to-identity solver); odd prime \(p = 998244353\) (\(2 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\)).
  • Postconditions (Output): \(G(x) \bmod x^N\) satisfying \(G(G(x)) \equiv F(x) \pmod{x^N}\), or -1 if none exists.
  • Time & Space Complexity: \(O(N^2)\) time for general \(f_0, f_1\) at \(N \le 8000\); \(O(N \log^2 N)\) time for tangent-to-identity \(F(x) = x + \dots\) at \(N \le 2.5 \times 10^5\), \(O(N)\) space.
  • Loop / Algebraic Invariant: At each degree \(k \ge 2\), \([x^k] G(G(x)) = g_1(1 + g_1^{k-1}) g_k + [x^k] \sum_{m=2}^{k-1} g_m G_{<k}(x)^m\).
  • Failure Modes & Edge Cases: When \(g_1 \ne 1\), if \(g_1^{k-1} \equiv -1 \pmod p\) at some degree \(k \ge 2\), the leading coefficient \(g_1(1 + g_1^{k-1}) \equiv 0 \pmod p\) vanishes (resonance); if the right-hand side is non-zero at that \(k\), no solution exists for that branch of \(g_1\), so both square roots \(\pm \sqrt{f_1} \bmod p\) must be tested.

Historical & Pedagogical Notes

  • From Brent–Kung (1978) to Kinoshita–Li (FOCS 2024): R. P. Brent and H. T. Kung (Fast Algorithms for Manipulating Formal Power Series, JACM 1978) established the \(O((N \log N)^{3/2})\) baby-step giant-step algorithm for composition and inversion, as well as the duality between composition and power projection via the transposition principle. While Kedlaya and Umans (FOCS 2008 / SICOMP 2011) achieved theoretical \(N^{1 + o(1)}\) algebraic complexity over finite fields via multimodular reduction, no quasi-linear algebraic algorithm (using only ring/field operations) was known until Yasunori Kinoshita (noshi91) and Baitian Li (Elegia / EI) published Power Projection and Composition of Formal Power Series in \(O(n \log^2 n)\) Operations at FOCS 2024.
  • Competitive Programming Optimizations (noshi91 & Nachia, 2024): Within days of the preprint’s release, Nachia introduced the frequency-domain half-swap optimization (k_x ^ Lx), reducing the 2D FFT count per level from \(5\) to \(3\) (and \(2\) in the denominator tree), making Kinoshita–Li compose faster than Brent–Kung for all \(N \ge 2048\).
  • Curriculum Reference Registry & Verification Benchmarks (R-32, R-33, R-34, R-35): The Kinoshita–Li \(O(N \log^2 N)\) algorithms in this chapter correspond to R-32 (Kinoshita and Li (FOCS 2024) — Power Projection and Composition of Formal Power Series in \(O(n \log^2 n)\) Operations) and R-33 (noshi91 & Nachia (2024) — \(O(N \log^2 N)\) FPS Composition and Compositional Inverse Blog Notes), and can be unit-tested on the Library Checker benchmarks R-34 (composition_of_formal_power_series_large) and R-35 (compositional_inverse_of_formal_power_series_large).
