03 — Advanced Summatory Sieves

Ordinary sieves compute every value through \(N\); summatory sieves compute only

\[F(N)=\sum_{n\le N}f(n)\]

when \(N\) is too large to visit. The art is choosing the correct structure:

  • Du Jiao sieve: an easy Dirichlet convolution isolates \(F(N)\).
  • Min_25 sieve: \(f\) is multiplicative and \(f(p)\) is a low-degree polynomial in \(p\).
  • Powerful-number sieve: remove an easy function agreeing with \(f\) on primes, leaving support only on numbers whose prime exponents are never one.

Before this chapter, master multiplicative functions, Möbius inversion, and quotient grouping in Chapter 02.

1. The \(O(\sqrt{N})\) quotient-state space

Every recursive call in these methods should normally be one of the distinct values \(\lfloor N/i\rfloor\). There are fewer than \(2\sqrt{N}\) such values. Two common indexing schemes are:

  • store large values in a hash map and precompute all values up to a cutoff;
  • map \(v\) to \(v\) itself if \(v≤\sqrt{N}\), otherwise to \(N/v\) in a second array.

Never accidentally memoize arbitrary arguments: that can turn an intended \(O(\sqrt{N})\) state set into linear space.

2. Du Jiao sieve from a convolution identity

Suppose \(h=f*g\) and prefix sums of \(h\) and \(g\) are cheap. Summing the convolution and swapping variables gives

\[H(N)=\sum_{d\le N}f(d)G(\lfloor N/d\rfloor).\]

If \(g(1)=1\), isolate \(d=1\):

\[F(N)=H(N)-\sum_{d=2}^{N} f(d)G(\lfloor N/d\rfloor).\]

Often it is more convenient to swap the names of \(f,g\) so that the unknown prefix appears as \(F(\lfloor N/d\rfloor)\) and the coefficient can be summed over quotient blocks.

Mertens and totient prefixes

From \(\mu*1=ε\), for \(N≥1\),

\[M(N)=\sum_{n\le N}\mu(n) =1-\sum_{l=2}^{N}(r-l+1)M(\lfloor N/l\rfloor), \quad r=\left\lfloor N/\lfloor N/l\rfloor\right\rfloor.\]

From \(\varphi*1=id\), with \(T(N)=N(N+1)/2\),

\[\Phi(N)=T(N)-\sum_{l=2}^{N}(r-l+1)\Phi(\lfloor N/l\rfloor).\]

Precompute both functions to \(B\) by linear sieve and memoize only larger quotient states. A cutoff around \(N^{2/3}\) gives the classic practical complexity near \(O(N^{2/3})\), though tuning and the query model affect constants.

The quadratic scale of \(\sum\varphi(n)\) requires wide intermediates. Reduce modulo the problem modulus inside every operation if that is what the statement asks.

Derivation workflow

  1. Name the wanted prefix \(F\).
  2. Find \(g\) so \(h=f*g\) has an elementary prefix.
  3. Sum \(h(n)\) for \(n≤N\), swap \(n=dk\), and isolate \(F(N)\).
  4. Express coefficient sums on \([l,r]\) with prefix formulas.
  5. Confirm every recursive argument is \(\lfloor N/l\rfloor\) and smaller than \(N\).

3. Prime-sum sieve: the engine inside Min_25

Let the distinct quotient states be \(w=N/l\). Initialize

\[g_k(w)=\sum_{i=2}^{w}i^{k}.\]

Process primes \(p\) in increasing order. For every state \(w≥p²\), subtract numbers whose smallest prime factor is \(p\):

\[g_k(w)\mathrel{-}=p^{k}\left(g_k(\lfloor w/p\rfloor)-S_k(p-1)\right),\]

where \(S_k(p-1)=\sum_{q<p,q prime}q^{k}\). After processing all \(p≤\sqrt{N}\), \(g_k(w)=\sum_{q≤w,q prime}q^{k}\).

Why \(p²\)? Any composite still being removed has least prime factor \(p\), hence it is \(p·x\) with \(x≥p\). Why subtract \(S_k(p-1)\)? Those smaller-prime cofactors were never eligible in this stage. Updating states in descending order prevents using values already modified by the current prime.

For \(k=0\), this computes \(\pi(w)\). Its practical complexity for one \(N\) is about \(O(N^{3/4}/\log N)\) time and \(O(\sqrt{N})\) memory.

4. Full Min_25 sieve

Assume \(f\) is multiplicative, each \(f(p^{e})\) is computable in \(O(1)\), and

\[f(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}c_kp^{k}\]

for small fixed \(K\). Run the prime-sum sieve for each required power \(k\); combine the arrays to obtain

\[G_f(x)=\sum_{p\le x}f(p).\]

Then enumerate integers by their least prime. Define \(S(x,y)\) as the sum of \(f(n)\) for \(2≤n≤x\) whose least prime is at least \(p_y\). Its prime-only base contribution is \(G_f(x)-G_f(p_{y-1})\). For every possible least prime \(p_i\), enumerate its exponent:

\[ S(x,y)=G_f(x)-G_f(p_{y-1})+ \sum_{\substack{i\ge y\\p_i^2\le x}} \sum_{\substack{e\ge1\\p_i^{e+1}\le x}} \left(f(p_i^e)S(\lfloor x/p_i^e\rfloor,i+1)+f(p_i^{e+1})\right). \]

Finally \(1+S(N,0)\) is the prefix including \(f(1)\). The separate \(f(pe*p)\) term represents the pure next prime power; \(S(x/pe,i+1)\) supplies a nontrivial cofactor with larger least prime. This decomposition is unique.

Typical complexity is \(O(K N^{3/4}/\log N)\) for the prime sums plus roughly \(O(N^{3/4}/\log N)\) enumeration, with \(O((K+1)\sqrt{N})\) storage. Exact bounds and constants depend on the implementation and \(f\).

Applicability signals. \(N\) is around \(10^{9}–10^{12}\), the statement gives a multiplicative function through its prime powers, and its value at a prime is polynomial (examples: \(\varphi(p)=p-1\), \(\mu(p)=-1\), \(\sigma_k(p)=1+p^{k}\)).

Non-applicable cases. If \(f(p)\) depends on an arbitrary property of \(p\) for which no fast prime prefix exists, standard Min_25 has no base data. If only \(\mu/\varphi\) prefixes are needed, Du Jiao is simpler.

5. Powerful-number support

A positive integer is powerful if every prime dividing it has exponent at least two. The count through \(N\) is \(O(\sqrt{N})\): uniquely write

\[n=a^2b^3,\qquad b\text{ square-free}.\]

This sparsity yields another generic strategy. Choose an easy multiplicative \(g\) agreeing with \(f\) on primes, and define

\[h=f*g^{-1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f=h*g.\]

At a prime, \(h(p)=f(p)-g(p)=0\). Multiplicativity then forces \(h(n)=0\) whenever any prime has exponent exactly one, so \(h\) is supported on powerful numbers. Therefore

\[F(N)=\sum_{d\le N\atop d\text{ powerful}}h(d)G(\lfloor N/d\rfloor).\]

Compute local prime-power coefficients from

\[f(p^{e})=\sum_{j=0}^{e}h(p^{j})g(p^{e-j}),\]

so \(h(1)=1\), \(h(p)=0\), and for \(e≥2\),

\[h(p^{e})=f(p^{e})-\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}h(p^{j})g(p^{e-j}).\]

Enumerate powerful numbers by DFS over increasing primes, choosing exponent zero or at least two; accumulate \(h(d)G(N/d)\). Prune when \(d·p²>N\). Alternatively enumerate the unique \(a²b³\) representation when the coefficient is easier to recover that way.

This method is attractive when \(G(x)\) is elementary or already available and \(h(p^{e})\) is cheap. Complexity is commonly around \(O(\sqrt{N} \operatorname{polylog} N)\) plus the cost of obtaining \(G\) and primes, but must be derived for the exact enumeration.

A common special case: perfect powers

To count integers representable as \(a^{b}\) with \(a,b>1\), enumerate exponents but remove duplicate representations. Every exponent has a square-free “primitive” gcd structure; Möbius inversion over exponents or counting by maximal exponent avoids double counting. Integer kth roots must be corrected with exact 128-bit power comparisons—floating-point \(pow\) alone is unsafe near \(10^{18}\).

6. Choosing a method

Signal First choice Reason
known simple \(f*g=h\) Du Jiao shortest recurrence, quotient memoization
polynomial \(f(p)\) and arbitrary manageable \(f(p^{e})\) Min_25 sieves prime contributions then enumerates least primes
can match \(f(p)\) with an easy \(g\); differences begin at \(p²\) powerful-number sieve transformed support is sparse
only prime count/sums are wanted prime-sum core no full multiplicative DFS needed

Pitfalls

  • Prove the convolution identity before optimizing it; a swapped divisor argument silently computes the wrong function.
  • In prime-sum DP, process \(w\) descending and only while \(w≥p²\).
  • Distinguish “primes \(<p\)” from “primes \(≤p\)” in the base subtraction.
  • Min_25 recursion must enforce strictly increasing prime factors after choosing the least prime.
  • Include \(f(1)\) exactly once.
  • Use 128-bit arithmetic for \(p²\), prime-power growth, polynomial sums, and unmodded answers.
  • Modular polynomial sums may require division by \(2,6,…\); use integer formulas before reduction if the modulus is not guaranteed coprime to denominators.
  • Hash-map memoization should be cleared between unrelated \(N\) values unless states are keyed by the full query.

2. A Simple Math Problem — Luogu P3768

  • Level: Capstone · Native: provincial-team problem · Language: Chinese
  • Prerequisites: totient convolution, Du Jiao sieve, polynomial block sums.
  • Editorial: Luogu solution index (Chinese)
Hint 1 Rewrite the gcd-weighted double sum by extracting the gcd and using a totient identity.
Hint 2 The remaining expression needs prefix sums of a weighted totient function, not plain \(\varphi\).
Hint 3 Derive its convolution recurrence, evaluate polynomial coefficients over quotient blocks, and reduce throughout modulo the given (not necessarily prime) modulus.
