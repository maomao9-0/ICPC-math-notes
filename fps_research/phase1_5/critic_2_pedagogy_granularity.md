## Dimension 1: Chapter Count, Granularity & Cognitive Load Audit (20 vs. 24 vs. 26 Chapters)

A textbook targeting competitive programmers from `Stepping Stone` (Codeforces Div. 2D / ABC F) to `Mastery` (IOI / NOI / ICPC World Finals / Universal Cup) must obey the **Single-Cognitive-Pivot Principle**:
> *Every chapter must introduce at most ONE new algebraic ring/representation OR one cohesive family of combinatorial/algorithmic reductions, supported by a full 7–8 problem 4-tier ladder.*

When a chapter crams three unrelated algebraic structures into 8 problem slots, each structure receives only 2–3 problems—destroying the 4-tier progression (`Stepping Stone` $\to$ `Intermediate` $\to$ `Advanced` $\to$ `Mastery`) and creating an vertical cliff between consecutive problems. Below is a domain-by-domain granularity audit across Proposals A, B, and C.

### 1.2 Domain Audit 2: Unlabelled Multisets, Euler Transform & Pólya Cycle Index vs. Bitwise FWT / SOS Transform

Look closely at what happens in **Chapter 04 of Proposal A** and **Chapter 04 of Proposal C**:
- In **Proposal A (`Ch 04: Unlabelled Multisets, Harmonic Sieve, Pólya Cycle Index & Bitwise Convolutions`)**, the chapter starts with the Unlabelled Multiset (`MSET`) operator $\prod (1 - x^k)^{-a_k} = \exp\left(\sum_{j \ge 1}\frac{A(x^j)}{j}\right)$ (`Luogu P4389 付公主的背包`), Burnside's Lemma, and Pólya's Cycle Index $Z(C_n)/Z(D_n)$ (`Luogu P4980`, `CF 1065E`)... and then suddenly pivots mid-chapter to **Bitwise OR/AND/XOR Fast Walsh–Hadamard Transforms (FWT) and Zero-Divisor Pairs $(a, c) = a \cdot 0^c$** (`ABC 367 F`, `CF 662C`, `ABC 212 H`)!

Merging them into one textbook chapter violates `AGENTS.md`'s rule against preserving research-wave artifacts:
  1. **Mathematical mismatch**: The Euler Transform $\exp\left(\sum_{k \ge 1}\frac{A(x^k)}{k}\right)$ is an operation on $K[[x]]$ rooted in Pólya's cycle index of the symmetric group $S_n$ and harmonic divisor sieves ($O(N \log N)$), whereas FWT/SOS is discrete Fourier analysis over the boolean group algebra $K[\mathbb{Z}_2^d]$!

### 1.4 Domain Audit 4: Causal/Online Convolutions (CDQ & Relaxed) vs. Implicit Functional Equations (Newton) vs. Elementary $O(NK)$ Sparse ODEs

This is one of the sharpest architectural divergences between the three proposals:

1. **Where does the Elementary $O(NK)$ Sparse Power / Logarithmic-Derivative Trick ($P G' = \alpha P' G$) belong?**
   - In **Proposal A**, the $O(NK)$ sparse polynomial power recurrence (`CF 1096G — Lucky Tickets`, `yukicoder 1939`) is delayed until **Chapter 19** (`Part V: Advanced Sparse FPS Composition, Creative Telescoping & Truncated Taylor ODEs`). That is **far too late**: `CF 1096G` is a Div. 2E /入门 FPS problem that needs zero NTTs if done in $O(NK)$ via $P(x) G'(x) = \alpha P'(x) G(x)$!
   - In **Proposal C**, `CF 1096G` is placed in **Chapter 10 (`10-1`)**, right alongside `LOJ #6728` ($p$-adic indicial root lifting for P-recursive sequences, `10-7`) and `UOJ #514` (`EI 科技` truncated Taylor ODEs, `10-8`)! Putting a Div. 2E 10-line loop (`CF 1096G`) in the same 8-problem ladder as Universal OJ #514 (`EI 科技`, one of the hardest ODE problems in competitive programming history) creates an absurd difficulty cliff.
   - In **Proposal B**, the elementary $O(NK)$ sparse `inv`/`ln`/`exp`/`pow` recurrence is taught in **Chapter 04** (`Part I`, right after polynomial `inv`, `ln`, `exp`), while advanced D-Finite / Holonomic / P-recursive series are taught in **Chapter 19** (`Part V`).
   - **Verdict**: **Proposal B's two-stage split of ODEs is pedagogically optimal!** In **Part I (`Canonical Ch 03`)**, right after introducing the formal derivative $D_x$, formal integral $\int$, and $\ln/\exp$, teach the **Elementary $O(NK)$ Sparse Recurrence Table** ($P G = 1$, $G = \ln P$, $G = \exp P$, $G = P^\alpha \implies P G' = \alpha P' G$). Then, in **Part V (`Canonical Ch 19` & `Ch 20`)**, teach the full theory of **D-Finite (Holonomic) Series, P-Recursive Unrolling, Non-Linear ODE Linearization, Creative Telescoping & `EI 科技` Truncated Taylor ODEs**.

2. **Should Causal Convolutions (CDQ NTT & Relaxed Convolution) and Newton-Raphson Iteration be in the same chapter or separate chapters?**
   - **Proposal A (`Ch 09`)** crams Semi-Online CDQ NTT, Full-Online Relaxed Convolution (`lowbit`), 1D Quadratic Newton Iteration ($F_{2d} = F_d - \Phi/\Phi'$), Coupled $2\times 2$ Jacobian Newton, AND Integrating-Factor First-Order ODE Newton (`UOJ #50`) into a **single 8-problem chapter**. That is 4 major algorithmic templates in 8 problems!
   - **Proposal B** splits Newton Iteration (`Ch 12`), CDQ NTT (`Ch 18`), and Online Relaxed Convolution (`Ch 20`), but places CDQ and Relaxed Convolution in Part V *after* Graph Enumeration (`Ch 15`), forcing Graph Enumeration to rely solely on Newton Iteration even when CDQ/Relaxed is simpler.
   - **Proposal C** places **CDQ NTT & Online Relaxed Convolution** together in **`Ch 11`** (before Part IV Walks/Graphs!), and gives **Implicit Functional Equations, Pólya Cycle-Index Operators & Newton-Raphson Iteration** its own dedicated chapter in **`Ch 14`**.
   - **Verdict**: **Proposal C's separation of `CDQ + Online Relaxed Convolution` (1 Chapter) from `Implicit Equations + Newton Iteration` (1 Chapter)—both placed in Part III *before* Tree/Graph Enumeration—is the ideal cognitive balance!**
     - In **Causal Convolutions (`Canonical Ch 09`)**, the student focuses 100% on *time-ordered / index-ordered recurrences* ($O(N \log^2 N)$ offline CDQ divide-and-conquer, the $l > 0$ symmetric self-convolution rule, and van der Hoeven's 0-indexed `lowbit(q+1)` dyadic block schedule for full-online `RelaxedConvolution`).
     - In **Implicit Equations & Newton Iteration (`Canonical Ch 10`)**, the student focuses 100% on *valuation-doubling in $K[[x]]/(x^{2^k})$* ($O(N \log N)$ quadratic convergence, `sqrt`, `exp`, Pólya harmonic invariance $F(x^k) \bmod x^{2m}$ depending only on degree $< m$, integrating-factor ODE Newton `UOJ #50`, and $2 \times 2$ Jacobian systems `fps_24_x`).
