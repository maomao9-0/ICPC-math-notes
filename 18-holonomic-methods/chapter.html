18 — Holonomic Methods: D-Finite Series and P-Recursive Sequences

Why this topic matters

Some counting sequences evolve in a fixed number of dimensions but need multipliers depending on the current index. Factorials, central binomial coefficients, and involution counts have this form. Constant-coefficient recurrence tools cannot directly express “there are \(n\) choices for the partner of the new label.” Polynomial-coefficient recurrences can.

Prerequisites: polynomials, formal differentiation, generating functions, and linear recurrences and systems. Work over a characteristic-zero field such as \(\mathbb Q\) when deriving the theory. Reduction modulo a prime comes after checking every denominator.

The objectives are to translate a differential equation into a recurrence with correct boundaries, prove a guessed recurrence, use closure properties with their hypotheses, and evaluate a distant term without pretending an index-dependent matrix is constant.

Definitions and a first comparison

A sequence \((a_n)_{n\ge0}\) is P-recursive if there are polynomials \(P_0,\ldots,P_r\), not all zero, such that

\[\sum_{j=0}^rP_j(n)a_{n+j}=0\qquad(n\ge n_0)\]

for some fixed starting index \(n_0\ge0\). Here \(r\) and the polynomial degrees are fixed, independent of the target index. The leading coefficient for forward evaluation is \(P_r(n)\).

An ordinary formal series \(F(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nx^n\) is D-finite if polynomials \(Q_0,\ldots,Q_s\), not all zero, satisfy

\[\sum_{j=0}^sQ_j(x)F^{(j)}(x)=0.\]

No numerical convergence is required: this is an identity of formal coefficients. D-finite means the series obeys a linear differential equation of finite order. Holonomic is often used for this framework; multivariate versions require a definition involving all partial derivatives, not just one equation in one variable.

Fibonacci numbers have a constant-coefficient recurrence. Factorials instead satisfy \(a_{n+1}=(n+1)a_n\). Both are P-recursive, but factorials do not satisfy a nontrivial constant-coefficient recurrence: such recurrences have at most exponential times polynomial growth, whereas factorials grow faster.

Translation between series and sequences

Differentiation attaches the original exponent as a multiplier. For \(i,j\ge0\),

\[[x^n]\,x^iF^{(j)}(x)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{(n-i+j)!}{(n-i)!}\,a_{n-i+j},&n\ge i,\\ 0,&n<i. \end{cases}\]

The factorial quotient denotes the product of \(j\) consecutive integers, so it is a polynomial multiplier and does not require modular inversion. To derive it, differentiate \(a_mx^m\) exactly \(j\) times and solve \(m-j+i=n\) for \(m\). The condition \(m\ge j\) becomes \(n\ge i\).

Thus every polynomial differential equation gives a polynomial-coefficient recurrence once the initial zero cases are separated. Conversely, let \(\theta=x\,d/dx\). Then \(\theta F=\sum n a_nx^n\), and \(P(\theta)\) multiplies coefficient \(a_n\) by \(P(n)\). Shifts in a recurrence correspond to powers of \(x\) and removal of finitely many initial terms. Summing the recurrence therefore gives a differential equation with a polynomial right-hand side. Differentiating more times than that polynomial’s degree makes the equation homogeneous. This proves, in characteristic zero,

\[F\text{ is D-finite}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(a_n)\text{ is P-recursive}.\]

Worked example: central binomial coefficients

Let \(a_n=\binom{2n}{n}\), the number of ways to choose \(n\) objects from two labelled groups of \(n\) objects. The factorial ratio gives

\[\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} =\frac{(2n+2)(2n+1)}{(n+1)^2} =\frac{2(2n+1)}{n+1}.\]

Over \(\mathbb Q\), multiply through to obtain \((n+1)a_{n+1}=2(2n+1)a_n\). Starting at \(a_0=1\), this gives \(1,2,6,20,70\). Multiplying by \(x^n\) and summing for \(n\ge0\) yields

\[F'=4xF'+2F,\qquad (1-4x)F'-2F=0.\]

The unique formal solution with \(F(0)=1\) is \((1-4x)^{-1/2}\), interpreted by the formal binomial expansion. The recurrence and differential equation encode the same coefficient choices.

A denominator failure. Modulo 3, at \(n=2\) this recurrence becomes \(0\cdot a_3=10a_2=0\). It gives no information about \(a_3\), whose actual value is \(20\equiv2\). A valid characteristic-zero identity can lose its ability to determine the next term after reduction.

Worked example: involutions

An involution is a permutation in which each cycle has length one or two. Let \(I_n\) count involutions on \(n\) labels. The final label is either fixed, leaving \(I_{n-1}\) choices, or paired with one of \(n-1\) other labels, leaving \(I_{n-2}\) choices. Therefore

\[I_n=I_{n-1}+(n-1)I_{n-2}\quad(n\ge2),\qquad I_0=I_1=1.\]

The first terms are \(1,1,2,4,10,26\). The labelled component model gives the EGF

\[E(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}I_n\frac{x^n}{n!}=\exp(x+x^2/2),\qquad E'=(1+x)E.\]

Equating coefficients of \(x^n/n!\) gives \(I_{n+1}=I_n+nI_{n-1}\), with the \(n=0\) term handled separately. This example also shows why OGF and EGF coefficients must not be silently interchanged: the ordinary coefficient of \(E\) is \(I_n/n!\), not \(I_n\).

Closure: finite-dimensional derivatives

D-finiteness is equivalent to the derivatives \(F,F',F'',\ldots\) spanning a finite-dimensional vector space over rational functions in \(x\). A differential equation expresses sufficiently high derivatives in terms of a fixed finite list; differentiating the relation preserves a finite span after rational coefficients are allowed.

This viewpoint proves useful closure rules. For two D-finite series \(F,G\):

  • The derivatives of \(F+G\) lie in the sum of their finite derivative spaces.
  • By the product rule, derivatives of \(FG\) lie in the span of finitely many products \(F^{(i)}G^{(j)}\) from derivative bases.
  • Differentiation stays in the same space; an antiderivative needs at most one additional basis element.

Every algebraic formal series is D-finite in characteristic zero. If \(y\) satisfies a minimal polynomial equation \(P(x,y)=0\), implicit differentiation gives \(y'=-P_x/P_y\) in the finite extension field \(\mathbb Q(x,y)\). The denominator is a nonzero field element because characteristic zero makes the minimal polynomial separable. All further derivatives remain in that finite-dimensional extension, so they must be linearly dependent over \(\mathbb Q(x)\).

Composition \(F(G(x))\) is D-finite when \(F\) is D-finite and \(G\) is algebraic with \(G(0)=0\): the chain rule keeps derivatives in a finite span over the finite algebraic extension generated by \(G\). The zero constant term makes formal substitution well-defined. Arbitrary composition of D-finite series is not closed; \(\exp(\exp x)\) is a classical analytic counterexample. In purely formal calculations, use the well-defined counterpart \(\exp(\exp x-1)\), which has the same non-D-finiteness.

A further useful theorem is that the diagonal \(\sum_{n\ge0}[x_1^n\cdots x_k^n]R\,t^n\) of a rational function \(R\) regular at the origin is D-finite in characteristic zero. This is a case of Lipshitz’s deeper diagonal theorem; see the research paper Single-exponential bounds for diagonals of D-finite power series for a modern treatment. It is not a claim that setting all variables equal extracts a diagonal. For instance, the diagonal of \(1/(1-x-y)\) is \(\sum\binom{2n}{n}t^n\), whereas substituting \(x=y=t\) gives \(1/(1-2t)\). The coefficients are different.

A boundary of the method. Unrestricted labelled simple graph counts \(a_n=2^{\binom n2}\) are not P-recursive over \(\mathbb Q\). A fixed polynomial recurrence, solved for its last term beyond all integer roots of its leading coefficient, bounds each new absolute value by \(Cn^K\) times the maximum of a fixed number of previous values. Iteration gives a bound of the form \(C_1^n(n!)^{K_1}\), whose logarithm is \(O(n\log n)\). The graph count has logarithm proportional to \(n^2\), contradicting that bound. A graph interpretation alone does not justify D-finite guessing.

Guessing and certification

For proposed order \(r\) and degree \(d\), write \(P_j(n)=\sum_{k=0}^d c_{jk}n^k\). Each known recurrence window gives one homogeneous linear equation in the \((r+1)(d+1)\) unknowns \(c_{jk}\). Solve over exact rationals or a selected prime field. Enough equations of suitable rank are needed; merely having more terms than unknowns does not promise a unique relation. Remove a common polynomial factor and choose a scalar normalization when comparing candidates.

Unused terms are a valuable bug check, not a proof. For any finite prefix, a second sequence can agree on that prefix and then differ. Certification must connect the guessed relation to the original object by a differential equation, an algebraic identity, or a telescoping certificate.

A complete telescoping certificate

Let \(S_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk\). Guessing suggests \(S_{n+1}=2S_n\). Extend binomial coefficients by zero outside \(0\le k\le n\), and define \(H(n,k)=-\binom n{k-1}\). Pascal’s identity gives

\[\binom{n+1}k-2\binom nk =\binom n{k-1}-\binom nk =H(n,k+1)-H(n,k).\]

Sum for \(k=0,\ldots,n+1\). The right side telescopes to \(H(n,n+2)-H(n,0)=0\). Hence the recurrence holds for all \(n\ge0\), and \(S_0=1\) gives \(S_n=2^n\). The endpoint convention is part of the certificate.

Creative telescoping searches for such a relation after combining several shifted summands with polynomial coefficients in \(n\). A hypergeometric summand has rational successive-term ratios, which makes the certificate search algebraic. Finite sums of properly controlled hypergeometric terms are strong candidates, but summation bounds and boundary contributions must be included. “It is a binomial sum” is a recognition signal, not a universal proof of a particular recurrence.

Evaluation: from a linear scan to square-root blocks

For \(P_r(n)\ne0\), solve the recurrence for \(a_{n+r}\) from the previous \(r\) values. A direct scan takes \(O(rN)\) field operations after coefficient evaluation (or \(O(rdN)\) with direct degree-\(d\) polynomial evaluation) and \(O(r)\) state. Initial values must cover the starting window and every singular index needs separate mathematical handling. Over a general ring, the leading coefficient must be a unit, not merely nonzero.

For a distant term, form a column state \(v_t\) of \(r\) consecutive values so \(v_{t+1}=A(t)v_t\). Entries of the companion matrix are rational functions. Clear a scalar denominator:

\[A(t)=D(t)^{-1}B(t),\]

where \(B\) is a polynomial matrix of fixed degree \(d\) and \(D(t)\ne0\) at every required step. Choose a block length \(b\) and form the polynomial matrix

\[C_b(X)=B(X+b-1)\cdots B(X+1)B(X).\]

Its entry degrees are at most \(bd\). Build it by a product tree, then evaluate its entries at \(0,b,2b,\ldots\) for the complete blocks. The invariant is chronological order: the earliest transition is rightmost because it acts first on a column vector. Multiply the resulting constant matrices in that same order and handle the last fewer-than-\(b\) transitions separately. Compute denominator products similarly, and divide only by their nonzero total.

For fixed \(r,d\), a polynomial product tree and fast multipoint evaluation cost \(\widetilde O(b+N/b)\) field operations, and the constant block products and tail have the same bound. Taking \(b\) near \(\sqrt N\) yields \(\widetilde O(\sqrt N)\). The tilde hides logarithmic factors and assumes quasi-linear polynomial multiplication. Matrix order and coefficient degree are fixed here; if they grow with \(N\), their costs must be restored. A monic-remainder multipoint tree permits repeated evaluation points; a faster arithmetic-progression interpolation routine may instead require distinct differences and additional nonzero shift denominators. State which library contract you use.

Failed approach. Replacing \(A(N-1)\cdots A(0)\) by \(A(0)^N\) is invalid: even the scalar recurrence \(a_{n+1}=(n+1)a_n\) would incorrectly yield one instead of \(N!\). Grouping consecutive transitions preserves their changing multipliers; ordinary matrix exponentiation does not.

Problem-solving checklist

  • Are you studying OGF coefficients or factorial-normalized EGF coefficients?
  • What proves the sequence belongs to a finite recurrence class?
  • What are the recurrence’s exact first index and initial values?
  • Does the leading coefficient vanish at any required index modulo the chosen prime?
  • Is the guessed identity certified beyond sample agreement?
  • Do order, coefficient degree, and block-polynomial degrees fit the intended complexity?

Exercises and editorial guidance

  1. Translation. For \(F'=F\) and \(F(0)=1\), derive its coefficient recurrence.

    Hint: extract \(x^n\). Solution: \((n+1)a_{n+1}=a_n\), so \(a_n=1/n!\) over \(\mathbb Q\). This is not the sequence \(a_n=1\), which would be the EGF-labelled interpretation.

  2. Nontrivial component model. Give each two-cycle of an involution weight \(z\). Derive its recurrence.

    Solution: fixed final label contributes \(I_{n-1}(z)\); pairing contributes \((n-1)zI_{n-2}(z)\). Thus \(I_n(z)=I_{n-1}(z)+(n-1)zI_{n-2}(z)\) with \(I_0=I_1=1\). Its EGF is \(\exp(x+zx^2/2)\). At \(n=4\), \(I_4(z)=1+6z+3z^2\).

  3. Independent identity. Prove \(\sum_k\binom nk^2=\binom{2n}n\), then derive a recurrence for the sum.

    Hint: choose \(n\) objects from two groups of size \(n\). Solution: selecting \(k\) from the first forces \(n-k\) from the second, giving the stated double count. The central-binomial recurrence derived earlier is therefore a certificate for the sum, without trusting guessed samples.

  4. Foundation application — Carry Bit, CF1761D. Track carry-in \(c\in\{0,1\}\) and mark a carry-out by \(y\). From carry zero, three bit pairs give next carry zero and one gives carry one. From carry one, one pair gives zero and three give one. Thus the column-state transition is

    \[T(y)=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\y&3y\end{pmatrix}.\]

    Start with \((1,0)^T\) and sum both states after \(n\) bits. This gives a rational bivariate generating function in length and carry count, but an \(O(nk)\) coefficient DP is too slow for the full statement. Decomposing the carry-bit string into runs gives a one-sum binomial solution, derived in Chapter 19. The problem illustrates a useful restraint: a rational model is valuable even when no advanced recurrence acceleration is necessary.

  5. Advanced modelling — Chinese Elephant Chess, QOJ60. Interpret rows and columns as the two vertex colours of a bipartite graph with maximum degree two. Components are isolated vertices, paths, and even cycles. Use separate EGF variables for row and column labels; setting the two variables equal does not extract an arbitrary rectangular coefficient. Chapter 19 derives the component series, a constant-order differential operator, and a linear coefficient scan for the rectangular case.

  6. Frontier application — Grafy, QOJ11139. The verified statement counts simple loop-free directed graphs with every in/out degree two; its bound is \(n\le500\). Chapter 19 derives a cubic inclusion–exclusion sum from stub permutations. A shorter certified holonomic recurrence is an optional extension, not a requirement of the verified statement.

Summary and references

Object or operation What must be supplied

D-finite equation Coefficient domain and nontrivial linear differential operator
