Executive Overview: The 321-Problem Textbook Architecture

This capstone reference serves as the master navigational hub and bijective index for all 321 competitive programming problems cataloged across the textbook’s research waves (Waves 1A–5B). To guarantee both pedagogical rigor and strict anti-template-bloat hygiene, the 321 problems are partitioned into two disjoint universes:

  1. The 254 Accepted Non-Template Curriculum Problems (#001–#254): Every accepted problem requires a genuine combinatorial model, algebraic reformulation, or structural generating-function reduction before any polynomial library call is made. These 254 unique problems are bijectively assigned (0 duplicates, 0 omissions, 0 forward-reference violations) across three complementary learning arenas:
    • 192 Chapter Ladder Problems (24 Chapters × 8 Problems): Every chapter (Ch 01–Ch 24) concludes with an inline 8-problem, 4-tier progressive ladder — 2 Stepping Stone (SS-1, SS-2), 2 Intermediate (Int-1, Int-2), 2 Advanced (Adv-1, Adv-2), and 2 Mastery (Mas-1, Mas-2) — each equipped with three progressive hints (Diagnostic Signal, Algebraic Setup & Scratchpad, Coefficient Extraction & Contract) and a complete first-principles derivation.
    • 48 Part-End Blind Topic-Group Checkpoint Problems (6 Checkpoints × 8 Problems, CP-I–CP-VI): Placed at the end of each of the six textbook Parts (cp01–cp06), presenting spoiler-free problem statements that train diagnostic recognition before revealing the target chapter and algebraic reduction.
    • 14 Grandmaster Multi-Chapter Synthesis Reserve Problems (Syn-01–Syn-14): Housed in the companion Grandmaster Synthesis Reserve, where each problem is solved in full via two orthogonal algebraic paradigms from different Parts of the book.
  2. The 67 Quarantined Bare-Template Verification Problems (R-01–R-67): Pure black-box polynomial/FPS unit tests (such as Library Checker convolution_mod, inv_of_formal_power_series, exp_of_formal_power_series, multipoint_evaluation, composition_of_formal_power_series_large, and Luogu 【模板】 drills) are strictly excluded from all 254 pedagogical problem slots. Instead, each is routed in Section 4 to its exact home chapter (Ch 01–Ch 24) as a 1-line library verification test so you can validate your C++ polynomial primitives before tackling the chapter’s combinatorial ladder.
