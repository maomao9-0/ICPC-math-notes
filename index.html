Three self-contained textbooks for expert competitive programmers

This site hosts three independent, self-contained textbooks written from algebraic and combinatorial definitions for competitive programmers who already have a tested algorithm library. Use the top navigation bar or the links below to switch between textbooks; each book maintains its own sidebar navigation, study plan, notation reference, and problem archive.

Textbook I · Formal Power Series & Generating Functions (Ch 01–Ch 24)

Enter the Formal Power Series Textbook → · Study Plan (Ch 01–Ch 24) · Notation & 16 Failure Modes · 321-Problem Archive · Bibliography & Rosetta Stone

A rigorous, zero-forward-reference 6-Part, 24-Chapter monograph on Formal Power Series (\(K[[x]]\), \(K((x))\)) and Generating Functions for ICPC, Codeforces, AtCoder, and Universal Cup contests:

  • Scope: Valuation topology \(\nu(F)\) and 1D/coupled Newton iteration (inv, sqrt, ln, exp, pow), \(O(NK)\) sparse differential recurrences, the unlabeled (\(\operatorname{SEQ}, \operatorname{MSET}, \operatorname{PSET}\)) and labeled (\(\operatorname{SET}, \operatorname{CYC}\), Boxed Product) Symbolic Method, Bostan–Mori and Berlekamp–Massey, Snake Oil and Stirling/Taylor shifts, roots-of-unity filters and cyclotomic rings, run-length and Goulden–Jackson cluster sieves, online CDQ/relaxed convolutions, formal Laurent residues and 6-form Lagrange Inversion, cactus/block-cut/DAG/SCC graphical series, stochastic PGFs, 1D/2D kernel methods, the 5-ring bivariate hierarchy and Furstenberg diagonals, D-finite ODEs and \(p\)-adic indicial lifting, Tellegen transposition (mulT, multipoint eval/interp), Kinoshita–Li \(O(N \log^2 N)\) power_projection/compose/revert, Static Top Tree DP, \(q\)-analogues, Set Power Series \(\mathcal{R}_n\), and Dirichlet series \(\mathcal{D}_f\).
  • Practice & Assessment: 192 inline chapter ladder problems (2 Stepping Stones, 2 Intermediate, 2 Advanced, 2 Mastery per chapter), 6 Two-Stage Blind Topic-Group Checkpoints (CP-I–CP-VI, 48 problems), and a 14-problem Multi-Perspective Synthesis Reserve (Syn-01–Syn-14).

Textbook II · Network Flows, Minimum Cuts, Matching, Duality & Modelling (Ch 01–Ch 24)

Enter the Network Flows & Modelling Textbook → · Study Plan & 3-Axis Diagnostic · Notation & 24 Failure Modes · 320-Problem Archive · Bibliography & Rosetta Stone

A rigorous, zero-forward-reference 6-Part, 24-Chapter modelling-first monograph dedicated to recognizing hidden flow, minimum-cut, matching, and LP-dual structures in problem statements with no graphs mentioned at all (Direction A: Non-Graph -> Flow) and eliminating flow solvers into \(O(N \log N)\) algorithms when \(N \le 2 \times 10^5\) (Direction B: Flow -> Fast Algorithm):

  • Scope: Flow-cut duality, Picard–Queyranne residual condensation DAGs, feasible vs. essential cut edges ([AHOI2009]), incremental flow rollback (退流, [SDOI2014] LIS), vertex/time splitting, lower-bound circulations (上下界网络流) and Hoffman’s theorem, König–Hall–Dilworth duality, Dulmage–Mendelsohn (DM) decomposition and bipartite walk games, Birkhoff–von Neumann and regular edge coloring (AGC 037 D), segment-tree Hall Marriage elimination (ARC 076 F), Maximum Weight Closure (最大权闭合子图), submodular pseudo-Boolean cut compilation (燃やす埋める), Bipartite Variable Inversion (二部グラフの片側反転, AGC 038 F — Two Permutations), multi-state unary threshold chains and disjunctive OR cuts (ARC 107 F — Sum of Abs, ARC 142 E — Pairing Wizards), Ishikawa convex Monge penalties, the Discrete Coarea Formula (CF 1427 G — One Billion Shades of Grey), 0-1 fractional programming (Dinkelbach), Maximum Density Subgraphs, planar \(s\)-\(t\) and \(k\)-terminal dual shortest paths ([NOI2010] 海拔, [CSP-S 2021] 交通规划), min-cut DP (CF 724 E, CF 1919 F2), Gomory–Hu cut trees, MCMF Johnson potentials, negative-cycle saturation, convex marginal edge splitting, Consecutive Equation Differencing ([NOI2008] 志愿者招募), cycle covers, dynamic scheduling, coordinate-rank slots (AGC 031 E), Manhattan \(2^d\) hubs (AGC 034 D), explicit LP duality, Totally Unimodular (TU) matrices, DS-accelerated flow graphs (线段树/主席树优化建图), Edmonds’ Blossom and Tutte matrix, Simulated MCMF (模拟费用流 / 反悔贪心), and \(L_p\) Isotonic Regression (保序回归).
  • Practice & Assessment: 192 inline chapter ladder problems (#001–#192, each compiled via the 4-Step “Zero Magic Gadgets” Standard), 6 Part-End Blind Recognition Checkpoints (CP-I–CP-VI, #193–#240), and a 16-problem Grandmaster Synthesis Reserve (#241–#256).
  • Start here: Network Flows Overview or directly at Ch 01 · Conservation Laws, Residual Graphs, and Max-Flow Min-Cut Duality.

Textbook III · Competitive Programming Mathematics (00–20)

Enter the Competitive Programming Mathematics Textbook → · Study Plan (00–20) · Notation & Contracts · Sources & Policy

A broad 21-Chapter foundation and synthesis curriculum covering the general mathematical pillars of competitive programming from first principles:

  • Part I · Arithmetic and counting (00–05): Foundations and proof, modular algebra, multiplicative number theory, combinatorics, binomials modulo anything, and group actions (Burnside/Pólya).
  • Part II · Algebraic engines (06–11): Polynomials and FFT/NTT, linear algebra, subset and divisor transforms, formal power series overview, generating functions overview, and set power series.
  • Part III · Models and synthesis (12–15): Probability and Markov chains, discrete sums and floor geometry, summatory sieves, and mixed capstones.
  • Part IV · Frontier topics (16–20): \(q\)-analogues and subspaces, finite-field polynomials, algebraic graph methods (Pfaffians/Tutte), holonomic methods, and frontier capstones.
  • Start here: Mathematics Overview or directly at 00 · Mathematical foundations and proof.
