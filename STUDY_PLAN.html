Learning path and study plan

This route assumes expert programming skills and teaches the mathematics from definitions. Chapter numbers retain the site’s existing addresses; the order below follows prerequisites. Time estimates are study suggestions, not promises of a contest rating. Spend longer wherever you cannot reconstruct a proof.

Prerequisites, difficulty, and recognition

“Core” describes a mathematical foundation, “advanced” combines foundations, and “frontier” needs substantial algebraic maturity. A core chapter can still prepare you for a very hard contest problem.

Chapter 01’s order algorithms accept a factorization as input; Chapter 02 explains its computation. This does not require knowing factorization algorithms before learning what an order means. Likewise, read probability’s elementary sections early if desired, postponing cyclic linear systems.

Weekly rhythm

  • Session A (90 min): derive the chapter’s main results by hand.
  • Session B (90 min): reproduce one key derivation, then solve a problem that requires recognizing or adapting it.
  • Sessions C/D (2–3 h each): one ordered problem per session. Record when each hint was opened.
  • Review (30 min): re-solve or verbally reconstruct one problem from two or six weeks earlier.

Stop a theory session when you can derive the result, state its preconditions, and explain which statement clues suggest it. Reading the same proof again is less valuable than deriving it on a blank page.

A useful problem postmortem

Keep each entry short enough that you will actually reread it:

Problem / date / time before first hint:
Recognition clue I missed:
Mathematical object and decisive identity:
Target complexity and why the naive form failed:
Implementation invariant or bug:
Re-solve dates (+14d, +42d):

Mastery means you can recover the solution from the first two lines after six weeks. Memorizing an editorial’s loop structure does not count.

Team-contest use

During capstone weeks, use a three-person simulation even when practicing alone:

  1. Spend 10 minutes producing only the mathematical model and complexity target.
  2. Spend 10 minutes trying to falsify assumptions: prime modulus, invertibility, truncation degree, independence, overflow, and forgotten zero cases.
  3. Only then implement. Write down which observations should be communicated to a teammate before coding starts.

For a real team, assign one person to derive, one to inspect algebraic contracts, and one to build a small oracle. Rotate these roles across problems.

Weeks 27–33: frontier extension

Begin only after passing the Chapter 15 checkpoint. These weeks are optional for regional preparation but valuable for World Finals and strong team contests.

30–31 Algebraic graph methods Derive the Tutte cancellation and state a numerical Schwartz–Zippel error bound.
32 Holonomic methods Convert an ODE to a P-recurrence and certify one guessed recurrence.
33 Frontier capstones Finish two timed attempts and re-solve one after seven days without topic labels.

24-Chapter Formal Power Series & Generating Functions Study Plan (Ch 01–Ch 24)

For readers specializing in Formal Power Series (FPS) and Generating Functions, or entering after 06 · Polynomials, the 6-Part, 24-Chapter FPS Textbook Track (Ch 01–Ch 24) provides a self-contained, zero-forward-reference progression from the formal power series ring \(K[[x]]\) to Kinoshita–Li \(O(N \log^2 N)\) composition, bivariate diagonals, D-finite differential operators, Static Top Tree DP, \(q\)-series, and Set Power Series.
